Iran has reportedly suspended negotiations with the United States and is threatening to block the Strait of Hormuz—a critical waterway through which nearly one-third of the world's seaborne oil passes—according to reports from Iran's Tasnim news agency citing government sources.

The move comes as Israel has intensified military operations in southern Lebanon, targeting what Israeli forces describe as sites used by Hezbollah militants. Israeli troops have pushed deeper into Lebanese territory, seizing strategic positions including the historic Beaufort Castle, a key vantage point in the region. Despite a ceasefire announced in mid-April, hostilities between Israel and Hezbollah have continued.

According to the report, Tehran made an end to the conflict in Lebanon a precondition for Pakistani-mediated negotiations with Washington. Iran is now demanding an "immediate cessation of hostilities" in both Lebanon and Gaza as conditions for resuming contact with U.S. officials. Tehran has also signaled readiness to disrupt maritime traffic through other strategic waterways, including the Bab al-Mandab Strait.

The two nations reached a fragile ceasefire in early April following more than a month of direct hostilities. Recent days have seen renewed military confrontations, with U.S. Central Command reporting strikes over the weekend in response to what it called "aggressive Iranian actions," including the downing of a U.S. drone. Iran maintains the unmanned aircraft violated its airspace, and responded with retaliatory strikes against a regional airbase used by American forces.
