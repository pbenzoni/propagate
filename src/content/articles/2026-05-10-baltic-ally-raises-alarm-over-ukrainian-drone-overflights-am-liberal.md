Estonian Defense Minister Hanno Pevkur has called on Ukraine to prevent its drones from entering the Baltic nation's airspace, highlighting growing concerns among NATO's northeastern members about uncrewed aircraft overflights during the ongoing conflict with Russia.

Over recent weeks, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, and Finland have all reported incidents of Ukrainian UAVs crossing into their territory. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo similarly told President Volodymyr Zelensky that such incursions are "unacceptable," reflecting broader unease about the sovereignty implications of uncontrolled drone operations near NATO borders.

Pevkur stated that Estonian authorities "will start dealing with this very quickly now," emphasizing that "the easiest way for the Ukrainians to keep their drones away from our territory is to control their activities better." Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna, while affirming Ukraine's right to self-defense, expressed concern about potential escalation risks, including the theoretical possibility of compromised drones.

The incidents underscore the complex challenges facing Ukraine's supporters as the conflict continues. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrey Sibiga has proposed sending technical teams to help strengthen regional airspace monitoring. Meanwhile, Latvia's defense minister has taken a more sympathetic stance, attributing the breaches to the ongoing war rather than Ukrainian negligence.

The situation reflects the delicate balance NATO allies must strike between supporting Ukraine's defensive operations and maintaining their own territorial integrity and security protocols. As drone warfare becomes increasingly central to modern conflict, establishing clear protocols for airspace management has emerged as a pressing diplomatic priority.
