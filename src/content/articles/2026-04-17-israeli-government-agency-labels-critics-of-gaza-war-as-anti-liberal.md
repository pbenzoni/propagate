An Israeli government ministry has published a list designating ten global figures as top "anti-Semitic and anti-Zionist influencers," sparking concerns about efforts to conflate legitimate criticism of government policy with bigotry. The Ministry for Diaspora Affairs and Combating Antisemitism's roster includes right-wing commentators Tucker Carlson and Candace Owens, climate activist Greta Thunberg, and British-Palestinian journalist Abdel Bari Atwan.

The ministry defined those listed as individuals without formal political positions but with significant social media followings. Rankings were determined by what the agency termed "influence" and "risk," based on the frequency of statements it deemed critical of Israel or its policies. Six of the ten individuals listed are based in the United States.

Abdel Bari Atwan, editor-in-chief of the UK-based Arabic newspaper Rai Al-Youm, who ranked fifth on the list, rejected the characterization entirely. "Israel is not above criticism," he told journalists. "To accuse anybody who is criticizing Israel of anti-Semitism is actually very, very frightening." Atwan noted he is himself a Semite and advocates for Jewish-Arab coexistence, while opposing what he described as "Israeli wars against Lebanon, against Iran, against Yemen, against Gaza."

The list's release comes as American public opinion on Israel has shifted significantly. According to a recent Pew Research Center poll, 60% of American adults now hold an unfavorable view of Israel, up from 53% the previous year—a trend that has accelerated amid the ongoing Gaza conflict and military operations against Iran. Atwan argued that the Israeli government's actions and its claim to represent all Jewish people worldwide are themselves damaging to Jewish communities globally.
