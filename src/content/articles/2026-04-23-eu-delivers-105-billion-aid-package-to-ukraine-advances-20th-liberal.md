The European Union has formally approved a €90 billion ($105 billion) emergency loan package for Ukraine covering 2026–2027, while simultaneously adopting its 20th round of sanctions against Russia. The move represents a significant breakthrough in European unity after months of diplomatic gridlock.

European Council President António Costa framed the dual action as part of a broader strategy "to achieve a just and lasting peace in Ukraine," emphasizing that increased pressure on Russia remains central to ending the conflict on terms that respect Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity.

The approval became possible after Hungary lifted its longstanding veto following recent political developments in Budapest. The loan had been the focus of a protracted standoff, with outgoing Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán previously blocking the disbursement in retaliation for oil supply disruptions via the Soviet-era Druzhba pipeline. The approval followed Ukraine's recent decision to restart Russian oil flows to EU countries including Hungary and Slovakia, which remain heavily reliant on such energy imports.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen pledged swift implementation of both the financial assistance and the new sanctions regime, while EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas emphasized the bloc's commitment to providing Ukraine "what it needs to hold its ground." The €90 billion loan is backed by joint EU borrowing and structured so that repayment would only be required if Ukraine receives war reparations from Russia—a financing mechanism designed to minimize fiscal burden while maintaining solidarity with the embattled nation.
