Slovakia's Prime Minister Robert Fico announced plans to demand the European Union compensate his country for military equipment donated to Ukraine by the previous government—a move that underscores his administration's increasingly pro-Kremlin stance and departure from European solidarity with Ukraine's democratic resistance.

Fico, who halted all Slovak military aid to Ukraine after taking power in 2023, accused his predecessor Eduard Heger's government of leaving Slovakia "completely naked" by donating fighter jets, air-defense systems, and Soviet-era tanks worth approximately €700 million to help Ukraine defend itself against Russian aggression. The donated equipment included MiG-29 jets and S-300 missile systems that Ukraine desperately needed to protect its civilian population from relentless bombardment.

The Prime Minister's position stands in stark contrast to the broad European consensus supporting Ukraine's fight for sovereignty and democratic values. Fico was the only EU leader to attend this year's Victory Day commemorations in Moscow, where he warned against a "new Iron Curtain"—rhetoric that aligns with Russian President Vladimir Putin's framing rather than the transatlantic alliance's defense of international law and human rights.

Fico characterized his recent discussions with European Council President Antonio Costa as "frustrating," criticizing the EU's commitment to supporting Ukraine and claiming the bloc is determined to "wage a war" against Russia. His administration has consistently echoed Kremlin narratives about sanctions and energy prices, rather than acknowledging Russia's weaponization of energy supplies or its unprovoked invasion of a sovereign neighbor.

The previous Slovak government had negotiated for replacement equipment from Western allies, particularly Germany, though Fico's Defense Ministry claimed these pledges covered only about half of what was donated. Critics note that supporting Ukraine's defense strengthens European security and upholds the international rules-based order that protects all democracies from authoritarian aggression.
