The Pentagon has ordered the withdrawal of approximately 5,000 troops from Germany over the next six to 12 months, a move that underscores the deepening rift between the Trump administration and European NATO allies. The decision comes amid sharp disagreements over the administration's Iran policy, longstanding disputes about defense spending, and what sources describe as an "increasingly unpredictable" White House approach to transatlantic relations.

Trump suggested the withdrawal could expand significantly beyond the initial 5,000 figure, threatening to pull troops from Spain and Italy as well. The announcement followed public criticism from German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who questioned whether the U.S. "has no strategy" in its Iran policy. Trump responded with characteristic personal attacks, telling Merz he was "doing a terrible job" and advising him to focus on domestic issues including immigration and energy.

The chaotic nature of the decision has alarmed military and diplomatic circles. According to Associated Press sources, U.S. military leadership learned of the withdrawal "in real time" rather than through normal channels, though the Pentagon disputes this characterization. An unnamed European official described the situation as "schizophrenic," noting that U.S. military commanders in Europe were promoting cooperation even as Washington announced the pullback.

Germany hosts more than 36,000 active-duty U.S. personnel and critical infrastructure including Landstuhl Regional Medical Center, the largest U.S. military hospital abroad. The Biden administration had planned to deploy a battalion with long-range Tomahawk missiles to Germany—a plan now reportedly scrapped. Even Republican chairs of the House and Senate Armed Services committees expressed being "very concerned" by the drawdown, suggesting bipartisan unease with the abrupt policy shift.
