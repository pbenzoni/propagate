A senior British defense official is warning that the United Kingdom may need to reconsider strict limits on autonomous weapons systems, arguing that America's adversaries are already racing ahead without such constraints. Al Carns, parliamentary under-secretary of state for the armed forces, told the Financial Times that while human oversight remains ideal, Britain must be prepared to "take the human out of the loop" when facing enemies who won't impose similar limitations on themselves.

Current British policy requires "context-appropriate human involvement" in weapons that identify and attack targets. But Carns, a former commando, argues this approach could leave UK forces at a dangerous disadvantage. "Our adversaries won't care about having a human in the loop," he warned at a military drone conference in Latvia.

The debate comes as nations worldwide deploy increasingly sophisticated AI-powered weapons. The United States and Ukraine are already using artificial intelligence for battlefield targeting, and Russia is believed to be pursuing similar capabilities. However, serious concerns remain about AI reliability—faulty analysis reportedly contributed to a missile strike that killed over 150 civilians, including many children, at an Iranian school during recent military operations.

Russian President Vladimir Putin has stated that while "AI can advise, and the advice can even be better than anything a human can come up with," ultimate responsibility must remain with human decision-makers. Public opinion in Western nations reflects similar caution: surveys show only 22% or fewer Americans, Britons, Canadians, and French citizens support allowing AI systems to make independent lethal decisions on the battlefield. The British Ministry of Defense announced it is reviewing regulations governing autonomous weapons to ensure they address current threats while maintaining appropriate safeguards.
