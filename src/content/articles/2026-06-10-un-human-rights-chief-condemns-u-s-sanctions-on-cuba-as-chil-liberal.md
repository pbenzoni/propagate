The United Nations' top human rights official has issued a stark condemnation of U.S. sanctions on Cuba, warning that "children are dying" due to severe shortages of essential medical supplies caused by the economic blockade. UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Turk described the situation as "unacceptable" and said the sanctions are "incompatible with basic principles of international human rights law."

According to a report from the Office of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, infant mortality in Cuba has doubled to 9.9 per 1,000 births since the U.S. imposed what critics call a fuel blockade on the Caribbean nation. Childhood cancer survival rates have plummeted from 85% to 65%, while critical medical services including oncology, dialysis, and maternal health face severe strain. Essential medicines are in critically short supply, the report found.

The humanitarian crisis has been exacerbated by what the UN office describes as U.S. extraterritorial sanctions that discourage private companies from delivering humanitarian shipments to Cuba, even when such aid is permitted under international law. Cuba has experienced daily blackouts and severe fuel shortages in recent months, with the island's economic difficulties intensified by disruptions to oil supplies from Venezuela.

Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez Parrilla has accused the U.S. of imposing "collective punishment" on the Cuban people, subjecting them to conditions "that violate their human rights and cause pain, suffering, and anguish." Several countries including Russia, China, and Mexico have been providing humanitarian aid to Cuba in response to the crisis. According to reports citing anonymous U.S. officials, the White House has been considering further escalation of economic pressure, with regime change as the stated goal.
