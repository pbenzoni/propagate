U.S. Central Command announced early Saturday that American forces intercepted multiple Iranian drones allegedly targeting commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway that handles roughly a quarter of global seaborne oil and natural gas shipments. The incident underscores the fragility of ongoing diplomatic efforts between the two nations.

The confrontation comes as Washington and Tehran attempt to negotiate an end to hostilities that erupted on February 28, when joint U.S.-Israeli military operations struck Iran, resulting in the deaths of senior officials including Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. The attacks precipitated a months-long crisis that has severely disrupted international shipping through the strategic chokepoint.

According to CENTCOM's statement, all drones were successfully intercepted and commercial traffic continues to flow through the strait. However, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi signaled Friday that Tehran does not intend to return to pre-conflict arrangements, emphasizing Iran and Oman's sovereign authority over the waterway and indicating plans for a new joint administrative framework.

Reuters reported Saturday that a potential agreement could be signed as soon as Sunday in Geneva, with U.S. Vice President J.D. Vance and Iranian parliamentary speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf representing their respective governments. The proposed memorandum would reportedly address reopening the strait and lifting U.S. naval restrictions on Iranian ports, with discussions on Iran's nuclear program—the Trump administration's stated justification for military action—to follow subsequently.
