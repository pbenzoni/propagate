House Democrats have filed articles of impeachment against Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth, citing a pattern of alleged abuses tied to military operations in Iran. The resolution, introduced by Representative Yassamin Ansari and supported by eight Democratic co-sponsors along with anti-war advocacy organizations, outlines six charges including violation of oath of office, endangering service members, and potential war crimes.

Central to the impeachment effort are allegations that Hegseth authorized or failed to prevent operations resulting in significant civilian casualties. One article specifically references a February 28 strike on a school in Minab, Iran, which killed at least 15 people, predominantly children. According to reports, Pentagon internal reviews determined the strike was carried out by U.S. forces based on outdated intelligence, yet no formal apology has been issued.

The resolution also addresses what lawmakers describe as systematic obstruction of congressional oversight. Representatives argue that Hegseth withheld complete information about military operations from Congress, undermining the legislative branch's constitutional role in overseeing armed conflict. Additional articles accuse him of politicizing the armed forces and abusing his position to advance partisan objectives, eroding the long-standing principle of military nonpartisanship.

"Only Congress can declare war," stated Ansari, the first Iranian American Democrat serving in Congress, emphasizing constitutional limits on executive military action. While the impeachment resolution faces virtually no prospect of advancing in the Republican-controlled House, supporters view the effort as an essential assertion of congressional authority and accountability, particularly regarding undeclared military conflicts that bypass constitutional war powers.
