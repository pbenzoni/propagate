Incoming Hungarian Prime Minister Peter Magyar has named Judit Lannert as education minister, a move that has raised alarm bells among those who supported Hungary's previous approach to parental rights and child protection. Lannert is widely known as an outspoken critic of Viktor Orban's government and has publicly embraced progressive causes, including LGBT activism and pro-Ukraine positions.

The appointment comes as a stark departure from expectations. Many had anticipated that Rita Rubovszky, headmistress of a Cistercian Catholic school, would be tapped for the role. Instead, Magyar selected Lannert, who has displayed LGBT-themed imagery on social media during debates over Hungary's constitutional protections for children and families.

Under Orban's leadership, Hungary enacted a 2021 child protection law that restricted how gender and sexuality topics could be presented to minors in schools and media. Orban defended the legislation as ensuring parents—not government institutions or outside influences—retain authority over their children's sexual education. He framed it as a matter of national sovereignty against what he called Western liberal pressure.

Just over a week after Orban's election defeat, the European Court of Justice ruled that Hungary's reforms violate EU law, ordering the country to roll back restrictions on promoting homosexuality and gender transition content to minors. Orban responded on social media, warning that "the Brusselian steamroller is already in motion" and vowing his party would continue fighting for "the soul of Europe."

The timing of Lannert's appointment—alongside a proposal submitted to Hungary's media regulator for an LGBTQ-themed TV channel—has intensified concerns about the direction of the country's education system under the new government.
