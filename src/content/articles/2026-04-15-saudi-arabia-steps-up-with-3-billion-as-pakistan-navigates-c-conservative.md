Saudi Arabia has committed $3 billion in financial assistance to Pakistan, according to Pakistani Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb, who announced the pledge while attending the World Bank–IMF Spring Meetings in Washington. The funds are expected to be disbursed within days as Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif prepares for an official visit to the Kingdom.

The Saudi assistance comes at a critical moment as Pakistan faces pressure to repay $3.5 billion to the United Arab Emirates this month. The UAE loan, originally provided in 2019 to help Pakistan through a balance of payments crisis, has been rolled over multiple times with increasingly shorter extensions—signaling Abu Dhabi's growing reluctance to continue the arrangement.

Pakistan's economic struggles stem from multiple pressures: the aftermath of pandemic lockdowns, supply chain disruptions linked to the Ukraine conflict, and devastating floods that impacted a third of the nation. The International Monetary Fund intervened in 2024 with a three-year, $7 billion package, requiring Pakistan to secure approximately $12.5 billion in rollovers from China, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE to meet external financing requirements.

The shifting financial landscape reflects broader changes in regional alignments. While Pakistan has strengthened its relationship with Saudi Arabia—including signing a mutual defense pact with Riyadh—ties with the UAE have deteriorated. Abu Dhabi has been building closer security partnerships with India and recently imposed visa restrictions affecting Pakistani nationals. Reports indicate Qatar may step in to replace the UAE's role in Pakistan's financial support network, underscoring the fluid nature of Middle Eastern diplomatic and economic relationships.
