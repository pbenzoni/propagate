The Pentagon's official estimate of $25 billion for the ongoing war against Iran significantly understates the conflict's true cost to American taxpayers, according to CNN reporting based on three sources familiar with internal assessments. The figure provided to Congress this week excludes billions in expenses needed to rebuild U.S. military bases damaged by Iranian counterstrikes across the Middle East.

Insiders suggest the real cost could approach $40-50 billion when accounting for extensive damage to at least nine U.S. military installations in Bahrain, Kuwait, Iraq, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar. Iranian strikes reportedly destroyed multiple radar systems, including components of a THAAD missile defense battery in Jordan, and damaged facilities housing critical defense infrastructure. An Air Force E-3 Sentry aircraft was also destroyed at a Saudi air base.

During congressional testimony, Democratic Representative Ro Khanna challenged the Pentagon's accounting, noting the $25 billion figure contradicts earlier statements that the conflict cost $11 billion in just its first six days. War Secretary Pete Hegseth declined to clarify whether base reconstruction costs were included in the estimate, while Pentagon officials acknowledged they lack a final damage assessment and that repair expenses are not reflected in the Defense Department's $1.5 trillion budget request for 2027.

Lawmakers from both parties have expressed concern over the war's escalating economic burden, rising fuel costs stemming from the Strait of Hormuz blockade, and questions about the conflict's original justification. The war, launched in late February and initially projected to last weeks, has resulted in over 1,300 civilian deaths and triggered a global energy crisis that continues to affect American consumers.
