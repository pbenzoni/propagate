Bulgaria's unelected acting Prime Minister Andrey Gyurov has signed a sweeping ten-year military cooperation agreement with Ukraine, despite holding office for fewer than eight weeks and trailing badly in upcoming elections. The move has sparked outcry over whether a caretaker government should bind the nation to such consequential commitments without voter approval.

Gyurov's 'We Continue the Change' coalition is polling at just 10% ahead of Sunday's election, while former President Rumen Radev's coalition leads by nine points. Radev has been a vocal skeptic of indefinite military aid to Ukraine, previously vetoing arms transfers and telling Zelensky directly that "more weapons will not solve" the conflict. The acting PM's rush to finalize the deal before the election appears designed to circumvent the likely next government's ability to chart a different course.

Under the agreement signed in Kiev on March 30, Bulgaria commits to a decade of military aid, joint weapons production, and support for Ukraine's NATO and EU membership bids. Radev called the caretaker prime minister "practically illegitimate" for signing such a binding pact without an electoral mandate. Even Boyko Borissov's center-right GERB party, which leads Radev by a narrower margin, withdrew its support for the deal in 2024, saying it would wait for a peace agreement before committing.

The controversy highlights tensions between Brussels-aligned officials and voters skeptical of open-ended commitments. Bulgaria has cycled through multiple caretaker governments since 2021, with none previously moving forward on the Ukraine pact. Gyurov defended his decision by insisting Bulgaria "cannot wait for the 'right moment' when it comes to security," though critics argue that moment should come after voters have their say.
