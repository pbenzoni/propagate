China is effectively placing its leading artificial intelligence experts under travel restrictions previously reserved for nuclear scientists, according to a new Bloomberg report—a dramatic escalation in Beijing's efforts to prevent American access to critical technology and talent.

Chinese authorities have begun requiring AI researchers, executives, and engineers at major firms including Alibaba and DeepSeek to obtain government permission before traveling abroad, anonymous sources told the outlet. The move comes as the communist regime pushes for what it calls "self-reliance" in artificial intelligence, robotics, and semiconductor manufacturing—industries where America has maintained technological superiority.

The travel crackdown follows a pattern of increasingly aggressive Chinese export controls aimed squarely at undermining U.S. technological leadership. Last year, Beijing blocked exports of 14 rare earth minerals essential for high-tech military equipment, along with advanced semiconductors that power AI supercomputers. China controls roughly 60% of global rare earth extraction and processes approximately 90% of these critical materials, giving the regime enormous leverage in ongoing trade disputes with Washington.

Beijing's current five-year development plan explicitly calls for "extraordinary measures" to achieve self-sufficiency in semiconductors and AI by 2030. The regime recently blocked Meta's planned $2 billion acquisition of Chinese AI startup Manus, reportedly detaining two company executives and preventing them from leaving the country during the investigation. While sources claim the broader travel restrictions aren't directly tied to that incident, the timing underscores how the Chinese Communist Party now views AI talent as a national security asset it cannot afford to lose to the West.
