World Health Organization Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus is working to calm public anxiety as the MV Hondius cruise ship, carrying passengers affected by a hantavirus outbreak, arrives off the coast of Tenerife. The vessel has been at the center of international health concerns after three passengers died from the Andes strain of hantavirus, a rare pathogen typically transmitted through contact with infected rodent excretions.

In a public letter Saturday, Ghebreyesus emphasized that hantavirus "is not another COVID" and assured Tenerife residents that "the risk to you, living your daily life in Tenerife, is low." The WHO chief personally traveled to the Spanish island to oversee what health officials describe as a carefully coordinated response involving strict medical protocols and international cooperation.

The 147 passengers and crew aboard the Dutch-flagged vessel will be medically assessed and evacuated through stringent safety measures. According to Ghebreyesus, passengers will be transported "in sealed, guarded vehicles, through a completely cordoned-off corridor" at an industrial port far from residential areas before being repatriated to their home countries. Currently, no symptomatic passengers remain aboard the ship, and a WHO expert is monitoring the situation.

The outbreak has resulted in eight reported cases, including three fatalities, with health authorities working to trace individuals who disembarked before the outbreak was confirmed. While the Andes strain is notable for its rare capacity for limited human-to-human transmission through close contact, public health officials stress that community risk remains minimal with proper containment protocols in place.
