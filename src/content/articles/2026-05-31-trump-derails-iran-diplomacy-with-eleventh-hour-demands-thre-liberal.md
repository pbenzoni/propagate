The sudden reversal threatens to unravel weeks of painstaking negotiations aimed at preventing further escalation in the region.

After the two countries reportedly reached a memorandum of understanding on Thursday to extend an April ceasefire and restart nuclear negotiations, Trump convened senior advisers the following day to impose stricter terms. According to the Times, the president grew concerned about provisions that would unfreeze Iranian assets—funds that belong to Iran but have been blocked by sanctions—and pushed for more aggressive language around seizing Iran's enriched uranium stockpile.

Iranian officials responded by reaffirming their position that nuclear energy development for peaceful purposes remains a sovereign right, and accused Trump of "betraying diplomacy." Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi emphasized that military threats will not compel Iran to surrender its internationally recognized rights under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty. Tehran has consistently denied seeking nuclear weapons and rejected demands to hand over enriched uranium to the United States.
