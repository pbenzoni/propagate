Iran has announced it will permit passage for commercial vessels through the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz during a 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire, marking a temporary shift from Tehran's previous prohibition on American and Israeli ships. However, President Donald Trump made clear that America's naval blockade of Iranian ports will remain firmly in place.

Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi stated the strait is "completely open" for approved commercial traffic during the truce period, which began Friday after weeks of deadly fighting between Israel and Hezbollah that claimed over 2,000 lives in Lebanon. President Trump acknowledged the development but emphasized American resolve, saying the strait is "open and ready for business" while stressing that the U.S. naval blockade "remains in full force" until a comprehensive agreement is "100% complete."

The temporary ceasefire comes as more than 10,000 American sailors, Marines, and airmen continue enforcing the blockade against vessels attempting to enter or leave Iranian ports, according to U.S. Central Command. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps has warned that the U.S. and Israel will face consequences if attacks resume. An Iranian parliamentarian dismissed Trump's blockade as "a joke," while claiming some commercial ships would be permitted through "upon payment of tolls."

The Malta-flagged cruise ship Celestyal Discovery became the first passenger vessel to transit the strait since hostilities began, crossing without passengers after 47 days in Dubai port. Iran's IRGC Navy stated that civilian vessels may only pass under Iranian supervision and coordination with Tehran's forces while the fragile ceasefire holds.
