The Trump administration is threatening to revoke visas for Palestinian diplomats if their UN envoy Riyad Mansour does not withdraw his candidacy for a vice-presidential position in the General Assembly, according to reports of an internal State Department cable.

The cable instructed U.S. diplomats to tell Mansour that his bid for the largely ceremonial position "fuels tensions" and could undermine Trump's proposed Gaza peace plan. This marks the second time this year that U.S. pressure has forced Mansour to abandon a General Assembly leadership bid, following his withdrawal from consideration for the presidency earlier in 2025.

The threatened visa revocations raise serious concerns about U.S. obligations under international law. A 1947 headquarters agreement requires the United States to allow UN representatives to travel to New York for official business. While Washington has previously denied visas citing national security concerns—including to former Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat—using visa threats to influence routine UN institutional votes represents an escalation in diplomatic coercion.

The pressure campaign comes as Israel continues to withhold tax and customs revenues that constitute much of the Palestinian Authority's budget—funds frozen since the October 2023 Hamas attack and subsequent Israeli military operations in Gaza that have killed tens of thousands of Palestinians and displaced most of the population. U.S. diplomats were reportedly instructed to link progress on recovering those essential funds to Palestinian "good faith" engagement, effectively conditioning basic governmental revenue on diplomatic capitulation.

Palestine currently holds non-member observer state status at the UN without voting rights in the 193-member General Assembly. While the Assembly voted to support full Palestinian membership in 2024, any upgrade requires Security Council approval, where the United States has repeatedly exercised its veto power to block Palestinian statehood recognition.
