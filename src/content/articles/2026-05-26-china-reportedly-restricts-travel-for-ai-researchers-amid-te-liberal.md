China has reportedly imposed travel restrictions on leading artificial intelligence researchers at major companies including Alibaba and DeepSeek, according to a Bloomberg report citing anonymous sources. The alleged policy would require AI specialists, executives, and engineers to obtain government approval before traveling abroad—a significant expansion of controls previously limited to nuclear scientists and state enterprise leaders.

The reported restrictions come as tensions escalate over technology competition between Beijing and Washington. Chinese regulators recently blocked Meta's planned $2 billion acquisition of Manus, a Chinese AI startup, and allegedly prevented two Manus executives from leaving the country during an investigation. While sources told Bloomberg the travel restrictions are separate from that case, they appear part of a broader strategy to prevent critical technology from flowing to Western competitors.

Beijing has neither confirmed nor denied the travel policy, but it aligns with China's existing export controls on strategic technologies. Last year, China restricted exports of 14 rare earth minerals essential for high-tech manufacturing and banned sales of advanced semiconductors used in AI systems. China controls approximately 60% of global rare earth extraction and 90% of processing capacity, giving it substantial leverage in tech supply chains.

The alleged travel restrictions raise serious questions about academic freedom, scientific collaboration, and the future of global AI development. If confirmed, such policies could stifle international research partnerships and hinder the cross-border exchange of ideas that has historically driven technological innovation. China's current five-year development plan emphasizes achieving self-sufficiency in semiconductors, AI, and advanced manufacturing through what it describes as "extraordinary measures."
