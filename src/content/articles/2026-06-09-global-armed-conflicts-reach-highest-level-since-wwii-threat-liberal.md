The number of armed conflicts worldwide has reached its highest level since World War II, according to a sobering new study published by the Peace Research Institute Oslo (PRIO). Researchers documented 65 state-based conflicts—those involving at least one government actor—in 2025, the highest number since systematic recordkeeping began in 1946.

The human toll has been devastating. An estimated 245,000 people were killed in battle-related violence last year alone, making 2025 one of the deadliest years in recent decades. The research paints a picture of a "far more fragmented" world, with an "unprecedented" number of simultaneous wars driven by both long-running humanitarian crises and new outbreaks of large-scale fighting.

The conflicts identified span 35 countries across multiple continents, with several nations embroiled in multiple wars simultaneously. Africa has been the region most affected by state-based violence, followed by Asia, the Middle East, the Americas, and Europe. Interstate conflicts—wars between nations—doubled from the previous year to reach a record eight in 2025.

Perhaps most alarming, PRIO's data shows that more than 930,000 people have been killed in state-based conflicts since 2021—roughly matching the total recorded during the previous two decades combined. The findings underscore the urgent need for renewed international commitment to conflict prevention, diplomacy, and multilateral institutions designed to protect human security and advance peaceful resolution of disputes.
