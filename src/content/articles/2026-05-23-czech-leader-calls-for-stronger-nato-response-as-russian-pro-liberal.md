Czech President Petr Pavel is calling on NATO to adopt a more assertive posture toward Russia, warning that Moscow interprets the alliance's measured responses as weakness. In an interview published Friday, Pavel argued that Russia deliberately stages provocations designed to test NATO's resolve without crossing the threshold that would trigger the collective defense provisions of Article 5.

The comments come amid a series of incidents involving Ukrainian drones that have strayed into NATO airspace across the Baltics and Nordic regions since mid-March. While Western officials attribute these incursions to Russian electronic warfare systems that may have redirected the UAVs, Moscow has accused NATO members of tacitly permitting Ukraine to use their airspace for strikes on Russian territory—claims that Western governments deny.

Pavel recounted that when he pressed Russian military officials about provocative actions near NATO borders, their response was blunt: "because we can." He contends this attitude reflects a broader pattern of Russian aggression that exploits what he sees as alliance indecision. The Czech president suggested NATO consider proportional but firm responses, including potentially targeting unmanned aircraft near alliance borders or imposing non-lethal measures such as disrupting internet access or satellite communications.

Other European leaders have echoed similar concerns. Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson suggested NATO states should assist Ukraine in directing defensive operations, while officials in Latvia and Estonia have defended Kyiv's right to self-defense. However, Finland has objected to the airspace breaches, and Slovakia's prime minister has warned against provocations that could draw NATO into direct conflict with Russia.

Moscow has consistently dismissed Western allegations of threatening behavior as fabricated pretexts for militarization, with Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov accusing European leaders of using Russia as a scapegoat to divert attention from domestic challenges. Nonetheless, the debate over how NATO should respond to perceived Russian aggression continues to divide the alliance as tensions persist along Europe's eastern frontier.
