Hungary's incoming Prime Minister Peter Magyar has pledged to suspend news operations at the state broadcaster MTVA, which he accuses of serving as a propaganda arm for outgoing Prime Minister Viktor Orbán's authoritarian government. Magyar's Tisza party won a decisive victory in Sunday's parliamentary election, ending Orbán's 16-year grip on power.

During tense interviews on Wednesday, Magyar condemned the broadcaster's track record, comparing its propaganda tactics to those of Joseph Goebbels and North Korean state media. "What has been happening here since 2010 is something that Goebbels or the North Korean leadership would admire – not a single true word being spoken. This cannot continue," he said. Magyar later stated that MTVA staff had worked "under constant intimidation and political pressure" and view the election outcome "as a form of liberation."

Magyar, who formerly belonged to Orbán's Fidesz party before breaking away, campaigned on repairing Hungary's relationship with the European Union and unlocking more than €16 billion in frozen COVID-19 recovery funds that Brussels withheld over rule-of-law concerns. EU officials have long criticized Orbán for systematically undermining independent media to consolidate authoritarian control.

While promising to restore press freedom domestically, Magyar indicated his government would maintain some policies associated with his predecessor, including opposition to fast-tracking Ukraine's EU membership and continued reliance on diversified energy sources, including Russian oil imports. He stated that broadcasting operations would resume once "all conditions for impartial and objective journalism are fully restored."
