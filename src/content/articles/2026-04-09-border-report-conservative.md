A new border security report finally puts on paper what every trucker, every rancher, and every small-town sheriff has been saying for years: the system is not working. The authors — many of them career professionals who have watched this problem grow on their own shift — call for more boots on the ground, better technology at the line, and an honest accounting of who is coming across and why.

None of this should be controversial. A country that cannot say who is inside its own borders is a country that has stopped doing one of the few things a country is actually for. The citizens of the affected communities have been patient, have hosted the meetings, have absorbed the costs. They are owed a plan that treats their towns as homes, not waypoints.

The report is not a magic wand. It is a starting point. The next step is a Congress willing to read it and an executive willing to act on it. The step after that is accountability for everyone who spent the last decade telling the country that there was no problem here worth solving.
