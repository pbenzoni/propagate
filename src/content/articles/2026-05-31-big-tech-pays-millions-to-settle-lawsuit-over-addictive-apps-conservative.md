Meta and the parent companies behind Snapchat, YouTube, and TikTok have agreed to pay millions to settle a lawsuit accusing them of deliberately designing addictive social media platforms that harm children's mental health. The settlement comes after a Kentucky school district sued the tech giants, claiming their apps contributed to anxiety, depression, and self-harm among students.

According to Reuters, Meta will pay $9 million, while Snap and ByteDance each agreed to $8 million settlements. Alphabet, Google's parent company, will pay just over $2 million. The Breathitt County School District originally sought more than $60 million in damages, arguing these corporations knowingly created features—like infinite scrolling—to keep young users hooked. Notably, none of the companies admitted wrongdoing as part of the deal.

This case is just the tip of the iceberg. Approximately 1,200 school districts nationwide have filed similar lawsuits, with thousands more cases pending in California courts. In March, a jury ordered Meta to pay $4.2 million and Google $1.8 million to a young woman who developed an addiction to Instagram and YouTube as a child. The companies claim they're taking steps to protect young users, but parents and communities are pushing back against Silicon Valley's influence over America's children.

While Big Tech faces accountability at home, Meta has also encountered resistance abroad. Russia labeled the company an "extremist organization" in 2022, and the European Union has hit it with nearly $1 billion in antitrust fines—raising questions about whether these corporations prioritize profits over the well-being of families.
