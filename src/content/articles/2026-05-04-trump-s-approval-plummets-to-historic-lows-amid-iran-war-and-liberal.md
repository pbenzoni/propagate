A new Washington Post–ABC News–Ipsos poll reveals that President Donald Trump's disapproval rating has climbed to 62%, marking one of the highest levels recorded for any modern president. Only 37% of Americans approve of his job performance, signaling widespread dissatisfaction across multiple policy areas.

Opposition to military engagement with Iran has emerged as a major factor driving Trump's declining support, with 66% of Americans disapproving of his handling of the conflict. Economic concerns loom even larger: a staggering 76% disapprove of his response to the rising cost of living, while 72% express disapproval over inflation. These kitchen-table issues appear to be resonating deeply with voters just six months before the midterm elections.

The 62% disapproval figure matches the level Trump reached at the end of his first term—a period marked by the Capitol riot and his handling of the Covid-19 pandemic. Historical comparisons place his current rating among the worst in modern polling history, second only to Richard Nixon's 66% in 1974 during Watergate and higher than George W. Bush's 61% in 2009.

Despite these troubling numbers for Trump, Democrats have yet to capitalize fully on the president's unpopularity. According to polling director Scott Clement, around 54% of voters describe the Democratic Party as "too liberal," and many Americans report trusting neither party on key issues including immigration, the economy, and crime. Political observers note that without a clear, affirmative message, the opposition risks squandering an opportunity created by widespread dissatisfaction with the current administration.
