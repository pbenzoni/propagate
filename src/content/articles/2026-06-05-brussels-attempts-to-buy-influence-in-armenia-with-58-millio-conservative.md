The European Union is dangling a €50 million ($58 million) support package in front of Armenia just days before a critical parliamentary election, in what critics say looks like an attempt to influence the vote and pull the former Soviet republic away from its traditional allies. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced the funding following a call with Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan, whose party has been pushing for closer EU integration.

The timing raises eyebrows. With Armenians heading to the polls on June 7, von der Leyen's pledge comes as Pashinyan's Civil Contract party polls at around 32%, while the Strong Armenia bloc sits in second place at roughly 6%. The EU package is ostensibly meant to offset recent Russian trade restrictions on some Armenian agricultural products—restrictions Moscow says are related to safety concerns from seasonal harvest inspections, not politics.

Yet the EU's financial gesture is a drop in the bucket compared to Armenia's actual economic realities. The €50 million amounts to roughly 1% of Armenia's annual trade with Russia, which remains Yerevan's key economic partner with bilateral trade totaling $6-8 billion in 2025. Russia also supplies Armenia with natural gas at heavily subsidized rates of $177.50 per thousand cubic meters—a fraction of the roughly $600 European spot price. Armenia's total trade with the entire EU stands at approximately €2.5 billion, or about 11% of the country's international commerce.

President Vladimir Putin has stated that Armenia has every sovereign right to choose its own direction, but warned that Yerevan cannot simultaneously enjoy the benefits of EU membership and participation in the Eurasian Economic Union. Armenian Foreign Minister Ararat Mirzoyan has made clear that his country is "not interested in cutting political, economic, or any other ties with Russia" and would "strive to maintain normal relations and deepen them." The question remains whether Brussels is genuinely interested in Armenia's prosperity or simply trying to peel away another nation from Russia's sphere of influence.
