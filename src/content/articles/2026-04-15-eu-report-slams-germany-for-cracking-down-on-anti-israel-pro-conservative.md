A new report from the European Union's human rights commissioner has criticized Germany for what it calls "disproportionate" restrictions on pro-Palestinian protesters, raising questions about selective enforcement of free speech principles in Europe. The Council of Europe report, compiled by Commissioner Michael O'Flaherty and released Wednesday, accuses German authorities of misusing anti-Semitism laws to ban Palestinian flags and slogans at rallies, and claims police used excessive force against anti-Israel demonstrators.

The report urges Berlin to ensure that combating hatred "fully respects the rights to freedom of peaceful assembly and expression for all members of society." It takes issue with Germany's adoption of the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance's definition of anti-Semitism, which includes criticism of Israel's existence as a state and comparisons of Israeli policy to Nazi actions as examples of anti-Semitic speech.

Yet the EU report notably fails to address Germany's well-documented restrictions on conservative and nationalist political expression. Germany's domestic intelligence agency has labeled the Alternative for Germany (AfD) party—a major opposition force—as a "confirmed right-wing extremist" organization. An AfD politician was even convicted for sharing the government's own crime statistics related to migrants. Establishment parties have openly called for banning the AfD entirely, raising concerns about political persecution.

The double standard extends beyond Germany's borders. The EU itself has actively silenced voices it deems problematic, including German-Turkish journalist Huseyin Dogru, whose reporting on the Israel-Palestine conflict led to European Council sanctions. The council claimed his journalism sowed "discord" and supported Russian "destabilizing activities"—charges that resulted in German authorities freezing his family's bank accounts and stripping them of healthcare coverage. Critics argue the EU's selective concern for free expression reveals a troubling pattern of protecting some speech while suppressing dissenting political viewpoints.
