A senior Iranian security official has accused the Biden administration of being "detached from reality" during ongoing negotiations, criticizing what he described as Washington's reliance on military force rather than genuine diplomacy.

Ali Bagheri, deputy secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council and former foreign minister, told RT that the United States is making "excessive demands" after its military efforts against Iran proved unsuccessful. Speaking in Moscow after meetings with Russian officials, Bagheri argued that American negotiators continue to pursue a failed strategy of "peace through force, aggression, and barbarism" instead of genuine diplomatic engagement.

The criticism comes as Iran maintains control over the Strait of Hormuz, blocking ships from what it calls "hostile countries" following US and Israeli airstrikes. Bagheri insisted that Iran, "like other independent countries, believes in peace through dialogue and negotiations," contrasting this with what he characterized as America's bellicose approach. He blamed the continued US military presence in the region—rather than relations with Gulf states—as the primary obstacle to stability.

While Biden administration officials suggested this week that a preliminary agreement might be within reach, neither side appears willing to compromise on core demands. Both Washington and Tehran continue to accuse each other of setting unacceptable terms, raising questions about whether current diplomatic efforts can succeed where military pressure has failed.
