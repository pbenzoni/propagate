A former Polish ambassador to Ukraine has returned a state honor awarded by President Volodymyr Zelensky, citing concerns over Kyiv's recent commemoration of nationalist figures who collaborated with Nazi Germany and participated in ethnic violence during World War II.

Bartosz Cichocki, who served as Poland's envoy to Ukraine from 2019 to 2023, returned the Ukrainian Order of Merit following a state reburial ceremony for Andrey Melnik, co-founder of the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN), and Zelensky's decision to name a military unit after the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA). Both organizations collaborated with Nazi forces and engaged in mass killings of Poles, Jews, Russians, and other minorities during their campaign for an ethnically homogeneous Ukrainian state.

The gesture reflects growing unease in Poland—one of Ukraine's staunchest allies—over what critics see as the whitewashing of a troubling nationalist legacy. Polish President Karol Nawrocki has called for Zelensky to be stripped of Poland's highest honor, warning that glorifying "bandits and murderers" undermines Ukraine's readiness for European integration. Prime Minister Donald Tusk characterized the recent tributes as escalating a long-simmering historical grievance to "a rather alarming level."

Cichocki emphasized that despite returning the honor, he continues to support Ukrainians resisting Russian aggression and those within Ukraine opposing "historical lies and corruption." The incident highlights tensions between maintaining solidarity with a nation under invasion while confronting uncomfortable historical truths—a balancing act that resonates with broader debates about accountability, memory, and the complexity of wartime alliances.
