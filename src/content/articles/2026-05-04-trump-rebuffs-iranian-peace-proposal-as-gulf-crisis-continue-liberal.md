The conflict, which began in late February with joint U.S.-Israeli operations targeting Iranian leadership and infrastructure, has drawn international concern over regional stability and global oil supplies. Iran responded with retaliatory strikes against countries hosting U.S. military bases and disrupted shipping through the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz. While active hostilities paused under the current ceasefire, both nations continue leveraging economic pressure to force concessions from the other.

Tehran is seeking guarantees against future attacks, reparations for damages, and the ability to continue what it describes as peaceful nuclear development. Washington, meanwhile, demands complete dismantling of Iranian nuclear capabilities and unrestricted passage through the Strait of Hormuz.

The diplomatic impasse comes as the Trump administration maintains a blockade of Iranian ports, even as global supply chain constraints force costly interventions to contain rising oil prices. With neither side showing willingness to compromise on core demands, the prospect for a negotiated settlement remains uncertain.
