House Democrats have filed six articles of impeachment against Secretary of War Pete Hegseth in what Republicans are calling the latest chapter in a pattern of partisan weaponization of the impeachment process. The resolution, led by Democratic Representative Yassamin Ansari and backed by eight Democratic co-sponsors along with several anti-war groups, accuses Hegseth of various charges related to military operations in Iran.

The articles include allegations of violating his oath of office, endangering U.S. service members, and war crimes. Democrats point to a February 28 strike in Minab, Iran that killed at least 15 people, mostly children, at a school. The Pentagon has reportedly determined the strike was based on outdated intelligence. Additional charges relate to the so-called 'Signalgate' leak and claims that Hegseth obstructed congressional oversight by withholding information about military operations.

Pentagon press secretary Kingsley Wilson dismissed the impeachment effort, stating: "This is just another Democrat trying to make headlines as the Department of War decisively and overwhelmingly achieved the President's objectives in Iran." Axios noted that the measure has virtually no chance of advancing in the current Congress, where Republicans control the House of Representatives and can block any effort to bring the articles to a floor vote.

The initiative is widely viewed as an effort to apply political pressure on one of President Trump's most senior cabinet officials, even though removal from office is extremely unlikely. Democrats have recently pursued unsuccessful impeachment efforts against former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and former Attorney General Pam Bondi. "Only Congress can declare war," said Ansari, the first Iranian American Democrat in Congress, though critics note Congress has repeatedly delegated war-making authority to the executive branch for decades.
