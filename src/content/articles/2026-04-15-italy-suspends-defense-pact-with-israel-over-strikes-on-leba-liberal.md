Italy has suspended a longstanding defense cooperation agreement with Israel, citing the devastating toll of Israeli military operations in Lebanon that have killed more than 2,100 people since early March.

Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, whose right-wing government had previously been considered among Israel's strongest European allies, announced the suspension Tuesday in Verona. "When there are things we don't agree with, we act accordingly," Meloni stated, marking a significant shift in Italy's position on the conflict.

The decision follows an incident last week in which Israeli troops fired warning shots at an Italian peacekeeping convoy outside Beirut—an action Meloni called "completely unacceptable." Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani summoned Israel's ambassador over the incident and separately condemned what he termed "unacceptable attacks by Israel against the civilian population" in Lebanon.

The 2005 agreement, which had been automatically renewed every five years, covered defense industry cooperation, military equipment exchanges, and personnel training. Italy joins a growing number of European nations reassessing their relationship with Israel's government. Spain previously canceled over $1 billion in contracts with Israeli arms manufacturers, with Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez characterizing Israel's Gaza campaign as genocide—a characterization Israel has denied.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has responded to European criticism by claiming Israel is "defending Europe" through its military campaigns and accusing European governments of "deep moral weakness" for questioning his government's actions.
