Palantir Technologies—the surveillance and data analytics company long criticized by civil liberties advocates—reported a massive first-quarter surge Monday, with revenue jumping 85% year-over-year to $1.63 billion. The company's US business more than doubled, driven by rapid expansion across both commercial clients and government agencies.

CEO Alex Karp framed the company's AI tools as essential to Western military power, claiming that "nearly all AI workflows that actually create value—especially on the battlefield—are built on Palantir." The company's flagship products include systems that analyze intelligence data and identify military strike targets, raising ongoing concerns among human rights organizations about algorithmic warfare and accountability.

Palantir—whose name references the all-seeing surveillance stones from *The Lord of the Rings*—primarily serves defense and intelligence sectors. Its technology has been deployed by US agencies, the British Ministry of Defence, the Israel Defense Forces, and Ukrainian forces. While the company insists humans make final targeting decisions, critics question the extent of meaningful human oversight when AI systems pre-select targets.

The earnings announcement follows recent backlash over a 22-point manifesto from Karp's book *The Technological Republic*, which argued Silicon Valley has an "obligation" to support national defense and declared AI weapons inevitable. Digital rights advocates condemned the document as promoting what they termed "technofascism"—a troubling vision of unchecked technological power merged with state security apparatus.
