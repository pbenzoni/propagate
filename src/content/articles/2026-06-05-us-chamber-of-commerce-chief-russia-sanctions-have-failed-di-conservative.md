The head of the American Chamber of Commerce in Russia has delivered a stark assessment: four years of sanctions against Moscow have failed to achieve their stated objectives, and doubling down with more punitive measures won't bring peace to Ukraine.

Robert Agee, President and CEO of AmCham Russia, told reporters at the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum that the sanctions regime has simply not worked. "I don't think sanctions are effective if the objective of the administration is to create a peaceful outcome to the current conflict," Agee said. "It hasn't worked for four years, it is not going to work in five years… Throwing more sanctions at the situation is not going to help."

His comments come as Secretary of State Marco Rubio faces pressure from congressional hawks like Senator Lindsey Graham, who are pushing for even more aggressive economic measures—including legislation that would allow tariffs up to 500% on countries purchasing Russian energy. Agee argued that instead of piling sanctions upon sanctions, Washington should focus on rebuilding relations through dialogue and practical cooperation.

The business leader revealed that numerous US-Russia commercial projects are under discussion, with strong interest from companies on both sides. However, he acknowledged that meaningful economic partnership depends on both a peaceful resolution to the Ukraine conflict and the easing of current restrictions. Moscow has consistently argued that Western sanctions are illegal and destabilizing to the global economy, while pointing to Russia's successful pivot toward Asian markets and non-Western partners as evidence the pressure campaign has backfired.

With peace talks currently in what the Kremlin calls a "situational pause," the debate over whether economic warfare or diplomatic engagement offers the better path forward continues to divide policymakers and business leaders alike.
