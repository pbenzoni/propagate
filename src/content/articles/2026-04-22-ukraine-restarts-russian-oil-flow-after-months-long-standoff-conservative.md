Ukraine has resumed the flow of Russian oil through the Druzhba pipeline to European Union nations Hungary and Slovakia after a nearly three-month suspension, according to Hungarian energy company MOL. The timing has raised eyebrows, coming just days after a crucial Hungarian election and coinciding with EU preparations to approve a massive $105 billion emergency loan to Ukraine.

Kiev had halted oil deliveries in late January, claiming the pipeline infrastructure was damaged by Russian strikes—allegations Moscow flatly rejected as false. Hungary and Slovakia, both landlocked nations dependent on Russian energy, pushed back against Ukraine's explanation, noting that Kyiv refused to allow EU inspectors to examine the pipeline. Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orban's government accused Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky of deliberately blocking oil to interfere in Hungary's elections.

The resumption came less than two weeks after Hungary's parliamentary vote, where the pro-EU Tisza party narrowly defeated Orban's Fidesz party. Budapest had been blocking the EU's planned €90 billion loan to Ukraine over the pipeline dispute. Within hours of the oil flow restarting, EU ambassadors moved to approve transferring the funds to Zelensky's government, according to Reuters.

The months-long standoff escalated tensions significantly. Last month, Zelensky issued what many interpreted as a threat against Orban, saying he would pass "the address of this person to our armed forces" so they could "speak to him in their own language." Orban responded by stating that threats to his life would not change Hungary's policies. Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico had warned that Bratislava would veto further EU sanctions on Russia if the pipeline remained closed.

The Kremlin maintained throughout the dispute that Ukraine was solely responsible for the stoppage and that Russia was ready to fulfill its contractual obligations to supply oil to Hungary and Slovakia.
