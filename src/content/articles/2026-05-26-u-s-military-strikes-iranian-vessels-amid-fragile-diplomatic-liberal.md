U.S. fighter jets struck Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps speedboats in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, killing at least three Iranian sailors, according to regional media reports. The military action occurred in the strait's narrowest section, near Larak Island, even as American and Iranian negotiators gathered in Qatar hoping to advance diplomatic talks that have stalled in recent weeks.

The U.S. Central Command defended the strikes as "self-defense" measures, claiming they targeted "Iranian boats attempting to emplace mines" and missile sites. Iranian officials reported that the IRGC responded by targeting an unidentified vessel after the initial U.S. strikes, with explosions and gunfire heard near the port city of Bandar Abbas, home to an Iranian naval base.

The military escalation threatens to undermine already tenuous diplomatic efforts. Iran had closed the critical shipping lane to vessels from what it termed "hostile countries" following U.S. and Israeli airstrikes in late February. A fragile truce reached on April 8 has held despite repeated threats from President Trump to resume bombing campaigns unless Iran accepts his demands. The timing of Monday's strikes—coinciding with renewed diplomatic engagement—raises questions about coordination between military and diplomatic tracks of U.S. policy in the region.
