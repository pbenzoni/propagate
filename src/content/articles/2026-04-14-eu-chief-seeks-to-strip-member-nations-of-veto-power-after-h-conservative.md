European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen is moving quickly to consolidate power in Brussels following the recent defeat of Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orban, calling for an end to individual member states' ability to veto EU foreign policy decisions.

Von der Leyen made her push for centralized control less than 24 hours after Orban's loss to the pro-EU Tisza party led by Peter Magyar. The timing reveals how Brussels views the Hungarian election as an opportunity to eliminate the unanimity requirement that has allowed sovereign nations to resist unpopular policies from unelected EU bureaucrats.

For years, Orban used Hungary's veto power to block initiatives he viewed as harmful to his nation's interests, including a €90 billion emergency loan package for Ukraine. Budapest had justified its position by citing Kiev's refusal to resume Russian oil supplies to Hungary through the Druzhba pipeline—a practical energy concern dismissed by Brussels.

The European Commission chief has long sought fundamental changes to EU governance structures, including creating a two-tier system that would fast-track Ukrainian membership despite that country failing to meet standard requirements. Von der Leyen stated the EU should "use the momentum now" from Orban's defeat to push through these controversial changes.

Brussels is currently withholding €35 billion in funds from Hungary over policy disagreements on issues including migration and LGBTQ rights—a financial pressure tactic critics describe as punishing nations that refuse to surrender sovereignty. The incoming Hungarian government, while more amenable to Brussels, has already indicated it will continue energy cooperation with Russia and questioned accelerated Ukrainian EU accession, suggesting the clash between national sovereignty and centralized EU control is far from over.
