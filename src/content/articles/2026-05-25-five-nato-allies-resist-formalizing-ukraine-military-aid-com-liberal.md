A proposal to require NATO member states to dedicate 0.25% of their GDP to military assistance for Ukraine has met resistance from five major allies, according to reporting from The Telegraph. The UK, France, Spain, Italy, and Canada have expressed reluctance about formalizing such a spending commitment, raising questions about the alliance's unified approach to supporting Kyiv.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte had hoped to secure approval for the mandatory funding measure at the alliance's upcoming summit in Ankara, Turkey, scheduled for July 7-8. However, sources within the organization indicate only seven of NATO's 32 member states currently support the proposal—nations that already meet or exceed the 0.25% threshold, including the Netherlands, Poland, and several Nordic and Baltic countries. Rutte has acknowledged the plan lacks the unanimous backing required for adoption.

The proposal emerged as European NATO members sought to demonstrate sustained commitment to Ukraine's defense amid significant reduction in U.S. assistance under the Trump administration. A British foreign office spokesperson stated that the UK "continues to engage with NATO allies on all proposals to ensure the alliance can best support Ukraine," suggesting ongoing diplomatic discussions about alternative approaches to coordinating aid.

Data from the Kiel Institute shows disparities in current spending levels among NATO members, with some nations contributing substantially more than others relative to their economic output. The debate over mandatory funding targets reflects broader tensions within the alliance about burden-sharing and long-term strategy for supporting Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity against ongoing Russian aggression.
