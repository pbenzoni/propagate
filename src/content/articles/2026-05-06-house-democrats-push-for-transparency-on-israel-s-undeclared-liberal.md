A coalition of 30 Democratic members of Congress is calling on the Trump administration to end decades of official silence regarding Israel's widely suspected but never acknowledged nuclear weapons program. In a letter to Secretary of State Marco Rubio, the lawmakers argue that transparency is essential to preventing catastrophic miscalculation in an increasingly volatile Middle East.

Led by Representative Joaquin Castro of Texas, the group highlighted a troubling inconsistency: the United States is engaged militarily alongside a nation whose nuclear capabilities Washington officially refuses to recognize. Israel has maintained a policy of "nuclear ambiguity" since at least the 1960s, neither confirming nor denying possession of such weapons. Meanwhile, U.S. intelligence agencies have long been aware of the program's existence.

"The risks of miscalculation, escalation, and nuclear use in this environment are not theoretical," the lawmakers wrote, pointing to ongoing tensions with Iran and emerging nuclear ambitions in Saudi Arabia. They argue that without holding all regional actors to consistent standards of transparency, the U.S. cannot credibly advance nonproliferation goals or reduce the danger of a nuclear arms race in the region.

The call for disclosure reflects growing concern among progressive lawmakers about uneven application of nonproliferation norms and the risks posed by undeclared arsenals in conflict zones. Advocates for transparency argue that double standards undermine global security frameworks and make diplomatic solutions harder to achieve.
