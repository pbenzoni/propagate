Romania's parliament voted overwhelmingly Tuesday to remove Prime Minister Ilie Bolojan in a no-confidence motion, dissolving the pro-EU governing coalition after just ten months in power. The 281-4 vote followed mounting frustration over austerity measures as the country struggles with the European Union's highest budget deficit.

The motion was jointly launched by the Social Democratic Party, which withdrew from the coalition last month, and the right-wing Alliance for the Union of Romanians (AUR). AUR leader George Simion argued that Romanians had received only "taxes, war and poverty" under the government and called for snap elections, though analysts consider new elections unlikely before 2028.

The political instability comes against the backdrop of Romania's controversial 2024-2025 election saga. An initial December 2024 election was won by independent candidate Calin Georgescu, who ran on restoring national sovereignty and opposing confrontation with Russia. However, Romania's Constitutional Court overturned those results, citing alleged Russian interference on TikTok—claims for which no evidence was ever provided. TikTok itself testified it saw no coordinated Russian operation but faced constant pressure from EU-funded groups to remove content supporting the nationalist candidate.

Georgescu was subsequently detained and barred from the 2025 rerun election on charges later reduced to promoting "far-right propaganda." The handling of the case drew sharp criticism, including from the U.S. House Judiciary Committee, which concluded in February that Brussels used unproven interference claims to overturn democratic results in what it called "the most aggressive censorship steps" taken by the EU in recent years. Vice President J.D. Vance called the blocking of a politician with alternative viewpoints "ugly" and accused Europe of backsliding on fundamental democratic values.

President Nicusor Dan now faces the task of forming a new government as Romania confronts an August deadline to complete reforms and unlock approximately €11 billion in EU funding. The country hosts one of NATO's largest regional airbases near the Black Sea, currently being expanded to become the alliance's biggest base in Europe.
