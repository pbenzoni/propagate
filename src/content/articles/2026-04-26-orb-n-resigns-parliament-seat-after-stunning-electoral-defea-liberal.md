His Fidesz party, which had maintained an iron grip on Hungarian politics for over a decade, was overwhelmingly rejected by voters in favor of opposition leader Peter Magyar's Tisza party, which secured 141 of 199 parliamentary seats.

In a video statement, Orbán said he would focus on "reorganizing the patriotic movement"—a term critics say is cover for regrouping Hungary's nationalist forces. While stepping back from parliament, Orbán made clear he intends to remain party leader, positioning himself to continue influencing Hungarian politics from behind the scenes. The move comes as no surprise to observers who have watched Orbán systematically erode democratic institutions, press freedoms, and the rule of law during his tenure.

The election loss is expected to fundamentally reshape Hungary's relationships with the European Union and NATO allies. Orbán had become a key obstacle to Western unity on Ukraine, repeatedly blocking aid packages and maintaining close ties with Vladimir Putin's Russia. Just weeks before the election, he froze Ukrainian funding in retaliation for oil supply disruptions—a move many saw as political theater designed to boost his flagging campaign.

The timing proved disastrous for Orbán. After his defeat, oil flows quickly resumed and Hungary lifted its veto on €90 billion in EU emergency funding for Ukraine.
