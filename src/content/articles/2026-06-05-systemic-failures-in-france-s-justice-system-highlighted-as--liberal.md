A body has been discovered in southwestern France during the search for 11-year-old Lyhanna, who disappeared on May 29 after reportedly being seen entering a vehicle outside her school near the town of Fleurance. While formal identification is still underway, the discovery follows days of intensive searching by approximately 170 police officers, volunteers, and specialized search teams.

The case has ignited public outrage and renewed calls for criminal justice reform after prosecutors revealed that the 41-year-old suspect had been the subject of multiple prior complaints involving sexual abuse and rape of minors—all of which were either dropped or dismissed. One case from 2018 was closed after a teenage victim stated the relationship was consensual. Another complaint alleging rape of a child under 15 was dismissed in 2024 due to insufficient evidence. Most troublingly, a separate complaint filed in August 2024 alleged ongoing abuse of a young girl, but the suspect had not yet been questioned when Lyhanna disappeared.

"What we're discovering day after day is absolutely unbearable," government spokeswoman Maud Bregeon told reporters. "It raises a profound question: what importance do we give to victims' testimonies? How are investigations conducted?" The suspect, who acknowledged giving Lyhanna a ride but claimed he dropped her off at a swimming pool, has been placed under formal investigation for alleged kidnapping and confinement of a minor.

Advocates for survivors are demanding systemic change. Anne-Cécile Mailfert of the Women's Foundation expressed being "stunned" and "angry" that a 10-year-old girl found the "immense courage" to report abuse last year, yet "nothing happened." Mailfert called for comprehensive legislation to combat sexual violence, stating bluntly: "The system doesn't work."
