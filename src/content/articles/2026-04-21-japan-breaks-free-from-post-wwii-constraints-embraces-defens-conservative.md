In a decisive move to confront mounting security challenges from China and North Korea, Japan has lifted decades-old restrictions on lethal arms sales, allowing defense exports to 17 allied nations including the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, a conservative leader who has long advocated for stronger national defense, announced the policy shift on Tuesday. She emphasized that the decision responds to a deteriorating security environment where "no single country can now protect its own peace and security alone." The change enables Japanese defense manufacturers to export a full range of military equipment to nations with existing defense cooperation agreements, moving beyond the limited categories of search-and-rescue and surveillance gear previously permitted.

The policy represents a pragmatic evolution of Japan's defense posture rather than an abandonment of peaceful principles, supporters argue. Takaichi insisted the nation remains committed to its post-war path as a "peace-loving nation," even as it takes concrete steps to support allied defenses. The Japan Self-Defense Forces have quietly evolved into a sophisticated modern military over recent decades, equipped with cutting-edge technology.

This latest development builds on previous policy adjustments, including Japan's 2023 decision to supply Patriot missile components to the United States—manufactured domestically by Mitsubishi Heavy Industries under American license. The move strengthens the web of defense partnerships among Pacific allies facing shared threats from authoritarian regimes, while creating new opportunities for Japanese defense industries to contribute to free world security.
