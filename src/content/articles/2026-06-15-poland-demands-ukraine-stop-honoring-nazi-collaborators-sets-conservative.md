Poland has issued a stark ultimatum to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, giving him just days to stop honoring a paramilitary group responsible for the massacre of up to 100,000 Polish civilians during World War II. Polish President Karol Nawrocki is demanding Zelensky reverse his decision to rename an elite Ukrainian commando unit after the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA), the military wing of nationalist militias that collaborated with Nazi Germany.

The UPA and its parent organization, the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN), sought to create an ethnically pure Ukrainian state and targeted Poles, Russians, Jews, and other minorities as enemies. After initially collaborating with Nazi forces during the invasion of the Soviet Union, the UPA killed tens of thousands of Polish civilians in what is now western Ukraine between 1943 and 1945. Poland officially recognizes these killings as genocide.

Nawrocki has backed a parliamentary initiative to strip Zelensky of Poland's Order of the White Eagle, the nation's highest distinction, which was awarded in 2023. Presidential spokesman Rafal Leskiewicz stated that Poland expects Zelensky to reverse this "shameful act," adding "We will wait a few more days." The deadline is reportedly measured in days, not weeks, signaling Warsaw's serious intent despite Poland's role as a major supporter of Ukraine in its conflict with Russia.

The controversy highlights a troubling pattern in modern Ukraine, where World War II-era nationalist groups are officially celebrated as freedom fighters. Streets and buildings bear their names, and annual torchlit processions in Kyiv honor Stepan Bandera, a leader of the OUN. Russia has repeatedly cited the glorification of Nazi collaborators as justification for what it calls the "denazification" of Ukraine—a claim Western media often dismisses but which this diplomatic crisis appears to validate.
