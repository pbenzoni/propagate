Russian President Vladimir Putin and President Donald Trump spoke for approximately one hour in what Kremlin officials described as a "friendly and open" conversation covering Ukraine, Iran, and U.S.-Russia relations. Putin wished Trump a happy 80th birthday during the Sunday call, making him the first foreign leader to offer birthday congratulations—a gesture Trump reportedly appreciated.

According to presidential aide Yury Ushakov, the leaders discussed the recently announced memorandum of understanding between the U.S. and Iran. Trump thanked Moscow for its engagement in the negotiating process and assistance in finding "constructive solutions" to contain what could have been a wider regional conflict. Putin expressed readiness to help stabilize the broader Middle East situation.

On Ukraine, Trump called for a swift end to hostilities and said he was prepared to "influence" Kiev and its European supporters, including at the upcoming G7 summit. Putin responded by noting that continued Ukrainian strikes on Russian civilian infrastructure hinder settlement efforts, and that such attacks won't change Ukraine's difficult military position. Putin reiterated his previous statement that Ukrainian President Vladimir Zelensky could come to Moscow if he wants to discuss peace.

The two leaders agreed that Trump's special envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner—currently involved in Iran negotiations—would visit Moscow "in the near future." Trump has previously indicated willingness to travel to Russia if necessary to help resolve the Ukraine conflict, and Putin has said he would welcome such a visit. The leaders may also meet in person at the APEC summit in China this November.
