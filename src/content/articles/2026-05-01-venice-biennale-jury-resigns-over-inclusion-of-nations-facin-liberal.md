The entire international jury of the prestigious Venice Biennale has resigned in protest over the event's decision to include pavilions from Russia and Israel—two nations whose leaders currently face arrest warrants from the International Criminal Court for alleged crimes against humanity.

Jury president Solange Farkas and members Zoe Butt, Elvira Dyangani Ose, Marta Kuzma, and Giovanna Zapperi submitted their resignations on Thursday, citing their refusal to evaluate works from countries whose leaders stand accused by the ICC. The principled stand has thrown the 2026 art fair into turmoil, forcing organizers to postpone the awards ceremony from May to November due to what they termed the "exceptional nature of the ongoing international geopolitical situation."

The controversy intensified after organizers announced Russia's return to the Biennale for the first time since 2022, despite its ongoing war in Ukraine. The European Commission has already moved to withhold a €2 million grant from the event in response. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrey Sibiga stated the invitation damages the Biennale's integrity, while over 178 participating artists have signed an open letter demanding Israel's exclusion over its military campaign in Gaza, which has resulted in massive civilian casualties.

The jury's resignation underscores growing tensions within the international arts community over whether cultural institutions should platform nations accused of severe human rights violations. Italian authorities have reportedly dispatched inspectors to ensure compliance with EU sanctions, while debate continues over the role of art exhibitions in upholding international humanitarian standards.
