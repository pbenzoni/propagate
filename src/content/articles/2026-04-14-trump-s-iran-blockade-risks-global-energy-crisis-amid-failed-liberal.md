The Trump administration has announced a naval blockade targeting Iranian oil shipments in the Persian Gulf, a decision that threatens to destabilize already fragile global energy markets. The move comes after unsuccessful diplomatic efforts to de-escalate the conflict following a US-Israeli bombing campaign earlier this year.

US Central Command stated the blockade will apply to vessels traveling to and from Iranian ports, covering both the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. The restrictions took effect Monday and will be "enforced impartially against vessels of all nations," though enforcement details remain unclear. The blockade targets the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint through which roughly a fifth of global oil supplies transit.

The decision has drawn sharp criticism from China, which issued warnings regarding US naval activity in the region. Shipping companies and energy traders have reportedly paused operations amid confusion over how the blockade will be enforced. Brent crude prices spiked above $100 per barrel following the announcement, underscoring the potential for global economic disruption.

Critics argue the blockade reverses earlier efforts to stabilize energy markets and represents an escalation of economic warfare that could harm American allies. Indirect peace talks hosted by Pakistan over the weekend failed to produce a breakthrough, with Vice President J.D. Vance framing the impasse as conditional on Iran reopening the Strait of Hormuz.
