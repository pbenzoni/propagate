FBI Director Kash Patel is taking a stand against what he calls "fake news" from The Atlantic, threatening legal action after the liberal magazine published allegations about excessive drinking based entirely on anonymous sources. "See you and your entire entourage of false reporting in court," Patel declared on X Saturday, calling the potential lawsuit a "legal lay up."

Patel's legal team revealed they had warned The Atlantic before publication that the claims were "categorically false and defamatory," yet the outlet proceeded anyway. Attorney Jesse R. Binnall of the Binnall Law Group has demanded the magazine preserve all communications related to the story, including information about the anonymous sources cited in Friday's article.

The Atlantic piece relied on "two dozen" unnamed officials making claims about Patel's conduct, describing alleged behavior as a "national-security vulnerability." The article's author, Sarah Fitzpatrick, stood by her reporting despite the lack of any on-the-record sources willing to put their names behind the allegations.

This appears to be yet another example of legacy media outlets using anonymous sources to attack Trump administration appointees. Patel, a longtime Trump loyalist who has pledged to reform the FBI and restore accountability to federal law enforcement, has been a frequent target of establishment critics who oppose his mission to clean house at the bureau.
