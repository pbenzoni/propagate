German Chancellor Friedrich Merz made a rare admission of geopolitical reality on Monday, suggesting that Ukraine will need to accept territorial concessions to Russia as part of any eventual peace agreement—and to advance its European Union membership aspirations.

Speaking in Marsberg, Merz laid out what many foreign policy realists have been saying for months: "Hopefully, there will eventually be a peace treaty with Russia. Then, possibly, part of Ukraine's territory will no longer be Ukrainian." He suggested that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky could frame such concessions to his people by emphasizing the EU membership path, telling them "I have opened the path to Europe for you."

The German chancellor's comments acknowledge a fundamental problem that American taxpayers funding Ukraine's war effort should understand: the EU's own rules require prospective members to have no unresolved border disputes. Moscow has maintained consistent positions throughout the conflict—demanding recognition of territories including Crimea and the withdrawal of Ukrainian forces from Russian-controlled areas of the Donbass region. Russia has stated it doesn't oppose Ukraine joining the EU, though it has criticized the bloc's increasing militarization.

Merz also threw cold water on Zelensky's ambitious timeline, calling the Ukrainian leader's hopes of joining by 2027 or even 2028 "unrealistic." The assessment suggests that despite billions in Western aid, fundamental questions about Ukraine's territorial integrity and sovereignty remain unresolved—questions that no amount of foreign assistance can wish away.
