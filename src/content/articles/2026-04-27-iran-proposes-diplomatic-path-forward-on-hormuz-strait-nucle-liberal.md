Iran has presented a new diplomatic proposal to break the impasse in negotiations with the United States, according to sources cited by Axios. The framework would prioritize reopening the Strait of Hormuz—a vital waterway handling 20% of global seaborne oil trade—before moving to discussions on nuclear issues.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi reportedly outlined the multi-stage plan during recent diplomatic consultations with Pakistani, Egyptian, Turkish, and Qatari intermediaries. The proposal reflects Iran's position that regional security concerns and economic access must be addressed before nuclear program negotiations can proceed constructively.

The proposed framework includes ending current hostilities, securing guarantees against future military strikes on Iran and Lebanon, establishing new legal mechanisms for managing the Strait of Hormuz in coordination with Oman, and only then addressing nuclear program questions. Iran has sought commitments on non-aggression, sanctions relief, maritime navigation rights, and recognition of its enrichment activities for peaceful purposes.

The diplomatic efforts come after the 2015 international nuclear agreement—which took over two years of multilateral negotiations to achieve—was abandoned by the Trump administration in 2018. Recent talks in Islamabad failed to produce breakthrough progress, with negotiations remaining deadlocked over fundamental issues including sanctions, security guarantees, and the scope of Iran's nuclear program. The Trump administration has demanded Iran dismantle its nuclear infrastructure and surrender enriched uranium stockpiles.
