Vice President J.D. Vance announced Monday that the United States has accomplished its core objectives in Iran and is now positioned to wind down military operations, preferably through successful diplomatic negotiations.

Speaking to Fox News after weekend talks in Pakistan, Vance emphasized that America has made significant progress toward its goals, particularly on critical national security issues involving Iran's nuclear program. "I do think that we're in a place where we've accomplished our objectives. We can start to wind this thing down," the Vice President said, adding that he would "much rather wind this thing down with a big successful negotiation."

The Vice President reported meaningful movement from Iranian negotiators on key American demands, including the removal of nuclear material and preventing future uranium enrichment. "They moved in our direction," Vance stated, though he noted Iranian officials needed approval from Tehran before finalizing any agreement. He made clear that "the ball is in the Iranian court" on whether negotiations will succeed.

Vance outlined that if U.S. "red lines" on Iran's nuclear program are met, the resulting agreement "can be a very, very good deal for both countries." His comments came as President Trump implemented new pressure tactics at the Strait of Hormuz to bring Iran back to the negotiating table and reopen critical oil shipping routes.

Iranian officials, however, have expressed skepticism about American intentions and insist Washington must accept their terms to reach any final agreement.
