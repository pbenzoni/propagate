The Pentagon has revised its estimate for the Iran conflict to $29 billion, but independent analysts are sounding the alarm that American taxpayers may be footing a far larger bill. Acting Pentagon Comptroller Jules Hurst told the House Appropriations Committee this week that most costs stem from munitions used against Iran—up from an April estimate of $25 billion.

Yet according to journalist Stephen Semler of the Security Policy Reform Institute, Washington spent an estimated $71.8 billion in just the first 60 days of fighting. His analysis, based on military procurement data and official statements, found that expended munitions alone exceeded $41 billion, with operational costs and damage to U.S. bases accounting for the remainder. This disconnect between official figures and independent research raises serious questions about transparency and fiscal responsibility.

The conflict has also taken a heavy toll on America's military readiness. The Center for Strategic and International Studies warns that the U.S. has burned through roughly half its stockpile of critical interceptors like Patriot and THAAD missiles—possibly as much as 80% of THAAD inventory, "the most critical due to their low inventory and lack of alternatives." Meanwhile, despite President Trump's claims of largely destroying Iran's capabilities, U.S. intelligence estimates cited by the New York Times indicate Tehran has retained approximately 70% of its mobile launchers and missile stockpile, and restored 90% of its underground facilities and 30 of 33 coastal sites along the strategic Strait of Hormuz.

As negotiations remain deadlocked and the U.S. blockade continues, Americans deserve a full accounting of what this conflict is truly costing in both dollars and military preparedness.
