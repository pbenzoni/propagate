Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced Friday that Spain has been expelled from a U.S.-backed coordination center overseeing the Gaza ceasefire, framing the move as retaliation for Madrid's criticism of Israeli military operations in Lebanon.

The confrontation escalated after Spanish officials condemned Israeli airstrikes in Lebanon on April 8—launched just hours after a two-week U.S.-Iran ceasefire was announced. Lebanese authorities reported at least 357 people killed, most of them civilians, and more than 1,223 wounded in what the IDF called a ten-minute operation targeting Hezbollah. Spanish Foreign Minister Jose Manuel Albares described the attacks as "indiscriminate bombings" that violated international law, while Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez called Netanyahu's actions "intolerable" and urged the European Union to suspend its association agreement with Israel.

Spain has emerged as one of Europe's most outspoken critics of the U.S.-Israeli military campaign against Iran, which began in late February. Last month, Madrid closed its airspace to U.S. military aircraft associated with the conflict and permanently withdrew its ambassador from Israel. On Thursday, Spain announced it would reopen its embassy in Tehran and return its ambassador—a diplomatic gesture aimed at supporting de-escalation efforts and ongoing peace talks in Pakistan.

The broader regional conflict remains volatile as senior Iranian and U.S. negotiators meet in Islamabad for what Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif characterized as a "make-or-break moment" to transform the fragile ceasefire into a more comprehensive agreement. Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf expressed cautious optimism tempered by mistrust, while U.S. Vice President J.D. Vance said he expects productive discussions but warned Iran against negotiating in bad faith.
