A new Gallup survey released Tuesday shows Americans are stepping back from decades of moral permissiveness, with significant declines in acceptance of behaviors that have long concerned traditional families and faith communities.

The poll found the sharpest year-over-year drops in acceptance of having children outside marriage (down 9 percentage points to 58%), gambling (down 6 points to 57%), and sex between teenagers (standing at just 35%). Perhaps most notably, six behaviors tracked by Gallup have now reached record lows in moral acceptability, including changing one's gender (38%) and animal cloning (27%).

While majorities of Americans still consider some behaviors acceptable—including divorce (74%) and sex between unmarried adults (65%)—the polling suggests the culture may be shifting. Opinion on abortion remained closely divided, with 49% calling it morally acceptable and 41% saying it's morally wrong.

The survey, conducted among 1,001 adults nationwide between May 1 and May 17, also revealed sharp partisan divides. Democrats were far more likely than Republicans to view abortion, gender transition, and same-sex relations as morally acceptable, while Republicans showed stronger support for the death penalty.

According to Gallup, while Americans remain more accepting of most tracked behaviors than they were two decades ago, the findings suggest that the long march toward greater moral permissiveness has "largely leveled off and, in some cases, may be beginning to reverse." The data may signal growing concern among everyday Americans about the social costs of abandoning time-tested moral standards.
