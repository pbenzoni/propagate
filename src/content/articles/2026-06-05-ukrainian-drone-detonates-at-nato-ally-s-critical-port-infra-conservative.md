A Ukrainian maritime drone carrying explosives detonated Friday morning in Romania's Constanta port, triggering evacuations and a major emergency response near critical oil terminal infrastructure. Romanian authorities confirmed the unmanned vessel resembled the MAGURA V5 model used by Kiev's military intelligence, capable of carrying hundreds of kilograms of explosives.

Romania's Defense Ministry stated the drone self-detonated around 10:30 AM after becoming stuck in an anti-pollution barrier several hundred meters from the oil terminal. Officials emphasized the drone did not belong to the Romanian military and was "of the type used in the war in Ukraine." Local authorities issued a Code Red warning, ordering residents to stay at least one kilometer from the shoreline due to threats of additional explosions.

Constanta County Prefect Adrian Picoiu identified the drones as Ukrainian in origin, while local reports indicated three additional drone detonations occurred—one near Constanta and two in Ukrainian waters. The Russian Embassy in Romania characterized the devices as "Ukrainian unmanned maritime vehicles, used by the Kiev regime to commit terrorist acts against civilian ships and to create threats to the safety of navigation in the Black Sea."

The incident represents the latest in a pattern of Ukrainian drones straying into neighboring countries' territory, including NATO members and neutral states. In recent months, Ukrainian unmanned aircraft have repeatedly entered the airspace of Baltic nations and Finland, triggering warnings, airport shutdowns, and infrastructure damage. Critics note Western governments have consistently downplayed such incidents when Ukrainian responsibility is confirmed, raising concerns about accountability and the risk of drawing NATO into direct confrontation through cross-border provocations targeting alliance member states.
