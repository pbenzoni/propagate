British fighter jets stationed in Romania were granted authorization to shoot down Russian drones operating near the NATO member's border with Ukraine, according to statements from Romania's Defense Ministry. The incident, which occurred early Saturday morning, underscores the dangerous spillover effects of Russia's ongoing invasion and the risks to civilian populations in neighboring countries.

Two RAF Eurofighter Typhoon jets scrambled from an air base in Fetesti at 2 AM after establishing radar contact with targets approximately 1.5 kilometers from the Ukrainian border town of Reni. While the pilots received clearance to engage the drones, officials from both the UK and Romania later clarified that the aircraft never entered Ukrainian airspace and did not fire any weapons, returning to base after completing their surveillance mission.

The incident comes amid reports of drone debris falling in Romanian territory near Galati, where fragments damaged civilian infrastructure including an outbuilding and electrical pole, though fortunately no casualties were reported. Romanian officials condemned what they called Russia's "irresponsible" actions and disregard for international law, warning that such incidents endanger both Romanian citizens and NATO's collective security.

Since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the Danube River has become an increasingly important supply route for humanitarian and defensive aid flowing to Ukraine from European allies.
