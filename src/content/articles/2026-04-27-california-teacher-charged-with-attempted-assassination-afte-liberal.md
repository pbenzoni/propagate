Federal prosecutors have charged Cole Allen, a 31-year-old teacher from California, with attempted assassination of the president and related weapons offenses following an alleged security breach at the White House Correspondents' Dinner on Saturday.

According to Assistant U.S. Attorney Jocelyn Ballantine, Allen was armed with a 12-gauge pump action shotgun, a .38-caliber semi-automatic pistol, and three knives when arrested after allegedly attempting to rush the high-profile event. U.S. Magistrate Judge Matthew Sharbaugh ordered Allen detained while the case proceeds, with a preliminary hearing scheduled for May 11.

U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche emphasized in a press conference that the suspect never posed an imminent threat to the president. "This man was a floor above the ballroom, with hundreds of federal agents between him and the president of the U.S.," Blanche stated. The incident resulted in gunfire, with Blanche noting that "there were five shots that law enforcement fired," though investigators are still examining the ballistics evidence to determine exactly what occurred.

Allen faces charges including attempted assassination of the president, interstate transportation of weapons, and discharge of a firearm during a violent crime. The case has raised questions about security protocols at high-profile events and the protection of journalists and officials at the annual correspondents' dinner, a tradition celebrating press freedom.
