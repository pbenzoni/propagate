Iran will not engage in diplomatic talks while facing U.S. military threats and a blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, according to Tehran's ambassador to China, Abdolreza Rahmani Fazli. In an interview, the envoy emphasized that Iran maintained compliance with International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) standards even after the United States withdrew from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA)—the multilateral nuclear agreement negotiated during the Obama administration.

Fazli criticized the current U.S. approach as lacking reciprocity, stating that Washington's conditions amount to demanding "surrender" rather than genuine dialogue. He pointed to the military threats and naval blockade as undermining any possibility of productive negotiations. "What they expect from Iran is not dialogue, but surrender," the envoy said, adding that such coercive tactics are "affecting the atmosphere of the talks."

The diplomat maintained that Iran's nuclear program serves peaceful purposes aligned with international law and the country's legitimate energy needs. He accused the U.S. of breaking promises during previous negotiation rounds and stressed that Iran's right to peaceful nuclear energy enrichment remains non-negotiable. "Iran's right to the peaceful use of nuclear energy and imposition of illogical requests upon Iran. That is our red line," Fazli stated.

These comments come as negotiators from both nations prepare for talks in Pakistan, where a fragile ceasefire is set to expire. The Iranian delegation will be led by Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, while the U.S. side will reportedly include Vice President J.D. Vance and presidential envoys.
