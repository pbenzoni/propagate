The peace deal taking shape between the United States and Iran is drawing sharp criticism from Israeli opposition leader Yair Lapid, who argues the agreement falls short of addressing fundamental security concerns. The former prime minister said Saturday that the emerging framework "achieves none of Israel's war objectives," leaving Iran's governing regime in place while preserving its missile program and nuclear infrastructure.

President Trump announced the deal would be signed Sunday, though Iranian officials indicated a memorandum of understanding would be finalized in the coming days without confirming the specific timeline. According to available details, Iran would pledge not to pursue nuclear weapons while the U.S. would dilute and destroy the country's enriched uranium stockpile. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Friday the understanding would focus on ending hostilities and reopening the Strait of Hormuz, with a 60-day window for additional negotiations on nuclear matters.

The deal comes after the U.S. and Israel launched a joint military campaign against Iran in late February, prompting retaliatory strikes across the Middle East and leading Tehran to close the critical Strait of Hormuz to most shipping—a move that triggered global energy price spikes. A truce was reached in April after weeks of escalating tensions.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, meanwhile, has maintained his hardline stance on Iran's nuclear ambitions. "As long as I am prime minister of Israel, Iran will not obtain nuclear weapons," Netanyahu said Friday, emphasizing complete agreement with President Trump on the issue. However, reports from Axios and CNN suggest the Israeli leader was caught off guard by how close the deal was to completion. Trump reportedly acknowledged pressuring Netanyahu to halt airstrikes in Lebanon during several tense phone conversations, stating Israel would have "no choice" but to accept the agreement.

Critics both in the Democratic Party and among some Republicans have questioned whether the military action served American interests or primarily benefited Israel. President Trump has firmly rejected such characterizations, insisting his actions prioritized United States security and strategic objectives in the volatile region.
