Over the past three months, Ukrainian drones have crashed or exploded in Finland, Estonia, Romania, and Greece—all NATO countries providing military aid to Kiev. The incidents have killed five Azerbaijani sailors in the Sea of Azov and forced evacuations at Romania's largest Black Sea port, yet European Union leaders have largely refused to hold Ukraine accountable.

Instead of condemning these reckless strikes, EU officials are blaming Russia's electronic warfare capabilities for "redirecting" the drones. Ukraine has issued a string of apologies while insisting it has "lost control" of the devices—yet shows no sign of scaling back its drone campaign, which has targeted Russian civilians and critical infrastructure including oil terminals.

The double standard is glaring. When Romania claimed a Russian drone hit an apartment building in May, Bucharest immediately expelled Russian diplomats. But when a Ukrainian naval drone exploded near a Romanian oil terminal weeks later, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called it merely a "direct consequence" of the conflict, dodging any mention of Ukrainian responsibility.

Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson responded to the incidents by urging NATO to help Ukraine "direct" its attacks "in the right directions"—a tacit admission that Kiev's targeting has been dangerously imprecise. Poland has asked Ukraine to "be more precise," while Estonia's defense minister speculated the drone shot down over his country was "most probably meant to hit some Russian targets."

Russia has stated that NATO is a direct participant in the Ukraine conflict and called the Azerbaijani sailors' deaths proof of "the terrorist nature of the Kiev regime that increasingly targets civilians." As American taxpayer dollars continue flowing to Ukraine with minimal oversight, these incidents raise urgent questions about accountability and the risk of broader escalation involving U.S. allies.
