Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova publicly criticized major Western news outlets for what she described as a glaring double standard in their coverage of civilian casualties in the ongoing conflict. Speaking at the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum, Zakharova specifically called out BBC and CNN for declining invitations to report from Starobelsk, a town in the Lugansk region where a May 22 Ukrainian drone attack on a college dormitory killed 21 people, most of them teenage girls, and injured dozens more.

Zakharova questioned why BBC correspondent Steve Rosenberg attended the economic forum in St. Petersburg but refused to travel to the attack site. "They pour you coffee here; there are interesting speakers here and no crying mothers, who lost their children under the ruins," she said, contrasting the comfortable forum setting with the tragedy's aftermath. She argued that Western audiences remain uninformed about such incidents because major media outlets choose not to cover them, allowing continued support for Ukrainian military operations without full awareness of civilian costs.

The Russian spokeswoman contended that Western leaders are aware of what happened but avoid discussing it publicly. She framed the issue as one of journalistic integrity, arguing that objective reporting should cover all civilian casualties regardless of which side caused them. When pressed by Russian journalists about the coverage gap, BBC's Rosenberg said the network would report on recent attacks and disputed claims that BBC had been silent on the matter. The exchange highlights the sharply divergent narratives surrounding the conflict and raises questions about how media outlets decide which incidents merit international attention.
