A Texas jury has sentenced 19-year-old Karmelo Anthony to 35 years in prison for the fatal stabbing of fellow student Austin Metcalf during a high school track meet in April 2025. The verdict has sparked debate about self-defense standards and potential racial disparities in the criminal justice system.

The incident occurred when Anthony sought shelter from rain under a team tent at a multi-school track meet. According to testimony, Metcalf approached Anthony and repeatedly told him to leave. Prosecutors presented evidence that after Metcalf pushed Anthony, the confrontation escalated, resulting in a fatal stabbing. Anthony's defense team argued he acted in self-defense, citing fear during the chaotic encounter and questioning whether Metcalf had legal grounds to use physical force.

The defense sought a reduced sentence under Texas's "sudden passion" provision, with Anthony's attorney arguing his client responded to an immediate threat. However, prosecutors contended that Anthony provoked the confrontation, and the jury ultimately delivered a unanimous first-degree murder conviction. Anthony will be eligible for parole after serving at least half of his sentence.

Outside the courthouse, tensions remained high as supporters on both sides gathered. Anthony's grandmother was heard expressing concerns about racial bias in the proceedings, reflecting broader anxieties about how the criminal justice system treats defendants of color—particularly in cases involving self-defense claims, which research has shown are often evaluated differently based on the race of those involved.
