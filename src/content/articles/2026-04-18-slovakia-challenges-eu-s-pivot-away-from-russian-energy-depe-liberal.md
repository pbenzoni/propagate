Slovakia announced plans to file a legal challenge at the EU's Court of Justice over the bloc's recent decision to phase out Russian pipeline gas imports by 2027. Prime Minister Robert Fico framed the lawsuit as a defense of member state sovereignty within EU governance structures.

The challenge centers on procedural concerns about how the decision was reached. "We object that where it was not possible to use a qualified majority, it was used, and that the right of a sovereign EU member state to veto something was circumvented," Fico stated at a Friday press conference. Justice Minister Boris Susko indicated the lawsuit would be filed next week, with Slovakia also seeking an injunction to suspend the regulation.

The January EU decision overrode vetoes from both Slovakia and Hungary, reflecting tensions within the bloc over energy policy and sanctions related to Russia's actions in Ukraine. Hungary filed a similar legal challenge in February. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has separately advocated for limiting member state veto powers to prevent obstruction of aid to Ukraine.

The legal dispute highlights ongoing debates within the EU about balancing collective action on foreign policy with individual member states' economic interests and governance rights. Energy security remains a contentious issue as European nations navigate their transition away from Russian energy sources while managing domestic economic pressures.
