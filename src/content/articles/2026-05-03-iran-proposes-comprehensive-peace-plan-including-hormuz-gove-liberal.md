Iran has submitted a detailed peace proposal to the United States through Pakistani intermediaries, seeking what officials describe as a decisive and lasting diplomatic resolution rather than temporary measures, according to reports from Tehran. Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi confirmed the submission, stating that the initiative now awaits Washington's response.

The 14-point plan includes provisions for guarantees against future military strikes, the lifting of economic sanctions that have devastated Iran's economy and impacted its civilian population, the withdrawal of U.S. military forces from the region, and a new international framework for governing the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz—a waterway through which roughly one-fifth of global oil supplies pass. The proposal calls for resolving outstanding issues within a 30-day timeframe.

President Trump indicated he would review the offer but expressed skepticism about its viability, later posting on social media that he "can't imagine" accepting the proposal. His remarks underscored the significant diplomatic challenges facing efforts to de-escalate tensions in the region. The contrasting approaches—Iran's pursuit of a comprehensive diplomatic framework versus the administration's stance—highlight the complex dynamics at play as multiple nations seek pathways to regional stability and away from potential military escalation.
