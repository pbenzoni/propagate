President Donald Trump announced Saturday that Abu-Bilal al-Minuki, the second-in-command of the Islamic State worldwide, has been killed in Nigeria following a joint U.S.-Nigerian military operation he personally authorized.

Writing on Truth Social, Trump described the mission as "meticulously planned and very complex," crediting American intelligence and military coordination with Nigerian forces. "The most active terrorist in the world… thought he could hide in Africa, but little did he know we had sources who kept us informed on what he was doing," the president stated. "He will no longer terrorize the people of Africa, or help plan operations to target Americans."

The strike targeted an Islamic State compound in the Lake Chad Basin, eliminating al-Minuki along with several of his top lieutenants. U.S. Africa Command released aerial footage of the bombardment. Nigerian President Bola Ahmed Tinubu confirmed the operation's success in eliminating multiple high-value ISIS targets.

Al-Minuki, a Nigerian national, was designated a "specially designated global terrorist" by the State Department in 2023. Officials said he directed operational guidance and funding for Islamic State activities around the world. The killing follows Trump's earlier deployment of 200 American troops to provide training and intelligence support—though not combat operations—to Nigerian forces fighting jihadist groups in the region.

This marks another major counterterrorism victory for Trump, who in 2019 during his first term ordered the raid that killed ISIS founder Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi in Syria. The president emphasized that with al-Minuki's removal, "ISIS's global operation is greatly diminished."
