Bacterial sexually transmitted infections have surged to record levels across Europe, according to new data from the European Center for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC). The 2024 report reveals that gonorrhoea reached its highest point since EU monitoring began in 2009, with over 106,000 confirmed cases across 28 EU and EEA countries.

Syphilis infections more than doubled during the monitoring period, reaching 45,577 cases, while chlamydia remained the most commonly reported STI with 213,443 cases. According to the ECDC, men accounted for the vast majority of reported gonorrhoea (62%) and syphilis (69%) cases, with infection rates among men significantly outpacing those among women.

"Sexually transmitted infections have been on the rise for 10 years and reached record high levels in 2024," said Bruno Ciancio, head of ECDC's unit for directly transmitted and vaccine-preventable diseases. He warned that untreated infections can cause severe complications including chronic pain, infertility, and in the case of syphilis, problems with the heart or nervous system.

The ECDC attributed the increase to failures in prevention and screening efforts, along with unequal access to testing across Europe. The report noted that 13 of 29 reporting countries require patients to pay out-of-pocket for basic STI tests. While several EU nations have embraced progressive social policies, others including Bulgaria, Romania, Lithuania, and Slovakia have maintained more traditional approaches to family policy. Hungary recently shifted course after its election, while Russia has implemented laws restricting the promotion of non-traditional lifestyles to minors while maintaining that relationships between consenting adults remain a private matter.
