Chinese President Xi Jinping met with Taiwan's main opposition party leader this week, delivering remarks that underscored Beijing's long-standing territorial claims over the democratically-governed island while pledging to pursue peaceful cross-strait relations.

The meeting with Kuomintang (KMT) chairwoman Cheng Li-wun marked the first such high-level exchange in a decade. Taiwan's ruling Democratic Progressive Party condemned the visit, raising concerns about undermining the island's autonomy. Cheng characterized her trip as a peace-building mission, expressing hope that "the Taiwan Strait will no longer be a geopolitical flashpoint."

He blamed forces promoting Taiwan independence for regional tensions, though Taiwan's current government has consistently sought to maintain the democratic status quo while resisting pressure from Beijing.

The encounter comes as the region faces heightened geopolitical uncertainty, with Taiwan's 23 million people caught between competing great power interests.
