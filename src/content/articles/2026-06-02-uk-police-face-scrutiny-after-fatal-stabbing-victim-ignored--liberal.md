British authorities have launched an investigation after bodycam footage revealed that police officers handcuffed a dying stabbing victim while believing false claims made by his attacker. The case has raised urgent questions about implicit bias in policing and the need for systemic reform.

Henry Nowak, 18, told officers five times that he had been stabbed, but was handcuffed and arrested while his attacker, Vickrum Digwa, 23, remained free. Digwa had falsely claimed he was the victim of a racist attack. Nowak lost consciousness and died in custody as officers failed to call an ambulance. Digwa was convicted of murder last week and sentenced to 21 years in prison, with prosecutors calling his racism claim "a wicked lie."

Nowak's father described the treatment his son received as "inhumane and degrading," noting the stark contrast with how police treated Digwa, who was not handcuffed and was even allowed to choose his food. The UK's Independent Office for Police Conduct is investigating the officers' actions.

Prime Minister Keir Starmer's office called the case "shocking and harrowing" while maintaining there is no systemic disparity in policing. However, civil rights advocates point to the incident as evidence of how unconscious bias and assumptions about race can have deadly consequences, underscoring the urgent need for comprehensive police training reform and accountability measures to prevent similar tragedies.
