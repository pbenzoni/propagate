Bulgarian voters turned out in higher-than-expected numbers Sunday for a critical election that will determine the country's future relationship with the European Union and its stance on the war in Ukraine. The contest pits former Prime Minister Boyko Borissov, whose center-right GERB-SDS party has pledged continued support for Ukraine and alignment with EU foreign policy, against former President Rumen Radev, who has called for a more balanced approach to international relations.

Radev, who served as Bulgaria's president from 2017 to 2026, campaigned on an anti-corruption platform, vowing to dismantle what he calls the "oligarchic mafia" controlling Bulgarian politics. His Progressive Bulgaria faction has promised to maintain the country's European orientation while pursuing "practical relations with Russia based on mutual respect." Borissov, who led the country for 12 years through various coalition governments, ruled out forming another coalition after this election despite his party polling at only around 20%.

The election has drawn scrutiny from Brussels, which activated its Rapid Response System to monitor and remove disinformation on social media platforms during the voting period—a tool the EU has deployed in several recent European elections. Turnout reached 17% by noon, notably higher than the 13.5% recorded at the same point during October 2024's snap elections, suggesting renewed civic engagement after years of electoral fatigue that saw just 33-38% participation in last year's contests.

The vote represents Bulgaria's eighth election in five years, reflecting persistent political instability that has challenged the country's democratic institutions and its ability to address pressing economic and social issues facing its citizens.
