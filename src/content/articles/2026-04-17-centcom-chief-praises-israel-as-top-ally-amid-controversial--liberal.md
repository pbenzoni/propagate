US Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper publicly lauded Israel as an "exceptional" military partner during the ongoing conflict with Iran, calling it the nation's "best teammate" in a Pentagon press conference Thursday. His comments came as concerns mount over how the United States entered the war and whether the Trump administration prioritized Israeli interests over American ones.

Cooper praised multiple Middle Eastern nations hosting U.S. bases, including Jordan, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, and the UAE. However, he singled out Israel for special recognition, noting that coordination between Israeli and American air forces had "exceeded" military objectives. The admiral confirmed recent coordination meetings with Israeli military leadership to ensure the allies remained "closely aligned."

The remarks have intensified an ongoing debate about the war's origins. Democrats, some Republicans, and former National Counterterrorism Center chief Joe Kent have argued that Trump was pressured by Israel to launch military action. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has characterized the conflict as Trump abandoning his "America First" campaign promise in favor of an "Israel First" approach. Kent, who resigned in protest last month, has stated that intelligence agencies could not verify Trump's claims about Iranian nuclear weapons development—a key justification for the conflict.

Secretary of War Pete Hegseth pushed back against criticisms during the same press conference, insisting Trump "makes his own decisions about the interests of this country." The exchange comes as a two-week truce nears expiration and reports suggest possible peace talks may occur this week.
