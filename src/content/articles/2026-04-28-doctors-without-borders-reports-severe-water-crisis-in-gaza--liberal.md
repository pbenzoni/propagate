Doctors Without Borders (MSF) has released a report documenting what it describes as a severe humanitarian crisis in Gaza, where water scarcity has reached levels "incompatible with human dignity and survival." The international medical organization states that access to water, sanitation, and hygiene has been critically compromised since October 2023, triggering a sharp increase in waterborne diseases including diarrhea, skin infections, and infected wounds.

Gaza lacks natural freshwater sources and depends on treated groundwater and seawater. According to MSF, much of the critical infrastructure—including desalination plants, boreholes, pipelines, and sewage systems—has become inoperable or inaccessible. The collapse of public water systems has forced MSF to become the largest non-governmental water provider in the territory, operating mobile desalination units and distributing water by truck to affected communities.

The humanitarian threshold for water access is approximately 15 liters per person per day, including 6 liters for drinking. UNICEF reports that Gazans are at best receiving this bare minimum for survival, with many unable to access even that amount of safe drinking water. The water shortage is exacerbating malnutrition and significantly impacting mental health across the population.

Israeli authorities have disputed MSF's characterization, with COGAT stating that Israel is facilitating water access through four supply lines, infrastructure repairs, and provision of fuel and electricity for water systems. Despite a ceasefire agreement, the UN reports that over 700 Palestinians have been killed since the truce began, with total casualties since October 2023 exceeding 72,000 according to Gaza's health authorities.
