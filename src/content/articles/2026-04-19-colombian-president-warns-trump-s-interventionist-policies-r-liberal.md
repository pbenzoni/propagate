Colombian President Gustavo Petro has issued a stark warning that the Trump administration's aggressive interventions in Latin America could trigger a historic regional backlash. In an interview published Saturday, Petro drew parallels between current U.S. policies and colonial-era oppression, suggesting such approaches could ignite widespread resistance across the continent.

The Colombian leader specifically criticized U.S. sanctions against him and his family members, calling them tools of political coercion rather than legitimate anti-drug measures. "This instrument for fighting drug trafficking is used as a mechanism of extortion against those of us who express different political views," Petro said, comparing the system to Spanish colonial rule. "And what was the Latin American response? Rebellion. That will happen now if the U.S. government isn't able to rethink its relationship with Latin America."

Petro's comments come amid heightened tensions following a January raid on Caracas that resulted in the detention of Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro. The Colombian president described the incident as a shocking violation of sovereignty, noting that Caracas became "the first Latin American city to be bombed in its history." He warned that such actions create wounds that force regional leaders into a position of fearful submission.

Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva echoed similar concerns in a separate interview, criticizing what he described as the Trump administration's unilateral approach to global politics. Lula, who has been among the most vocal critics of recent U.S. actions in the region, warned that the president "has no right to wake up in the morning and threaten a country," adding that such tactics ultimately create problems for the United States itself. He characterized recent interventions as recalling "the worst moments of interference in the politics of Latin America."
