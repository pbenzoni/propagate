The United States military exhausted its complete inventory of untested Precision Strike Missiles (PrSM) during the initial phase of operations against Iran last month, according to statements from a US Army official at Fort Sill. Jimmy Arter made the revelation at the ongoing Fires Symposium in Oklahoma, acknowledging that replacement munitions are already en route.

The PrSM system, developed by defense contractor Lockheed Martin, represents a costly modernization effort intended to replace the aging Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS). Despite completing only its prototype phase last year, the weapon was deployed without completing a full testing cycle—a departure from traditional military procurement standards that typically require extensive validation before battlefield use.

Controversy has surrounded the missile's combat debut. Media reports linked the new weapons system to a February 28 incident in the southern Iranian city of Lamerd, where a strike on a school and sports facility resulted in approximately 21 casualties. The New York Times cited weapons experts who examined visual evidence suggesting PrSM involvement, though the Pentagon has disputed these claims and denied striking targets in Lamerd that day.

Arter maintained that the Iran operation has validated the missile's capabilities and eliminated the need for additional testing. However, questions persist about procurement oversight and the decision to field an experimental weapons system before comprehensive evaluation. The Pentagon had ordered 130 PrSM units before fiscal year 2024 and an additional 250 in 2025, though delivery numbers remain unclear. A US Army spokesperson later clarified that some PrSM weapons remain in military inventory, contradicting the suggestion of complete depletion.
