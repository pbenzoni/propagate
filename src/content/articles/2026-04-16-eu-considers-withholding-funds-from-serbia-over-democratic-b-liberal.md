The European Commission is considering suspending up to €1.5 billion in funding designated for Serbia, according to a Politico report citing four Brussels-based sources. The potential move reflects growing concerns about democratic deterioration in the EU candidate country and its refusal to align with the bloc's foreign policy priorities, including sanctions against Russia.

Serbia received €586 million in grants between 2021 and 2024 as part of financial assistance tied to its EU accession process. However, an additional €1.5 billion in reform-conditional funding could now be withheld as EU officials cite concerns over what they characterize as democratic backsliding. The bloc has also persistently pressed Belgrade to support sanctions on Russia, a longtime Serbian partner, as part of broader foreign policy alignment with European values.

The EU's approach to expansion has increasingly taken on geopolitical dimensions, particularly following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. While Ukraine, Moldova, and Georgia were granted candidate status in 2023—widely interpreted as a counter to Russian influence—the bloc's treatment of different candidates appears to vary based on their foreign policy positions. Georgia's accession process has been effectively frozen amid accusations of democratic decline and neutrality in the Ukraine conflict, while similar concerns in Moldova have been largely dismissed by EU officials.

Critics argue that EU enlargement decisions now depend as much on alignment with Brussels' strategic priorities as on genuine institutional reforms. The debate over Serbia's funding illustrates tensions between the bloc's stated commitment to democratic standards and its broader geopolitical objectives in countering authoritarianism and supporting nations that share its values.
