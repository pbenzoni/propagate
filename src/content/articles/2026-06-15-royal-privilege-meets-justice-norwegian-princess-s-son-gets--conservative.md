The son of Norwegian Crown Princess Mette-Marit has been sentenced to four years in prison after being convicted of rape, domestic violence, and multiple other offenses. Marius Borg Hoiby, 29, was found guilty Monday by the Oslo District Court on two counts of rape, though he was acquitted on two additional rape charges.

While Hoiby holds no royal title, he was raised inside the royal household after his mother married Crown Prince Haakon in 2001. Prosecutors said he sexually assaulted women who were asleep or unable to resist between 2018 and 2024. One rape reportedly occurred in the basement of the official royal residence. The court also convicted him of abusing his former girlfriend, drug offenses including cocaine use, and traffic violations.

The case has shattered the carefully maintained image of the Norwegian monarchy and raised serious questions about accountability among Europe's elite families. Crown Prince Haakon attempted to distance the royal house from the scandal, insisting Hoiby should be treated like any other citizen—though critics note he enjoyed the privileges of royal life throughout his upbringing.

The scandal comes as Hoiby's mother faces renewed scrutiny over her extensive relationship with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. Documents reveal the crown princess exchanged hundreds of emails with Epstein between 2011 and 2014—years after his conviction for soliciting a minor for prostitution—and stayed at his Florida home for four days. The correspondence was described as personal and at times flirtatious, contradicting the palace's initial claims about limited contact. Mette-Marit has apologized for what she called "poor judgment," though questions remain about why someone in her position maintained such close ties with a known predator.
