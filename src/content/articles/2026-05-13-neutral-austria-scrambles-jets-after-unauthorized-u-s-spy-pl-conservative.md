Austria deployed fighter jets to intercept a pair of U.S. Air Force reconnaissance planes that entered the country's airspace without authorization over two consecutive days, according to the Austrian Defense Ministry.

Defense Ministry spokesman Michael Bauer confirmed that two PC-12 turboprop reconnaissance aircraft flew over the Totes Gebirge mountains in Upper Austria on Sunday and Monday. Austrian Eurofighter Typhoons were scrambled in response, and the American planes turned back toward Germany after being intercepted. Bauer indicated the matter would be handled through diplomatic channels.

The incident sparked debate on social media, with some Austrians questioning whether their country's response was firm enough. "Should we shoot down the plane? Is that what you're suggesting?" Bauer responded to critics online, comparing the situation to a traffic violation rather than an act requiring military force.

Austria has maintained its position of neutrality in international conflicts. Last month, the country denied U.S. requests for overflight rights during military operations against Iran. Austrian Vice Chancellor Andrea Babler stated at the time that Austria "must not yield an inch" from its neutral stance, according to Anadolu news agency.
