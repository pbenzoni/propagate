Up to 50,000 residents have been ordered to evacuate parts of Orange County after a pressurized chemical tank at a GKN Aerospace facility in Garden Grove began threatening explosion. The vessel contains up to 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate (MMA), a highly flammable industrial chemical used in plastic production, raising serious questions about industrial safety oversight in the state.

Governor Gavin Newsom declared a state of emergency as the crisis unfolded. What emergency crews initially believed was a stabilized situation deteriorated rapidly when they discovered their drone temperature readings had only measured the tank's outer shell. Manual readings revealed internal temperatures were still climbing dangerously—reaching around 90 degrees Fahrenheit by Saturday and rising approximately one degree per hour. Damaged valves are preventing crews from removing the chemical or relieving the mounting pressure.

Orange County Fire Authority Chief Craig Covey called the incident a "worst-case scenario," with the threat of a toxic leak or explosion remaining possible. The evacuation zone has expanded to include parts of six cities: Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park, and Westminster. If the container ruptures, the substance could ignite and release toxic fumes causing respiratory problems, neurological symptoms, and potentially unconsciousness.

Emergency teams have established containment barriers and attempted to cool the vessel while adding neutralizing agents, but the crisis highlights concerns about regulatory oversight of hazardous materials storage in densely populated areas. The facility sits just 38 miles from Los Angeles, with residential neighborhoods in immediate proximity to industrial operations handling volatile chemicals.
