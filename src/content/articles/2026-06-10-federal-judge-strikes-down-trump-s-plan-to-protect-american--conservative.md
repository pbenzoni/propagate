A federal judge has overturned President Trump's executive action imposing a $100,000 fee on new H-1B skilled-worker visa applications, dealing a blow to efforts aimed at protecting American jobs from outsourcing.

The fee, announced in September, targeted the H-1B visa program—a system capped at 85,000 new visas annually that has long been criticized for allowing corporations to bypass qualified American workers in favor of cheaper foreign labor. Major technology companies are among the heaviest users of the program. President Trump argued the surcharge would combat widespread abuse by companies using the visa system to replace Americans with lower-paid foreign workers while also protecting national security.

US District Judge Leo Sorokin sided with 20 states—led by California—that challenged the policy, ruling that the fee amounted to a tax that required Congressional approval. The Department of Homeland Security condemned the decision as "blatant judicial activism," maintaining that the fee was a necessary measure to safeguard American workers and prevent exploitation of employment-based visa programs.

The lawsuit was spearheaded by states claiming the fee would harm public institutions like schools, universities, and hospitals that employ foreign workers. However, the H-1B program remains deeply controversial, with critics arguing it enables corporations and staffing firms to undercut wages and job opportunities for American citizens. Supporters counter that it fills specialized roles, though concerns about corporate abuse of the system persist across the political spectrum.
