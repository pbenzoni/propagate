Croatian President Zoran Milanović has refused to approve Israel's proposed ambassador to Zagreb, marking a rare diplomatic rebuke from a NATO member state over the Israeli government's conduct in Gaza. The presidential office announced Monday that the decision stems from fundamental disagreements with policies pursued by the current Israeli authorities.

The move represents an escalating rift between Milanović and the Netanyahu government. The Croatian president has previously condemned what he described as "bestial war crimes" in Gaza, where Palestinian authorities report nearly 73,000 deaths since October 2023. The conflict began after a Hamas attack killed approximately 1,200 Israelis and resulted in more than 250 kidnappings.

Israel had deviated from standard diplomatic protocol by publicly announcing their proposed ambassador before receiving Croatian approval, the president's office noted. The statement emphasized that "public or political pressure, in this case from the Israeli side, will not alter the President of the Republic's decision."

Tensions between Milanović and outgoing Israeli Ambassador Gary Koren had already escalated in recent months. After Koren suggested Iranian espionage activities in Croatia, Milanović summoned the ambassador and declared: "We don't want other people's infections and germs in Croatia, neither Iranian nor Israeli." Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar responded by accusing the president of using "hate-filled language about Israel and Zionism."

The proposed replacement, diplomat Nisan Amdor, will instead arrive as chargé d'affaires—a lower-level position that bypasses the need for presidential consent. The current ambassador's term expires at the end of May.
