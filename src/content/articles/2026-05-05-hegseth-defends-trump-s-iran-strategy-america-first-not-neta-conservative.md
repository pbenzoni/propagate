Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth forcefully rejected suggestions that President Donald Trump was "pulled into" conflict with Iran at Israel's behest, insisting instead that the Commander-in-Chief has acted solely on American national interests throughout the crisis.

At a Pentagon press briefing Tuesday, Hegseth was asked whether Washington risked "getting roped back into a war" by Israel, given that Israeli officials have publicly stated their intent to continue military operations. The Defense Secretary called that premise fundamentally flawed. "Just to be clear, President Trump has led at every step of this based on his view of American interests and 'America First,'" Hegseth stated, emphasizing that while the U.S. values its alliance with Israel, "they may have some objectives at times that are slightly different than ours."

The comments come amid continued tension in the region, with Israel reportedly conducting strikes in southern Lebanon despite Trump's direct orders to halt such operations since mid-April. West Jerusalem maintains it is targeting Hezbollah positions, though reports indicate widespread destruction of villages and allegations of looting by Israeli Defense Forces. Lebanese authorities report over 2,700 killed and more than 8,000 wounded since early March.

Hegseth's remarks underscore the Trump administration's determination to chart its own course in the Middle East—one driven by American strategic priorities rather than the preferences of regional partners. Despite recent naval confrontations in the Strait of Hormuz and ongoing tensions over Washington's naval presence near Iranian ports, the Defense Secretary maintained that ceasefire arrangements remain in effect. Iranian officials, however, have signaled growing impatience with what they view as American violations of agreed-upon terms.
