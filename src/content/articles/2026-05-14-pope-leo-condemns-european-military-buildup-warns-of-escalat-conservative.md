Addressing students at Rome's Sapienza University, the pontiff pointed to sobering statistics: global military spending approached a record $3 trillion in 2025, with Europe recording its sharpest regional increase since the Cold War. According to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI), this surge stems from the Ukraine conflict, tensions with Russia, and mounting pressure from the United States for greater NATO burden-sharing.

"Let's not call 'defense' a rearmament that increases tensions and insecurity, depletes investments in education and healthcare, undermines trust in diplomacy, and enriches elites who care nothing for the common good," Pope Leo declared. He argued that young people are rightly questioning what kind of world previous generations are leaving behind—"a world unfortunately maimed by wars."
