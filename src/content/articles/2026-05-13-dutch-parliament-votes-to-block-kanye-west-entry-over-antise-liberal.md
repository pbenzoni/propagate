A broad majority of Dutch lawmakers have voted to urge their government to deny entry to rapper Kanye West ahead of scheduled concerts in Arnhem this June, according to local broadcaster NOS. The move reflects growing European resistance to platforming the artist after his repeated antisemitic statements and open admiration for Adolf Hitler.

West, who has won 24 Grammy Awards, sparked international condemnation last year when he publicly praised Nazism and Adolf Hitler, and marketed clothing featuring swastika imagery. Although he issued an apology earlier this year, the damage prompted multiple governments to examine whether they could legally bar him from performing.

The Dutch action follows similar steps across Europe. UK authorities recently refused West entry, forcing cancellation of his Wireless Festival appearance. Concerts in Poland and Switzerland have also been canceled by venue operators. Justice Minister David van Weel acknowledged the legal complexity, noting that the Netherlands maintains "a fairly high threshold" for denying entry and that officials are still exploring whether West's case meets the standard of threatening public order or security.

Only a handful of European stops—in Turkey, Italy, Spain, and Portugal—remain on West's announced tour. The pattern of cancellations underscores a broader European consensus that hate speech, even from celebrity artists, carries consequences that can include exclusion from public platforms.
