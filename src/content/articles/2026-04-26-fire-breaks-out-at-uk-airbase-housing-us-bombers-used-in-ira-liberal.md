A significant fire broke out early Sunday morning at RAF Fairford, a Royal Air Force base in western England that currently serves as a forward operating location for US Air Force bombers conducting operations against Iran. The Gloucestershire Fire and Rescue Service responded shortly after midnight to what they described as a fire involving a single-story industrial storage building.

According to official statements, the fire was brought under control safely with no casualties reported. Videos shared on social media showed large flames and an enormous plume of smoke rising from warehouse buildings at the facility. Preliminary assessments by authorities suggest the fire was accidental.

The timing of the blaze has drawn attention given the base's controversial role in US military operations. Just one day before the fire, hundreds of anti-war demonstrators rallied outside RAF Fairford demanding that the UK government shut down military facilities being used to launch strikes on Iran. The base currently hosts US B-52 and B-1 bombers as part of Washington's regional military presence.

The incident comes amid growing scrutiny of US military operations in the Middle East and the UK's support for those missions. Critics argue that hosting foreign military assets on British soil draws the country deeper into conflicts that lack broad public support and raises questions about sovereignty and democratic accountability over decisions of war and peace.
