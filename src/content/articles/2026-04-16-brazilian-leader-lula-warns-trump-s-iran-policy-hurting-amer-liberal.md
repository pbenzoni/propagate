Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva issued a stark warning about the consequences of President Donald Trump's aggressive approach toward Iran, arguing that ordinary Americans are bearing the financial burden of the administration's military escalation.

In an interview with El País published Thursday, Lula criticized what he called Trump's "very dangerous game," saying the president "operates on the premise that American economic, military, and technological power dictates the rules of the game. Ultimately, it ends up creating problems for the United States."

The Brazilian leader specifically pointed to skyrocketing fuel costs as evidence of the policy's impact on working families. "When he decided to attack Iran, I don't know if he realized that fuel prices would rise and that the people would be the ones to pay the price," Lula said. According to the American Automobile Association, gas prices averaged $4.09 per gallon on Thursday—a sharp increase from $2.94 in late February, before the Iran conflict intensified.

The price surge follows disruptions to oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping corridor that Iran has restricted for vessels from nations it considers hostile. Damage to petrochemical facilities in the region has further strained global energy markets. While a tentative ceasefire took hold last week, negotiations between Washington and Tehran have stalled, with Iran citing "unrealistic demands" from the U.S. The White House announced a blockade of the strait for Tehran-linked vessels in response.

Lula's comments reflect growing international concern about the humanitarian and economic costs of the escalating confrontation, particularly for civilians caught in the crossfire of geopolitical brinkmanship.
