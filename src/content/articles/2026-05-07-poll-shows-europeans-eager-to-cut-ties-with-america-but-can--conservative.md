A new survey from Germany's Bertelsmann Stiftung research foundation shows that roughly 70% of Europeans—including those in the United Kingdom—want their nations to pursue greater independence from the United States. The poll, conducted in March among 18,000 EU respondents and 2,000 in the UK, found that 73% of EU citizens believe it's time for Europe to "go its own way."

The findings reflect a significant erosion of trust in American leadership, with only 42% of Europeans now viewing Washington as a reliable partner—down from 46% just a year earlier. This sentiment has been building steadily, rising from 63% in 2024 to today's levels. The survey covered people aged 18 to 69 across all 27 EU member states, with results weighted to reflect current population distributions.

European politicians have increasingly called for strategic autonomy from the United States, particularly on foreign policy and defense matters. These calls intensified following President Donald Trump's return to office, after which tensions emerged over trade policy, differing approaches to the Ukraine conflict, and Washington's stance on Greenland. Most recently, European capitals declined to support the U.S.-Israeli operation against Iran, highlighting the growing policy divide.

The question remains whether European nations—many of which have relied on American military protection through NATO for decades while maintaining relatively modest defense budgets—possess the capability and political will to truly chart an independent course. Energy disruptions stemming from Middle East conflicts continue to expose European vulnerabilities, raising doubts about the continent's ability to manage global security challenges without American partnership.
