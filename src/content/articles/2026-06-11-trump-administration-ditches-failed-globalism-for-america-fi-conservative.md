While media attention focused on China's absence from this year's Shangri-La Dialogue security conference, the real story unfolded behind the scenes in Hawaii. American and Chinese military officials met to discuss maritime safety and crisis management—a sign that the Trump administration is pursuing a fundamentally different approach to China than the failed policies of previous decades.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth delivered what may be the clearest statement yet of this new direction. Speaking at the Shangri-La Dialogue, Hegseth announced that America has abandoned what he called the "old toothless, utopian, and globalist course of foreign policy." Instead of appeals to so-called universal values and abstract international norms, the administration is centering foreign policy on American national interests, military strength, and strategic realism. Stability comes from credible deterrence and sustainable power balances—not from moral posturing or wishful thinking.

This shift affects both allies and rivals. For too long, U.S. allies have relied on American security guarantees while maintaining inadequate defense capabilities of their own. Hegseth questioned this model directly, arguing that allies must become genuine security partners rather than permanent dependents. The administration recognizes that after decades of Chinese military expansion and the rise of other regional powers, the Indo-Pacific has become increasingly multipolar. Rather than chasing the impossible dream of restoring uncontested American primacy, Washington is focused on preserving an advantageous position through smart partnerships with Japan, South Korea, Australia, the Philippines, and India.

Notably, Hegseth's remarks on China emphasized stable relations and continued military engagement while avoiding the inflammatory rhetoric that has dominated Washington in recent years. Taiwan—one of the most sensitive flashpoints—received minimal attention in his speech, suggesting an effort to manage competition without manufacturing unnecessary crises. This represents a mature, realistic approach: strength through preparedness, not weakness through provocation or the globalist fantasies that weakened America for decades.
