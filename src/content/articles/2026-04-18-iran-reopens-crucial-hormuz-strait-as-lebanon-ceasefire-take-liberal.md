Iran announced Friday that the Strait of Hormuz—a vital chokepoint for global oil shipments—will remain open to commercial vessels for the duration of the ceasefire in Lebanon, according to Iranian Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi. The declaration comes as a 10-day truce between Israeli forces and Hezbollah takes effect, marking a potential de-escalation in regional tensions.

"In line with the ceasefire in Lebanon, the passage for all commercial vessels through Strait of Hormuz is declared completely open for the remaining period of ceasefire," Araghchi wrote on X. The minister noted that vessels would follow a "coordinated route" established by Iran's Ports and Maritime Organisation, indicating Tehran maintains oversight of the waterway.

The strait has been closed since late February following military strikes on Iran by the U.S. and Israel. That closure has squeezed global energy markets and disrupted a shipping lane that handles roughly 20% of the world's crude oil exports. Oil prices dropped more than 10% immediately after the announcement, with crude falling to just over $83 per barrel and Brent to around $88.

President Trump responded to the news on Truth Social, appearing to acknowledge Iran's decision to reopen what he called the "strait of Iran." The reopening offers temporary relief from an energy crisis that has rippled through the global economy, though its duration remains tied to the fragile ceasefire in Lebanon.
