Telegram founder Pavel Durov issued a strong defense of free speech this week, standing with Elon Musk as both tech leaders face what they characterize as politically motivated persecution by the French government. The controversy erupted after the Wall Street Journal revealed that the U.S. Department of Justice rejected a French request to assist in investigating X (formerly Twitter), with DOJ officials stating the probe appeared designed "to use the criminal legal system in France to regulate a public square for the free expression of ideas."

Durov, who holds citizenship in multiple countries including France, didn't mince words in his criticism of President Emmanuel Macron's administration. "France is losing legitimacy as it weaponizes criminal investigations to suppress free speech and privacy," he stated, adding that "in Macron's France, being investigated is the new Legion d'honneur." The entrepreneur challenged the independence of French prosecutors, noting they "are hired, fired, and promoted by the government" – raising serious questions about political interference in what should be impartial legal proceedings.

Both tech leaders have experienced firsthand what they describe as government overreach. French prosecutors launched their investigation into X in early 2025, initially citing concerns about algorithmic bias and alleged foreign interference, before expanding the probe to include content moderation issues. Durov himself was arrested at a Paris airport in August 2024 and charged with 12 counts related to Telegram's content policies, charges he has called "legally and logically absurd." His travel restrictions were only fully lifted in November 2025.

The clash highlights a growing divide between American principles of free expression and European regulatory approaches that critics say grant governments excessive control over digital platforms. The DOJ's refusal to assist the French investigation signals Washington's concern that such prosecutions could ensnare U.S. companies and citizens in "politically charged" foreign legal proceedings that undermine First Amendment protections.
