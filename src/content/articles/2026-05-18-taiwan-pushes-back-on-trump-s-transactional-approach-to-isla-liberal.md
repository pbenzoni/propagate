Taiwanese President Lai Ching-te has defended his government's acquisition of American military equipment following remarks by President Donald Trump characterizing weapons deliveries as leverage in negotiations with Beijing.

During Trump's recent visit to China, President Xi Jinping emphasized that Taiwan's status remains the most sensitive issue in US-China relations. Beijing has consistently opposed US arms sales to Taiwan, viewing them as interference in what it considers an internal matter and a breach of the long-standing One-China framework that has guided diplomatic relations for decades.

In a social media statement, Lai described the arms purchases as "the most important deterrent" against regional conflict, thanking Trump for support dating back to his first term. "We thank President Trump for his continued support for peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait," Lai wrote, while affirming that Taiwan "will not provoke" conflict but also would not "give up national sovereignty and dignity."

The exchange highlights growing uncertainty about US policy toward the self-governing island. Trump approved an $11 billion weapons package in December but has since indicated he may withhold a proposed $14 billion package. "I'm holding that in abeyance and it depends on China," Trump told Fox News. "It's a very good negotiating chip for us, frankly."

China claims Taiwan as part of its territory—a position recognized by most nations globally. While Beijing has stated it seeks peaceful reunification, Xi has warned that military force remains an option if provoked. The US maintains unofficial ties with Taipei despite not formally recognizing Taiwan as an independent country, a balancing act that Beijing views as contradictory to the One-China policy.
