Senior congressional leaders are expressing frustration with the Pentagon's handling of an investigation into a February strike in Iran that resulted in significant civilian casualties. Representative Adam Smith pressed U.S. Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper during a House Armed Services Committee hearing this week, accusing military leadership of "endless stalling" in acknowledging what happened.

The strike occurred on February 28 during U.S.-Israeli military operations against Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps facilities. Iranian officials reported 175 fatalities at the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school in southern Iran. According to reports, an internal military investigation found U.S. forces may have used "outdated targeting data" that misidentified the school as part of a nearby IRGC naval base.

Admiral Cooper defended the ongoing investigation during Tuesday's hearing, stating the school was located "on an active IRGC cruise missile base" and emphasizing that "the U.S. does not deliberately target civilians." However, archived records and satellite imagery indicate the school was adjacent to—not inside—the military compound, separated by a visible fence. Cooper promised lawmakers a full report once the probe concludes but declined to provide preliminary findings or acknowledge responsibility.

The incident highlights ongoing tensions about military transparency and rules of engagement. While lawmakers from both parties have pressed for answers, the Pentagon maintains the investigation's complexity requires more time. President Trump has signaled willingness to resume military pressure on Iran if nuclear negotiations fail, telling reporters the U.S. may need to deliver "another big hit" within days if Tehran doesn't accept American terms for a broader peace deal.
