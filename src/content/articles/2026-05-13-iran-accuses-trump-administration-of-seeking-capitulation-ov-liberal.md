Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi sharply criticized the Trump administration's approach to ongoing negotiations, arguing that Washington's rejection of Tehran's proposed peace terms reveals an unwillingness to pursue genuine diplomacy.

"When a party directly involved in war, blockade, sanctions, and threats of force rejects Iran's response simply because it is not a letter of surrender, it becomes obvious that the issue is not peace but the imposition of political will through intimidation and pressure," Gharibabadi wrote on social media Tuesday. Iran and the United States remain engaged in indirect talks following a fragile ceasefire announced last month after weeks of military confrontation.

Tehran's proposed conditions include compensation for damage from US and Israeli strikes in late February, guarantees against future aggression, and recognition of Iran's sovereign rights—demands the Iranian government characterizes as "the minimum necessary for any serious and durable arrangement under the UN Charter." Iran also insists on its right to peaceful nuclear energy under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty and maintains control over the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz, where it introduced a maritime toll system during the conflict.

The Trump administration has demanded Iran fully halt uranium enrichment and restore unrestricted shipping through the strait while launching a naval blockade of Iranian ports that has further disrupted global energy markets. Trump warned last week that observers would know the ceasefire had collapsed when they see "one big glow coming out of Iran"—language critics have characterized as threatening nuclear escalation.

Diplomatic efforts mediated by Pakistan have stalled as tensions remain high. Trump is currently visiting China, where the administration has imposed sanctions on Chinese entities accused of assisting Tehran, though Beijing maintains that unilateral American sanctions lack legitimacy under international law without UN Security Council authorization.
