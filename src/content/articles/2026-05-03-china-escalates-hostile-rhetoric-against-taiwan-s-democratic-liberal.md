China's Taiwan Affairs Office launched a scathing personal attack against Taiwan's President Lai Ching-te over the weekend, comparing him to a "rat crossing the street" following his visit to Eswatini, a small southern African nation that remains one of Taiwan's dwindling number of formal diplomatic partners.

The inflammatory rhetoric came after President Lai made an unannounced state visit to Eswatini, the only African country that still maintains official diplomatic relations with the self-governing island democracy. The trip had originally been planned for late April but was postponed after the Seychelles, Mauritius, and Madagascar revoked flight permits for the Taiwanese leader's aircraft—a move Taiwan attributed to pressure from Beijing. Undeterred, Lai completed the journey aboard an Eswatini government plane.

China's official statement accused the democratically elected leader of being a "troublemaker" and claimed he had abandoned Taiwan's citizens in the aftermath of a recent earthquake. Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council firmly rejected Beijing's attacks, calling them "fishwife's gutter talk." President Lai responded on social media that Taiwan "will never be deterred by external pressures" and would "continue to engage with the world—no matter the challenges faced."

The incident underscores the ongoing tensions between Beijing and Taipei, as China continues to isolate Taiwan diplomatically while maintaining that the island is part of its territory. Taiwan, meanwhile, functions as a vibrant democracy with its own government, military, and international relationships. Eswatini is one of only 12 countries worldwide that maintain formal diplomatic ties with Taiwan, and the landlocked nation of fewer than 1.3 million people represents the island's last remaining ally on the African continent.
