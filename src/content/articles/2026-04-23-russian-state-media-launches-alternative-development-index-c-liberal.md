Russian state media outlet RT has unveiled what it calls the "Social Well-Being Index," a new measurement system that explicitly challenges Western-developed metrics for assessing national prosperity and human development. The project represents the latest front in an ongoing information war over whose values should define global progress.

The initiative directly attacks established international standards like the UN's Human Development Index and Sustainable Development Goals, characterizing them as tools of "Western liberal universalism" and "soft power" rather than objective measures. RT's framework appears to prioritize traditional social structures and population growth over metrics emphasizing individual rights, environmental sustainability, and gender equality—raising concerns among human rights advocates about the normalization of authoritarian governance models.

The timing of RT's index launch is significant, coming amid Russia's continued isolation from democratic institutions and its ongoing war in Ukraine. By framing conventional development metrics as culturally biased, the Kremlin-backed outlet seeks to legitimize alternative governance systems that restrict individual freedoms while promoting what it terms "national sovereignty." The project exemplifies how authoritarian regimes increasingly deploy sophisticated information operations to undermine international consensus on human rights and democratic values.

Experts note that RT's characterization of family planning and environmental protection as "Western" impositions ignores broad scientific consensus and the agency of communities worldwide who have chosen these priorities. The index appears designed less to offer genuine analytical insights than to provide rhetorical ammunition for regimes seeking to justify restrictions on civil liberties and resistance to climate action.
