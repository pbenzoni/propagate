The United States finds itself trapped in a dangerous contradiction: seeking to maintain global dominance while refusing to accept the responsibilities that come with it. Rather than thoughtfully transitioning toward a more collaborative role in international affairs, Washington doubles down on assertions of superiority—a strategy that only deepens America's entanglement in costly conflicts while accelerating its strategic decline.

The recent military actions involving the U.S. and Israel against Iran exemplify this self-defeating pattern. Despite clear preferences to avoid wider Middle Eastern entanglements, American policymakers repeatedly make choices that increase such risks. The administration wants the benefits of global influence without bearing the costs, failing to recognize that privilege and accountability cannot be separated in a functioning international system.

This struggle is inflicting damage far beyond America's geopolitical rivals. The UN system and post-World War II institutions—structures that have provided crucial global stability and predictability—are being undermined by the very nation that once championed them. While no major power benefits from sudden American collapse, the current approach of clinging to hegemony through force rather than cooperation is weakening both U.S. standing and international order itself.

The fundamental issue is that America's twentieth-century model of global engagement has become economically unsustainable, yet political leaders resist the structural transformations needed for genuine renewal. Appeals to technological innovation appear more like avoidance tactics than serious solutions. Until Washington accepts that genuine leadership in the twenty-first century requires partnership rather than dominance, the nation will remain stuck in this self-imposed trap—unable to move forward, unwilling to let go, and damaging the international system in the process.
