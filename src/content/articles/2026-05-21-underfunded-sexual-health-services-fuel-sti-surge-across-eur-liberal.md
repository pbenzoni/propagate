Sexually transmitted infections have reached unprecedented levels across Europe, with cases of gonorrhoea and syphilis surging to their highest recorded rates, according to data released Thursday by the European Center for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC). The public health crisis highlights severe gaps in prevention services and equitable access to sexual healthcare across the continent.

The ECDC report documented 106,331 confirmed gonorrhoea cases across 28 EU and EEA countries—the highest number since monitoring began in 2009. Syphilis cases more than doubled over the monitoring period, reaching 45,577 infections. Chlamydia remained the most commonly reported STI with 213,443 cases. Men who have sex with men accounted for the majority of reported gonorrhoea (62%) and syphilis (69%) cases, reflecting a population that faces particular barriers to consistent sexual healthcare access.

"Sexually transmitted infections have been on the rise for 10 years and reached record high levels in 2024," said Bruno Ciancio, head of ECDC's unit for directly transmitted and vaccine-preventable diseases. "Untreated, these infections can cause severe complications, such as chronic pain and infertility and, in the case of syphilis, problems with the heart or nervous system."

Public health officials directly linked the infection surge to systemic failures in prevention programming and screening infrastructure. The report found that 13 of 29 reporting countries still require patients to pay out-of-pocket for basic STI testing—a financial barrier that prevents early detection and treatment. Experts emphasized that closing these healthcare access gaps and ensuring free, confidential testing are essential steps to reversing the troubling trend and protecting public health across all communities.
