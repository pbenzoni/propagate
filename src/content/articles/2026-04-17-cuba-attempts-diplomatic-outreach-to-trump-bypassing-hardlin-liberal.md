The Cuban government attempted to establish direct communication with President Donald Trump last week through an unofficial diplomatic channel, according to a Wall Street Journal report citing anonymous sources. The effort appears to have been designed to circumvent Secretary of State Marco Rubio, a vocal advocate for regime change policies toward the island nation.

According to the report, a private courier carrying a sealed letter with an official Cuban government seal was stopped by security officers at Miami International Airport. The letter allegedly proposed economic cooperation agreements and sanctions relief, while expressing concerns about potential US military action against the island. The courier was reportedly sent back to Cuba without delivering the message.

The attempted outreach comes as Cuba faces what observers describe as its worst economic crisis in decades, including recurring power blackouts and severe fuel shortages. The humanitarian situation on the island has deteriorated significantly amid longstanding US economic sanctions and recent disruptions to oil supplies from Venezuela.

Policy experts have questioned the wisdom of attempting to bypass official diplomatic channels. Ricardo Herrero, executive director of the Washington-based Cuba Study Group, characterized the approach as "downright foolish and bound to backfire," noting that using an intermediary without established White House connections undermined the effort's credibility. The incident highlights ongoing tensions between advocates for diplomatic engagement with Cuba and those favoring continued pressure through sanctions and isolation.
