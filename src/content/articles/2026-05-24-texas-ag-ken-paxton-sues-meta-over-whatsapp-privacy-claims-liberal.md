Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton has filed a lawsuit against Meta, alleging the tech company made false claims about WhatsApp's privacy protections. The suit accuses Meta of misleading users about the scope of its end-to-end encryption, raising concerns about corporate accountability in the digital age.

WhatsApp's website states that "no one outside of the chat, not even WhatsApp, can read, listen to, or share what a user says." However, Paxton's lawsuit argues that Meta's promotional materials have "led millions of users to believe their communications are fully private," when the reality may be quite different. The complaint cites media reports and whistleblower accounts suggesting these privacy claims are inaccurate.

Meta spokesperson Andy Stone pushed back against the allegations, insisting that "WhatsApp cannot access people's encrypted communications and any suggestion to the contrary is false." Yet these assurances come against a backdrop of growing scrutiny. WhatsApp co-founder Brian Acton admitted in a 2018 Forbes interview that he "sold my users' privacy to a larger benefit" when the app was acquired by Facebook (now Meta) for $22 billion in 2014.

The Texas lawsuit is part of a broader pattern of legal challenges facing Meta over WhatsApp's privacy practices. A separate class-action lawsuit filed in federal court alleges that Meta and WhatsApp "store, analyze, and can access virtually all of WhatsApp users' purportedly 'private' communications." Bloomberg has reported that federal authorities have been investigating similar allegations, underscoring the serious questions about whether major tech platforms are being transparent with users about how their personal data is handled.
