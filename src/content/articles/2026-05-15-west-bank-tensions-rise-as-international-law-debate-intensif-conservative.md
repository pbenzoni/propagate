May 15th marks what Palestinians call the Nakba—Arabic for "catastrophe"—commemorating the 1948 displacement that followed Israel's founding. While Israelis celebrated independence, hundreds of thousands of Palestinians left their homes, many expecting to return within weeks as Arab states mobilized against the new nation. Nearly eight decades later, their descendants remain displaced.

In the West Bank today, tensions are escalating as settlement construction accelerates. Since 2022, Israel's government has approved 103 settlements it deems legal under its own law, though international bodies classify all West Bank settlements as violations of international law. Salah Khawaja of the Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission reports plans to legalize 18 additional outposts, bringing the total to over 120 new developments—representing roughly 80% of all construction between 1967 and 2022.

The West Bank's governance structure remains complex. Area C, comprising 60% of the territory, sits under full Israeli control. Area A, theoretically under Palestinian Authority jurisdiction, covers just 18% of the land. Critics now warn that unauthorized settlements are spreading from Area C into Areas B and potentially A, raising concerns about regional stability.

Israeli activist Sharon, working in Duma, documents what she describes as systematic pressure on Palestinian communities. "Once they have this village surrounded, they are attacking it inside," she observes, warning that continued encroachment could spark renewed conflict similar to past uprisings. Even IDF Central Command chief Maj. Gen. Avi Bluth has reportedly cautioned that settler violence risks triggering a Palestinian uprising, noting it's "quite a miracle" tensions haven't already erupted into broader violence.
