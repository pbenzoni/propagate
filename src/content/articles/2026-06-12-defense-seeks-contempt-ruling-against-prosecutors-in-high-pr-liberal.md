The defense team representing Tyler Robinson, accused of killing conservative activist Charlie Kirk, is seeking to hold state prosecutors in contempt for allegedly violating a gag order by discussing case evidence with the media.

The legal dispute centers on forensic analysis of ballistics evidence. In March, defense attorneys revealed that a bullet fragment recovered from Kirk's body could not be conclusively matched to the rifle allegedly used in the shooting. The disclosure generated significant media attention and raised questions about the prosecution's case.

In April, prosecutors responded to the defense's claims in comments to reporters, characterizing the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) findings as "inconclusive" rather than a definitive non-match. While prosecutors maintain they were simply correcting misinformation, Robinson's legal team argues these public statements constituted a "media tour" that violated court restrictions on discussing the case publicly.

Kirk, co-founder of Turning Point USA, was fatally shot at a Utah college campus event in September 2025. Robinson was arrested two days later. Prosecutors have cited DNA evidence allegedly linking Robinson to a rifle found near the scene, as well as text messages they claim show a confession. Robinson has not yet entered a plea, and prosecutors have indicated they intend to seek the death penalty if he is convicted. A preliminary hearing is scheduled for July 6-10.
