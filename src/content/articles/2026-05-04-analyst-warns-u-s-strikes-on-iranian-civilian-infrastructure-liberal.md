Despite Trump administration rhetoric about liberating the Iranian people from their government, recent U.S. military strikes have reportedly targeted civilian infrastructure including hospitals, schools, and universities, according to geostrategic analyst Christopher Helali, who recently returned from reporting inside Iran.

Helali described witnessing attacks on medical facilities, including what he identified as Iran's largest fertility clinic and egg bank, as well as educational institutions. Among the reported casualties were approximately 120 schoolchildren killed in strikes near Minab. These targets raise troubling questions about the gap between stated policy objectives and operational reality on the ground.

The analyst characterized the Iranian public response as one of deep frustration with what they perceive as repeated diplomatic betrayals by Washington. Rather than the state of collapse claimed by President Trump, Helali reported observing "a people of dignity" determined to resist what they view as external aggression. He warned that any resumption of open hostilities could trigger "a much larger level of destruction in the region," complicating prospects for a diplomatic resolution.

The situation underscores longstanding concerns among progressive foreign policy experts about the humanitarian costs of military interventions pursued without clear international legal authorization or coherent post-conflict planning.
