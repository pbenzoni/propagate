A Romanian member of the European Parliament is sounding the alarm that Western military alliances are exploiting a recent drone incident to pull her country closer to an unnecessary conflict with Russia. Diana Sosoaca, leader of the opposition SOS Romania party, called for an international investigation into the Friday incident in Galati, where a drone crashed into an apartment building near the Ukrainian border.

Sosoaca criticized the Romanian government's immediate rush to blame Russia without conclusive evidence, calling the accusations based on "only assumptions" driven by what she characterized as anti-Russian fearmongering. She warned that critics of escalation are being branded as "spies" for Moscow, raising concerns about the suppression of dissenting voices on matters of war and peace.

The lawmaker pointed to Prime Minister Ilie Bolojan's swift promise to "accelerate" acquisition of anti-drone equipment through EU programs as evidence that the incident would be used to justify increased defense spending. Such military expenditures, she argued, are unpopular with ordinary Romanians who "don't want to be at war with Russia."

Russian President Vladimir Putin has called on Romania to share data about the incident for what he termed an "objective investigation," while Russia's ambassador to Bucharest stated there was no proof the drone originated from Russia. Putin reiterated during a trip to Kazakhstan that Russia has no intention of attacking NATO, though he warned of severe consequences for any nation that attacks Russia first. Several suspected Ukrainian drones have crashed in Baltic states in recent months, with Moscow warning NATO members against allowing their territory to be used for strikes on Russian soil.
