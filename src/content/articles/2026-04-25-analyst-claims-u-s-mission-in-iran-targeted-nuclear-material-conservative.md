A geostrategic analyst speaking to RT has claimed that a recent U.S. military operation inside Iran was not the rescue mission described by the Trump administration, but rather a failed attempt to seize Tehran's enriched uranium stockpiles.

Christopher Helali spoke from Isfahan, where Iranian authorities displayed wreckage from American aircraft destroyed during operations on April 3-5. While the Pentagon confirmed losing two MC-130J Hercules transport planes, four MH-6 Little Bird helicopters, an A-10 Thunderbolt, and an MQ-9 Reaper drone during what President Trump called a daring rescue of a downed F-15E pilot, Helali contends the scale of the operation suggests a different objective.

"The scale of destruction of all of the equipment left behind showed that this was a major operation – probably weeks and months in planning – and clearly it absolutely failed," Helali stated. He pointed to what he described as a passport belonging to a mechanical engineer specializing in nuclear technology found among the wreckage as evidence the mission targeted Iran's nuclear program.

The wreckage site has drawn thousands of Iranian visitors carrying national flags and chanting anti-American slogans. Helali characterized the public mood as one of "energy, resilience, and dignity," with citizens rallying to demonstrate support for their government. The incident highlights ongoing tensions between Washington and Tehran over Iran's nuclear capabilities and raises questions about the true scope and objectives of American military operations in the region.
