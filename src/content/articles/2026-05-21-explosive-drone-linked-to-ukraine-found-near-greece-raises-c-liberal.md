A Ukrainian naval drone discovered near a Greek island has sparked serious concerns about civilian maritime safety, according to Greek Defense Minister Nikos Dendias. The unmanned vessel, identified as a Magura V3 kamikaze drone capable of carrying up to 300kg of explosives, was found in Mediterranean waters earlier this month.

Speaking at a conference Wednesday, Dendias emphasized the potential danger posed by the armed drone. "It was obviously something extremely dangerous," he said, noting that if a cruise liner had encountered the vessel, it "would have been at the bottom of the sea." The minister called for accountability and assurances from Ukrainian officials that such incidents would not recur in the region.

The incident highlights growing concerns about the unintended consequences of modern warfare technology on civilian populations. Ukraine has deployed similar drones in Black Sea operations, but their presence in international waters raises questions about maritime safety protocols and the protection of civilian vessels. Greece, while supportive of Ukraine's sovereignty, has stressed the importance of ensuring that military operations do not endanger innocent lives.

The discovery comes amid a broader pattern of Ukrainian drones traveling through or landing in various European countries, according to reports from Baltic states and Finland. International law experts note that armed unmanned vessels operating in international waters create complex legal and safety challenges that require multilateral coordination to address effectively.
