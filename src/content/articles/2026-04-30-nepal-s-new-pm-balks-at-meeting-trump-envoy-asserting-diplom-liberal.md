Nepal's recently elected Prime Minister Balendra Shah is reportedly refusing to meet with Trump administration envoy Sergio Gor during his visit to Kathmandu this week, according to local media reports. The standoff highlights Shah's stated policy of meeting only with ministerial-level or higher officials from foreign countries—a position that challenges traditional diplomatic protocols that have often favored major powers.

Shah, who took office in late March following his Rastriya Swatantra Party's electoral victory, previously declined a meeting with a visiting U.S. Assistant Secretary of State earlier this month. Sources within Nepal's Prime Minister's Office and Ministry of Foreign Affairs indicate that Shah has not agreed to meet Gor, despite requests made through various diplomatic channels. Instead, Gor is scheduled to meet with Nepal's Finance Minister, Foreign Minister, and other officials.

The prime minister's approach appears designed to establish clearer boundaries in Nepal's foreign relations, with Shah having met ambassadors from multiple countries jointly in early April to underscore his diplomatic framework. This stance comes as the Trump administration's South Asia envoy faces mixed receptions across the region—Maldives President Mohamed Muizzu canceled his meeting with Gor in protest of U.S. support for Israel's military operations against Iran.

While Gor successfully engaged with leaders in Bangladesh, Bhutan, and Sri Lanka, Shah's position suggests a reassertion of diplomatic equality from a smaller nation navigating relationships with global powers. The episode raises questions about how the current U.S. administration approaches diplomatic engagement in South Asia, particularly with newly elected governments seeking to establish their own protocols.
