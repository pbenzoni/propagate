Berlin is grappling with a worsening infestation of oak processionary moth caterpillars, a climate-sensitive pest whose toxic hairs can cause severe allergic reactions and respiratory distress. Parks, sports facilities, and residential areas across multiple districts have been closed as authorities issue health warnings about the insects, which experts say are thriving in increasingly warm, dry conditions.

The caterpillars develop hundreds of thousands of microscopic toxic hairs that can become airborne and travel up to 200 meters on the wind, triggering rashes, eye irritation, breathing problems, and in severe cases, anaphylactic shock. The hardest-hit neighborhoods include Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf, Treptow-Köpenick, Steglitz-Zehlendorf, and Spandau. In one housing estate of more than 11,000 residents, the caterpillars have infested building facades, vehicles, and outdoor fixtures.

Local advocates and residents are criticizing what they describe as inadequate governmental coordination and response. Because the caterpillars are classified as an allergen rather than a public-health pest, health agencies face limited enforcement powers, while environmental regulations restrict pesticide use. More than 4,500 people have signed a petition demanding a comprehensive protection plan.

Climate scientists warn that the outbreak will likely intensify as warming temperatures create more favorable conditions for the pest's spread. Forecasters expect hotter and drier-than-average weather across Germany and Central Europe this summer. The oak processionary moth, now established across much of Europe including parts of the UK, exemplifies how the climate crisis is reshaping public health challenges in urban environments.
