Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched multiple ballistic missiles targeting American military installations in Kuwait and Bahrain after U.S. Central Command conducted what it described as self-defense strikes on an Iranian military facility on Qeshm Island.

The escalation began when a U.S. warplane fired a Hellfire missile at an Iranian-linked tanker near the Strait of Hormuz late Tuesday, damaging its engine room. The Pentagon said the Botswana-flagged vessel was attempting to breach America's unilaterally imposed naval blockade by heading toward Iran's Kharg Island. Iran retaliated by targeting what it claimed was a U.S.-affiliated vessel with naval missiles, prompting CENTCOM to strike an IRGC telecommunications tower on Qeshm Island.

The IRGC then escalated with ballistic missile strikes on what it described as "a U.S. air and helicopter base in a regional country" and "the Fifth Fleet's command center" in Bahrain. The Defense Department reported that Iran launched several ballistic missiles toward regional states, but stated that all failed to hit their targets. Two missiles fired at Kuwait fell short or broke apart in flight, while three missiles launched at Bahrain were intercepted by U.S. and Bahraini air defense forces.

Alarm sirens sounded in Kuwait at least twice overnight as the Kuwaiti army responded to missile and drone attacks. Bahrain's Interior Ministry urged citizens to "remain calm and head to the nearest safe place" during the air alert. The exchange represents the most serious direct confrontation between U.S. and Iranian forces since a fragile ceasefire was established in early April, while negotiations over Tehran's nuclear program and control of the Strait of Hormuz remain stalled.

CENTCOM said its forces "remain vigilant and ready to defend against unwarranted Iranian aggression during the ongoing ceasefire." The IRGC warned that any violation of the ceasefire would bring a "harsh price" for the U.S. military.
