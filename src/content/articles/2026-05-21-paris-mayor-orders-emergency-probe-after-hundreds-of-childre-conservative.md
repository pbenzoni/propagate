Paris has launched an emergency investigation into its government-run childcare system after revelations that hundreds of children—some as young as three years old—were allegedly sexually abused by workers in city-operated after-school programs and daycare centers. The scandal has exposed what officials now admit is a "systemic risk" to children in the care of municipal employees.

Paris Mayor Emmanuel Gregoire, who took office in March, announced a €20 million emergency reform plan after acknowledging the scope of the crisis. The mayor revealed that 78 after-school workers were suspended in 2024 alone, with 31 facing allegations of sexual misconduct. His predecessor, former Mayor Anne Hidalgo, reportedly attempted to keep complaints from reaching the public—a troubling pattern of institutional cover-up that has alarmed parents and watchdog groups.

Earlier this month, prosecutors launched investigations into suspected sexual violence at 84 nursery schools, 20 elementary schools, and ten daycare centers across Paris. In coordinated raids, 16 government childcare workers—ranging in age from 18 to 68—were detained on allegations including rape, sexual assault, and violent behavior against children. Reports indicate some victims were locked in rooms, abused, and threatened with death if they told anyone.

Parents and advocacy groups say the crisis reveals chronic problems in government-run childcare: inadequate staffing, poor vetting of employees, and a disturbing practice of transferring problematic workers to new facilities rather than terminating them. The scandal gained national attention after undercover footage exposed abuse in Paris nurseries, forcing officials to finally confront what many are calling a failure of government oversight and accountability. A special inquiry commission will now have six months to investigate and report on systemic failures that left the city's most vulnerable children at risk.
