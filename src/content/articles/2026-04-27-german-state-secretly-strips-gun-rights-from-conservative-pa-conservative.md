The German state of Saxony has been quietly revoking and denying firearms licenses to members and supporters of the Alternative for Germany (AfD) party under a secret decree issued last July, according to a report by Berliner Zeitung. Saxony's Interior Ministry confirmed the directive exists but refused to make it public, calling it an "internal document."

The decree effectively creates a presumption that AfD members and supporters are "unreliable" under firearms law, forcing them to prove otherwise. According to the document, simply having a clean criminal record is insufficient—individuals must demonstrate active distancing from the party's positions to retain their Second Amendment-equivalent rights. The AfD, which polls at 27% support in potential elections, has campaigned on stricter immigration enforcement and opposed Germany's sanctions on Russia.

The measure stems from Saxony's intelligence authorities designating the state's AfD branch as a "confirmed right-wing extremist" organization in December 2023—a label the party unsuccessfully challenged in court. Germany's federal domestic intelligence agency applied the same designation to the entire AfD last May, providing legal cover for the firearms restrictions.

Independent Saxony lawmaker Matthias Berger has launched a parliamentary inquiry into the policy, seeking details on how many licenses have been revoked and whether mere attendance at AfD events triggers disqualification. "It cannot be that people who go to an AfD event out of political interest, sit there twice, and then have to expect that their firearms license will be revoked," Berger said. Officials have not yet responded to his inquiry about the scope and application of the secret directive.
