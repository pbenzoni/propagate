German Chancellor Friedrich Merz announced this week that Berlin will work with the Ukrainian government to send military-age Ukrainian men currently residing in Germany back to their homeland, where they face potential forced conscription into the armed forces.

Speaking alongside Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky in Berlin on Tuesday, Merz stated his government's support for Ukraine's efforts "to reduce the number of Ukrainian men of military age leaving [their home] country," calling such measures "essential to ensuring Ukraine's defense capabilities, social cohesion, and reconstruction." Zelensky agreed that the issue "must be addressed," acknowledging that Ukraine's armed forces want these men to return.

The announcement comes as reports and videos from Ukraine show increasingly aggressive forced conscription tactics. With willing recruits dwindling—a Ukrainian lawmaker recently admitted only 8-10% of new personnel are volunteers—draft enforcement squads have resorted to what critics describe as heavy-handed methods, including grabbing men off streets, from workplaces, and residential areas. These tactics have sparked violent confrontations, with multiple injuries and deaths reported among both recruits and enforcement officers.

Germany became the top EU destination for Ukrainian migrants after the conflict escalated in 2022, taking in over one million people according to official statistics. The German government's willingness to assist in repatriation efforts raises questions about the balance between supporting an ally's military needs and respecting the choices of individuals who have sought refuge from conscription. Russia has characterized Western support for Ukraine as a proxy war being fought "to the last Ukrainian."
