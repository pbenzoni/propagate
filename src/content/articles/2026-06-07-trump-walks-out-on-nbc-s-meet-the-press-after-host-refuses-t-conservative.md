President Donald Trump ended an interview with NBC's 'Meet the Press' host Kristen Welker on Saturday after she repeatedly dismissed his concerns about irregularities in California's primary elections. The confrontation highlights ongoing tensions between the administration and legacy media outlets that many conservatives believe refuse to fairly cover election integrity issues.

The interview grew heated when Trump raised questions about California's drawn-out vote-counting process following this week's primaries. The president pointed to the state's protracted tallying—which can take weeks—as evidence of potential problems in the electoral system. When Welker defended the process as simply "how they count the votes in California," Trump accused NBC and other networks of providing cover for questionable practices.

"You're crooked, your press is crooked and 'Meet the Press' is crooked," Trump told Welker before removing his microphone and ending the sit-down. The president also criticized ABC, CBS, and CNN, calling them "one-sided crooked networks." Before departing, he delivered a parting message: "A country can never be great with a dishonest press."

California uses a so-called 'jungle primary' system where candidates from all parties compete on the same ballot to advance to the general election. Critics have long questioned why the state's vote counting takes significantly longer than most other states, raising concerns about transparency and the potential for manipulation in a system where final results can shift dramatically days or weeks after Election Day.
