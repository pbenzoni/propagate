Farmers and small business owners across Northern Ireland are taking to the streets—and roads—in protest of skyrocketing fuel prices that threaten their livelihoods. Slow-moving convoys of tractors brought traffic to a standstill near Belfast City Airport this week, forcing stranded passengers to walk alongside major routes with their luggage in hand.

The protests, which began in the Republic of Ireland last week, come as global fuel prices surge following disruptions to oil shipping through the Strait of Hormuz amid the US-Israeli military operations against Iran. That critical waterway once carried roughly a quarter of the world's seaborne oil before the conflict began. In Ireland, gasoline is up 15% and diesel has jumped nearly 30% since mid-February—with government taxes accounting for nearly 60% of what consumers pay at the pump.

Protesters are demanding immediate tax relief, arguing that working families, farmers, and truckers shouldn't bear the burden of both foreign conflicts and excessive government taxation. Former British MP Andrew Bridgen pointed to EU "green policies" that pile a 16% green levy on top of already punishing 50% fuel taxes. "It's very difficult to see how businesses can cope with energy costs moving up at this rate," he said.

Adding insult to injury, Brussels recently suspended subsidies to Irish farmers even as fuel and fertilizer costs climb. Police have begun issuing fines to demonstrators and used pepper spray to clear some blockades, but organizers are calling for expanded protests across Northern Ireland on April 24. Meanwhile, fuel stations across the Republic of Ireland have run dry as the crisis deepens.
