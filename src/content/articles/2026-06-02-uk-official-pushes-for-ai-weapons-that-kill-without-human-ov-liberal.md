A senior British defense official is calling for the development of weapons systems capable of killing without human authorization, sparking concern among those who warn of the ethical and humanitarian dangers of removing humans from life-or-death decisions.

Armed Forces Under-secretary Al Carns told the *Financial Times* that the UK must prepare for "taking the human out of the loop" in weapons targeting, arguing that adversaries would deploy such systems without hesitation. Current British policy requires "context-appropriate human involvement" in weapons that identify and attack targets, but Carns suggested those rules may need to be relaxed.

The push comes amid growing use of AI-assisted targeting by militaries worldwide. Faulty AI analysis is widely believed to have contributed to a missile strike on an Iranian elementary school that killed more than 150 people, many of them children, during the U.S.-Israeli bombing campaign earlier this year—underscoring the catastrophic risks when technology supplants human judgment.

Public opinion remains deeply skeptical of autonomous lethal weapons. A February survey found that support for AI systems making independent kill decisions did not exceed 22% in the U.S., UK, Canada, or France. Even Russian President Vladimir Putin has stated that "responsibility for the final decision must always fall on a particular person," emphasizing human accountability.

Advocates for arms control and digital rights have long warned that fully autonomous weapons could erode accountability, escalate conflicts unpredictably, and disproportionately harm civilians—particularly in regions already vulnerable to military overreach. The British Ministry of Defense announced a legal review of its framework governing autonomous weapons in February, saying current rules must be "updated" for modern threats.
