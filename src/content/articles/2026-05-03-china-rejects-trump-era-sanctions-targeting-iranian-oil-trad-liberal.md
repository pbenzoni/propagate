China's Ministry of Commerce announced Saturday it will not comply with U.S. sanctions targeting domestic oil refineries accused of purchasing Iranian crude, marking an escalation in tensions between Washington and Beijing over unilateral trade restrictions.

The Trump administration's Treasury Department recently warned financial institutions against dealing with smaller Chinese refineries—known as "teapots"—that allegedly account for the majority of oil China purchases from Iran. Beijing has long maintained that sanctions imposed without United Nations authorization violate international law, and stated the U.S. restrictions "interfere with normal trade" and threaten "national sovereignty, security and development interests."

The dispute unfolds against a backdrop of deepening Middle East crisis. Oil prices have surged past $120 per barrel following U.S. and Israeli military strikes on Iran in late February, which prompted Tehran to close the Strait of Hormuz to what it calls "hostile ships." The vital waterway typically handles roughly one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas flows, and its closure has sent energy markets into turmoil.

The conflict has strained transatlantic relations, with European governments declining to support U.S. operations against Iran. Trump's approval ratings have fallen to record lows amid the crisis, which shows no clear path to resolution despite a ceasefire declared nearly a month ago. Both sides continue to accuse each other of demanding unacceptable terms for a lasting peace agreement.

China's government maintains it has not directly purchased Iranian crude since 2023, according to customs data, though the Treasury Department alleges indirect transactions continue through private refineries. A Chinese government spokesperson emphasized the policy would not affect the country's international obligations or protections for foreign-invested companies.
