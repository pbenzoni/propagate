The Pentagon has redesignated its press office as a classified space, barring reporters from entering an area that for decades served as a central point of contact between journalists and Defense Department officials. Acting Pentagon Press Secretary Joel Valdez confirmed the move, explaining that speechwriters for Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth routinely handle classified information in the space, necessitating the security upgrade.

"This is the most transparent War Department in history," Valdez stated. "No amount of spin from the Fake News media will change that." He noted that senior public affairs officials would remain available to reporters by appointment, though journalists would no longer have the informal walk-in access they previously enjoyed.

The restriction marks the latest development in an ongoing dispute between the Pentagon and major media organizations over access and transparency policies. Under Hegseth's leadership, the department has implemented new security protocols, including escort requirements inside the building and limits on previously open areas. The Pentagon also introduced a policy requiring journalists to pledge not to seek unauthorized information, including unclassified material—a requirement that major outlets including Fox News, CNN, the Associated Press, and The New York Times refused to sign.

Hundreds of reporters surrendered their Pentagon credentials in protest of the new rules, prompting multiple legal challenges. In March, a federal judge struck down portions of the policy after a lawsuit by The New York Times, though the Pentagon has appealed that ruling and implemented interim escort requirements. The Times filed a second lawsuit in May, arguing the restrictions constitute an unconstitutional attempt to limit independent reporting on military affairs. The litigation remains pending as both sides continue to clash over the proper balance between operational security and press freedom.
