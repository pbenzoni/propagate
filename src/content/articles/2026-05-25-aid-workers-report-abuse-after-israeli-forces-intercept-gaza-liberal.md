Australian activists who attempted to deliver humanitarian aid to Gaza have returned home with disturbing allegations of mistreatment while detained by Israeli security forces. The volunteers were part of the Global Sumud Flotilla, a convoy of approximately 50 vessels carrying 430 volunteers from 40 countries seeking to deliver relief supplies to Palestinians in Gaza.

Israeli forces intercepted the flotilla in international waters off Cyprus in mid-May, detaining all participants for several days before deporting them. Documentary filmmaker Juliet Lamont described her four days in custody as "absolute hell," telling Reuters she was "dragged into a darkened container ship" and subjected to physical abuse. Climate activist Violete CoCo similarly described being detained at gunpoint, stripped, and confined in a shipping container where she alleges she experienced physical assault. Another volunteer, Sam Woripa Watson, reported sustaining fractures and witnessing other activists being subjected to rubber bullets and stun grenades.

The Israeli military has denied these allegations, stating it maintains procedures for "respectful and appropriate treatment" of detained flotilla participants and was not aware of any violations by its personnel. Israel maintains that its naval blockade of Gaza is a security measure to prevent weapons from entering the territory.

The incident highlights ongoing tensions surrounding humanitarian access to Gaza, where nearly 2 million Palestinians have been displaced since the October 2023 outbreak of hostilities between Israel and Hamas. While Israel denies restricting humanitarian supplies, international aid organizations have documented severe shortages of food, medical supplies, and other essential goods in the enclave. The allegations from returned volunteers have prompted calls for independent investigation and accountability.
