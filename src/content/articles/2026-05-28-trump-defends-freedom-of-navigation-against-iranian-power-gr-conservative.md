President Donald Trump issued a forceful warning this week that the United States will not tolerate any attempt to restrict access to the Strait of Hormuz, a critical international waterway that handles a quarter of the world's seaborne oil trade.

Speaking to reporters during a cabinet meeting Wednesday, Trump made clear that the strait—which has been effectively closed since U.S. and Israeli military operations against Iran in February—must remain open to all nations. "The strait is going to be open to everybody," the president stated. "We'll watch over it, but nobody's going to control it."

The comments came after Iranian state media reported on a draft agreement that would give Iran and Oman joint control over maritime traffic through the strategic chokepoint. Trump rejected any such arrangement, emphasizing that the passage through international waters cannot be monopolized by any single nation or coalition. Iran had previously closed the route to ships from what it called "hostile nations" following the military strikes, while also claiming a "sovereign right" to manage traffic and collect tolls.

The Strait of Hormuz, which lies between Iran and U.S. ally Oman, is a lifeline for global energy markets, carrying roughly 20% of liquefied natural gas shipments in addition to the massive oil traffic. Iran's reported peace terms include demands for reparations, withdrawal of U.S. forces from the region, and an end to Israeli military operations. The United States has maintained that Iran must completely dismantle its nuclear program—which Tehran insists is peaceful—as part of any comprehensive agreement.
