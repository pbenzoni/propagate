The United States is losing ground to Russia and China in the development of artificial intelligence-powered combat drones, according to a troubling New York Times report citing defense and intelligence officials. The new generation of unmanned military aircraft can identify and strike targets without human command—a capability that could prove decisive in future conflicts.

Pentagon officials became alarmed after witnessing China's military parade last September, which showcased numerous autonomous drones demonstrating capabilities that apparently exceed America's current systems. The New York Times reports that Russia is also believed to be ahead in building production facilities for advanced drones, using the Ukraine battlefield as a proving ground to test and refine their technology. Russia has made significant progress equipping its Lancet loitering munition with autonomous targeting features.

China's approach has leveraged what experts call "civil-military fusion"—integrating commercial tech companies and startups directly into military development. This strategy, combined with China's manufacturing dominance, enables Beijing to produce autonomous weapons at a scale the Pentagon cannot currently match. One striking example is China's Jiutian drone, a heavyweight jet-powered unmanned aerial vehicle capable of carrying up to 100 smaller AI-guided kamikaze drones along with various munitions.

The root cause of America's disadvantage appears to be the Pentagon's unwieldy procurement system, built around legacy defense contractors and protracted timelines that have proven inefficient in this rapidly evolving technological landscape. While Washington has recently committed billions of dollars to catch up, U.S. defense contractors have struggled to produce the small, inexpensive drones that modern warfare increasingly demands. For years, the defense industry has concentrated on large, expensive systems like jets and tanks rather than adapting to emerging threats. The question now facing national security leaders is whether America can overcome these structural obstacles before the gap becomes insurmountable.
