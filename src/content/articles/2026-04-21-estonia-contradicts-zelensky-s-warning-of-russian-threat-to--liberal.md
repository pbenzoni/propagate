Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna has publicly disputed Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky's recent warnings that Russia may be preparing military action against Baltic NATO members. Speaking to public broadcaster ERR, Tsahkna stated that Estonian intelligence agencies see no evidence of Russian force concentrations or military preparations for attacks on NATO or the Baltic region.

Zelensky told media over the weekend that Moscow could be planning either expanded mobilization for its ongoing war in Ukraine or a smaller operation against Baltic states, which he characterized as unprepared for sustained resistance. However, Tsahkna noted that similar warnings from Kyiv have been issued repeatedly since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, and none have aligned with Estonian intelligence assessments.

Estonia's Foreign Intelligence Service reached similar conclusions in its February threat assessment, finding no imminent Russian threat to Estonia and stating there is "no cause for panic." Despite this assessment, Estonia remains one of Ukraine's strongest supporters and continues advocating for increased European defense spending.

The divergence highlights ongoing debates within the transatlantic alliance about threat assessment and military preparedness. While NATO members have committed to significant defense spending increases—including proposals to raise spending to 5% of GDP—intelligence assessments about immediate Russian intentions toward NATO territory vary among member states. Russia has consistently denied any plans to attack NATO countries unless provoked, though its February 2022 invasion of Ukraine has fundamentally reshaped European security calculations.
