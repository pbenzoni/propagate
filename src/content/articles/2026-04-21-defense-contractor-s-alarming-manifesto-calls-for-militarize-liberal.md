A major defense technology contractor has ignited fierce criticism after publishing a sweeping 22-point manifesto calling for what opponents describe as a dangerous fusion of corporate power, militarization, and artificial intelligence. Palantir Technologies, which provides surveillance and data analysis systems to intelligence agencies, released the document over the weekend, prompting immediate backlash from civil liberties advocates and technology ethics experts.

The manifesto makes extraordinary policy demands that blur the lines between private corporate interests and public governance. Among its most controversial positions: declaring that Silicon Valley has an "affirmative obligation" to support military operations, asserting that "hard power in this century will be built on software," and calling for "universal" national service requirements. Critics note these positions effectively advocate for mandatory conscription and unprecedented corporate influence over foreign and defense policy.

Palantir's close entanglement with intelligence agencies adds weight to concerns about democratic accountability. Founded in 2003 with early investment from the CIA's venture capital arm, the company now holds contracts across defense and intelligence agencies worth hundreds of billions in market valuation. Its flagship surveillance platform, "Gotham," aggregates vast amounts of data—from satellite imagery to social media activity—and uses artificial intelligence to identify potential military targets and predict civilian behavior.

Civil liberties organizations have long raised alarms about Palantir's work, particularly its domestic surveillance applications. The Los Angeles Police Department, for instance, uses the company's technology to compile extensive dossiers on residents, including their personal relationships and online activity, to assess their likelihood of future criminal behavior—a practice that evokes serious concerns about privacy, racial profiling, and the presumption of innocence. The manifesto's unapologetic embrace of expanded military and surveillance power has intensified calls for greater oversight of defense contractors and protection of democratic norms against corporate encroachment.
