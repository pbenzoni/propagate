A Texas jury delivered a unanimous guilty verdict Tuesday, convicting 19-year-old Karmelo Anthony of first-degree murder in the fatal stabbing of 17-year-old Austin Metcalf during a high school track meet in April 2025. The Collin County jury sentenced Anthony to 35 years in prison, with eligibility for parole after serving half his sentence.

The incident occurred at a Frisco Independent School District stadium when Anthony sought shelter under a team tent belonging to another school during rainfall. According to witness testimony presented at trial, Metcalf approached Anthony and repeatedly asked him to leave the tent designated for his team. Prosecutors stated that Anthony responded with "Touch me and see what happens" while reaching into a bag. When Metcalf pushed him, Anthony pulled out a folding knife and stabbed the teen in the chest, inflicting a fatal wound.

Collin County First Assistant District Attorney Bill Wirskye argued during closing arguments that Anthony had provoked the confrontation and that the stabbing was not justified. The defense team sought a reduced sentence under Texas's "sudden passion" provision, with attorney Mike Howard contending his client acted out of "fear and chaos" and that Metcalf had "no legal right" to use force against him.

Despite the victim's father, Jeff Metcalf, repeatedly stating the case was never about race or politics, some supporters of Anthony have framed the prosecution through a racial lens. Tensions were visible outside the courthouse following the verdict, with Anthony's grandmother reportedly shouting accusations of racism and bias as she departed. The jury's decision affirms that Texas law does not permit deadly force in response to being asked to leave an area or to a push, regardless of the circumstances surrounding the confrontation.
