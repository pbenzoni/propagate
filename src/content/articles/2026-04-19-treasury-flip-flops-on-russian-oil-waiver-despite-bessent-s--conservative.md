The U.S. Treasury Department has extended a sanctions waiver allowing Russian crude oil and petroleum products already loaded onto tankers to reach their destinations, despite Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's explicit statement just two days earlier that such waivers would not be renewed.

On Friday, Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued a general license covering Russian oil loaded as of April 17, authorizing transactions through May 16. The waiver—which replaces an earlier 30-day order from March 19—extends to essential services including safe docking, crew safety, emergency repairs, and insurance, even for previously sanctioned vessels. The administration justified the move as necessary to contain oil price spikes triggered by the Iran conflict and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

The reversal has drawn sharp criticism from European allies. EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen declared it is "not the time to relax sanctions against Russia," while Ukrainian leader Vladimir Zelensky estimated the exemption "could provide Russia with about $10 billion for the war." Russian officials, meanwhile, have downplayed the waiver's significance. Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov called it "meaningless," noting that "tankers that are already at sea and heading to their destinations continue moving exactly as they did before," and adding that sanctions "do not exist for us."

The policy whipsaw has raised questions about administrative consistency and America's commitment to its stated sanctions regime. Russia has reportedly gained an estimated $150 million in additional daily revenue due to soaring oil prices caused by the Middle East crisis, with Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov noting a "sharp rise in demand" while emphasizing Russia "has been and remains a reliable supplier of both oil and gas."
