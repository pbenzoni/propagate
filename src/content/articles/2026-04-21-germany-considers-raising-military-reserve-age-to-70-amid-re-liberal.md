Germany's military is grappling with a significant personnel shortage as it attempts to expand from 186,000 to 260,000 active-duty service members by the mid-2030s. Now, the newly elected president of the German Reservists' Association is proposing an unconventional solution: raising the maximum age for military reservists from 65 to 70.

Bastian Ernst argues that as retirement ages increase across society and people maintain their physical fitness longer, the military should adapt accordingly. "If we are lamenting a shortage of young recruits, then we should also address the other end of the age pyramid," Ernst told German media outlet RND. He also advocated for removing employer veto power over whether reservists can participate in military training when called up.

The proposal reflects broader challenges facing European militaries in the wake of the Ukraine conflict. Finland recently announced it would raise its reserve age limit from 50 to 65 starting in 2026, while the United Kingdom increased its maximum reservist age from 55 to 65. These changes come as European nations reassess their defense capabilities and readiness.

Germany's recruitment efforts have sparked domestic controversy, with critics expressing concern that new voluntary service legislation introduced in January could serve as a stepping stone toward reinstating compulsory conscription, which was suspended in 2011. The debate highlights the tension between security concerns and preserving civil liberties in democratic societies facing evolving geopolitical pressures.
