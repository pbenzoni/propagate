After more than a decade and a half in power, Viktor Orbán is finally stepping down as Hungary's prime minister. Across Europe, his departure is being welcomed as a chance for democratic renewal. Peter Magyar, leader of the Tisza party that won Sunday's elections, campaigned on restoring Hungary's relationships with NATO and the European Union—a stark departure from Orbán's years of democratic backsliding and autocratic governance.

Yet while the change in leadership style may be significant, experts caution that some of Hungary's underlying policy positions may prove more durable than initially hoped. Two key issues have defined Budapest's complex relationship with Ukraine: energy transit and the rights of ethnic Hungarian minorities in Ukraine's Transcarpathia region. Throughout the conflict, Hungary has quietly allowed critical civilian and military supplies—including electricity and fuel—to flow through its territory to Ukraine, even as Orbán publicly clashed with President Zelensky.

The energy dimension remains particularly fraught. Until recently, Hungary received Russian gas through pipelines crossing Ukrainian territory, benefiting from lower energy costs even as other European nations moved to divest from Russian fossil fuels. When Ukraine moved to shut down these transit routes as part of its broader strategy to end Russian energy flows to Europe, tensions escalated dramatically. Hungary threatened to cut emergency electricity supplies to Ukraine, though it ultimately refrained from doing so.

Meanwhile, Hungary has long pressed for protections for ethnic Hungarians living in Transcarpathia, a region that was historically part of Austria-Hungary. Ukraine's language and education policies, which prioritize Ukrainian over minority languages, have been a persistent source of friction. While Magyar's more diplomatic approach may lower the temperature of these disputes, the fundamental policy disagreements—rooted in energy security, geopolitical alignment, and minority rights—are likely to persist beyond Orbán's tenure.
