A rare albino buffalo in Bangladesh narrowly escaped slaughter during the Muslim festival of Eid al-Adha after government officials intervened to protect the genetically unusual animal that had become a viral sensation online.

The nearly 700-kilogram buffalo, distinguished by its cream-colored coat and blond forelock—a result of a genetic condition causing reduced pigmentation—had already been sold for ritual sacrifice when Interior Minister Salahuddin Ahmed ordered authorities to halt the proceedings. Officials refunded the buyer and relocated the animal, though reports differ on whether it was taken to Dhaka's national zoo or returned temporarily to its original farm.

The animal's owner, Ziauddin Mridha, noted that despite the buffalo's striking appearance, "it is very calm by nature." The intervention came after videos of the rare creature spread widely on social media, drawing daily crowds of visitors hoping to photograph the unusual animal. Albino buffaloes are extremely rare in Bangladesh due to the genetic rarity of the condition.

Some visitors who traveled with their families to see the buffalo expressed disappointment at arriving after authorities had already removed it. The buyer, identified in local reports as Moniruzzaman, was told he would receive either a refund or an animal of equivalent value for the religious observance. The case highlights tensions between cultural traditions and the preservation of genetically rare animals that capture public attention.
