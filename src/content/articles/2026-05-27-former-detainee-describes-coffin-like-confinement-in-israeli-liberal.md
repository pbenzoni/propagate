A Palestinian man has come forward with disturbing allegations about his treatment while in Israeli detention, describing being confined in a coffin-like metal container for over two weeks after refusing to become an informant for Israeli forces.

Iman Nabhan told reporters he was held in "an iron container with a wooden box inside it" with his hands and feet bound. He said he was fed through a hole and allowed only one minute to use the toilet during his 15-day confinement. "It seemed they wanted to make me feel like I was dead so they could get whatever information they wanted," Nabhan said, adding that Israeli personnel offered him money, foreign travel opportunities, and medical treatment for his mother in exchange for cooperation—all of which he declined.

The testimony adds to growing concerns about treatment of Palestinians and activists in Israeli custody. Earlier this week, Australian members of a Gaza-bound humanitarian flotilla reported experiencing assault, humiliation, and mistreatment after being detained and deported by Israeli forces. National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir, who oversees Israel's prison system, personally posted footage of detained flotilla activists kneeling with bound hands, and was filmed taunting them while waving an Israeli flag.

Ben-Gvir has advocated for harsh treatment of Palestinian detainees, stating publicly that they should receive "the minimum of the minimum" in food and championing capital punishment legislation. The minister has also denied Palestinian national identity entirely, claiming in November that "there is no such thing as 'Palestinian people.'" These incidents have intensified international scrutiny of detention conditions and treatment of Palestinians in Israeli custody.
