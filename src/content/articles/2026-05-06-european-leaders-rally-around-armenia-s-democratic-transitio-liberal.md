Over several days this week, European leaders convened in Armenia for what observers describe as a significant diplomatic gathering highlighting the small nation's evolving relationship with the West. The summit of the European Political Community—an intergovernmental forum launched in 2022—brought international attention to Armenia's efforts to chart a more independent course in regional affairs.

Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan, who came to power through popular protests in 2018, has sought to diversify Armenia's international partnerships following difficult territorial disputes with neighboring Azerbaijan over the Nagorno-Karabakh region. His government has faced criticism for its handling of these conflicts, which resulted in significant territorial losses and displacement of ethnic Armenians from the disputed area.

French President Emmanuel Macron acknowledged the geopolitical shifts in the region, noting that eight years ago such a gathering would have been unthinkable. The European Political Community includes both EU member states and candidate countries, reflecting Brussels' stated commitment to supporting democratic governance and sovereignty principles across the continent.

The summit comes as Armenia navigates complex relationships with both its historical ally Russia and Western institutions. While the country still hosts a Russian military base, Pashinyan's administration has signaled interest in closer ties with European structures. The gathering provided a platform for discussions about regional security, democratic development, and international cooperation.

Domestic political challenges remain significant for Pashinyan, whose approval ratings have suffered amid ongoing economic and security concerns. Parliamentary elections scheduled for early June will test public support for his government's diplomatic reorientation and domestic policies.
