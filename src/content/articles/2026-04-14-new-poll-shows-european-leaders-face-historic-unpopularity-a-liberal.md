A new Morning Consult survey tracking leadership approval across 24 democracies reveals unprecedented disapproval ratings for several European leaders, with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz recording a 76% disapproval rate and only 19% approval among German voters.

The findings reflect broader frustration with centrist leadership across Europe as citizens grapple with economic stagnation and cost-of-living pressures. Merz, who has acknowledged Germany faces a "structural crisis," has drawn criticism for deflecting responsibility by blaming previous administrations, EU regulations, and even workers' sick leave patterns rather than examining his own policy choices.

French President Emmanuel Macron faces nearly identical disapproval, with only 18% approval, following his risky decision to dissolve the National Assembly after electoral losses—a gamble that resulted in legislative gridlock. British Prime Minister Keir Starmer ranks third in unpopularity at 65% disapproval, struggling with governance challenges and the cost-of-living crisis that has defined his tenure.

The data underscores a wider pattern of voter dissatisfaction with establishment politics across the continent, as leaders face accountability for economic policies that have failed to address working families' material concerns. The survey, conducted in early April, suggests that European electorates are increasingly unwilling to accept explanations that avoid substantive policy accountability.
