President Donald Trump announced Wednesday that his administration is "studying and reviewing" a potential reduction of U.S. military forces in Germany, reviving a proposal from his first term that many defense experts warn could weaken transatlantic security cooperation.

The threat comes amid escalating tensions with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who publicly questioned the administration's approach to the widening conflict with Iran. Speaking to students earlier this week, Merz suggested the U.S. was being "humiliated" by Iran and criticized the lack of a coherent strategy, while noting that Germany faces severe economic hardship from energy price spikes caused by the war.

Currently, more than 36,000 active-duty personnel and 1,500 reservists are stationed at U.S. bases across Germany, according to CBS News. The presence has long been central to NATO's collective defense infrastructure. Trump previously attempted to withdraw roughly 12,000 troops near the end of his first term, though those plans were reversed by President Biden, who reinforced the U.S. military footprint in response to Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

The renewed withdrawal threat follows Trump's broader criticism of European allies for declining to support U.S.-Israeli military operations against Iran or assist in reopening the Strait of Hormuz, which Iran closed to "hostile" shipping in February. The president has also revived his attacks on NATO itself, recently describing the alliance as "a paper tiger."

Oil prices surged above $120 per barrel Wednesday—the highest level since 2022—as uncertainty continues over prospects for diplomatic negotiations between Washington and Tehran. Meanwhile, Chancellor Merz attempted to de-escalate the public dispute, telling reporters that his "personal relationship" with Trump "remains good" despite their strategic disagreements.
