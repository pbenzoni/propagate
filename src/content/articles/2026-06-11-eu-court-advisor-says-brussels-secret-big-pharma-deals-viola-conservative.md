A legal advisor to Europe's highest court delivered a sharp rebuke to Brussels bureaucrats Thursday, declaring the European Commission had no right to hide its massive COVID-19 vaccine contracts from public scrutiny. The opinion comes as Commission President Ursula von der Leyen faces mounting questions over her secret text-message negotiations with Pfizer's CEO.

Advocate General Athanasios Rantos argued that the Commission's wall of secrecy made it "impossible to know" whether vaccine negotiators had conflicts of interest with the pharmaceutical giants they handed $82 billion in public contracts. Between 2020 and 2021, Brussels signed sweetheart deals worth €71 billion with companies including Pfizer, AstraZeneca, and Moderna—all while refusing to disclose basic details about who negotiated the contracts or what terms were agreed to.

When Green party members and over 3,000 citizens demanded transparency, Commission lawyers claimed the redactions were necessary to protect negotiators from "conspiracy theorists." But the court wasn't buying it. This is the second major legal defeat for von der Leyen in the so-called 'Pfizergate' scandal, where she admitted negotiating a separate €35 billion deal for 900 million vaccine doses via text messages with Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla—then conveniently claimed those messages were "lost."

The Court of Justice ruled last year that Brussels failed to provide "credible explanations" for why the texts disappeared, raising obvious questions about accountability when unelected officials commit taxpayers to tens of billions in spending. Von der Leyen survived a no-confidence vote over the affair last July, but the legal advisor's new opinion—while not binding—will inform the court's final ruling and keep pressure on Brussels to explain what it's been hiding.
