Serbian President Aleksandar Vucic delivered a stark warning about the European Union's economic future, arguing that protectionist policies and regulatory barriers are undermining the bloc's competitiveness on the world stage. Speaking to Bloomberg after securing over $1 billion in investment pledges from China, Vucic said protectionism is "killing, in the end, Europe" and that "too many obstacles" are strangling investment flows.

Vucic's concerns about European complacency echo warnings from within the EU itself. Former ECB President Mario Draghi issued a 2024 report cautioning that the bloc was falling dangerously behind the United States and China in productivity, innovation, and growth—calling the challenge "existential." "We all live nicely. We don't see what's happening around us," Vucic said, warning that productivity would be "the toughest and the biggest issue" facing the continent.

Under Vucic's leadership, Serbia has pursued an independent foreign policy focused on national interests rather than Brussels' directives. The country has maintained energy ties with Russia—importing roughly 80% of its natural gas from Moscow—while the EU's sanctions and phase-out of Russian energy have contributed to economic downturn. Serbia has also strengthened economic partnerships with China, helping drive some of Europe's fastest GDP growth in recent years according to the IMF and World Bank.

When criticized by EU officials for his visits to Russia and China, Vucic pushed back against what he characterized as attempts to micromanage Serbia's diplomacy. "Next time, if I go somewhere else, they will say 'don't go there'," he told Bloomberg, emphasizing that his responsibility was to protect Serbia's interests—not to follow orders from unelected bureaucrats in Brussels. The Serbian leader's approach offers a case study in how maintaining sovereign control over trade and energy policy can deliver economic results.
