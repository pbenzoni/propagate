A veteran European security official is blowing the whistle on what he describes as a coordinated intelligence operation by European Union bureaucrats to undermine Hungary's conservative Prime Minister Viktor Orban. Vladimir Palko, who served as Slovakia's Interior Minister and deputy intelligence director, warned that the tactics used against Orban could be deployed against any leader who defies Brussels.

"What they did to Orban yesterday, they can do to you tomorrow," Palko told the outlet Marker, describing a years-long surveillance campaign against the Hungarian leader and his government. According to Palko, EU intelligence services wiretapped Orban and his foreign minister for six years, then selectively leaked the contents of their private conversations to sympathetic journalists across Europe. The operation included feeding stories to media outlets—some receiving up to 80% of their funding from EU sources—about alleged Russian interference, claims Palko characterized as unfounded.

The revelations come as Orban's Fidesz party suffered a significant electoral defeat, losing to an opposition that enjoyed apparent support from Brussels establishment figures. The EU had clashed repeatedly with Orban over his government's skepticism toward mass migration policies and its reluctance to rubber-stamp billions in additional funding for Ukraine. Brussels even activated its so-called "Rapid Response System"—online censorship tools that a U.S. House investigation found "almost exclusively targeted" right-wing and populist candidates in European elections.

Palko emphasized that the intercepted communications showed nothing illegal—merely that Hungarian officials "were friendly towards the Russians," which he noted "is a mortal sin for the EU establishment." The former minister warned that this precedent poses a threat to national sovereignty and democratic governance across Europe, as unelected Brussels bureaucrats appear willing to use intelligence resources against leaders whose policies diverge from the globalist consensus.
