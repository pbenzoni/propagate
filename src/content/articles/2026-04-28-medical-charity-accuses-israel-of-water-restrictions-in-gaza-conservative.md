Doctors Without Borders (MSF) has released a report accusing Israel of restricting water access in Gaza as a form of collective punishment against Palestinians. The international medical charity claims Israel has "engineered" water scarcity in the territory, creating conditions they describe as incompatible with human survival. The organization reports sharp increases in waterborne diseases including diarrhea, skin infections, and other health complications since October 2023.

Israel's Coordinator of Government Activities in the Territories (COGAT) firmly rejected the allegations, calling them "baseless" and "factually incorrect." Israeli officials stated they are actively facilitating water access through four supply lines, conducting infrastructure repairs, and providing fuel and electricity for water systems. COGAT accused MSF of biased reporting and operational failures.

The disputed territory has no natural freshwater sources and depends on treated groundwater and seawater. MSF acknowledges it has become the largest non-governmental water producer in Gaza through mobile desalination units. The organization notes that multiple supply trucks have been attacked, though responsibility for these attacks remains a point of contention. UNICEF reports that residents are receiving at best the minimum humanitarian threshold of 15 liters per person per day, with many receiving less.

The conflict continues despite a ceasefire agreement, with both sides presenting sharply different accounts of conditions on the ground. The dispute highlights ongoing tensions over responsibility for humanitarian conditions in the region, with international organizations and Israeli authorities offering contradictory assessments of water infrastructure access and security concerns.
