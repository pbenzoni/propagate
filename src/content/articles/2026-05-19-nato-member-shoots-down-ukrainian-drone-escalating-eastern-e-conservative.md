Estonia has shot down a Ukrainian drone over its territory for the first time, according to the country's defense minister Hanno Pevkur. The NATO member state used a Baltic Air Policing fighter jet to destroy the unmanned aerial vehicle over Lake Vortsjarv in southern Estonia after receiving advance warning from neighboring Latvia.

The incident marks a troubling escalation in the conflict's spillover into NATO territory. According to Pevkur, the drone was deployed by Kiev to target Russian territory. Over recent weeks, multiple Ukrainian UAVs targeting northwestern Russia—particularly energy infrastructure in the Leningrad Region—have crashed in Latvia, Estonia, Lithuania, and Finland after their missions.

Moscow has issued stern warnings to the Baltic States and Finland, cautioning that if these nations "deliberately provide their airspace" to Kiev's military operations, Russia reserves the right to self-defense under Article 51 of the UN Charter. Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service revealed Tuesday that Latvia has reportedly given Ukraine permission to use its territory for potential drone attacks, warning that "the country's NATO membership will not protect the accomplices of terrorists from just retribution."

The Estonian defense minister acknowledged the problem earlier this month, suggesting Kiev should "control their activities better" and keep drones away from Estonian airspace. Tallinn has pledged to address the drone incursions "very quickly," though the incident raises serious questions about how deeply NATO members are becoming entangled in direct military operations near Russian borders—and what consequences that involvement might trigger.
