U.S. Central Command has requested deployment of the Dark Eagle hypersonic missile system to the Middle East following intelligence reports that Iran has repositioned its ballistic missile launchers beyond the range of current American weapons, according to Bloomberg.

The Long-Range Hypersonic Weapon (LRHW), known as Dark Eagle, represents America's effort to maintain military superiority against adversaries like Russia and China, who have already deployed hypersonic technology. The Pentagon has invested over $12 billion in the program since 2018, though the system has not yet reached full operational status. The deployment request underscores the urgency of countering Iranian military capabilities in a volatile region where American forces and allies face persistent threats.

The request comes as existing U.S. missile inventory faces strain. American forces recently exhausted their entire supply of Precision Strike Missiles (PrSM) during operations in the region, raising concerns about readiness if tensions with Iran escalate further. The PrSM, designed to replace the Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS), has a shorter range than the proposed hypersonic option, limiting its effectiveness against repositioned Iranian targets.

While no final decision has been made on deploying the Dark Eagle system, the request highlights the strategic challenges facing U.S. military commanders tasked with protecting American interests and allies in the Middle East.
