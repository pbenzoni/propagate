A senior Trump administration official personally intervened to help a prominent Polish conservative politician escape what the State Department views as politically motivated prosecution by Poland's liberal government, according to Reuters.

Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau instructed State Department officials to approve a visa for former Polish Justice Minister Zbigniew Ziobro, who faces criminal charges in his home country. Landau, who learned of the case from U.S. Ambassador to Warsaw Tom Rose, reportedly believed Ziobro was being unjustly targeted and described the matter as "a national security issue."

Ziobro served under Poland's conservative Law and Justice (PiS) party government from 2015 to 2023. After liberal Prime Minister Donald Tusk's coalition took power, the new government launched multiple investigations into the previous administration. Prosecutors accuse Ziobro of abuse of power related to the purchase of Israeli-made Pegasus spyware, though Ziobro maintains the inquiry is illegal and politically motivated.

Poland's current Justice Minister expressed surprise at the report, saying Warsaw expected consultation on such matters. However, the move aligns with the Trump administration's stated position that conservative politicians in the European Union are frequently targeted through "lawfare"—the weaponization of judicial systems against political opponents.

Ziobro initially received asylum in Hungary under former Prime Minister Viktor Orban before traveling to the United States. The case highlights growing tensions between the Trump administration and liberal European governments over what conservatives view as the political persecution of right-leaning officials.
