American families are feeling the pinch as inflation accelerated to a three-year high in May, driven primarily by soaring energy costs linked to ongoing military operations against Iran. Consumer prices rose 4.2% from a year earlier, with gasoline prices climbing a staggering 40.5% annually, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data released Wednesday.

The price surge is hitting household budgets hard across the country. What used to be a $40-$50 fill-up now costs $60-$70 at the pump, adding an estimated $750 per household to annual fuel bills. Rural families are bearing an even heavier burden, paying an extra $26 per week compared to pre-conflict prices. Energy costs alone accounted for more than 60% of May's monthly price increases.
