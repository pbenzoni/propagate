Immigration and Customs Enforcement has awarded a one-year contract to MVM, a Virginia-based private security firm currently facing serious allegations in federal court, according to reporting by The Guardian. The company has been tasked with locating migrant children who entered the U.S. unaccompanied and were released into communities while awaiting immigration proceedings.

MVM is the defendant in an ongoing lawsuit filed in California that alleges "torture, enforced disappearance and cruel, inhuman, and degrading treatment" related to family separation policies in 2017. The complaint describes how company employees "physically took thousands of children away from their parents" and transported them "using unmarked vehicles, commercial airlines, and makeshift detention centers."

While DHS maintains that MVM has "zero immigration enforcement authority" and that the contract focuses on welfare checks to protect children from exploitation, internal ICE documents reviewed by The Guardian last year suggest a broader enforcement agenda that could include pursuing deportation cases or criminal charges against children or their caregivers.

Rep. Delia Ramirez (D-IL) has called for congressional oversight of the arrangement, stating that hiring "a company of dangerous bounty hunters, with a concerning track record of abuse, to 'track' immigrant children" represents a reckless threat to vulnerable populations. Civil liberties advocates continue to express concern about the expansion of private contractors in immigration enforcement operations.
