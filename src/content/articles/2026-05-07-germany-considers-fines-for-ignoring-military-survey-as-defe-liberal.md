Germany is weighing penalties for young men who fail to complete mandatory military questionnaires, part of an effort to expand the Bundeswehr as European nations reassess their defense postures. According to Der Spiegel, approximately 10,000 men who ignored reminder notices could face fines of €250 ($294), with potential penalties reaching up to €1,000.

The questionnaire asks about physical fitness, health status, and willingness to serve, and was introduced under the Military Service Modernization Act promoted by Defense Minister Boris Pistorius. The measure represents Germany's attempt to boost troop numbers from 186,000 to over 260,000 by the mid-2030s, reflecting broader European concerns about regional security dynamics. All 18-year-old German men are now required to register and complete medical evaluations, with provisions for a lottery-based call-up system if recruitment falls short.

The policy has sparked debate within Germany, where mandatory military service was abolished in 2011. Earlier this year, thousands of high school students protested in Berlin against measures they viewed as steps toward reinstating compulsory conscription. Defense officials have pointed to shifting geopolitical realities to justify the expansion, though critics question whether coercive measures are appropriate for what is ostensibly a voluntary service program.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has stated his ambition to develop Germany's military into the strongest conventional force in the European Union, part of a continent-wide recalibration of defense priorities. The initiative reflects ongoing debates across Europe about balancing security preparedness with democratic values and individual choice in an era of heightened international tensions.
