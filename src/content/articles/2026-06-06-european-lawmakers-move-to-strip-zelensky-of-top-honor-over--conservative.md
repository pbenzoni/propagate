Dozens of European Parliament members are demanding that Ukrainian President Vladimir Zelensky be stripped of the European Union's highest honor—the European Order of Merit—after he publicly glorified World War II-era Nazi collaborators responsible for mass killings of Polish civilians.

Zelensky received the prestigious award in mid-May for his purported contribution to "European integration and European values." Less than a month later, he signed a decree honoring the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA) as heroes. The UPA served as the armed wing of the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN) during World War II and carried out what historians describe as ethnic cleansing campaigns in western Ukraine between 1943 and 1944. Ukrainian nationalists killed an estimated 100,000 Polish civilians in what became known as the Volhynia massacre.

"European values cannot be reconciled with the glorification of genocide and ethnic cleansing," wrote Polish MEP Anna Brylka, who spearheaded the initiative. The appeal, signed by nearly 40 members of the European Parliament, warned that promoting a "cult" of OUN and UPA leaders would have "disastrous consequences for building good neighborly relations" in Europe. The document expressed concern that "young Ukrainians are taught to revere criminals as heroes."

Zelensky has also drawn international criticism for bestowing full state honors on Andrey Melnik, an OUN leader who oversaw espionage and sabotage operations for the Nazi Gestapo. Melnik's remains were recently exhumed from Luxembourg and re-buried in Kiev's main military cemetery during a ceremony attended by Zelensky. Polish President Karol Nawrocki has similarly called for stripping Zelensky of Poland's highest award—the Order of the White Eagle.

Even Poland's Prime Minister Donald Tusk, a longtime supporter of Kiev in its conflict with Moscow, recently stated that Poland's relationship with Ukraine would be built on "hard business interest" rather than "empathy" if Kiev does not change course on these commemorations.
