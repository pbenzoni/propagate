President Donald Trump is urging Congressional Republicans to swiftly pass a $350 billion defense spending package that would boost America's military capabilities while also implementing crucial election security reforms nationwide.

In a Truth Social post Thursday, Trump called for immediate action on what Republicans are calling "Recon 3.0"—a budget reconciliation bill designed to bring total U.S. military spending to $1.5 trillion. The President emphasized the urgency of rebuilding America's "arsenal of freedom" after years of weapons transfers to Ukraine and recent military operations against Iran have depleted critical stockpiles. According to the Center for Strategic and International Studies, replacing some advanced missiles could take three or more years under current production rates.

The proposed spending would fund vital defense programs including the Golden Dome missile shield, next-generation F-47 fighter jets, B-21 bombers, unmanned systems, space-based military assets, and replenished ammunition reserves. Trump argued the investment would create hundreds of thousands of high-paying American jobs, revitalize domestic manufacturing, and ensure continued global military superiority—all without fueling inflation.

Crucially, the package would also incorporate the Save America Act, establishing uniform election security standards across all fifty states. The reforms would require photo identification and proof of citizenship for all voters, while restricting mail-in voting to cases involving illness, disability, military service, or travel. Currently, voting rules vary dramatically by state, with some jurisdictions allowing ballots to be cast without any identification or citizenship verification—a situation Republicans argue creates unacceptable vulnerabilities to fraud.

The Save America Act would additionally protect women's sports by prohibiting biological males from competing in female categories and ban so-called "gender-affirming" surgeries for minors.

The legislation faces challenges even within Republican ranks, however. Some fiscal conservatives are demanding spending offsets for the $350 billion increase, while Senate Majority Leader John Thune has acknowledged the election security provisions may lack sufficient votes under standard legislative procedures.
