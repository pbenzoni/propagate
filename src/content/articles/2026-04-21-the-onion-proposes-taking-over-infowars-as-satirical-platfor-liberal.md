The Onion is pursuing a court-supervised agreement to temporarily operate Alex Jones' InfoWars platform as a satirical parody site, in a proposal tied to Jones' massive defamation judgment owed to Sandy Hook families. The plan would grant Global Tetrahedron, The Onion's parent company, a six-month lease over InfoWars' intellectual property and operations for $81,000 monthly, with an option to extend.

The proposal has garnered support from both the court-appointed receiver overseeing Jones' assets and attorneys representing the Sandy Hook families who won a roughly $1.4 billion defamation judgment against Jones for his false claims about the 2012 elementary school shooting. The Onion plans to repurpose the InfoWars website and social media accounts for comedy content, with comedian Tim Heidecker set to serve as creative director.

Jones has opposed the arrangement, claiming it would misrepresent his brand and confuse audiences. He has stated his intention to continue broadcasting through alternative platforms and pursue legal challenges. The broadcaster's media company, Free Speech Systems, entered bankruptcy proceedings due to the substantial defamation judgments against him after years of spreading conspiracy theories about the Sandy Hook tragedy.

This marks The Onion's second attempt to acquire control of InfoWars, after a bankruptcy judge blocked an earlier auction bid in December 2024. A hearing on the licensing arrangement is scheduled for April 30 in Travis County, Texas, where a judge will determine whether to approve the proposal that would see one of America's most notorious conspiracy platforms transformed into a vehicle for satire.
