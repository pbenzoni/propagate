Radev's newly formed Progressive Bulgaria party secured a decisive 44.7% of the vote, crushing the former ruling party and delivering what the leader called a "victory of hope over mistrust."

Radev emphasized that Bulgaria would pursue "more critical thinking and pragmatism" rather than continuing down the path of virtue-signaling that has left Europe economically weakened. He pointed out that major European leaders, including French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, have already acknowledged that dialogue with Russia must be restored if Europe wants real security and economic competitiveness.

The landslide victory marks Bulgaria's eighth election in five years, a period of political chaos that began when the previous government resigned amid corruption scandals in 2021. Radev, who served as president from 2017 until stepping down in January to run for prime minister, campaigned on breaking the political deadlock and ending the influence of unaccountable Brussels bureaucrats over Bulgarian sovereignty.
