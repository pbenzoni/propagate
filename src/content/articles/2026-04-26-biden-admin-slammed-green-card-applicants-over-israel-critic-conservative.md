Internal Department of Homeland Security training materials reveal that the Biden administration directed immigration officers to deny green cards to applicants who criticized Israel or participated in pro-Palestinian protests, according to documents reviewed by the New York Times.

The guidance, issued to US Citizenship and Immigration Services officers, characterized participation in pro-Palestinian demonstrations and public criticism of Israel as "overwhelmingly negative" factors in permanent residency decisions. Officers were instructed to flag social media posts such as "Stop Israeli terror in Palestine" or images showing the Israeli flag crossed out as potentially disqualifying speech. The materials also cited maps showing Palestine in place of Israel as examples of antisemitic content.

Immigration officials were specifically told to "focus particularly on aliens who engaged in on-campus anti-American and antisemitic activities," including students who participated in pro-Palestinian campus protests following the October 2023 Hamas attack. Notably, the guidance even flagged desecration of the American flag as a negative factor—despite the Supreme Court's longstanding First Amendment protection for flag burning.

A White House spokesperson defended the policy, claiming it had "nothing to do with free speech" but was instead about protecting "American institutions, the safety of citizens, national security and the freedoms of the US." The vetting directive emerged from a broader August initiative to screen immigrants for "anti-American" behavior and ties to terrorist organizations. According to CNN, over 6,000 student visas were revoked since early 2025, while the Wall Street Journal reported that 2025 marked the first year since 1935 where more people left the United States than entered.
