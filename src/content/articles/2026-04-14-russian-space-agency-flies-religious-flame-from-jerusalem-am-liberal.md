Russia's space agency Roscosmos dispatched a dedicated aircraft to Jerusalem on Saturday to transport the Holy Fire—a flame lit annually at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre—back to Moscow in time for Orthodox Easter services. The centuries-old ritual, which takes place at a site traditionally associated with the burial of Jesus Christ, involves the Patriarch of Jerusalem distributing fire to clergy and pilgrims in a darkened chapel.

A delegation led by the St. Andrew the First-Called Foundation received the flame in Jerusalem and flew it to Moscow's Vnukovo Airport, where representatives from multiple dioceses collected it for distribution to churches across the capital and eventually to regions throughout Russia. Roscosmos chief Dmitry Bakanov acknowledged the operation proceeded despite ongoing tensions in the Middle East, stating that delivering the flame was important for millions of Orthodox Christians observing the holiday.

The tradition of transporting the Holy Fire to Russia on special flights became an annual practice in the early 2000s, following its reintroduction in the post-Soviet era. This year's Easter observance coincided with Cosmonautics Day on April 12, commemorating Yuri Gagarin's historic 1961 spaceflight. Patriarch Kirill, head of the Russian Orthodox Church, exchanged greetings with Russian cosmonauts aboard the International Space Station as part of the dual celebration.

The coordination of religious observance with state aerospace infrastructure reflects the ongoing intersection of Orthodox tradition and Russian governmental institutions, a relationship that has deepened considerably since the collapse of the Soviet Union in 1991.
