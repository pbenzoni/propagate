In a rare bipartisan moment, executives from America's leading AI companies have joined national security experts to urge mandatory safety screening for synthetic DNA purchases. The public letter to Congress warns that rapid advances in artificial intelligence could significantly weaken the barriers that have historically prevented the development of biological weapons.

The appeal was signed by top executives including OpenAI's Sam Altman, Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, and Microsoft AI's Mustafa Suleyman and Eric Horvitz, alongside dozens of biotechnology and national security professionals. They're calling for common-sense safeguards on synthetic DNA—artificially produced genetic material that can currently be ordered online like any other lab supply.

While synthetic DNA has legitimate uses in vaccine development, medical research, and data storage, the signatories warn that malicious actors could exploit the current lack of regulation to order DNA sequences capable of recreating dangerous pathogens. Although some companies voluntarily screen customers, there's no legal requirement to do so. The letter describes mandatory screening and record-keeping as "one of the best understood and least disruptive biosecurity measures available."

The urgency stems from AI's accelerating capabilities. Today's AI systems can already outperform PhD-level virologists on complex laboratory questions, the experts note. As these systems continue improving, the specialized knowledge that once limited access to biological weapons could rapidly erode. The signatories emphasize this is a global security issue, as synthetic DNA can be purchased and shipped across international borders with minimal oversight.
