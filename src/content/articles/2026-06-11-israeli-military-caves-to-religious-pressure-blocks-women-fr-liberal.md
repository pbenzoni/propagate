The Israel Defense Forces has abandoned plans to allow women in frontline tank crews following pressure from Religious Zionist rabbis, according to Israeli media reports. The decision represents a significant setback for gender equality in the military, even as the IDF faces what it describes as critical personnel shortages requiring approximately 12,000 additional recruits.

Influential rabbis had threatened to discourage students from enlisting in armored units if the integration proceeded, arguing that mixed-gender crews would violate religious norms. The IDF capitulated to these demands on Wednesday, announcing that tank crews would remain single-sex while emphasizing it "needs every male and female combat soldier."

The controversy follows an April ruling by Israel's High Court of Justice directing the military to proceed with a long-delayed pilot program expanding women's access to combat positions. That initiative, originally scheduled for 2024, has been postponed multiple times and is now slated to begin later this year—though the exclusion of tank crews significantly narrows its scope.

The dispute highlights ongoing tensions between secular legal frameworks and religious authority within Israeli institutions. Many Religious Zionist community members serve in combat units through the Hesder program, which combines military service with religious studies, giving religious leaders substantial leverage over recruitment. Israeli media reported Thursday that additional religious institutions have joined the campaign against gender integration, suggesting further resistance to women's expanded military roles.
