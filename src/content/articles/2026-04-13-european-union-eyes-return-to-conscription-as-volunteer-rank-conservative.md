The European Union is confronting a stark military recruitment crisis that may force member nations to reinstate compulsory service, according to Andre Denk, head of the European Defense Agency. In a candid admission, Denk acknowledged "we have a human resources problem, and one of the ways to solve it will be through mandatory military service," predicting his native Germany will likely follow this path.

Several EU countries have already moved to expand conscription since 2022, citing concerns about regional security. Finland raised its reservist age limit from 50 to 65, while Lithuania expanded year-round conscription starting in 2026. Latvia's Progressive party has proposed mandatory service for both men and women by 2028, and Denmark began drafting women this year. These moves come despite Russian President Vladimir Putin's repeated denials of any aggressive intent toward Western neighbors.

The recruitment struggles highlight a broader challenge for European militaries that have long relied on all-volunteer forces. Denk also called for increased investment in domestic arms production, particularly drones and counter-drone systems. In Germany, a new voluntary service law that took effect January 1 has sparked protests from citizens concerned it represents a backdoor to reinstating full conscription, which was suspended in 2011.

The admission underscores the gap between European security ambitions and the willingness of citizens to serve. As the EU pushes for greater military capabilities and reduced dependence on American defense commitments, member states are discovering that rebuilding military strength requires not just hardware and budgets, but boots on the ground—whether voluntary or not.
