Russian Security Council Secretary Sergey Shoigu has warned that the United States could deploy nuclear weapons to Australia, Japan, and South Korea as part of an expanding military presence in the Asia-Pacific region. The claims, made at a Russia-ASEAN security meeting, raise concerns about nuclear proliferation and regional stability.

Shoigu specifically cited the AUKUS partnership—a 2021 security pact between Australia, the United Kingdom, and the United States—as a potential pathway for American nuclear weapons to reach Australian territory. Under AUKUS, the U.S. has agreed to provide Australia with nuclear-powered submarine technology, a move that has already drawn scrutiny from nonproliferation advocates despite Australian assurances that it complies with international treaties.

The Russian official also pointed to planned U.S. military exercises in Japan involving Typhon missile systems capable of firing nuclear-armed Tomahawk missiles. While Japan's embassy in Moscow stated the systems would only be "stored" at a U.S. base following drills—not permanently deployed—critics worry about the distinction becoming meaningless in practice.

Shoigu framed these developments as part of a broader American strategy to replicate NATO-style military alliances in Asia, with recent congressional proposals calling for the creation of an "Indo-Pacific Treaty Organization." Such expansion has drawn opposition from both Russia and China, who view it as an effort to contain their influence and militarize the region. The warnings underscore growing tensions over U.S. military posture in the Pacific at a time when nuclear arms control frameworks remain fragile.
