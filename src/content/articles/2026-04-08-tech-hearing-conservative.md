A parade of tech executives rolled through the Capitol again this week. The opening statements were polished, the apologies were pre-written, and the promises to "do better" were indistinguishable from the promises made the last five times. Meanwhile, in the real country outside the hearing room, ordinary people keep noticing that their posts underperform when they say things the platforms do not like.

The First Amendment does not stop existing because a moderation team in a glass tower finds a viewpoint inconvenient. It is not a technicality. It is the thing the whole project is supposed to rest on. Every time one of these companies decides unilaterally what a citizen is allowed to hear, the country loses a little more of the trust that holds it together.

The path forward is simple in outline and hard in practice: transparency about how these systems actually rank and remove speech, consequences for companies that enforce their rules against one side and not the other, and a Congress willing to stop posing for the cameras and start writing the law. The voters have been patient long enough.
