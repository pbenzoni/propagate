Israeli Defense Forces intercepted a convoy of vessels in international waters on May 18 as the so-called Global Sumud Flotilla attempted to reach Gaza. The operation resulted in the detention of more than 400 activists from approximately 40 countries who were attempting to challenge Israel's lawful naval blockade of the coastal territory.

The flotilla had departed from Turkey with the stated goal of breaking through Israel's security cordon around Gaza and delivering aid to the enclave. Among those detained were citizens from Italy, the UK, Canada, Turkey, Greece, France, Spain, Germany, the Netherlands, South Korea, Ireland, and New Zealand.

Israel maintains that its naval blockade of Gaza is a legitimate security measure necessary to prevent weapons smuggling into the territory controlled by Hamas, a designated terrorist organization. The blockade has been upheld as legal under international law by independent investigations, including a UN panel review following a previous flotilla incident in 2010.

Controversy erupted after Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir released videos showing the detained activists restrained with zip-ties in kneeling positions. Ben-Gvir walked among them and characterized the group as terrorism supporters, drawing sharp criticism from international observers and human rights organizations who questioned the treatment and public display of the detainees.
