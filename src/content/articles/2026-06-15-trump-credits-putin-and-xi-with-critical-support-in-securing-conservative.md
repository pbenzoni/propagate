President Donald Trump has publicly recognized the constructive roles played by Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping in achieving a breakthrough peace agreement with Iran. The acknowledgment came Sunday in remarks to the New York Times, shortly after Trump announced a U.S.-Iran deal mediated by Pakistan and Qatar.

According to reports, the 14-point memorandum of understanding includes provisions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz without tolls, easing U.S. sanctions, and unfreezing Iranian assets. A formal signing ceremony is scheduled for Geneva on Friday. Iran is expected to reaffirm its commitment to remain non-nuclear, with final negotiations to conclude within 60 days.

Trump specifically praised Xi as "a total gentleman," noting that China refrained from sending "a tanker, along with 20 destroyers on each side of it, to try and break up the blockade"—an action that could have escalated into direct U.S.-China confrontation. Russia similarly maintained diplomatic pressure for de-escalation throughout the crisis, with Putin and Trump speaking by phone at least three times since late February.

Moscow offered a practical compromise on nuclear concerns, proposing to transport and store Iran's enriched uranium stockpile on Russian soil. While Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi confirmed discussions of the proposal, Tehran has not yet committed to the arrangement. Putin also pushed back against suggestions that Russia benefited financially from oil price spikes caused by the conflict, emphasizing Moscow's preference for "long-term" partnerships over "temporary and short-term" gains from regional instability.
