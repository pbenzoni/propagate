Iran has once again restricted passage through the Strait of Hormuz, the strategic waterway through which nearly one-fifth of the world's oil supply flows, after President Trump made clear that American pressure on the regime will not ease until a comprehensive peace deal is reached.

The Iranian military initially opened the strait for commercial vessels during a ten-day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon, briefly sending oil prices lower. But when Trump announced the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports would continue in full force until Tehran meets American demands—including handing over its enriched uranium stockpile—Iranian authorities reversed course and shut down free passage again.

Iranian officials claim the U.S. is engaging in "piracy," though American demands center on dismantling Tehran's nuclear program and ending its destabilizing activities across the Middle East. While Trump stated Iran had "agreed to everything," Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei insisted the regime would never surrender its uranium stockpile, calling it "as sacred as Iranian soil."

The UK Maritime Trade Operations reported that Iranian gunboats fired on a tanker in the strait, though no injuries were reported. Egypt and Pakistan are reportedly working to broker a final agreement between Washington and Tehran. The standoff underscores the Trump administration's commitment to maximum pressure on the Iranian regime until it abandons its nuclear ambitions and agrees to verifiable terms that protect American interests and regional stability.
