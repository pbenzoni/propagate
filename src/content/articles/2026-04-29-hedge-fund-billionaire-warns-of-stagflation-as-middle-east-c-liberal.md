The U.S. economy is showing troubling signs of stagflation—a combination of stagnant growth and rising prices—according to billionaire investor Ray Dalio, who previously predicted the 2008 financial crisis. Speaking Monday, Dalio pointed to the ongoing conflict with Iran as a key factor disrupting global energy markets and contributing to economic uncertainty.

Inflation reached 3.3% in March, well above the Federal Reserve's 2% target, while economic growth slowed dramatically to just 0.5% in the fourth quarter of 2025, down from 4.4% the previous quarter. The situation echoes—though doesn't fully mirror—the 1970s stagflation crisis, when an OPEC oil embargo triggered a painful combination of high prices and economic malaise that required years of aggressive interest rate hikes to resolve.

However, Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell has pushed back on the stagflation characterization, noting that while the economy faces "a very difficult situation," current unemployment at 4.3% remains far below the double-digit levels that defined the 1970s crisis. The debate highlights concerns about how military conflicts in energy-rich regions can destabilize economies and disproportionately impact working families through higher prices at the gas pump and grocery store.

Dalio also warned of broader geopolitical shifts, including questions about whether the U.S. has the sustained commitment for prolonged military engagement and how conflicts affect critical shipping routes like the Strait of Hormuz. He characterized the current moment as part of a larger breakdown in international cooperation, with the world moving away from rules-based governance—a shift that could have profound implications for global stability and American economic security.
