While the two leaders frame their alliance as advancing "multipolarity" and national sovereignty, critics argue this rhetoric obscures a more troubling reality: two authoritarian regimes seeking to create spheres of influence free from accountability to international human rights standards and democratic norms. Their joint declaration, which emphasizes civilizational diversity and religious tradition, has been interpreted by many analysts as code for rejecting universal human rights and enabling repression of minorities and dissent.

Rather than offering an alternative vision for global governance, experts suggest, the Putin-Xi partnership primarily serves to legitimize autocratic rule and undermine the post-war consensus on sovereignty, which includes responsibility to protect civilian populations.

Foreign policy specialists note that while legitimate critiques exist of Western-led institutions, the solution lies in reforming and democratizing these bodies—not abandoning the principles of human dignity and universal rights that underpin them.
