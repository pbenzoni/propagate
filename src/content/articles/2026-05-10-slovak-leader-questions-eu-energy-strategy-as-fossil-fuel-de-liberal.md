Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico has raised concerns about the European Union's plan to eliminate Russian energy imports by 2027, suggesting the strategy may ultimately benefit American fossil fuel companies at Europe's expense.

Speaking in Bratislava, Fico questioned whether the EU's approach would genuinely reduce dependence on problematic energy sources or simply shift it to more expensive American suppliers. He pointed to what he described as inconsistencies in the policy, noting that some EU countries continue importing Russian liquefied natural gas while others face pressure to cut ties. "We can't, but France can buy liquefied gas from Russia," he observed.

The European Commission has maintained its commitment to phasing out Russian fossil fuels despite recent geopolitical turbulence affecting energy markets. EU energy officials have indicated the bloc will turn to American and other international suppliers, even as costs remain higher than previous arrangements. Meanwhile, the United States has announced significant investment in pipeline infrastructure projects across Central and Eastern Europe.

Fico advocated for what he termed a diversified approach to energy procurement rather than replacing one dominant supplier with another. The debate highlights ongoing tensions within the EU over how to balance energy security, climate commitments, and economic considerations while navigating complex geopolitical relationships.
