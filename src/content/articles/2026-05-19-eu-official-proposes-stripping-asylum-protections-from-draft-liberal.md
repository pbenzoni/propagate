A senior EU official has suggested removing temporary protection status from Ukrainian men of fighting age who have sought refuge in Europe, raising concerns among human rights advocates about the fate of those fleeing what observers describe as an increasingly aggressive conscription system.

Ylva Johansson, the EU's special envoy for Ukrainians, told German state media that it "does not seem quite logical" for the bloc to provide sanctuary to men whom Ukraine's government bars from leaving. Approximately a quarter of the 4.3 million Ukrainians under temporary protection in the EU are men aged 18-64, potentially affecting up to a million people. Johansson characterized the current policy as sending a "controversial signal" to Kyiv, particularly given European military support for Ukraine.

The proposal comes amid troubling reports about Ukraine's mobilization efforts. Hundreds of videos circulating online appear to show military recruitment officers forcibly detaining men from public spaces, while tens of thousands have fled across borders—including over 30,000 into Romania alone since 2022—to escape conscription. Human rights observers have documented what they describe as an increasingly coercive system, with mass desertions reflecting desperation among those facing deployment.

While European nations have maintained strong support for Ukraine in its conflict with Russia, refugee advocates warn that compelling men to return could place them at risk of forced military service. The debate highlights tensions between geopolitical alliances and traditional asylum principles that protect individuals from compulsory military duty. EU member states have yet to reach consensus on the proposal, with the policy's implications for both humanitarian protections and regional security remaining subjects of intense discussion.
