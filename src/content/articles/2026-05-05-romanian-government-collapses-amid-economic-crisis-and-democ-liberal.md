Romania's governing coalition fell apart on Tuesday when lawmakers voted 281-4 to remove Prime Minister Ilie Bolojan through a no-confidence motion. The collapse comes after months of mounting tension over harsh austerity policies imposed as Romania grapples with the European Union's highest budget deficit.

The motion was brought jointly by the Social Democratic Party (PSD), which left the coalition last month, and the right-wing Alliance for the Union of Romanians (AUR). Critics argue that Bolojan's ten-month administration failed to deliver tangible improvements for ordinary citizens despite implementing painful cuts. AUR leader George Simion called for snap elections, though observers consider early voting unlikely before 2028.

The current government was formed in June 2025 following a controversial election rerun ordered by President Nicusor Dan. That rerun came after Romania's Constitutional Court annulled the December 2024 election, which had been won by independent candidate Calin Georgescu. The court cited allegations of Russian interference on TikTok, though no evidence was provided and TikTok itself testified it saw no coordinated Russian operation.

Georgescu, who ran on an anti-NATO platform emphasizing Romanian sovereignty, was subsequently detained and barred from the 2025 rerun on charges later reduced to promoting "far-right propaganda." The election annulment drew international criticism, including from a U.S. House committee report that described it as among the EU's "most aggressive censorship steps." Even U.S. Vice President J.D. Vance called the blocking of Georgescu "ugly" and accused Europe of democratic backsliding.

President Dan now faces the challenge of forming a new government while Romania confronts an August deadline to complete reforms necessary to unlock approximately €11 billion in EU funding. The country also faces potential credit rating downgrades as it navigates both economic pressures and ongoing questions about democratic processes.
