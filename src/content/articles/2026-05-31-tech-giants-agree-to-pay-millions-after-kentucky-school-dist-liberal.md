Major social media companies have agreed to pay millions of dollars to settle a lawsuit brought by a Kentucky school district that accused their platforms of contributing to a youth mental health crisis, according to Reuters.

The Breathitt County School District alleged that Meta Platforms, Snap Inc., ByteDance, and Alphabet deliberately designed features—such as infinite scrolling and algorithmic feeds—to be addictive to young users, leading to increased rates of anxiety, depression, and self-harm among students. Meta agreed to pay $9 million, while Snap and ByteDance each agreed to pay $8 million. Alphabet settled for slightly more than $2 million. The companies did not admit wrongdoing as part of the settlement.

The Kentucky case is considered a bellwether in a much larger wave of litigation against social media platforms. Approximately 1,200 school districts nationwide have filed similar lawsuits, and thousands of additional cases brought by individuals, municipalities, and states are pending in courts across the country. Many of these cases have been consolidated in federal court in California.

In March, a California jury ordered Meta to pay $4.2 million and Google $1.8 million in a lawsuit brought by a young woman who said she developed an addiction to Instagram and YouTube as a child. The verdicts and settlements reflect growing concern among educators, parents, and public health experts about the impact of social media on adolescent well-being, and increasing pressure on tech companies to implement stronger safeguards for young users.
