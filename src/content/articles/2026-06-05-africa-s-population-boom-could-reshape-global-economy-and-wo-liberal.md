By 2050, one in four people worldwide will be African, according to Tanzanian President Samia Suluhu Hassan, who spoke at an international economic forum last week. The demographic shift represents a major transformation in global population distribution, with profound implications for labor markets, economic development, and international relations.

UN projections estimate the global population will reach 9.66 billion by mid-century, with Africa accounting for approximately 2.5 billion people. "Africa will be the only continent still adding workers to the global labor force on a large scale," Hassan noted, adding that the continent will host nine of the world's 20 fastest-growing economies.

This growth stands in stark contrast to trends elsewhere. Europe's population is projected to decline from around 744 million to 703 million during the same period, driven by fertility rates averaging just 1.4 births per woman—well below the 2.1 replacement level. Similar demographic challenges face Russia and other developed nations with aging populations.

Hassan also emphasized the potential of the African Continental Free Trade Area, signed in 2018, which aims to create a continent-wide free trade zone across all 55 African Union member states. Once fully implemented, it would become the world's largest market by population, potentially reshaping global trade patterns and economic partnerships in the coming decades.
