Bulgarians head to the polls Sunday in the country's eighth election in just five years, a remarkable streak of political instability that reflects deep public frustration with corruption and governance failures. The frontrunner is former president Rumen Radev, whose newly formed Progressive Bulgaria coalition is leading in polls with a platform centered on dismantling what he calls the "mafia-oligarchic" power structures that have dominated the country since the fall of communism.

Radev's chief opponent is Boyko Borissov, a former communist official whose GERB party has controlled Bulgaria for 15 of the past 20 years. Borissov resigned as prime minister in 2021 amid mounting corruption scandals, and his tenure has been marked by allegations of ties to organized crime, intimidation of journalists, and embezzlement of EU funds. In 2020, *Politico* described Bulgaria under Borissov as "the EU's mafia state."

The election also centers on foreign policy, particularly regarding the war in Ukraine. Radev has been a vocal critic of the EU's approach, opposing Bulgaria's self-imposed embargo on Russian energy and arguing there is "no military solution" to the conflict. He has advocated for diplomatic engagement rather than continued arms shipments, a position that resonates with Bulgarians facing a rising cost of living.

Polls show Progressive Bulgaria leading GERB by margins ranging from five to twenty points, though coalition-building will likely be necessary to form a government. Voter turnout has declined sharply amid election fatigue, with recent contests drawing only 33-38% of eligible voters compared to the typical 45%. The outcome could reshape Bulgaria's relationship with both the EU and its approach to the Ukraine conflict.
