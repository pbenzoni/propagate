Despite targeted military strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities last year, intelligence assessments indicate Tehran's timeline for potentially developing a nuclear weapon remains essentially unchanged at approximately one year, according to a Reuters report citing multiple intelligence sources.

The strikes, which hit key facilities at Natanz, Fordow, and Isfahan in June, were intended to set back Iran's nuclear program. However, the core challenge remains: Iran's stockpile of highly enriched uranium is believed to be stored in deeply buried underground facilities that conventional munitions cannot reach. "Iran still possesses all of its nuclear material, as far as we know," former senior US intelligence analyst Eric Brewer told Reuters. "That material is probably located in deeply buried underground sites where US munitions can't penetrate."

The International Atomic Energy Agency reports being unable to verify the location of approximately 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60% purity—material that, if further enriched, could theoretically produce ten nuclear devices. About half is believed stored in underground tunnels at the Isfahan Nuclear Research Center.

Iran has consistently maintained it is not pursuing nuclear weapons, and IAEA chief Rafael Grossi stated last year that "we do not have any indication that there is a systemic program in Iran to manufacture a nuclear weapon." Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has been warning about Iranian nuclear ambitions since at least 1992, famously displaying a diagram at the 2012 UN General Assembly showing enrichment thresholds for weapons production.
