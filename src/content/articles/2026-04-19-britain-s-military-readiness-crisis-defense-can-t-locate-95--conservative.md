The United Kingdom's military preparedness has hit a stunning low point, with defense officials admitting they cannot locate approximately 95,000 reservists who would be essential in a national emergency. George Robertson, former NATO secretary general and lead adviser for Britain's Strategic Defense Review, revealed the alarming gap in the nation's defense capabilities during a lecture this week.

The admission comes as the UK government pushes an ambitious plan to achieve "warfighting readiness" by 2035, including raising defense spending to 2.5% of GDP by 2027. New legislation would expand call-up criteria and raise the recall age from 55 to 65. Yet Robertson bluntly stated that the Ministry of Defense "doesn't even know where most of them are," leaving officials scrambling to "round up those who are available and fit and willing."

The readiness crisis is compounded by Britain's already diminished standing forces—the British Army now fields fewer than 70,000 fully trained personnel ready for frontline deployment, its smallest force in over two centuries. The active reserve stands at just 32,000. The missing strategic reserve consists largely of former service members with whom contact is typically maintained only for six years after departure, leaving an entire cohort essentially unreachable.

Robertson criticized what he called Prime Minister Keir Starmer's "corrosive complacency" on defense matters, arguing the government has put the country "in peril" through funding delays and inadequate preparedness. The warning underscores broader concerns about Western military readiness at a time when defense spending debates dominate European capitals. Russia has dismissed UK claims of potential Russian aggression as justifications for increased military budgets rather than genuine security concerns.
