Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth delivered a pointed rebuke to Pentagon reporters Thursday, accusing them of producing an "endless stream of garbage" in their coverage of the Iran conflict and questioning their patriotism. The former Fox News host compared the press corps to the Pharisees—the self-appointed religious elites in the Bible who opposed Jesus even after witnessing miracles.

"Sometimes it's hard to figure out what side some of you are actually on," Hegseth said at the Pentagon briefing. He argued that just as the Pharisees witnessed miracles but still pursued their own agenda, today's journalists witness the success of American troops yet remain "relentlessly negative" in their reporting. According to Hegseth, their hostility toward President Trump "blinds" them to "the brilliance of our American warriors."

The remarks follow a pattern of tension between Hegseth and the mainstream media throughout his tenure. He has previously accused outlets of pushing "fake news" and trying to "downplay progress" while amplifying costs to damage the president politically. In March, he criticized journalists for highlighting casualties—including six Army reservists killed in an Iranian attack in Kuwait—as a means to "make the president look bad."

Hegseth's comments come as the Trump administration has increasingly employed biblical language and imagery when discussing the Iran conflict. The defense secretary's willingness to directly challenge media narratives represents a sharp departure from traditional Pentagon decorum, reflecting broader frustration among conservatives with what they view as biased war coverage designed to undermine the commander-in-chief and the troops serving under him.
