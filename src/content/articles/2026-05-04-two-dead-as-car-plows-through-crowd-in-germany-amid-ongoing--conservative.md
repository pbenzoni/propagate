At least two people were killed and several others injured Monday when a car drove into a crowd on a busy shopping street in Leipzig, Germany. Local authorities confirmed the driver has been detained, though the motive remains under investigation.

The incident occurred on Grimmaische Strasse, a central pedestrian zone linking the city's market square with major landmarks. Radio Leipzig reported witnessing a damaged Volkswagen SUV speeding through the area, with witnesses describing a person on top of the vehicle. Leipzig Mayor Burkhard Jung confirmed the preliminary death toll as police secured the surrounding area.

German authorities have not yet released the driver's identity or determined whether the incident was deliberate or accidental. The attack pattern echoes previous vehicular incidents that have shaken Germany in recent years. In December 2024, a Saudi psychiatrist drove into a Magdeburg Christmas market, killing five and injuring over 200. The year before, an Afghan national attacked a Munich rally, leaving dozens injured and killing a mother and her two-year-old daughter. The 2016 Berlin Christmas market attack by a rejected Tunisian asylum seeker claimed 12 lives.

These incidents have intensified national debate over border security and migration policy.
