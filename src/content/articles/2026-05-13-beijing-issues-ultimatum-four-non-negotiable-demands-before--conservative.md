As President Donald Trump prepares to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping, Beijing has laid out four "red lines" it claims cannot be crossed in U.S.-China relations. The demands touch on Taiwan, so-called human rights issues, China's political system, and what the regime calls its "development right."

China continues to insist that America must not interfere in what it considers internal matters, including supporting Taiwan's democratic government, which Beijing views as a breakaway province. The communist regime has long demanded the U.S. stay silent on its domestic governance while simultaneously expanding its own global influence.

The meeting comes amid heightened tensions over China's relationship with Iran during the ongoing conflict. The U.S. Treasury recently sanctioned a Chinese refinery for allegedly purchasing Iranian oil in violation of American restrictions. Beijing responded defiantly, ordering its private refineries to ignore U.S. sanctions—restrictions Chinese officials called illegal interference in their economic sovereignty.

Trump told reporters Tuesday that Xi has been "relatively good" on the Iran situation and described the Chinese leader as "a friend." The President said he plans to have "a long talk" about the Iran conflict during their upcoming meeting. China has denied providing military assistance to Iran but condemned American sanctions on Chinese satellite companies accused of providing intelligence data to Tehran.

A Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesman said Beijing is ready to work with Washington to "manage differences in the spirit of equality, respect and mutual benefit"—though on China's terms, according to the four demands the regime laid out ahead of the summit.
