A hantavirus outbreak aboard the Dutch-flagged cruise ship MV Hondius has resulted in three deaths and multiple hospitalizations, according to the World Health Organization. The vessel, which departed from Ushuaia, Argentina on April 1, was finally cleared to dock in the Canary Islands on Wednesday after evacuating critically ill passengers at Cape Verde.

Seven people contracted the virus during the voyage, with one person remaining in critical condition and several others showing symptoms. Health officials are now tracking suspected cases in France, Germany, and Switzerland among passengers who disembarked at earlier ports. The Andes strain of hantavirus—typically found in South America—carries a 40% fatality rate and spreads through contact with rodent droppings, urine, or saliva.

Argentinian officials believe a Dutch couple contracted the virus while birdwatching at a landfill site before boarding, though investigations continue. Concerningly, the Andes strain is the only hantavirus known to transmit between humans through prolonged close contact, raising questions about cruise ship safety protocols and environmental health standards at tourist destinations.

The outbreak underscores broader concerns about zoonotic disease transmission and the vulnerability of densely populated spaces like cruise ships to infectious disease spread. With 149 passengers and crew aboard—including citizens from multiple countries—the incident highlights the need for enhanced public health surveillance, improved sanitation standards near tourist areas, and better preparedness for emerging infectious diseases in an interconnected world.
