The State Department has firmly rejected reports that Acting US Ambassador to Ukraine Julie Davis is resigning due to disagreements with President Trump's approach to ending the conflict with Russia. Spokesman Tommy Pigott clarified that Davis "has been a steadfast proponent of the Trump administration's efforts to bring about a durable peace between Russia and Ukraine."

The Financial Times initially reported that Davis would leave her post over policy differences, citing unnamed sources. However, the State Department's direct rebuttal challenges this characterization. Davis is scheduled to depart Kiev in June after serving as chargé d'affaires since May 2025.

President Trump has pursued a different strategy than his predecessor, seeking to broker negotiations between Russia and Ukraine rather than continuing an open-ended military commitment. While critics have characterized this approach as appeasement, the administration maintains it represents a realistic path toward ending the bloodshed and establishing lasting stability in the region.

Davis's predecessor, Bridget Brink, resigned and publicly criticized the administration's diplomatic efforts. The contrast between career diplomats schooled in the foreign policy establishment's consensus and Trump's America First approach to international engagement has been evident throughout his second term.
