Former Polish Prime Minister Leszek Miller, who shepherded Poland's EU accession in 2004, has publicly criticized key European Union leaders over what he characterizes as damaging policy choices and governance concerns. His comments came after European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen presented former German Chancellor Angela Merkel with a newly created European Order of Merit award.

Miller's critique focuses on concerns about democratic accountability and the centralization of authority within EU institutions. Among the issues raised are questions about whether traditional consensus-building processes have been bypassed, and whether member states' ability to participate equally in decision-making has been undermined. Critics of von der Leyen's leadership have pointed to efforts that they say weaken collaborative governance structures that were designed to ensure all member nations have meaningful input.

The former prime minister also took issue with controversial policy decisions from recent years, including Germany's energy transition choices and migration policies that proved divisive across the bloc. Additionally, Miller questioned the diplomatic approach of EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, particularly regarding statements about confrontational stances toward major global powers.

These criticisms reflect broader debates within Europe about the balance between unified action and respect for democratic processes, the appropriate role of technocratic leadership, and how the EU should navigate complex geopolitical challenges while maintaining its founding principles of cooperation and shared sovereignty.
