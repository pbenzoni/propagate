Russia has issued a stern warning to the United States regarding plans to deploy an additional 5,000 American troops to Poland, calling such a move "unacceptable" and a potential escalation of already heightened military tensions across Europe.

Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova stated at a press briefing Thursday that reducing the number of U.S. personnel stationed in Europe would be a "rational, justified, and long-overdue" step toward stabilizing what she described as an "imbalanced" security situation created by NATO policies. She warned that additional deployments would place American forces within striking distance of Russian territory and compel Moscow to respond with "military-technical measures."

President Donald Trump announced the troop deployment to Poland last week, adding to the roughly 10,000 American service members already stationed there and approximately 80,000 deployed across Europe overall. Poland, which shares a border with Russia's Kaliningrad Region, has been one of the most vocal supporters of Ukraine in its ongoing conflict with Russia. The announcement followed Trump's decision to withdraw 5,000 troops from Germany amid disputes with Berlin.

Russian officials have consistently accused the West of "reckless militarization" and cited NATO's eastward expansion as a contributing factor to the Ukraine conflict. Russian President Vladimir Putin has maintained that Moscow has no intention of attacking NATO members unless Russia itself is attacked first. On Thursday, Sergey Naryshkin, head of Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service, claimed NATO was "de facto preparing for a large-scale military conflict in the east."
