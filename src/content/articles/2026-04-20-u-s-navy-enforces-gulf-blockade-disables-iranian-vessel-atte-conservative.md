The U.S. Central Command released video footage showing the USS Spruance, a guided-missile destroyer, firing on an Iranian-flagged cargo vessel in the Gulf of Oman. The M/V Touska was attempting to breach a U.S.-imposed naval blockade and reach the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas through the Strait of Hormuz when American forces intercepted it.

According to CENTCOM, the Touska's crew ignored repeated warnings over a six-hour period before the destroyer took action. After directing the vessel to evacuate its engine room, the Spruance disabled the ship's propulsion by firing several rounds from its 5-inch Mk 45 gun into the engine compartment. Marines from the 31st Marine Expeditionary Unit subsequently boarded the vessel.

Iranian officials criticized the blockade as illegal under international law, claiming it violates terms of a two-week ceasefire set to expire Wednesday. The Iranian military has pledged retaliation for the vessel seizure. Iran's Tasnim news agency later reported that Iranian forces launched a drone at U.S. ships, though the United States has not confirmed any attack on its vessels.

The incident highlights ongoing tensions in the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz, through which a significant portion of the world's oil supply passes. American naval forces maintain a persistent presence in the region to protect freedom of navigation and enforce maritime security operations.
