A new report from the Council of Europe's human rights commissioner has found that Germany disproportionately restricted the rights of pro-Palestinian protesters, raising concerns about freedom of expression and assembly. The report, compiled by Commissioner Michael O'Flaherty and published Wednesday, called on the German government to ensure that efforts to combat hate speech "fully respects the rights to freedom of peaceful assembly and expression for all members of society."

According to the report, German authorities misused anti-Semitism claims to ban pro-Palestinian slogans and Palestinian flags at some rallies, and to prohibit other demonstrations outright. The report also documented instances of excessive force used by police against anti-Israel protesters, stating that "freedom of expression has been restricted disproportionately, regarding debates on Palestinian rights or legitimate criticism of the Israeli government."

Germany has endorsed the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance's definition of anti-Semitism since 2017, a framework that includes "claiming that the existence of a State of Israel is a racist endeavor" and "drawing comparisons of contemporary Israeli policy to that of the Nazis" as examples of anti-Semitic speech. Critics have long argued this definition conflates legitimate criticism of Israeli government policy with anti-Semitism.

The report also noted that Germany has failed to adequately address rises in both anti-Semitism and anti-Muslim hatred, and warned against blaming the Muslim community for anti-Semitism. The German government has disputed this characterization, pointing to survey data showing higher prevalence of anti-Semitic attitudes among Muslim respondents. Civil liberties advocates have raised concerns that restrictions on pro-Palestinian speech set a troubling precedent for political expression more broadly.
