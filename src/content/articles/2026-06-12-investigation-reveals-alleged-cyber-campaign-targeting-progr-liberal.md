French government investigators have uncovered what they describe as a coordinated digital influence campaign targeting progressive politicians who support Palestinian rights in elections across France, the United States, Scotland, and Africa, according to officials in Paris.

The allegations center on BlackCore, an Israeli cyber firm that French authorities believe conducted online smear campaigns against candidates from France's left-wing France Unbowed party during March local elections in Marseille, Toulouse, and Roubaix. France's disinformation detection agency, Viginum, has now linked similar operations to elections in New York City, Scotland, Angola, and Togo.

According to Viginum chief Marc-Antoine Brillant, the suspected operations extended to Scotland's First Minister John Swinney, who has called Israel's Gaza campaign a "man-made humanitarian catastrophe," and New York's 2025 mayoral race, won by democratic socialist Zohran Mamdani—a longtime Palestine supporter who became the city's first Muslim mayor. BlackCore previously described itself as "an elite influence, cyber, and technology company built for the modern era of information warfare" before taking its website and LinkedIn page offline following media scrutiny.

While French investigators have not definitively identified who sponsored the alleged operations, the revelations come amid deteriorating France-Israel relations. Paris has condemned Israeli strikes in Lebanon that have caused civilian casualties and has barred Israel's far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir from entering France after he posted a video mocking aid workers, including French citizens, who were attempting to deliver humanitarian assistance to Gaza.
