The United States and World Cup organizers have revoked Iran's allocation of fan tickets just days before the tournament begins, according to the Football Federation of the Islamic Republic of Iran. The decision has left thousands of Iranian supporters who had already made travel arrangements unable to attend their national team's matches.

The World Cup kicks off Thursday, with Iran scheduled to face New Zealand and Belgium in Los Angeles on June 15 and 21, before playing Egypt in Seattle on June 26. Participating federations typically receive 8% of tickets for each match to distribute to their supporters, but Iran's federation says it cannot provide "even a single ticket" following the unexpected withdrawal.

The Football Federation of the Islamic Republic of Iran described the move as "contrary to the spirit governing international competitions and the principle of equality among participating countries." The organization called on FIFA and tournament organizers to "uphold the principles of neutrality, fairness, and established regulations."

The ticket dispute is the latest in a series of complications surrounding Iran's World Cup preparations. Tehran has reported that visa issues prevented approximately 15 administrative and management staff from entering the United States. Iran's team was also forced to relocate its training camp from Tucson, Arizona, to Tijuana, Mexico, just across the border. While Iranian players received U.S. visas last week, several support staff members were still denied entry as of that time.
