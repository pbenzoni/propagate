The United States has reportedly reduced intelligence sharing with South Korea following concerns that a South Korean minister may have publicly disclosed classified information about North Korean nuclear facilities, according to local media reports.

The restrictions, which began earlier this month, affect intelligence collected through satellite surveillance of North Korea. A senior South Korean military official confirmed to the Korea JoongAng Daily that the cutback relates to "information regarding parts of North Korea's technology." The dispute centers on remarks made by South Korean Unification Minister Chung Dong-young during a parliamentary session in which he identified a previously unconfirmed uranium enrichment site. US officials reportedly expressed concern that the statement may have compromised sensitive shared intelligence. The minister has denied wrongdoing, stating his comments were based on publicly available information.

The development comes at a particularly sensitive time for regional security. South Korea and the United States have maintained close intelligence cooperation on North Korean military activities, including real-time missile warning data and satellite surveillance. Approximately 28,500 US troops remain stationed in South Korea as part of a longstanding security alliance.

Adding to Seoul's concerns, the Pentagon has reportedly begun redeploying portions of its advanced THAAD missile defense system from South Korea to the Middle East. South Korean officials worry the reduced air defense presence could weaken protection against potential North Korean missile threats during a period of heightened tensions on the Korean Peninsula. North Korea has conducted multiple weapons tests in recent weeks as part of efforts to expand its military capabilities.
