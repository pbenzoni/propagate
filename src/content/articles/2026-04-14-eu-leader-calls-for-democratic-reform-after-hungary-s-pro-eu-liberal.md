European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has renewed calls for democratic reform of EU decision-making processes, arguing that majority voting in foreign policy matters would prevent individual member states from blocking the collective will of the union.

The proposal comes as Hungary transitions to new leadership following recent elections that saw the pro-European Tisza party defeat the incumbent government. The previous administration had repeatedly used veto power to obstruct EU initiatives on climate policy, refugee protection, LGBTQ rights, and support for Ukraine's defense against Russian aggression.

Von der Leyen emphasized that "moving to qualified majority voting in foreign policy is an important way to avoid systemic blockages, as we have seen in the past." The reform would align foreign policy decision-making with other EU areas that already operate on majority-vote principles, potentially enabling more responsive action on international crises and human rights issues.

The Commission has been withholding approximately €35 billion in funds previously earmarked for Hungary due to concerns over democratic backsliding, judicial independence, and LGBTQ rights. Brussels has indicated readiness to engage constructively with Hungary's new government, though expectations remain that Budapest will align more closely with European values on issues ranging from refugee policy to support for Ukraine's sovereignty.

The incoming Hungarian leadership has signaled some continued differences with Brussels on energy policy and the timeline for Ukrainian EU membership, while indicating openness to unblocking previously stalled aid packages for Kyiv.
