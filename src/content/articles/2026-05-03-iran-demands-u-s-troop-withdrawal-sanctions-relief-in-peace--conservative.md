Iran has submitted what it calls a comprehensive peace proposal through Pakistani mediators, but the terms appear designed to extract maximum concessions from the United States while offering little in return. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi announced Saturday that "the ball is in the US court," framing Tehran's demands as reasonable despite their sweeping scope.

The 14-point Iranian proposal reportedly includes demands for complete U.S. troop withdrawal from the region, lifting of all economic sanctions, security guarantees for the Islamic Republic, and financial compensation from Washington. Iran is also seeking a new framework for control of the vital Strait of Hormuz shipping lane and an end to what it terms the "imposed war" on all fronts, including in Lebanon where Iranian-backed forces have been active.

President Trump responded Saturday on Truth Social, stating he would review the proposal but could not "imagine that it would be acceptable." The president has previously made clear that any deal must address Iran's nuclear ambitions, telling journalists he was prepared to "blast the hell out of them and finish them forever" if diplomatic efforts fail. Washington's position requires Iran to completely abandon its nuclear program and surrender enriched uranium stockpiles—conditions Tehran has flatly rejected while insisting its nuclear program serves only civilian purposes.

Negotiations remain stalled nearly a month after an initial ceasefire, with both nations imposing blockades on the Strait of Hormuz and Israel resuming operations in Lebanon. Oil prices have surged past $120 per barrel this week for the first time since 2022, reflecting global concerns about stability in the region. The stark gap between American demands for verifiable nuclear disarmament and Iranian insistence on sanctions relief and regional military withdrawal suggests prospects for agreement remain distant.
