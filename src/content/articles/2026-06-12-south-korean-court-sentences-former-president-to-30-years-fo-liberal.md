A Seoul court has sentenced former South Korean President Yoon Suk Yeol to 30 years in prison after finding he deliberately escalated tensions with North Korea to justify an attempted authoritarian takeover. According to South Korea's Yonhap news agency, judges ruled that Yoon ordered unmanned aerial vehicle incursions into North Korean territory specifically to inflame border tensions and create a pretext for his December 2024 declaration of martial law.

The court found that Yoon abused his presidential powers and "benefited the enemy" through his actions, which also damaged South Korea's military interests by exposing capabilities and forcing Pyongyang into a stronger defensive posture. The martial law declaration cited legislative gridlock and alleged pro-Pyongyang influences within South Korea's democratic institutions—claims that the nation's parliament rejected within hours by formally overturning the decree, despite attempts by police and military forces to physically prevent lawmakers from accessing the National Assembly building.

Yoon's former Defense Minister Kim Yong-hyun received a matching 30-year sentence for his role in the scheme, while former Defense Counterintelligence Command chief Yeo In-hyung was sentenced to 15 years. This latest conviction adds to Yoon's mounting legal troubles: he received a life sentence in February for attempting insurrection and seizing power, with an appeals court in April increasing sentences for abuse of authority and obstruction of duty.

The case underscores ongoing accountability challenges in South Korean democracy, where four previous presidents have faced prison sentences after leaving office, though several were later pardoned.
