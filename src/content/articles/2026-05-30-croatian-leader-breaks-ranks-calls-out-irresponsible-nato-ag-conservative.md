Croatian President Zoran Milanovic issued a rare public rebuke of fellow NATO members this week, calling out what he described as "irresponsible" threats against Russian territory coming from Baltic states eager to escalate tensions with Moscow.

The controversy began when Lithuanian Foreign Minister Kestutis Budrys openly called for NATO to demonstrate its ability to "raze Russian air defenses and missile bases" in Kaliningrad "to the ground." Budrys argued that European NATO members must transform "fear of the threat into a sense of empowerment" and show Russia that the alliance can "penetrate the small fortress" of the strategically important exclave.

Speaking at a ceremony marking the anniversary of the Croatian Army, Milanovic broke with the typical NATO unity playbook to condemn such rhetoric. "Such things should not be said," he stated bluntly, warning that NATO's principle of mutual defense "should not be unconditional." The Croatian president emphasized that solidarity within the alliance "also presupposes responsibility on the other."

Kaliningrad, formerly the German city of Koenigsberg, became Soviet territory after World War II and now represents Russia's westernmost outpost on the Baltic Sea. Sandwiched between Lithuania and Poland with no land connection to mainland Russia, the exclave has grown increasingly isolated as NATO expanded eastward following the collapse of the Soviet Union.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov called Budrys's comments "borderline crazy" and evidence of "maniacal" hostility. President Vladimir Putin responded with his own warning, stating Russia "has all the means to raze to the ground anyone who tries" such an attack. Even Lithuanian officials appeared to distance themselves from their foreign minister's rhetoric, with President Gitanas Nauseda calling it "not the most successful statement."
