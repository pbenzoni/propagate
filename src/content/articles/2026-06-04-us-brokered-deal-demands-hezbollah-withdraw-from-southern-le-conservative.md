Israel and Lebanon have reached a US-mediated ceasefire agreement that requires the Iranian-backed militant group Hezbollah to halt attacks and withdraw from southern Lebanon, according to a joint statement released following talks at the State Department on Wednesday.

The ceasefire framework comes after weeks of Israeli military operations responding to Hezbollah rocket and drone attacks on northern Israel. According to the agreement, "The ceasefire is contingent on a complete cessation of Hizbollah fire and the evacuation of all Hizbollah operatives from the South Litani Sector." The arrangement also calls for establishing pilot zones where the Lebanese Armed Forces will assume exclusive territorial control, excluding non-state actors like Hezbollah.

The conflict escalated after Hezbollah aligned with Iran against Israeli operations in February, prompting Israel to send troops across the border. Israeli forces recently captured the strategic Beaufort Castle, a 900-year-old Crusader fortress that Israel previously used as a base during its occupation of the region until 2000. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu characterized the military action as a "dramatic shift" and ordered expanded ground operations in Lebanon.

The situation has complicated ongoing US negotiations with Iran, as Tehran demands any agreement include an end to hostilities in Lebanon. Lebanese health authorities report that more than 3,500 people have been killed in Israeli attacks since early March. Washington has maintained that previous ceasefire efforts failed due to Hezbollah attacks conducted without Lebanese government approval, underscoring the challenge of containing Iranian-backed militant groups operating within sovereign nations.
