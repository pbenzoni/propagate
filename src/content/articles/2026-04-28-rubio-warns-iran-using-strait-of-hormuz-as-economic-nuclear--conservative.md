Secretary of State Marco Rubio issued a stark warning this week that Iran is wielding control over the Strait of Hormuz like an "economic nuclear weapon," rejecting Tehran's latest proposal to reopen the vital waterway on its own terms while postponing negotiations over its nuclear program.

Speaking on Fox News, Rubio made clear the United States will not accept Iranian control over international waters. "If what they mean by opening the straits is, 'Yes, the straits are open, as long as you coordinate with Iran, get our permission, or we'll blow you up, and you pay us' – that's not opening the straits," he said. The Secretary emphasized that legitimizing Iran's control would set a dangerous precedent worldwide, noting "this is not the Suez Canal, this is not the Panama Canal, these are international waters."

Rubio took a hardline stance on Iran's nuclear ambitions, dismissing the notion of moderates within the Islamic Republic's leadership. "They're all hardliners in Iran," he stated, adding that "there's no doubt in my mind that at some point in the future if this radical clerical regime remains in charge in Iran, they will decide they want a nuclear weapon." The nuclear question, Rubio insisted, "is the reason why we're in this in the first place" and remains the core issue that must be confronted.

President Trump has reviewed Iran's proposal but remains unsatisfied, according to multiple sources. The White House appears unwilling to accept terms that would allow Tehran to maintain its enriched uranium stockpile and nuclear infrastructure—core elements of Trump's demand that Iran's nuclear program be dismantled. Iran continues to claim it does not seek nuclear weapons while refusing American demands to surrender its capabilities.
