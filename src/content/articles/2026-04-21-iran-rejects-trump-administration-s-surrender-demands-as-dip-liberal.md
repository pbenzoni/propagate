Iranian officials have rejected what they characterize as coercive negotiation tactics by the Trump administration, warning they will not participate in talks "under the shadow of threats" as a tenuous ceasefire approaches its Wednesday deadline.

Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf accused President Trump of attempting to impose what he called "a table of surrender" rather than engaging in good-faith diplomacy. "Trump, by imposing a siege and violating the ceasefire, seeks to turn this negotiating table—in his own imagination—into a table of surrender or to justify renewed warmongering," Ghalibaf stated on social media. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian similarly criticized the "non-constructive and contradictory" conduct of U.S. officials, saying their approach signals Washington is demanding Iran's capitulation rather than pursuing mutual understanding.

The escalating rhetoric comes after initial talks in Islamabad last weekend failed to produce progress, followed by Trump's imposition of a naval blockade on Iranian ports. On Sunday, Trump issued stark threats, stating that if Iran doesn't accept what he termed a "fair and reasonable deal," the U.S. would "knock out every single power plant, and every single bridge, in Iran."

Reports indicate Vice President J.D. Vance, along with Trump envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, was expected to travel to Islamabad for a second round of negotiations. According to sources, mediators from Pakistan, Egypt, and Turkey have urged both sides to return to the negotiating table before the ceasefire expires, though prospects for a diplomatic breakthrough remain uncertain amid the escalating tensions and maximalist positions on both sides.
