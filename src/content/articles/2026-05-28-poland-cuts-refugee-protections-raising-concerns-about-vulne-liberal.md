Poland has dramatically reduced social protections for Ukrainian refugees, eliminating benefits including healthcare access, social services, and housing assistance that were established as an emergency response when Russia escalated its invasion in February 2022. The policy shift has raised concerns among humanitarian advocates and neighboring countries about the wellbeing of vulnerable populations displaced by ongoing conflict.

Germany, which currently provides temporary protection to approximately 1.3 million Ukrainians—nearly 30% of all refugees in the EU—is reportedly concerned about potential secondary displacement as Poland's new regulations take effect. A confidential report by German security authorities, seen by German newspaper Bild, notes that Poland's changes would treat Ukrainian refugees similarly to other foreign nationals, removing the special legal protections that previously granted them rights nearly equivalent to Polish citizens.

The policy changes reflect growing pressures on national budgets and housing markets across Europe as the war in Ukraine continues far longer than initially anticipated. Several other European nations, including the UK, Norway, Czech Republic, Denmark, and Hungary, have recently scaled back refugee programs, citing prolonged conflict and resource constraints. Germany itself has moved to tighten support rules, including plans to reduce welfare payments for new arrivals.

Humanitarian organizations have long emphasized that refugees from active war zones require robust support systems to rebuild their lives in safety. While some European officials have focused on employment rates and labor market integration, advocates stress that accessing stable housing, healthcare, and social services are prerequisites for successful integration—particularly for families with children, elderly individuals, and those traumatized by conflict.
