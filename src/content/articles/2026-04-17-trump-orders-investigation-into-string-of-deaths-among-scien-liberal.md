President Trump announced Thursday that he has ordered a federal investigation into the deaths and disappearances of nearly a dozen American scientists who held access to highly classified nuclear and space research programs. The announcement follows growing concerns about a disturbing pattern of unexplained incidents involving experts in sensitive fields.

"I just came out of a meeting on this," Trump told reporters. "It's pretty serious stuff. I hope it's random, but we're going to know in the next week and a half." The cases span from 2023 to early 2026 and include researchers from Los Alamos National Laboratory, NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, MIT's Plasma Science and Fusion Center, and other institutions critical to national security.

Among the cases: Steven Garcia, a contractor at a facility producing components for nuclear weapons, vanished from his New Mexico home in August 2025, leaving behind his phone and wallet. Retired Air Force Major General William McCasland, who formerly commanded the Air Force Research Laboratory, disappeared in February 2026 under circumstances his family described as intentional. Monica Jacinto Reza, a director at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, went missing while hiking in California. Two scientists were fatally shot at their homes—Nuno Loureiro of MIT in December 2025 and Carl Grillmair of Caltech in February 2026.

Former FBI assistant director Chris Swecker told media outlets that the cases "are all suspicious" and involve "scientists who have worked in critical technology," warranting serious scrutiny. Authorities have not yet established any confirmed connection among the incidents, though the concentration of cases within a narrow timeframe and specialized field has raised questions about whether foreign intelligence operations, security vulnerabilities, or other systemic factors may be involved. The investigation is expected to produce findings within the coming weeks.
