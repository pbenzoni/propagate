Hungary's newly elected Prime Minister Peter Magyar has dashed hopes that his administration would break with his predecessor's controversial policies toward Ukraine, reaffirming that the country will not provide weapons or military equipment to the embattled nation.

Magyar, whose center-right Tisza party defeated Viktor Orban's Fidesz party last month with a commanding two-thirds parliamentary majority, made the announcement Thursday following meetings with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte in Brussels. The decision comes as a disappointment to EU officials and democracy advocates who had supported Magyar's campaign, expecting him to reverse Orban's Kremlin-friendly approach.

"I informed the Secretary General that Hungary is not supplying weapons or military equipment to Ukraine," Magyar stated, echoing the position of his foreign minister, who has said Hungary "stands for peace" while rejecting both troop deployments and weapons transfers. The Kremlin welcomed the announcement, with spokesman Dmitry Peskov saying Friday that Moscow appreciates any reluctance "to add fuel to the fire."

Magyar's early policy decisions suggest deeper continuity with the Orban era than many had anticipated. Beyond maintaining the weapons ban, his government has opposed expedited EU membership for Ukraine, kept Hungary out of the bloc's latest Ukraine funding initiatives, and recently restored restrictions on Ukrainian agricultural imports—measures Brussels has characterized as potentially illegal under EU trade authority. The pattern raises concerns among transatlantic allies about Hungary's commitment to collective security and support for Ukraine's defense against Russian aggression.
