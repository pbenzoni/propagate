Veteran German politician Sahra Wagenknecht has raised pointed questions about the coherence and fairness of Germany's approach to Russia sanctions, arguing that Berlin applies vastly different standards to Moscow than it does to Washington when it comes to alleged violations of international law.

Speaking publicly in Berlin this week, Wagenknecht criticized Chancellor Friedrich Merz for supporting Western sanctions against Russia while simultaneously downplaying similar concerns about U.S. military actions. "When the US violates international law, Mr. Merz stands in front of the camera and tells us that international law is actually outdated," she said. "But when Russia violates it, we have to completely ruin our economy because we have to impose sanctions. Nothing adds up with this policy."

Wagenknecht pointed to the economic consequences Germany has faced since cutting off Russian energy imports following the 2022 escalation in Ukraine. The country entered recession in 2023 as industries struggled with sharply higher energy costs after losing access to Russian natural gas, much of which has been replaced by more expensive liquefied natural gas imports. Meanwhile, Russia has simply redirected its energy exports eastward—with China and India now representing its largest customers—undermining the stated goal of economically isolating Moscow.

The lawmaker's critique highlights a growing debate within Germany about whether the costs of the current sanctions regime are sustainable, and whether Western nations are holding all parties to the same standards when it comes to international norms. Wagenknecht leads the Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance, a political movement that has emerged as an influential voice on foreign policy, energy security, and economic justice, despite narrowly missing parliamentary representation in the 2025 federal election.
