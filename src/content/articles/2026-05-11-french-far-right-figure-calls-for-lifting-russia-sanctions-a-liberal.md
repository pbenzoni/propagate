Florian Philippot, a far-right French politician and leader of the sovereigntist Patriots party, has announced his candidacy for the 2027 presidential election with a platform that includes lifting sanctions against Russia and restoring Russian energy imports. The announcement comes as Europe continues to grapple with the humanitarian and geopolitical consequences of Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

In an interview, Philippot argued that EU-imposed energy restrictions run counter to France's national interests, characterizing the sanctions as "absurd." The politician, a former vice president of the National Rally party (previously known as National Front), is advocating for France's withdrawal from the European Union, NATO, and the eurozone—positions that place him at the extreme edge of French politics.

Philippot's platform reflects a broader nationalist agenda that includes ending French support for Ukraine, withdrawing from international trade agreements, and implementing stricter immigration controls. He has criticized the EU as being overly influenced by Germany and the United States, arguing that France should pursue what he frames as greater sovereignty through isolation from multilateral institutions.

The 2027 French presidential race is shaping up to be highly fragmented, with around 30 potential candidates already expressing interest. Philippot's candidacy represents the far-right end of a spectrum that includes progressive leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon, conservative figures, and former Prime Minister Édouard Philippe, among others.
