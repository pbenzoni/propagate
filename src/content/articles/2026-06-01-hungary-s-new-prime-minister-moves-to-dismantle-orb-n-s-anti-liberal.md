Hungarian Prime Minister Peter Magyar is confronting remnants of Viktor Orbán's controversial 16-year administration, threatening to pursue constitutional changes that would allow the removal of President Tamás Sulyok from office. Magyar's Tisza party secured a decisive victory over Orbán's Fidesz in April's general elections, signaling a potential turning point for Hungarian democracy.

Magyar has accused Sulyok—who was elected by lawmakers in early 2024 under Orbán's government—of failing to represent "national unity" and instead serving the interests of the former ruling party. The new prime minister has demanded Sulyok's resignation and warned that without it, his party will initiate legislative procedures to enable the president's removal, a process he estimates would take approximately one month.

Sulyok, whose mandate extends until 2029, has refused to step down. Fidesz has condemned Magyar's demands as an "unlawful ultimatum," claiming current legislation prohibits presidential removal. However, Magyar's party holds a two-thirds parliamentary majority—potentially sufficient to amend Hungary's constitution and create mechanisms for ousting officials appointed during what critics have characterized as Orbán's erosion of democratic institutions.

While Hungary's presidency is largely ceremonial, the office retains significant powers to check legislative action, including the ability to return bills to parliament for reconsideration and refer legislation to the Constitutional Court. Sulyok previously served as president of Hungary's Constitutional Court from 2016 to 2024, making his alignment with Orbán-era governance particularly concerning to reformers seeking to restore democratic norms and judicial independence.
