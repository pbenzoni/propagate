Iran has submitted a sweeping 14-point peace proposal that includes a controversial demand for a new mechanism to govern the Strait of Hormuz, the critical waterway through which roughly one-fifth of the world's oil passes. The plan, delivered through Pakistani intermediaries, represents Tehran's counter-offer to previous American proposals.

According to reports from Tehran, the Islamic Republic is seeking guarantees against future military action, the lifting of economic sanctions, withdrawal of U.S. forces from the region, and resolution of all outstanding issues within just 30 days. Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi confirmed the proposal's submission, stating "the ball is in the US court."

President Trump reviewed the offer but expressed strong skepticism about its viability. In a social media post, the President made clear his position: "I can't imagine" the proposal will be acceptable, noting that Iran had not yet "paid a big enough price for what they have done to humanity and the world over the last 47 years." The reference to 47 years points to the 1979 Islamic Revolution that transformed Iran from a Western ally into an adversarial theocratic regime.

The proposal's demand for a new governance structure over the Strait of Hormuz raises immediate concerns about freedom of navigation and international commerce. Any Iranian control or influence over this strategic chokepoint could threaten global energy markets and undermine decades of U.S. commitments to keeping international waters open. Observers note that the "space for compromise" appears to be narrowing as both sides stake out firm positions.
