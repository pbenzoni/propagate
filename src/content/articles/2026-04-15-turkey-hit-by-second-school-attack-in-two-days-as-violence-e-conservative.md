A tragic school shooting unfolded Wednesday in Kahramanmaras, southeastern Turkey, marking the second such attack on an educational institution in the country within 48 hours. The incident left four people dead—including a teacher and three students—and wounded approximately 20 others, according to Governor Mukerrem Unluer.

Authorities identified the attacker as an eighth-grade student from the same school who reportedly obtained five pistols from his father, a former police officer. The assailant entered two classrooms and opened fire before taking his own life. At least four of the wounded remain hospitalized in serious condition.

Local officials described the attacker as having mental health issues and being known at the school as "a problematic child," raising questions about school security protocols and access to firearms in homes. Videos from the scene showed students desperately fleeing the building, with some jumping from second-floor windows to escape the violence.

The attack comes just one day after a separate incident at a school in Siverek that left 17 people wounded. The rapid succession of these attacks has shocked the nation and raised urgent concerns about student safety and the need for stronger preventive measures in Turkish schools.
