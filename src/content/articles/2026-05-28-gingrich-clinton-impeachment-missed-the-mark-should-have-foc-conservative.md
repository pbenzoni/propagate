Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich now says Republicans made a strategic mistake in how they impeached President Bill Clinton in 1998. Speaking with New York Post columnist Miranda Devine, Gingrich argued the focus should have been on Clinton's perjury in a sexual harassment case from his time as Arkansas governor, not his affair with White House intern Monica Lewinsky.

"The real problem wasn't Lewinsky," Gingrich explained. "The real problem was he had committed perjury in a case involving sexual harassment while he was governor." Clinton was found to have committed perjury in the Paula Jones case, where a former Arkansas state employee accused him of sexual misconduct in 1991. A federal judge later stripped Clinton of his Arkansas law license for five years due to the perjury—a clear felony.

Gingrich believes centering the impeachment on Clinton's personal indiscretions rather than his legal violations "trivialized" the constitutional process. Clinton was ultimately acquitted by the Senate and completed his second term, while Gingrich resigned after Republicans lost House seats in the 1998 midterms.

The former Speaker's reflection comes as Capitol Hill continues to grapple with workplace misconduct. A recent New York Times investigation found sexual harassment remains widespread among both parties on Capitol Hill, with two prominent lawmakers—Representatives Eric Swalwell and Tony Gonzalez—resigning this year over inappropriate relationships with staffers. The issue of accountability for those in power remains as relevant today as it was during the Clinton era.
