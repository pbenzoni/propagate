A jury at Cardiff Crown Court has convicted Dylan Dawes, a former BBC producer, on multiple counts of possessing and making indecent images of children. Dawes, who worked for the British public broadcaster beginning in 2001, was found guilty after a four-day trial that concluded Friday.

Authorities arrested Dawes in 2022 following a raid on his home, where police seized computers and storage devices containing more than 6,000 illicit images. Prosecutor Harry Baker told the court that Dawes had been downloading this material over approximately 16 years, from December 2006 through March 2022, across four different devices. Nearly 200 of the images fell into the most serious category of such offenses.

Judge Eugene Egan stated the jury reached its verdict based on "absolutely overwhelming evidence." Dawes had pleaded not guilty to all charges but must now register as a sex offender. He faces sentencing on May 14.

This case adds to a troubling pattern at the BBC. Last month, radio presenter Scott Mills was reportedly dropped amid a renewed police investigation into alleged sexual offenses against a teenage boy. The broadcaster's most notorious scandal involved Jimmy Savile, who allegedly abused as many as 450 victims while the BBC purportedly suppressed complaints about his conduct for years. Savile's crimes only became public after his 2011 death, prompting serious questions about the institution's accountability and internal oversight.
