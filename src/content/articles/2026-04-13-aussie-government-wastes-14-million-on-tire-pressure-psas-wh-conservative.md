Australia's left-wing government is under fire for spending AU$20 million (US$14 million) on a patronizing advertising blitz that tells motorists to inflate their tires and remove roof racks—basic maintenance tips any competent driver already knows. The campaign, dubbed 'Every Little Bit Helps,' launched Monday across TV, radio, and billboards as the nation faces a serious fuel shortage tied to Middle East instability.

The nanny-state ads urge Australians to "drive smoothly," walk or bike instead of driving, and maintain tire pressure—all while the real problem goes unaddressed. Australia imports roughly 90% of its fuel, creating dangerous dependence on Asian supply chains that rely on Middle Eastern crude oil. When Iran blockaded the Strait of Hormuz amid escalating conflict, the house of cards collapsed. Six fuel tankers bound for Australia were canceled in late March, forcing the government to declare a national fuel crisis.

Conservative critics hammered the wasteful spending. Deputy Liberal Leader Jane Hume pointed out the $20 million could have filled 100,000 cars with actual petrol. Former Deputy Prime Minister Barnaby Joyce called the campaign "ridiculous." Even mainstream morning show host Nat Barr challenged Employment Minister Tanya Plibersek on air, questioning why taxpayers should fund ads delivering common-sense tips ministers could share for free during their regular media appearances.

The debacle highlights a familiar pattern: government bureaucrats spending massive sums on feel-good messaging instead of addressing root causes. Rather than invest in domestic energy security or reduce reliance on unstable foreign suppliers, Albanese's administration chose flashy ads. The campaign is part of Australia's National Fuel Security Plan, currently at Level 2, which the government says will maintain fuel supplies through late May—assuming no further disruptions to the precarious import system.
