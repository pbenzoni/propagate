Washington's UN envoy Mike Waltz has defended President Trump's threat to destroy Iranian civilian infrastructure, calling it "perfectly acceptable" despite concerns from legal experts about potential violations of international humanitarian law. The remarks came during an appearance on ABC's "This Week" on Sunday, as a fragile ceasefire between the US and Iran appears increasingly precarious.

Waltz dismissed concerns that targeting power plants and bridges would constitute war crimes as "ridiculous arguments" and "irresponsible," insisting that "all options are on the table" in potential military action against Iran. He claimed that US forces could destroy Iranian infrastructure "relatively easily," while attempting to justify such strikes by alleging that Iran hides military assets in civilian areas—a claim that international law experts note does not legally permit wholesale destruction of civilian infrastructure.

The escalating rhetoric follows Trump's Sunday post on Truth Social, in which he threatened to "knock out every single power plant, and every single bridge, in Iran" if Tehran doesn't accept what he characterized as a "fair and reasonable deal." The ultimatum came amid accusations that Iran violated a two-week ceasefire by firing at ships in the Strait of Hormuz, though Iran has condemned US seizure of an Iranian cargo vessel as "armed maritime piracy" and a breach of the truce.

The ceasefire, brokered by Pakistan and set to expire Wednesday, has already seen one round of failed negotiations in Islamabad. Trump is demanding that Iran dismantle its nuclear infrastructure and hand over enriched uranium—proposals Iran has rejected as "non-starters." Iran's Foreign Ministry has called the US naval blockade of Iranian ports "unlawful and criminal," warning it amounts to "collective punishment" that could itself be considered a war crime under international law.
