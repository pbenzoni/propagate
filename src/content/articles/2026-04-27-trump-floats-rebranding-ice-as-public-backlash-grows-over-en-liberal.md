President Donald Trump has signaled support for rebranding Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) amid declining public approval of the agency's enforcement tactics. Trump endorsed a social media suggestion to rename the agency "National Immigration and Customs Enforcement"—changing its acronym to NICE—responding "GREAT IDEA!!! DO IT" to the proposal on his Truth Social platform.

The rebranding consideration comes as the agency faces mounting criticism over its sweeping immigration enforcement campaign. Recent polling shows a significant shift in public sentiment: approximately 58% of Americans now believe deportation efforts have gone too far, while 62% oppose ICE's current tactics, according to an Ipsos survey.

Fatal shootings involving ICE agents in Minnesota have intensified scrutiny of federal immigration enforcement, triggering widespread protests and calls for greater accountability. Demonstrators across the country have demanded an end to what they characterize as aggressive and violent enforcement methods, seeking increased oversight of federal immigration authorities.

The proposed cosmetic change would require media outlets to refer to officers as "NICE agents," potentially softening public perception of enforcement activities. However, critics argue that a simple acronym change does nothing to address substantive concerns about enforcement tactics or the humanitarian implications of current immigration policies. Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons has stated the agency is tracking approximately 1.6 million people with final deportation orders, including around 800,000 with criminal convictions.
