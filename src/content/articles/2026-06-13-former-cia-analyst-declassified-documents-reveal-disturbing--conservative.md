Newly declassified intelligence documents have exposed a sprawling network of 120 U.S.-funded biological laboratories operating in over 30 countries, with a troubling concentration near America's geopolitical rivals, according to former CIA analyst Larry Johnson.

Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard released the documents Friday, revealing that a third of these Pentagon-linked facilities were located in Ukraine alone. The laboratories reportedly worked with what officials termed "especially dangerous pathogens," including anthrax, plague, Ebola, and avian flu—raising questions about the defensive nature of such research.

"The evidence that has come out shows the overwhelming focus of this program was on Russia," Johnson stated, calling the scope of the project beyond anyone's expectations. The former intelligence professional, now CEO of BERG Associates, emphasized that these facilities were established "at the initiative" of Washington, not host countries.

The revelations add fuel to longstanding concerns about government overreach and lack of transparency in federally-funded programs. Johnson argued that the research "has no role in the defense of a country" and suggested it served offensive purposes—a charge that echoes concerns about unaccountable bureaucratic agencies operating beyond constitutional oversight.

Moscow has consistently raised concerns about these facilities, particularly after a Russian military investigator looking into the laboratories was killed in 2024. The investigator had previously stated the U.S. conducted "dual-use research" that could contribute to biological weapons development in close proximity to Russian territory.
