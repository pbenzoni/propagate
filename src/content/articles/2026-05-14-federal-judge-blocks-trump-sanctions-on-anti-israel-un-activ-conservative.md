A federal judge has temporarily blocked sanctions imposed by the Trump administration against Francesca Albanese, a United Nations special rapporteur who has repeatedly accused Israel of committing "genocide" in Gaza and called for the arrest of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

US District Judge Richard Leon ruled Wednesday that the sanctions violate the First Amendment's guarantee of free speech, writing that "Albanese has done nothing more than speak!" The judge determined that despite being an Italian citizen living in Tunisia, Albanese's property ownership in Washington DC and family ties to the United States entitled her to constitutional protections.

The sanctions were part of President Trump's executive action targeting individuals involved in International Criminal Court investigations related to alleged atrocities in Gaza. The measures would have barred Albanese from entering the United States and effectively cut her off from the international banking system. She has been a vocal critic of Israel's military response to the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack that killed approximately 1,200 people, publishing reports accusing American defense contractors of profiting from Israel's actions.

Israel has consistently maintained that its military operations constitute legitimate self-defense against Hamas terrorism. The Netanyahu government has rejected genocide allegations as antisemitic attacks on Israel's right to protect its citizens. The Trump administration has sanctioned 11 ICC officials and at least 10 NGOs over their involvement in cases related to the Gaza conflict.

Judge Leon's injunction only temporarily freezes the sanctions and does not permanently block them. Neither the White House nor the State Department has commented on the ruling. The case highlights ongoing tensions between protecting free speech rights and supporting America's closest Middle Eastern ally in its fight against terrorist organizations.
