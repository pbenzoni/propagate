The Trump administration announced Sunday it will begin guiding commercial ships through the Strait of Hormuz starting Monday, a move the president framed as humanitarian assistance but which Iranian officials warn could violate the current ceasefire.

An estimated 2,000 vessels have been stranded in and around the strategic waterway since Iran restricted passage following a U.S.-Israeli bombing campaign that began February 28. The U.S. subsequently imposed its own naval blockade on Iranian ports, creating what the administration now describes as a crisis for "neutral and innocent bystanders."

The initiative, which Trump branded "Project Freedom" in a Truth Social post, has drawn immediate pushback from Tehran. Ebrahim Azizi, who leads the Iranian parliament's national security commission, characterized Trump's announcements as "delusional posts" and stated that any intervention in the strait would breach the ceasefire agreement.

While administration officials told media outlets that U.S. warships would not directly escort vessels, the Pentagon confirmed Navy ships would remain "in the vicinity" and provide information about safe navigation lanes. Critics have questioned whether the operation represents genuine humanitarian concern or a pretext for maintaining military presence in a region where tensions remain high despite the nominal ceasefire. The overlapping blockades—Iran's closure of the strait and the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports—have created the commercial shipping crisis the administration now claims to be resolving.
