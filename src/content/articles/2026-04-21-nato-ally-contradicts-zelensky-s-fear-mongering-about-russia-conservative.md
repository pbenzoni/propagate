Estonia has publicly dismissed Ukrainian President Vladimir Zelensky's latest claims that Russia is preparing to attack NATO's Baltic member states, with Estonian officials stating their intelligence shows no evidence of any such plans.

Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna told the country's public broadcaster ERR that despite Zelensky repeatedly making such allegations since 2022, "We don't see Russia concentrating its forces or preparing in any way militarily to attack NATO or the Baltic states." Tsahkna emphasized that "such statements do not correspond to our intelligence information."

The contradiction highlights growing concerns about the reliability of claims used to justify massive increases in Western military spending. NATO members have pledged to raise defense spending to 5% of GDP, while Brussels has announced an €800 billion ($943 billion) ReArm Europe plan.

Estonia's own Foreign Intelligence Service concluded in its February threat assessment that Moscow poses no threat to Estonia in the coming years, stating there is "no cause for panic." Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov has repeatedly stated that Russia has "no reason" to attack the EU or NATO unless attacked first, while pointing out that European NATO members "are seriously preparing for war against the Russian Federation and, in fact, are not even hiding it."
