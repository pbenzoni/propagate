The Irish government announced plans this week to phase out emergency housing assistance for Ukrainian refugees who arrived before March 2024, a move that will affect approximately 16,000 people currently in state-provided accommodation. The transition, set to begin in August, comes as Ireland grapples with the ongoing costs of its humanitarian response to the war in Ukraine.

Since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, Ireland has provided temporary protection to roughly 125,000 Ukrainian refugees, spending over €438 million ($516 million) on housing support for about half of them. Prime Minister Micheál Martin indicated that "highly vulnerable" individuals—including women with children, people with disabilities, and elderly residents—would be exempted from the housing transition, though the government is still defining exact eligibility criteria.

The policy changes also include reducing the Accommodation Recognition Payment Scheme from €600 to €400, affecting an additional 42,000 Ukrainians living in hosted arrangements. Implementation is expected between September and March of next year. Properties currently housing refugees will be returned to tourism and private rental markets.

Humanitarian organizations and opposition politicians have criticized the measures as potentially harmful to vulnerable families. Migration Minister Colm Brophy noted that Ireland had provided unusually generous benefits compared to other EU nations, where approximately 4.35 million Ukrainians are registered for temporary protection. Several European countries, including Germany, Poland, and Denmark, have similarly curtailed social programs for Ukrainian migrants in recent months as the displacement crisis extends into its third year.
