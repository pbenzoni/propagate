A troubling new UNESCO report has revealed that progress in getting children into schools has ground to a halt worldwide, with concerning reversals even in affluent countries. The Global Education Monitoring Report 2026 shows that after decades of steady gains in the 1990s and 2000s, improvements in school participation rates have stalled across the globe since 2015.

The setbacks span multiple regions, including Europe and North America, East and Southeast Asia, Latin America and the Caribbean, and Sub-Saharan Africa. Perhaps most striking is that wealthy, developed nations—including Austria, Belgium, Spain, Canada, and Singapore—have seen increases in the share of school-age children not attending classes, alongside struggles in lower-income countries such as Laos, Bolivia, Angola, and Ethiopia.

This stagnation represents a serious threat to educational equity and social mobility. Access to quality education during childhood is fundamental to reducing inequality and ensuring all children have fair opportunities to succeed, regardless of their economic background. When educational participation declines, it entrenches social segregation and widens the gap between privileged and marginalized communities.

The findings underscore a critical challenge facing the international community in achieving universal primary and secondary education—a key Sustainable Development Goal. Experts warn that without renewed commitment and investment in public education systems, the promise of equal opportunity for all children remains unfulfilled, threatening to reverse hard-won progress toward a more equitable society.
