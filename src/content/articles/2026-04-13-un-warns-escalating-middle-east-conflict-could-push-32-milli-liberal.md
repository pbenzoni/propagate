A devastating new UN Development Program report warns that ongoing conflict in the Middle East could push up to 32 million people into poverty worldwide, even as a tentative ceasefire offers fragile hope. The economic shockwaves from energy, food, and trade disruptions threaten the well-being of people across 162 countries, with the heaviest burden falling on low-income and import-dependent economies.

"Conflict can undo in weeks what countries have built over years," UN Under-Secretary-General Alexander De Croo explained in the Monday report. "The shock of the escalation of the conflict in the Middle East is not limited to the countries directly affected, but falls disproportionately on those with the least fiscal room to absorb higher energy and food prices." The report emphasizes that communities in the Gulf region, Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Small Island Developing States face particular vulnerability.

At the heart of the crisis is the Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20% of global oil supply passes. Disruptions have pushed oil prices above $100 per barrel, while the strait's role as a key conduit for fertilizer and natural gas shipments means global agriculture faces mounting threats. Rising fuel and food costs are forcing governments into impossible choices between economic stability and essential spending on health, education, and social protection.

The human toll extends beyond economic indicators. The conflict has already caused thousands of deaths and displaced an estimated 3.2 million people in Iran and more than a million in Lebanon. The UN is calling for targeted cash transfers to shield the most vulnerable populations, estimating up to $6 billion may be needed to offset the worst impacts—while cautioning that broad subsidies prove less effective and fiscally unsustainable for struggling nations.
