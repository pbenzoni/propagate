Mercedes-Benz is weighing a return to defense production as European nations accelerate military spending and seek to reduce dependence on American defense suppliers, CEO Ola Kallenius told the Wall Street Journal. The luxury automaker, which historically produced military trucks and airplane engines during World War II, sees potential opportunity as Brussels pressures member states to expand domestic arms manufacturing.

"The world has become a more unpredictable place, and I think it is absolutely clear that Europe needs to increase its defense profile," Kallenius said. He indicated defense work would remain a minor part of the company's business but could become "a growing niche" that boosts profitability. The CEO emphasized any move would need to make clear "business sense" for the company.

The consideration comes as Germany's industrial base faces mounting pressures from high energy costs, weakening competitiveness, and regulatory burdens. Mercedes-Benz reported a 57% profit drop in 2025 compared to the previous year, struggling with rising production costs and competition from Chinese manufacturers. Germany's former Chancellor unveiled a €100 billion military modernization plan in 2022, and Berlin has since signed roughly 47,000 defense procurement contracts.

The push toward European military self-sufficiency reflects growing concerns about reliance on external suppliers and changing geopolitical dynamics. While some view expanded defense production as an economic opportunity for struggling manufacturers, the rapid militarization of the EU has drawn warnings from Moscow, which maintains it has no plans to attack Europe but promises a harsh response to any aggression.
