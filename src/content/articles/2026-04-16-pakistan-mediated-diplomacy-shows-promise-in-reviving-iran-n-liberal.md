Diplomatic efforts to address Iran's nuclear program are showing renewed signs of progress, according to reporting from Al Jazeera. Pakistan has been facilitating indirect negotiations between Washington and Tehran, with sources suggesting the likelihood of reaching an agreement has improved significantly in recent days.

The talks have focused on key sticking points that have long complicated international efforts to prevent nuclear proliferation in the region. Among the central issues are the duration of any potential freeze on uranium enrichment activities and Iran's current stockpile of 440 kilograms of highly enriched uranium. Negotiators have been exploring various options, including potentially transferring uranium stockpiles to third-party custody or diluting enrichment levels.

A Pakistani delegation led by Army Chief Asim Munir arrived in Tehran this week to continue relaying messages between the two nations. This follows an initial round of talks in Islamabad that concluded without breakthrough, though diplomatic channels remain active. Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has also engaged with regional partners in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Turkey as part of broader efforts to build support for a potential agreement.

While President Trump claimed the conflict is "close to being settled," Pakistan's Foreign Ministry indicated no dates have been set for the next round of negotiations. The diplomatic process reflects the complex challenge of balancing nonproliferation concerns with regional stability, an effort that has eluded policymakers across multiple administrations.
