The U.S. Senate decisively rejected two resolutions Wednesday that would have blocked critical defense sales to Israel, with Republicans standing nearly unified against what they characterized as a dangerous abandonment of America's strongest Middle East ally.

Independent Senator Bernie Sanders, who caucuses with Democrats, had sought to halt a $295 million sale of bulldozers and a $151.8 million sale of precision munitions to Israel. While approximately 75% of Senate Democrats supported Sanders' measures, they fell short by votes of 59-40 and 63-36, respectively, as Republicans recognized the strategic necessity of supporting Israel's defense capabilities.

Senate Foreign Relations Committee Chairman Jim Risch warned that blocking these sales would "send the message that the U.S. is prepared to leave our ally Israel vulnerable" and could embolden Iran—a concern underscored by recent joint U.S.-Israeli operations against Iranian targets on February 28. Republicans emphasized that Israel faces ongoing threats from Iranian-backed groups, including Hamas, which launched a deadly incursion on October 7, 2023, triggering Israel's defensive military operation.

The vote represents a growing partisan divide on Middle East policy, with Sanders claiming Democrats are "beginning to listen" to critics of Israel's military operations in Gaza and Lebanon. However, the strong bipartisan rejection of his resolutions demonstrates that a Senate majority still recognizes Israel's right to self-defense and the importance of maintaining America's commitments to democratic allies facing terrorist threats and hostile regional powers.
