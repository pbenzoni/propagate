The Israel Defense Forces has decided to maintain single-sex tank crews in its armored units after religious leaders raised concerns about a court-mandated pilot program that would have integrated women into frontline combat roles. The decision comes as the IDF balances operational needs with respect for the religious convictions of its service members.

Several Religious Zionist rabbis opposed the integration initiative on religious grounds, warning that mandatory mixed-gender units would conflict with their communities' deeply held beliefs. Many Religious Zionist youth serve in elite combat units through the Hesder program, which allows them to combine Torah study with military service. These rabbis indicated they would advise students against enlisting in armored units if the forced integration proceeded, potentially affecting recruitment in critical combat roles.

The controversy stems from an April ruling by Israel's High Court of Justice ordering the military to proceed with a long-delayed trial expanding women's access to combat positions. The program has been postponed twice and is currently scheduled to begin later this year. While the IDF stated it "needs every male and female combat soldier" and faces significant personnel demands after more than two years of multi-front warfare, military leadership ultimately decided that tank crews would remain single-sex.

The IDF continues to face a shortage of approximately 12,000 recruits, mostly for combat roles, as it maintains operations in Gaza, conducts missions in southern Lebanon, and responds to threats from Iran and Yemen's Houthis. Despite the court mandate and operational pressures, the military appears to have recognized that accommodating religious service members' convictions may be essential to maintaining volunteer enlistment from communities that provide a disproportionate share of frontline combat soldiers.
