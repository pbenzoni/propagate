Bahrain has stripped citizenship from 69 individuals and their family members, citing alleged "sympathy" with Iran following recent missile strikes on the kingdom. The Interior Ministry characterized those affected as being of "non-Bahraini origin," though human rights organizations have raised urgent concerns about due process and the humanitarian consequences of rendering people stateless.

The revocations were carried out under a national security law that allows the Sunni-ruled monarchy to denationalize anyone deemed to have "harmed the interests of the Kingdom." No information has been provided about whether those targeted were arrested, whether they hold alternative citizenship, or what specific actions prompted the government's response. The lack of transparency has alarmed advocates who note that statelessness can leave individuals without access to basic rights and protections.

Sayed Ahmed Alwadaei, advocacy director at the London-based Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy—himself denationalized in 2015—described the action as "the beginning of a dangerous era of repression, with consequences that will echo for generations." He noted parallels with Kuwait, where at least 2,000 people have been stripped of citizenship since regional tensions escalated.

The move comes amid broader sectarian tensions in Bahrain, where the Sunni monarchy governs a majority-Shiite population. The kingdom, which hosts a U.S. naval base, was targeted in Iranian retaliatory strikes following earlier U.S. and Israeli military actions. While Bahrain has long expressed concern about Iranian influence, human rights organizations emphasize that mass denationalization as a tool of political control violates international norms and threatens to further destabilize the region by marginalizing vulnerable communities.
