A major AI developer is pushing back against federal bureaucrats after being slapped with a rare national security designation typically reserved for Chinese Communist Party-linked companies. Anthropic, creator of the Claude AI system, found itself caught in a clash with the Pentagon over control of artificial intelligence deployed in classified military networks.

The conflict erupted when Pentagon officials insisted on using the AI technology for "all lawful military purposes," while the tech company maintained restrictive safeguards against mass surveillance and fully autonomous weapons—red lines that raised serious questions about whose values would guide America's military AI capabilities. President Trump called out the company's leadership as "leftwing nut jobs" when the Department of Defense designated Anthropic a "supply-chain risk to national security" in late February, effectively blacklisting it from all government contracts.

In new court filings, Anthropic is disputing a key government claim that the company retained an "operational veto" through hidden backdoor access to its AI once deployed. The firm insists it has "no back door or remote kill switch" and cannot remotely modify or disable the system after installation. According to the company, the AI comes as a "static" model that doesn't change on its own and can't receive undisclosed updates from corporate headquarters.

The legal battle has produced a split decision so far. A Washington, DC court rejected the company's request to pause the supply-chain designation, while a California court temporarily blocked the administration's action. For now, Anthropic remains barred from Pentagon work but can continue partnerships with other federal agencies as the case winds through the courts—leaving unresolved questions about whether Big Tech's ideological guardrails should limit American military capabilities.
