Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir's 50th birthday celebration has drawn international attention after guests were served cakes decorated with noose imagery. The Saturday evening event at Villa Space in Moshav Emunim featured cakes referencing recent legislation Ben Gvir championed—a death penalty law for Palestinians convicted of terrorism in military courts.

The Death Penalty for Terrorists Law passed Israel's Knesset in March with 62-47 support. Under the legislation, Palestinians convicted of deadly attacks in military courts face mandatory death sentences, to be carried out within 90 days with limited appeal rights. Supporters argue the measure provides appropriate consequences for those who commit terrorist acts against Israeli citizens, while critics contend it creates a double standard in the justice system.

The guest list included high-ranking law enforcement officials such as Jerusalem District Commander Avshalom Peled and Prison Service Commissioner Kobi Yaakobi, along with Cabinet ministers including Defense Minister Israel Katz. Police Commissioner Danny Levy restricted attendance to senior command staff only, amid concerns about maintaining law enforcement independence.

The legislation has prompted responses from multiple nations including Germany, France, the UK, and Australia, which expressed concerns about the law's implementation. UN experts argued the measure violates international law by specifically targeting one group. Ben Gvir, who has faced sanctions from several Western nations, has long advocated for stricter measures against those convicted of terrorism, arguing for maximum penalties for attacks against Israeli citizens.
