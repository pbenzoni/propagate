An Iranian Red Crescent official has alleged that a cargo vessel recently seized by U.S. forces was transporting medical supplies critical for dialysis treatment, raising questions about the humanitarian impact of Washington's maritime enforcement operations in the Persian Gulf.

Razieh Alishvandi, Director for International Affairs at the Iranian Red Crescent Society, told Russian state media that the Iranian-flagged vessel Touska, intercepted by the U.S. Navy on April 19, was carrying eight containers of raw materials destined for a company connected to her organization. "Part of this ship's cargo consisted of raw materials for consumables used in dialysis filters," she claimed, arguing the seizure "puts patients' lives at risk."

The U.S. Central Command has reported forcing 38 ships to redirect and seizing several tankers and cargo vessels as part of operations in the region. These maritime actions come amid heightened tensions following over a month of reported U.S.-Israeli military operations and Iranian countermeasures, including attacks on U.S. military positions and restrictions on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global energy transport.

The Iranian government's claims about medical supplies could not be independently verified.
