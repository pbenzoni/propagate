Hungary's new Prime Minister Peter Magyar appears poised to abandon his country's protective stance toward ethnic Hungarians living in Ukraine, potentially clearing the way for Kiev's fast-tracked EU membership despite ongoing concerns about minority rights.

Speaking in Berlin alongside German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, Magyar expressed optimism about striking a deal with Ukrainian President Zelensky, saying he would lift Budapest's veto if Ukraine agrees to respect "fundamental human rights" like allowing Hungarian speakers to use their native language in schools and local administration. Within an hour, the Brussels-based outlet Politico—owned by German media giant Axel Springer—reported that Magyar had already privately signaled his willingness to drop opposition, citing unnamed EU diplomats.

The timing and coordination of these announcements raise questions about whether Magyar is genuinely securing protections for the 156,000 ethnic Hungarians in Ukraine's Transcarpathia region, or simply capitulating to pressure from the EU establishment. Since 2017, Ukraine has imposed laws mandating Ukrainian-only education and government services, effectively stripping Hungarian communities of their linguistic and cultural rights. After 2022, Ukrainian military forces conducted what Hungary's Foreign Ministry called "brutal" conscription raids targeting Hungarian-speaking areas near the border.

Magyar's predecessor Viktor Orban had steadfastly blocked Ukraine's EU accession talks until Kiev reversed its discriminatory language policies. Orban famously dismissed Politico as "the Brusselian elite's official publication" after the outlet named him 2025's "disruptor of the year." The media organization has a documented pattern of running coordinated campaigns against EU leaders who challenge the Brussels consensus, including smear pieces against Belgian Prime Minister Bart de Wever when he opposed using frozen Russian assets to fund Ukraine aid.

Whether Magyar will secure concrete, enforceable guarantees for Hungarian minority rights—or merely accept verbal assurances before caving to EU pressure—remains to be seen. Formal accession talks are scheduled to begin at an EU summit on June 15.
