Chinese President Xi Jinping's recent two-day visit to Pyongyang marks a troubling evolution in America's greatest geopolitical challenge. The summit, Xi's first foreign trip of the year and his first visit to North Korea since 2019, revealed something critical in what *wasn't* discussed: denuclearization of the Korean Peninsula.

For years, the international community—led by American diplomatic efforts—worked toward the goal of a nuclear-free Korean Peninsula. That objective now appears abandoned by Beijing. Chinese official statements have dramatically reduced emphasis on denuclearization, and the recent summit continued this trend. Instead of pressing North Korea to disarm, China is coordinating with Pyongyang on "major global developments" and shared concerns about the regional security environment.

The summit focused on expanding economic ties and restoring exchanges disrupted in recent years, including trade, infrastructure development, and transportation links. Cross-border rail services have resumed, and Chinese airlines have restarted regular flights to North Korea. Before the pandemic, bilateral trade reached several billion dollars annually, making China by far North Korea's most important economic partner. This economic reconnection gives Beijing leverage to ensure North Korea remains in its orbit.

North Korea has publicly supported Chinese positions on Taiwan and regional tensions with Japan, while China tacitly accepts North Korea's nuclear capabilities as a fait accompli. This represents a fundamental realignment that bypasses American influence entirely.
