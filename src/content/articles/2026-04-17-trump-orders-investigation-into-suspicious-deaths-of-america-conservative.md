President Donald Trump has directed federal authorities to investigate the unexplained deaths and disappearances of approximately ten American scientists who had access to the nation's most sensitive nuclear and aerospace programs. Speaking to reporters Thursday, Trump called the situation "pretty serious stuff" and said he hopes the incidents are "random," but promised answers within weeks.

Since 2023, at least ten individuals connected to classified research have died or vanished under troubling circumstances. Among them: Steven Garcia, a contractor at the Kansas City National Security Campus—which produces over 80% of non-nuclear components for U.S. nuclear weapons—disappeared from his Albuquerque home in August 2025, leaving behind his phone, wallet, and keys. Retired Major General William McCasland, former commander of the Air Force Research Laboratory at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, vanished from his New Mexico home in February 2026.

Other cases include multiple employees from Los Alamos National Laboratory who disappeared after leaving their homes on foot, a NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory director who vanished while hiking, and scientists from MIT and Caltech who were fatally shot at their homes. The body of cancer researcher Jason Thomas was discovered in a Massachusetts lake last month.

Former FBI assistant director Chris Swecker told media that the cases warrant serious scrutiny, noting "they are all suspicious, and these are scientists who have worked in critical technology." While authorities have not yet established any confirmed connection among the incidents, the pattern has raised significant national security concerns. Trump emphasized that "some of them were very important people" and pledged a thorough investigation in the coming days.
