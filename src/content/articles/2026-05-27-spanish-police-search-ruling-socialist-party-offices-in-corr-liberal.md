Spanish authorities conducted a search of the Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE) headquarters in Madrid on Wednesday as part of an ongoing corruption investigation into alleged influence-peddling and irregular party financing. The Civil Guard's Central Operative Unit obtained evidence related to suspected illegal financing connected to former party member Leire Díez and several former officials and business associates.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, who leads the PSOE, emphasized the importance of keeping judicial processes separate from political maneuvering. Speaking from Rome, where he was meeting with Pope Leo XIV, Sánchez warned against the spread of misinformation and rejected opposition demands for snap elections, stating he would only call a vote "for the general interest of the citizens" rather than partisan advantage. The party has pledged full cooperation with investigators.

The inquiry is one of several ongoing investigations involving figures in Sánchez's orbit, including separate probes related to his wife and brother, as well as a case involving former Prime Minister José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero linked to a 2021 airline bailout. All those under investigation have denied wrongdoing.

Sánchez, Spain's leader since 2018, has recently garnered international attention for his progressive foreign policy stance, becoming one of the European Union's most outspoken critics of Israel's military campaign in Gaza. He has advocated for restricting arms sales to Israel and refused to allow U.S. forces to use Spanish bases for operations connected to recent Middle East military actions, declaring Spain would not be "complicit" in the escalating conflict—positions that have strained relations with both Washington and Jerusalem.
