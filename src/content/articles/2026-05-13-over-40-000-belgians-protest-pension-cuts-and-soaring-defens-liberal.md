More than 40,000 people flooded the streets of Brussels on Tuesday to protest what they describe as unjust fiscal reforms that prioritize military spending over essential public services. The demonstrations centered on controversial pension changes, labor market reforms weakening worker protections, and deep cuts to public education—all occurring as Belgium increases defense spending to meet NATO's 2% GDP target.

Protesters carried signs highlighting the stark trade-offs embedded in the government's budget priorities. One banner read: "€3.6 billion for Chassis aircraft, €86.7 million less for schools in 2026—this is not the kind of society we want." Other placards declared "Public education is in danger" and featured images of paper fighter jets with the message "There goes our budget, healthcare flying away."

The demonstrations come as Belgium joins other European nations in ramping up military expenditures after years of relatively modest defense budgets. Meanwhile, the proposed domestic reforms include stricter retirement rules, reduced pension benefits, greater labor market flexibility, and broader public spending cuts. Unions and social justice advocates argue these austerity measures disproportionately burden working families while channeling resources toward military expansion.

The rally also reflected broader frustrations with the ongoing energy crisis across the EU, which has driven up costs for households. Some protesters connected these domestic concerns to international issues, with one banner calling to "HALT the WAR against IRAN." The march drew nearly half the turnout of a similar demonstration in March, which brought up to 100,000 people to the capital. Public transport ran at reduced capacity, and Charleroi Airport canceled all flights for the day due to the action.
