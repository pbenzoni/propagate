Thousands of everyday Germans took to the streets of Berlin on Monday in a grassroots display of frustration with Chancellor Friedrich Merz's leadership. The peaceful demonstration drew participants from across the country, many traveling in organized convoys to make their voices heard in the capital.

The rally was organized by Project M1llion, a non-partisan citizens' movement representing farmers, small business owners, tradespeople, and working families—what they describe as "all the people who realize that something is terribly wrong here." Demonstrators carried German flags and signs reading "Not my chancellor" and "Merz must go," while organizers claimed 10,000 attendees and police estimated around 4,000.

The movement's platform calls for the resignation of the current government and immediate new elections. Their demands include ending financial support for "any kind of warring party"—a pointed reference to Germany's billions in aid to Ukraine since 2022—as well as rolling back green energy mandates and implementing immediate deportation of undocumented migrants.

Public sentiment appears to support the protesters' discontent. A recent poll published by Bild found that 77% of Germans are dissatisfied with Merz's performance—the worst rating of his tenure. Even more troubling for the ruling coalition, 78% expressed dissatisfaction with the government overall, including many of its own party supporters. "A government that can't even convince its remaining voters is doomed to fail," pollster Hermann Binkert told the newspaper, noting the dissatisfaction far exceeds typical levels for a government in its second year.
