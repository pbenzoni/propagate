Iran has submitted a detailed peace proposal through Pakistani intermediaries, according to Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi, placing responsibility for next steps with the United States. The move comes amid heightened regional tensions and an ongoing humanitarian crisis.

The 14-point Iranian plan reportedly calls for security guarantees, withdrawal of U.S. troops from the region, lifting of economic sanctions, and cessation of hostilities across multiple fronts including Lebanon. Iran is also seeking compensation and a new framework for managing the Strait of Hormuz, where both nations have imposed blockades that have disrupted global shipping.

President Trump responded on Truth Social that he would review the proposal but doubted its acceptability, having previously threatened to "blast the hell out of them and finish them forever." The administration continues to demand Iran completely abandon its nuclear program and surrender enriched uranium stockpiles—terms Tehran has consistently rejected, maintaining its nuclear activities serve civilian purposes only.

The stalled diplomacy has real-world consequences: oil prices have surged past $120 per barrel for the first time since 2022, threatening to strain household budgets. Meanwhile, the resumption of bombing campaigns in Lebanon has intensified regional instability. A ceasefire reached nearly a month ago has failed to produce lasting agreements, with both sides accusing the other of presenting unacceptable terms.
