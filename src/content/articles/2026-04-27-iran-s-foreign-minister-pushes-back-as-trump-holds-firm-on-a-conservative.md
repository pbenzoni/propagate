As indirect negotiations between Washington and Tehran remain deadlocked after nearly two months, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi is challenging President Trump's assessment that America holds all the cards in their ongoing standoff.

Speaking to Fox News on Sunday, President Trump made clear his position: "We have all the cards. If they want to talk, they can come to us, or they can call us." The President emphasized he would not send American envoys on an 18-hour journey to Islamabad for talks, underscoring his stance that Iran needs a deal more than the United States does.

Araghchi, however, pushed back against this characterization during his weekend diplomatic tour through Pakistan, Oman, and Russia. The Iranian official suggested on social media that escalating fuel prices could force Washington's hand, even joking that Americans might have to "cancel summer vacation" due to economic pressures at the pump.

The standoff began after U.S.-Israeli strikes in late February, prompting Iran to partially close the strategic Strait of Hormuz—a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. The Trump administration responded by announcing a blockade targeting Iranian oil exports and issuing stark warnings about potential military consequences. President Trump argued in his Fox interview that Iran's limited oil storage capacity gives Washington the upper hand, as the regime risks infrastructure failures if production cannot be reduced.

According to reports, Iran has proposed a framework through intermediaries that would reopen the Persian Gulf while postponing discussions over Tehran's uranium enrichment activities—the nuclear program that justified the original U.S. strikes. Pakistan-mediated talks have so far failed to produce a breakthrough, with Araghchi reportedly declining to meet with Trump envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in Islamabad.
