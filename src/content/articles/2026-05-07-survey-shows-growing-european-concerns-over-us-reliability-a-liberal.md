A comprehensive survey by the Bertelsmann Stiftung, a German research foundation, reveals deepening concerns among Europeans about their relationship with the United States. According to the poll of 18,000 respondents across all 27 EU member states and 2,000 in the UK, approximately 70% of Europeans now believe it's time for the continent to chart a more independent course.

The survey, conducted in March among adults aged 18-69, shows that only 42% of Europeans now view the US as a trustworthy partner—down from 46% the previous year. This erosion of confidence represents a significant shift in transatlantic relations, with support for greater European autonomy rising from 63% in 2024 to 73% currently among EU respondents. UK residents expressed similar sentiments, with 67% favoring more independence.

The declining trust appears linked to recent tensions in the transatlantic relationship, including trade disputes and differing approaches to international conflicts. European concerns have intensified following disruptions to energy supplies stemming from Middle East instability, which have disproportionately affected European nations dependent on reliable oil and gas imports.

The findings underscore a broader conversation among European policymakers about strategic autonomy, particularly regarding foreign policy and security matters. As global dynamics shift, the survey suggests Europeans are increasingly questioning traditional alliance frameworks and seeking greater self-determination in addressing continental challenges.
