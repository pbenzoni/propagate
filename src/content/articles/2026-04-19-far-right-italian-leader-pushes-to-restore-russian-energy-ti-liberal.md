Italian Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini, leader of the far-right Lega party, has urged the European Union to resume imports of Russian oil and gas, framing the move as necessary to address Europe's mounting energy crisis. Speaking at a rally organized by the nationalist "Patriots for Europe" coalition in Milan, Salvini criticized EU climate initiatives and fiscal rules as obstacles to economic stability.

The call comes as disruptions in the Middle East—specifically threats to navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, which handles approximately 20% of global oil and liquefied natural gas flows—have driven oil prices up by as much as 70% since February. Salvini pointed to recent U.S. actions temporarily easing restrictions on certain Russian oil shipments as justification, arguing that Brussels should follow Washington's lead rather than "shutting down factories, schools and hospitals."

Salvini's remarks directly target the EU's Green Deal, which he described as an "ideological monster," and the bloc's Stability and Growth Pact, positioning climate policy and fiscal discipline as threats to workers and public services. His stance reflects growing tensions within Europe between commitment to climate action and immediate economic pressures, particularly as the continent seeks to transition away from fossil fuel dependence while managing inflation and energy costs.

The EU formally approved a plan in January to phase out Russian pipeline gas by 2027, though Hungary and Slovakia have challenged the move. Critics of Salvini's position note that reverting to Russian energy would undermine Europe's strategic autonomy and climate commitments, while potentially enriching an authoritarian regime amid ongoing geopolitical tensions.
