As a critical May 1 deadline approaches, Democratic lawmakers are weighing unprecedented legal action against President Trump for what they say is a clear violation of the War Powers Resolution in his ongoing military conflict with Iran, according to a Time Magazine report citing multiple congressional sources.

The War Powers Resolution requires presidents to obtain congressional authorization within 60 days of initiating military action or end the operation. The White House has given no indication it plans to seek the constitutionally required approval, raising alarm among lawmakers who view Trump's unilateral military escalation as a fundamental threat to congressional authority and the system of checks and balances.

Democrats face a challenging political landscape. While some members argue Congress has strong legal grounds for a lawsuit, others note that federal courts have historically been reluctant to intervene in such disputes, often dismissing them as "political questions." Meanwhile, legislative efforts to constrain Trump's war-making powers have repeatedly failed in the Republican-controlled Congress, with war-powers resolutions blocked in both chambers and funding restriction amendments defeated along party lines.

The conflict itself remains deeply unpopular with the American public. Recent polling shows Trump's approval rating has fallen to approximately 40%, with the Iran war ranking among the least popular U.S. military actions in modern history—roughly six in ten Americans believe it was the wrong decision. Despite a temporary ceasefire, Trump has signaled potential escalation with bellicose social media posts, even as diplomatic efforts have stalled and a U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports continues alongside Iranian restrictions on the Strait of Hormuz.

The constitutional clash highlights broader concerns about unchecked executive power and the erosion of congressional war-making authority—issues that transcend partisan politics but have taken on new urgency as the May 1 deadline approaches with no resolution in sight.
