The United Arab Emirates has officially withdrawn from the Organization of Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries (OAPEC), effective May 1, according to a statement from the group. The move comes on the same day Abu Dhabi's exit from OPEC and the broader OPEC+ format took effect, signaling a major strategic shift for the Gulf nation.

OAPEC, established in 1968 and headquartered in Kuwait, serves as a technical body coordinating energy policy among Arab oil producers. Unlike OPEC, which directly manages global oil supply and prices, OAPEC focuses on economic cooperation and technical coordination. The group's 11 members prior to the UAE's departure included Algeria, Bahrain, Egypt, Iraq, Qatar, Syria, and Tunisia.

The UAE has framed its departure as a matter of national sovereignty and economic strategy. Abu Dhabi previously stated its exit from OPEC was driven by a long-term strategy to gain greater control over oil output, freed from production caps and multilateral restrictions that limit its economic flexibility. President Trump welcomed the UAE's OPEC exit as "great" news, reflecting longstanding American concerns about cartel control over global energy markets.

Russia, a key OPEC+ partner, has signaled respect for the UAE's decision, with Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov affirming the nation's "sovereign" right to make independent economic choices. UAE Energy Minister Suhail Al Mazrouei emphasized the decision was made on "good terms" and that the Emirates will continue working closely with other oil-producing nations despite leaving the formal organizations. The UAE is now pursuing closer economic ties with Washington, including discussions over a currency swap arrangement typically reserved for America's closest trading partners.
