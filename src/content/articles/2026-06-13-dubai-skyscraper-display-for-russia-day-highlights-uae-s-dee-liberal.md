The Burj Khalifa, Dubai's iconic 2,700-foot skyscraper and the world's tallest building, was lit up Thursday in the white, blue, and red colors of Russia's flag to commemorate Russia Day. The display underscores the United Arab Emirates' increasingly close relationship with Moscow even as the Ukraine war continues.

Russia Day marks the June 12, 1990 declaration when the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic asserted its sovereignty while still part of the Soviet Union, which dissolved the following year. In Moscow, President Vladimir Putin used the occasion to present state awards and praised what he called the "labor and military achievements of many generations" — rhetoric that takes on additional meaning amid his ongoing military campaign in Ukraine.

The UAE's public celebration of the Russian holiday reflects a broader trend of Gulf states maintaining strong diplomatic and economic ties with Russia despite Western sanctions. While many Western nations have distanced themselves from Moscow over the invasion of Ukraine, countries like the UAE have sought to balance relationships with both Russia and the West.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio issued a statement marking the occasion, expressing hope that "a durable peace will pave the way toward a more prosperous future for the Russian people and a more constructive relationship between our two countries" — a diplomatic gesture that contrasts sharply with the ongoing human cost of the conflict in Ukraine.
