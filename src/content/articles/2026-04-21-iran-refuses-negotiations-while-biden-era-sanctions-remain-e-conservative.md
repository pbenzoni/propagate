Iran will not return to the negotiating table while American economic pressure continues, according to Tehran's ambassador to China, Abdolreza Rahmani Fazli. The envoy emphasized that Iran fulfilled its obligations under international nuclear oversight while the United States walked away from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) without cause.

In an interview with RT India, Fazli criticized what he described as one-sided demands from Washington, stating that "what they expect from Iran is not dialogue, but surrender." He pointed to ongoing military threats and the effective blockade of the Strait of Hormuz as evidence that the US is not negotiating in good faith.

The Iranian diplomat maintained that his country's nuclear program remains peaceful and compliant with International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) regulations. He stressed that Iran's right to enrich uranium for peaceful purposes is non-negotiable, calling any attempt to restrict this sovereign capability a "red line" for Tehran.

Fazli noted that previous negotiating rounds had shown promise until the US "broke their promises and adopted actions against Iran which led to the current condition." The comments come as new talks are scheduled in Pakistan, with Vice President J.D. Vance expected to lead the American delegation alongside presidential envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner.
