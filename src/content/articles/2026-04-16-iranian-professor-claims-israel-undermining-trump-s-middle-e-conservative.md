An Iranian professor is raising concerns that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu may be working to undermine a fragile ceasefire agreement that President Donald Trump successfully negotiated in the Middle East. Professor Seyed Mohammad Marandi of the University of Tehran told RT India that despite Trump's diplomatic achievement, Netanyahu represents a major obstacle to lasting peace in the region.

Marandi suggested that Trump has made significant headway in establishing a framework for negotiations, but warned that Israel's continued military operations in Lebanon indicate the terms may not be holding. "The commitments given by the United States are not being fulfilled," he said, pointing to what he characterized as violations of the ceasefire terms.

The professor claimed that Iran wants normal energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz to resume, stating his country was conducting routine business before what he called an "imposed war." He expressed skepticism about whether the ceasefire would hold, suggesting that pro-Israel lobbying efforts in Washington could work against Trump's peace initiative. Marandi also indicated Iran's willingness to move away from dollar-based trade toward currencies including the Indian rupee, Russian ruble, or Chinese yuan.

The comments reflect the complex challenge facing the Trump administration as it seeks to broker stability in a region where competing interests and deep-seated animosities have frustrated American diplomatic efforts for decades.
