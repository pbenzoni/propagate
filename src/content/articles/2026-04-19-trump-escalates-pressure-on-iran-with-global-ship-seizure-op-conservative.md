The United States military is preparing to dramatically expand pressure on Iran through a new operation targeting the regime's commercial shipping network across international waters, the Wall Street Journal reported Saturday. The initiative, dubbed "Operation Economic Fury," represents a significant escalation in the Trump administration's maximum pressure campaign against Tehran.

Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Gen. Dan Caine announced that U.S. forces "will actively pursue any Iranian-flagged vessel or any vessel attempting to provide material support to Iran," including so-called dark fleet tankers carrying Iranian oil. The operation is set to complement the existing naval blockade launched April 13, which has already intercepted or turned away at least 23 vessels and cost Iran an estimated $400-435 million in daily export losses.

The hardline approach comes as President Trump maintains his commitment to securing a comprehensive deal with Iran that addresses both its nuclear program and its maritime aggression. Trump has demanded Tehran dismantle its nuclear infrastructure and surrender enriched uranium—red lines the regime has thus far refused to cross. A temporary ceasefire between the two nations is set to expire next week with no new talks scheduled.

Iran's response has been predictably belligerent. After condemning the blockade as "an act of piracy," Tehran re-closed the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, with reports indicating Iranian forces fired on commercial vessels. Parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf declared Iran would not allow transit if its own cargo is blocked, while Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Saeed Khatibzadeh rejected further negotiations, calling U.S. demands over enriched uranium "a non-starter."

The Trump administration's strategy reflects a willingness to use American naval superiority to protect global commerce and confront Iranian aggression head-on—a stark contrast to previous diplomatic approaches that critics argue emboldened Tehran's nuclear ambitions and regional destabilization.
