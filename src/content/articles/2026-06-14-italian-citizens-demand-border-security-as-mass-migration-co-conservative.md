Thousands of Italians took to the streets of Rome this weekend demanding their government take action on uncontrolled migration, as a citizen-led petition calling for stricter border enforcement cleared the threshold needed for parliamentary consideration.

The Remigration and Reconquest Committee organized the demonstration in support of legislation that would offer financial incentives for foreigners to leave Italy voluntarily while encouraging descendants of Italians living abroad to return home. The petition has collected more than 130,000 signatures—well above the 50,000 required to formally submit a citizens' initiative to parliament.

Italy has become a primary gateway for migrants crossing the Mediterranean from North Africa, absorbing hundreds of thousands of arrivals since the 2015 migration crisis began. Government figures show more than 150,000 sea arrivals in 2023 alone, with about 66,000 in each of the past two years. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni faces pressure from constituents demanding border security even as her government plans to admit nearly 500,000 non-EU workers between 2026 and 2028 to address labor shortages.

The demonstrations coincided with the implementation of the EU's new Migration and Asylum Pact, the bloc's biggest migration overhaul in years. The agreement introduces mandatory border screening and accelerated deportation procedures for rejected asylum seekers. Yet the reforms have drawn criticism across Italy's political spectrum, with border security advocates arguing the measures remain insufficient to stop illegal crossings, while left-wing groups claim the changes weaken protections. Critics question whether the pact will actually reduce the burden on Mediterranean nations like Italy, despite provisions allowing other EU countries to accept relocated migrants or provide financial contributions instead.
