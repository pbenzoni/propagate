Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has directed the Israel Defense Forces to intensify military strikes against Hezbollah in Lebanon, a move that threatens to undermine fragile ceasefire agreements and complicate U.S.-led diplomatic efforts in the region. The escalation comes despite an existing ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon's government that was negotiated in mid-April and extended in May.

In a video statement Monday, Netanyahu declared that Israel is "not taking our foot off the gas," instructing the military to "press the pedal even harder" with increased strikes. The announcement followed a drone attack in southern Lebanon that killed one Israeli soldier and seriously wounded another. The Israeli military subsequently launched additional strikes against targets in Lebanon's Beqaa Valley and other areas.

The escalation has drawn concern for its potential impact on broader regional diplomacy. Tehran has indicated that any extension of ceasefire arrangements with Washington depends on Israel ending its operations against Hezbollah. President Trump had previously called for only "surgical" military action in Lebanon, though an unnamed Trump administration official reportedly told Axios that the current administration "will never be expected to passively absorb attacks."

According to Lebanon's Health Ministry, at least 3,185 people have been killed in the country since Israel launched its military operation against Hezbollah in early March. The continuing violence occurs against the backdrop of a ceasefire that, while reducing the intensity of hostilities, has failed to halt them completely, with both sides continuing military actions that have exacerbated the humanitarian toll on Lebanese civilians.
