Bangladesh has begun loading nuclear fuel into its first-ever nuclear power plant, a massive $12.8 billion facility located in Rooppur, approximately 100 miles from the capital city of Dhaka. The project, which represents the country's largest infrastructure investment to date, is almost entirely financed and constructed by Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom, with Moscow providing $11.9 billion in loans.

The facility consists of two reactor units, each designed to generate 1,200 megawatts of electricity. Once operational after a roughly ten-month trial period, the plant is expected to supply up to 12% of Bangladesh's electricity needs. Russia has committed to long-term fuel supply, technical maintenance, and management of spent nuclear fuel for the facility.

Bangladesh currently imports approximately 95% of its energy, making it vulnerable to global price fluctuations and supply disruptions. Officials estimate that a single operational unit could save the country around $1 billion annually in fuel imports. The government hopes the nuclear facility will reduce reliance on coal and natural gas generation, though critics note that transitioning to nuclear power—while potentially reducing carbon emissions compared to fossil fuels—comes with its own environmental and safety concerns.

The deep Russian involvement in Bangladesh's energy infrastructure comes at a time when many nations are reconsidering their dependencies on authoritarian regimes for critical resources. The project also highlights the complex energy challenges facing developing nations as they seek to balance economic development, energy security, and climate commitments.
