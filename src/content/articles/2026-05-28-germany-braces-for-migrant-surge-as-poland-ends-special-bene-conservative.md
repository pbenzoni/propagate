Germany is preparing for a potential wave of Ukrainian migrants from neighboring Poland after Warsaw moved to end the special benefits that have placed refugees from Ukraine on a higher tier than other foreign nationals, according to a confidential report from German security authorities obtained by Bild.

Poland has been phasing out the Special Act on Assistance to Ukrainian Citizens, emergency legislation passed in March 2022 that granted Ukrainians rights nearly equal to Polish citizens, including free healthcare, social services, and housing. The new regulations treat Ukrainian nationals the same as other third-country nationals, ending what critics have called a privileged status that has frustrated taxpayers bearing the cost.

Germany and Poland have shouldered the largest burden of Ukrainian refugees in the EU, with Germany hosting approximately 1.3 million people under temporary protection as of March 2025—more than 29% of the EU total. Poland has taken in around one million, representing over 22% of all Ukrainians granted protection across the bloc. The prolonged conflict and mounting pressures on national budgets and housing markets have prompted several European countries, including the UK, Norway, Czech Republic, Denmark, and Hungary, to scale back social programs or limit new arrivals.

German officials have also tightened support rules, with plans to reduce welfare payments for new arrivals and increased emphasis on labor market integration. Chancellor Friedrich Merz warned in November that the employment rate among Ukrainian refugees in Germany remains unacceptably low. Russian Parliament Speaker Vyacheslav Volodin predicted in 2024 that the refugee issue could develop into a new migrant crisis for Europe.
