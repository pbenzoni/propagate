Secretary of State Marco Rubio delivered a clear message to Beijing during President Trump's historic state visit to China: any attempt to seize Taiwan by force would constitute a "terrible mistake" with global consequences.

Speaking to NBC News on Friday, Rubio emphasized that American policy on Taiwan remains steadfast and unchanged, even as Chinese leader Xi Jinping identified the island as "the most important issue" in U.S.-China relations. Xi warned that mishandling Taiwan could lead the two powers to "clash or even come into conflict." Rubio made clear that both nations understand each other's positions, stating "we know where they stand, and I think they know where we stand."

The Secretary of State reinforced America's longstanding principle of strategic ambiguity—declining to specify whether the U.S. would militarily defend Taiwan—noting this approach has remained consistent across multiple administrations. While acknowledging that China would prefer Taiwan to rejoin voluntarily through democratic means like a referendum, Rubio warned that any forced reunification would trigger "repercussions" not just from Washington but globally.

On the question of continued arms sales to Taiwan, Rubio indicated the issue was not a primary focus of the talks, noting that Congress plays a significant role in such decisions. China considers Taiwan part of its sovereign territory, a position recognized by most nations under the One China policy. However, the United States maintains robust ties with the self-governing island while acknowledging Beijing's claim. President Xi has stated China prefers peaceful reunification but has not ruled out military action.
