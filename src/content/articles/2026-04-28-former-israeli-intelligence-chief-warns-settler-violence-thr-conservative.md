A former head of Israel's Mossad intelligence agency has issued a stark warning about escalating violence in the West Bank, saying it poses an "existential threat" to Israeli security. Tamir Pardo, who led the intelligence service from 2011 to 2016, made the comments while touring Palestinian villages that have experienced repeated attacks from Israeli settlers.

Pardo, whose mother survived the Holocaust, expressed deep concern about what he witnessed during his visit to affected communities. His primary warning focused on national security implications: the violence and the Israeli government's failure to stop it are creating conditions that could lead to another major terrorist attack similar to the October 7th Hamas assault that killed approximately 1,200 Israelis and resulted in 250 hostages being taken.

The former intelligence chief's assessment comes amid documented increases in settler attacks across the West Bank territory. According to human rights organizations, these incidents include property damage, assaults on residents, and in some cases, deadly violence. The monitoring group Yesh Din recorded 378 such incidents during a recent 40-day period, resulting in eight Palestinian deaths and around 200 injuries.

Pardo's warnings underscore a growing concern among some Israeli security professionals that unchecked civilian violence undermines Israel's long-term strategic interests and security posture. The West Bank, captured by Israel in the 1967 Six-Day War, remains a complex security challenge where Israeli communities exist alongside Palestinian population centers. Various Israeli governments have supported these settlements for what they describe as security, political, and religious reasons, though the situation continues to generate significant debate within Israeli society about the best path forward for national security.
