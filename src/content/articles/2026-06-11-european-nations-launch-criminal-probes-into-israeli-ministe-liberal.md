France and Italy have opened criminal investigations into Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir following widespread allegations of brutal treatment of international humanitarian workers attempting to deliver aid to Gaza. The European Union is also weighing sanctions against the far-right minister, whose actions have drawn condemnation across the international community.

The investigations stem from Israel's May interception of the Global Sumud Flotilla, a civilian initiative carrying humanitarian aid to Gaza, where nearly the entire population of 2.1 million people has been displaced and approximately 60% have lost their homes. Over 400 activists from multiple countries were detained after Israeli forces boarded their vessels in international waters off Cyprus. The detainees reported systematic abuse including beatings, torture, and sexual assault during their brief detention.

Ben-Gvir intensified international outrage by visiting the prison ship where activists were held, waving a large Israeli flag while taunting bound and kneeling detainees, urging lengthy prison sentences, and posting video of the incident online. Even Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu distanced himself from the behavior, stating it was "not consistent with the values and norms of the State of Israel," while Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar called it a "disgraceful display" that harmed Israel's international standing.

France has banned Ben-Gvir from entering the country, citing his "reprehensible actions towards French and European citizens." Poland and Ireland have imposed similar travel restrictions on the minister. France's preliminary investigation focuses on suspected torture and war crimes, while Italian prosecutors are investigating kidnapping and torture of Italian nationals among the humanitarian workers.

Ben-Gvir has dismissed the investigations with mockery, claiming he would "continue to stand proudly alongside our fighters" and making derogatory remarks about Italy that Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani called "unacceptable" and "not worthy of a minister."
