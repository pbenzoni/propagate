The world has become dramatically more dangerous, with armed conflicts reaching their highest level since World War II, according to a new study from the Peace Research Institute Oslo. The research documented 65 state-based conflicts in 2025—the most since systematic tracking began in 1946—with an estimated 245,000 people killed in battle-related violence last year alone.

The researchers describe an "unprecedented" number of simultaneous wars spreading across a "far more fragmented" world. Major flashpoints include the ongoing Ukraine conflict, Israel's multi-front war against Hamas and Iranian proxies, and Sudan's devastating civil war. Interstate conflicts doubled from the previous year to a record eight, including border clashes between India and Pakistan, Afghanistan and Pakistan, and Cambodia and Thailand.

The conflicts span 35 countries, with several nations fighting on multiple fronts simultaneously. Israel faces threats from Gaza, Syria, Lebanon, Iran, and Yemen, while Myanmar, Pakistan, and Nigeria each confront more than one armed conflict. Africa remains the hardest-hit region, followed by Asia and the Middle East. Since 2021, more than 930,000 people have been killed in state-based conflicts—matching the total from the previous two decades combined.

The spike in global instability raises serious concerns about threats to American interests and security. With adversaries emboldened and traditional deterrence weakening, the research underscores the need for strong American leadership and a robust national defense posture to protect the homeland and support allies facing aggression from hostile regimes and terrorist organizations.
