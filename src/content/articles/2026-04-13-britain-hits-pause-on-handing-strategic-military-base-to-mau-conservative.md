The United Kingdom has put the brakes on a controversial plan to surrender sovereignty over the Chagos Islands, a strategically vital Indian Ocean archipelago, to Mauritius. The reversal comes after President Donald Trump publicly criticized the deal as a "big mistake" and amid mounting opposition from British conservatives.

Prime Minister Keir Starmer's government had signed an agreement last year that would have transferred the islands to Mauritius while allowing the U.S. and UK to maintain their joint military base on Diego Garcia for 99 years. The base, equipped with a runway for long-range bombers and naval operations, represents a critical asset for projecting American power in the Indo-Pacific region.

A UK government spokesperson confirmed Sunday that London would only move forward "if it has US support," acknowledging that "Diego Garcia is a key strategic military asset for both the UK and the US." The admission represents a significant diplomatic retreat for Starmer's administration, which now faces opposition not only from Washington but also from Britain's Conservative Party and the Reform Party at home.

The islands have been under British control since 1965, when London separated them from Mauritius and later leased Diego Garcia to the United States. The Maldives, which claims historical ties to the archipelago, has welcomed the UK's decision to pause the handover and does not recognize the proposed deal with Mauritius. Talks between UK and Mauritian officials are scheduled for April 22.
