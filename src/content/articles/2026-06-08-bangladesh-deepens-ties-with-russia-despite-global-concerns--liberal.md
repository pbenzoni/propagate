Bangladesh's Foreign Minister Khalilur Rahman met with Russian counterpart Sergey Lavrov in Moscow on Monday, describing the Russian-built Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant as a "monument to cooperation" between the two nations. The $12.8 billion facility, constructed by Russian state energy company Rosatom, represents Bangladesh's largest infrastructure investment and is nearing completion.

The Rooppur plant, located approximately 160 kilometers from the capital Dhaka, has begun fuel loading in its first unit as it approaches commercial operation. Russia has committed to long-term fuel supply, technical maintenance, and spent fuel management for the facility. Bangladesh currently imports roughly 95% of its energy needs, making energy security a critical national priority.

During the Moscow talks, Rahman expressed interest in expanded cooperation in space and nuclear energy technologies, while Lavrov offered Russian support for Bangladesh's potential membership in BRICS, the economic bloc positioning itself as an alternative to Western-led institutions. The meeting also addressed the humanitarian crisis of over one million Rohingya refugees from Myanmar currently sheltering in Bangladesh.

The deepening Bangladesh-Russia relationship comes amid international scrutiny of Moscow's actions in Ukraine and broader geopolitical concerns. Lavrov praised Bangladesh's "balanced stance" on the Ukraine conflict, a characterization that reflects the complex position many developing nations face as they balance energy and development needs against human rights considerations and international norms. The two countries plan to commemorate the 50th anniversary of diplomatic ties next year.
