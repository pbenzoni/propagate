In an interview with the Financial Times, Babiš framed the EU's transition away from fossil fuels as economic self-destruction, despite overwhelming scientific consensus on the urgency of climate action.

Babiš, whose ANO movement returned to power in December with 34.5% of the vote, has positioned himself as a vocal opponent of multilateral cooperation and evidence-based climate policy. His rhetoric comes as the EU faces mounting challenges including the ongoing fallout from Russia's invasion of Ukraine—a crisis that has actually accelerated Europe's push for renewable energy independence from authoritarian petrostates.

Babiš's position has put him at odds with Czech President Petr Pavel, a former NATO military chief and strong supporter of Ukraine. The clash between the two leaders reflects a deeper tension between nationalist populism and institutional cooperation—a divide that has become increasingly pronounced across Europe. While Babiš holds greater formal authority, Pavel remains an influential voice on matters of foreign policy and collective security, representing a competing vision for the Czech Republic's role in a democratic Europe facing authoritarian threats.
