The Hungarian government has leveled serious accusations against Facebook, claiming the social media giant is actively interfering in Sunday's parliamentary election by suppressing content from conservative Prime Minister Viktor Orban while boosting his left-wing opponent, Peter Magyar.

Government spokesman Zoltan Kovacs told Politico that Facebook's algorithm is "basically working against the government parties." A report from think tank MCC Brussels found that despite comparable video views, Magyar's posts generate nearly three times the engagement of Orban's. The study also documented a pattern of "disappearing comments" on content supporting Orban's Fidesz party—a phenomenon not observed on opposition pages. Orban's official government page faces stricter advertising limits and reduced organic reach, while Magyar operates a personal profile that enjoys greater algorithmic freedom.

This is not Facebook's first alleged interference in Hungarian politics. In late February, the platform temporarily blocked three pro-government news outlets, prompting the Hungarian National Media Association to condemn what it called an attack on press freedom. Last month, Fidesz members identified a Meta employee, Oskar Braszczynski, who serves as the company's 'Government and Social Impact Partner for Central and Eastern Europe,' as potentially responsible for the restrictions. Braszczynski's personal social media accounts reportedly feature pro-Ukraine, anti-Orban, and pro-LGBT content.

U.S. Vice President J.D. Vance visited Budapest this week in a show of support for Orban, calling the situation "one of the worst examples of foreign election interference" he has ever witnessed. Vance accused EU bureaucrats of attempting to "destroy the economy of Hungary" because of their opposition to Orban's conservative policies. Meta has denied the allegations, claiming "there are no restrictions on the prime minister's accounts, nor have any posts been removed."
