Palestinians commemorated the 78th anniversary of the Nakba on Friday—marking one of the largest forced displacement crises in modern history—as new reports indicate tens of thousands more families are being pushed from their homes in the West Bank.

The Nakba, Arabic for "catastrophe," refers to the mass displacement of Palestinians during the creation of Israel in 1948. While a 1947 UN partition plan called for the creation of separate Jewish and Arab states, only Israel was established, setting off decades of territorial conflict and humanitarian crises.

According to United Nations figures, approximately 40,000 Palestinians have been displaced in the West Bank since early 2025 due to intensified military operations, home demolitions, and settler violence. Members of Bedouin communities report systematic pressure to abandon their ancestral lands. "When we first came here, they told us this was Area B, Palestinian land. But then they turned around and told us, no, you are not allowed to stay here either," one resident told reporters. "Every single day, they come after us trying to force us out."

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov stated Friday that the prospects for Palestinian statehood—long supported by international consensus—are being "systematically undermined." He noted that current developments appear designed to "eliminate even the slightest possibility of creating a Palestinian state, in violation of United Nations decisions."
