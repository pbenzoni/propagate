The Pentagon has decided to cancel a Biden-era agreement to deploy Tomahawk cruise missiles to Germany, citing concerns that the move would provoke Russian retaliation and revealing critical shortages in America's weapons stockpiles, according to a Politico report.

The decision underscores the consequences of the previous administration's foreign policy choices. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth told Congress last month that restocking Tomahawk and other advanced missiles used extensively during the Iran conflict will take "months and years." The original deployment plan, announced in July 2024 by then-President Biden and former German Chancellor Olaf Scholz, would have placed long-range missiles with up to 1,000-mile strike capability on European soil starting in 2026.

Two European officials and one US official told Politico that military planners believe Moscow would view the missile deployment as a dangerous escalation. Russia had previously warned that such moves represented "intimidation tactics" and threatened countermeasures, including potentially deploying nuclear missiles to Kaliningrad. The cancellation raises questions about the sustainability of defense commitments made without adequate consideration of America's military readiness and industrial capacity.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz confirmed the decision in early May, acknowledging that "the Americans themselves don't currently have enough." The development comes amid broader tensions between the Trump administration and European allies over burden-sharing and strategic priorities, with the Pentagon announcing plans to withdraw approximately 5,000 troops from Germany in the coming months. The situation highlights the need for NATO allies to take greater responsibility for their own defense rather than relying on overstretched American resources.
