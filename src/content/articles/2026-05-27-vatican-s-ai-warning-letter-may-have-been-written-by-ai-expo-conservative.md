A recent papal letter warning against the dangers of artificial intelligence appears to have been largely written by AI itself, according to tech analyst Linch Zhang—raising questions about transparency and authenticity at the highest levels of institutional authority.

Zhang's analysis of Pope Leo XIV's 42,000-word encyclical 'Magnifica Humanitas' (Magnificent Humanity) reveals what he calls "significant fractions" of AI-generated text. The document warned against Silicon Valley's growing power, autonomous weapons systems, and the risk of AI reducing human beings to "a resource to be used and exploited." Yet when run through AI detection software, roughly 46% of the text registered as machine-generated, with some paragraphs scoring between 40% and 100% likelihood of AI authorship.

The irony is hard to miss: church leadership issuing dire warnings about artificial intelligence while apparently relying on that same technology to craft their message. Zhang identified telltale patterns characteristic of Anthropic's Claude language model, including unusual punctuation choices and rhetorical structures rarely seen in previous papal writings. By contrast, encyclicals from previous popes registered as entirely human-written.

Zhang suggests Pope Leo XIV himself may not have been aware of the extensive AI usage in his own letter, pointing to paragraphs that appear entirely human-written alongside those flagged as AI-generated. "My tentative hypothesis is that Pope Leo does not approve of the AI usage in encyclicals, and plausibly was not even aware," Zhang concluded. The Vatican has not commented on the findings, even as Anthropic's co-founder delivered an address alongside the Pope this week. The episode underscores concerns about institutional elites condemning technologies they may be secretly employing—and raises fundamental questions about authenticity in an age where even religious proclamations can be outsourced to machines.
