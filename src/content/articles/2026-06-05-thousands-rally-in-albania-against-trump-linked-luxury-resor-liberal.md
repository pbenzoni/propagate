Thousands of Albanians have taken to the streets of Tirana for four consecutive days, protesting a controversial $1.6 billion luxury resort project tied to Jared Kushner, Donald Trump's son-in-law. The massive development, spearheaded by Kushner's investment firm Affinity Partners, threatens the Vjosa-Narta protected area—a vital wetland ecosystem home to flamingos, endangered Mediterranean monk seals, and critical sea turtle nesting sites.

Environmental groups have sounded the alarm about the project's location on Sazan Island and along an undeveloped coastal stretch near the protected zone. Advocates warn that the high-end tourism development poses an existential threat to the fragile ecosystem, while also exposing deeper problems with government accountability and transparency in Albania.

The Albanian government has defended the investment as essential for attracting tourism revenue and supporting the country's bid to join the European Union. However, political analyst Nikola Vujinovic told reporters the controversy extends far beyond environmental concerns, reflecting wider anxieties about Prime Minister Edi Rama's close relationship with the Trump administration and mounting allegations of corruption and authoritarian governance practices.

The sustained protests highlight growing public resistance to development projects that prioritize profit over environmental protection, particularly when connected to politically controversial figures. As demonstrations continue, activists are calling for greater transparency and demanding that ecological preservation take precedence over luxury real estate ventures.
