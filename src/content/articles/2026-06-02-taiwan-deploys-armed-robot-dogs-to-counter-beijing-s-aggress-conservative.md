Taiwan has unveiled a new generation of weaponized robot dogs designed to bolster the island's defenses against mounting pressure from Communist China. The quadruped machines, demonstrated in Taipei this week, are intended for coastal patrols, reconnaissance missions, and high-risk military operations as tensions with Beijing continue to escalate.

The National Chung-Shan Institute of Science and Technology showcased three variants of the armed robots—reconnaissance, combat, and LiDAR-equipped models—capable of navigating difficult terrain in all weather conditions. Based on technology from American firm Ghost Robotics, the 115-pound machines can reach speeds of over five miles per hour and will support perimeter security, target tracking, and maritime surveillance.

Taipei has recently approved a special defense budget allocation of approximately $280 million for U.S. arms purchases, reflecting the urgent security needs facing the democratic island. Chinese President Xi Jinping has repeatedly threatened to use force to annex Taiwan, which Beijing claims as its territory despite the island's self-governance and distinct political system.

President Trump recently characterized arms deliveries to Taiwan as "a very good negotiating chip" with China while expressing support for maintaining the current status quo across the Taiwan Strait.
