Amid a nearly two-month standoff between Washington and Tehran, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has rejected President Trump's assertion that the United States holds all the leverage in their ongoing dispute. The disagreement comes as Pakistan-mediated negotiations have failed to produce meaningful progress toward de-escalation.

Trump claimed in a Fox News interview that "we have all the cards" and suggested Iran would soon face internal pressures from fuel storage capacity issues. Araghchi countered on social media, pointing to rising domestic fuel prices as evidence that American leverage may be overstated. "Add summer vacation to the [list of leverage] unless they want to cancel it for the US!" he wrote, highlighting potential economic pain for American consumers.

The standoff intensified following US-Israeli strikes in late February, prompting Iran to partially close the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz. In response, the Trump administration announced a blockade targeting Iranian oil exports and issued threats against Iran's infrastructure. According to reports, Iran has proposed a framework that would reopen the Persian Gulf while deferring discussions on its uranium enrichment program—a proposal that Araghchi reportedly conveyed through intermediaries rather than meeting directly with Trump's envoys.

Complicating matters further, the fragile ceasefire remains under strain due to continued Israeli military operations in Lebanon. Lebanon's Health Ministry reported at least 14 deaths from Israeli strikes over the weekend, with Beirut stating that more than 2,500 people have been killed since Israel escalated hostilities. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has justified continued strikes by accusing Hezbollah of "undermining the ceasefire," though the broader regional truce was expected to include a halt to operations in Lebanon as well.
