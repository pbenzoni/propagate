Germany's Interior Ministry is considering offering Syrian refugees up to $9,300 each to voluntarily return home, according to Focus magazine—a dramatic eightfold increase from current repatriation payments. The proposal comes as the nation grapples with the long-term consequences of former Chancellor Angela Merkel's controversial open-door migration policy during the 2014-2015 crisis.

More than 951,000 Syrians currently reside in Germany, with over 500,000 holding temporary residence permits tied to refugee status. Yet voluntary returns remain stubbornly low, leaving German taxpayers footing massive bills for accommodation and social benefits. Roman Poseck, interior minister of Hesse state, argued that even five-figure payments per refugee would prove cheaper than ongoing welfare costs. "Support payments in the four-figure range or sometimes even in the lower five-figure range would often still be a gain for the state when measured against the long-term costs of social benefits," he told Focus.

The proposal reflects mounting political pressure on Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who has seen his approval ratings plummet while the Alternative for Germany (AfD) party—which has campaigned aggressively on migration concerns—has surged to become the nation's most popular party in recent polling. Berlin now rejects 95% of new Syrian asylum applications, a stark reversal from the Merkel era.

Merz previously claimed that up to 80% of Syrians in Germany could return home over three years, though Syrian leader Ahmed al-Sharaa later disputed making such a statement. The debate underscores a broader reckoning across Europe over immigration policies that critics say prioritized ideological commitments over fiscal sustainability and public safety concerns.
