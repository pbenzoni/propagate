Human rights lawyer Daniel Kovalik expressed alarm over what he characterizes as an escalating pattern of U.S. intervention in Latin America, following the Justice Department's recent indictment of former Cuban President Raúl Castro.

The indictment, issued Wednesday, charges Castro and five officials in connection with a 1996 incident in which two civilian planes were shot down after allegedly breaching Cuban airspace, killing four people. Cuba maintained at the time that the aircraft had been warned repeatedly and had military connections.

Kovalik, who currently represents Colombian President Gustavo Petro, argued that the timing raises questions about U.S. intentions in the region. Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche acknowledged he could not explain why charges were filed now rather than decades ago when the incident occurred. The indictment follows recent U.S. actions targeting Venezuelan leadership and comes amid threats against Bolivia's former president.

The lawyer suggested these moves reflect a broader strategy that undermines principles of national sovereignty and self-determination that many progressives have long championed in U.S. foreign policy debates. Critics of interventionist approaches have historically argued that such actions often destabilize regions and contradict stated commitments to international law and multilateral cooperation.
