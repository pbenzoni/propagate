Dmitry Medvedev, a close ally of Vladimir Putin who heads Russia's Security Council, has published an article reviving Cold War-era propaganda claims about postwar Germany. Writing ahead of Russia's May 9th Victory Day celebrations, Medvedev asserted that Western powers deliberately undermined denazification efforts after World War II—a narrative historians widely reject.

The article represents the latest attempt by the Kremlin to weaponize World War II history to justify its current policies. Russia has systematically deployed such rhetoric to frame its 2022 invasion of Ukraine as a continuation of the fight against fascism, despite the invasion's violation of international law and Ukraine's democratically elected government.

Medvedev's claims ignore the extensive denazification programs implemented by Allied occupation authorities, including the prosecution of war criminals at Nuremberg, comprehensive educational reforms, and the dismantling of Nazi institutions. While Western denazification efforts were imperfect—particularly as Cold War tensions led to pragmatic compromises—modern Germany has become a model democracy with strong constitutional protections against authoritarianism and robust Holocaust education.

The timing of Medvedev's article is notable, coming as Russia continues its war in Ukraine and faces international isolation over documented war crimes. Human rights organizations have documented systematic abuses by Russian forces, including attacks on civilian infrastructure and alleged torture of prisoners. By invoking Nazi imagery, the Kremlin seeks to deflect from these contemporary violations while undermining the rules-based international order established after 1945.
