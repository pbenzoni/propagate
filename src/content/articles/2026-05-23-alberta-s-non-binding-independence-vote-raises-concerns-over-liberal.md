Canada's Alberta province will hold a non-binding public vote in October on whether to pursue steps toward independence, following a petition drive by the pro-independence group Stay Free Alberta that gathered nearly 302,000 signatures—far exceeding the required threshold of 177,732.

Premier Danielle Smith, who has stated she personally supports remaining in Canada, announced the October 19 vote would ask Albertans whether they want the province to stay in the country. While Smith stressed the ballot would not itself trigger separation, it would gauge public interest in holding a binding referendum later. The move comes despite significant legal and constitutional obstacles, including challenges from First Nations groups who argue that Alberta's potential secession would violate existing treaty rights.

Prime Minister Mark Carney responded by calling Alberta "essential" to Canada and pledging to build a stronger federation. The federal government has sought to address some of Alberta's grievances over energy policy and export market access, though tensions remain over environmental regulations and climate commitments. Legal experts note that even if a future binding referendum passed, Alberta could not unilaterally leave Canada—separation would require complex negotiations with the federal government and other provinces, while Indigenous communities' constitutional rights could present additional barriers.

Alberta has long been at odds with Ottawa over environmental regulations affecting its oil and gas sector, with separatist advocates claiming federal climate policies have constrained the province's resource economy. However, polling suggests support for full independence remains a minority position among Albertans, with many residents favoring reform of federal-provincial relations rather than separation.
