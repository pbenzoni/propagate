Greenland's health minister has sharply criticized an unsolicited visit by a US physician who traveled with American diplomatic officials to assess the island's healthcare system, calling the move "deeply problematic" and warning that "Greenlanders are not guinea pigs in a geopolitical project."

Health Minister Anna Wangenheim issued her rebuke after Dr. Joseph Griffin, part of a US delegation that included Ambassador Kenneth Howery and Trump envoy Jeff Landry, told Danish media he had been personally invited by Landry to "assess the medical needs" of Greenland. The visit comes amid repeated threats by President Trump to acquire the autonomous Danish territory, ostensibly for national security purposes.

While Wangenheim acknowledged legitimate healthcare challenges including staff shortages, she firmly rejected developing the system "through political envoys with hidden strategic interests." The incident has particular resonance given Greenland's colonial history: last year, Denmark's prime minister formally apologized for forcing contraceptive devices on thousands of Greenlandic women and girls without consent during the 1960s and 1970s.

Landry, Trump's special envoy to Greenland, has become a divisive figure on the island after backing Trump's proposal to send a US hospital ship—a plan Greenland officials rejected—and vowing to be "very aggressive in delivering healthcare to remote parts" of the territory. White House officials have previously stated that military force to acquire Greenland remains "always an option," framing the mineral-rich Arctic island as strategically vital against Russia and China. In apparent protest, Greenland's prime minister declined to attend the opening of a new US consular office in the capital, Nuuk.
