The State Department is reaching out to allied governments with a proposal to safeguard freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy markets. Secretary of State Marco Rubio directed U.S. embassies to present the plan, dubbed the Maritime Freedom Construct (MFC), to host nations this week.

According to reports from the Wall Street Journal and Reuters, the initiative would operate as a joint diplomatic and military effort coordinated by the State Department and CENTCOM. The cable emphasizes that "collective action is essential to demonstrate unified resolve and impose meaningful costs on Iranian obstruction of transit through the Strait." Notably, participating countries would not be required to commit military assets, keeping the threshold for involvement flexible.

The proposal explicitly excludes adversarial nations including Russia, China, Belarus, and Cuba from participation. This approach aligns with longstanding American efforts to build coalitions among free-world partners who share interests in keeping vital sea lanes open for commerce. Iran's recent actions—throttling shipping through the strait and launching attacks on U.S. partners in the region—underscore the strategic importance of maintaining a credible deterrent.

The MFC represents a diplomatic complement to the administration's maximum pressure campaign against Tehran. With approximately one-fifth of the world's petroleum passing through the Strait of Hormuz, ensuring its security remains a core American interest. The initiative comes amid broader tensions following Iran's aggression in the region and after a fragile ceasefire failed to produce lasting stability.
