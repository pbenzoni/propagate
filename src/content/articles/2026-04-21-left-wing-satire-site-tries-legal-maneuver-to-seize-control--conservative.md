The Onion, a satirical outlet owned by Global Tetrahedron, is pursuing a court-backed arrangement to take temporary control of Alex Jones' InfoWars platform, submitting a proposal that would grant them a six-month lease over the brand's intellectual property and operations at $81,000 per month.

The plan, filed in Texas state court, has backing from a court-appointed receiver and lawyers representing Sandy Hook families, though it still requires final judicial approval. The move comes as Jones faces more than $1 billion in defamation judgments stemming from his false statements about the 2012 Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting. The Supreme Court declined to hear his appeal, leaving the roughly $1.4 billion judgment in place.

Jones has strongly opposed the arrangement, arguing that parody does not entitle anyone to take over another platform's identity and potentially confuse viewers. He maintains that his team has consulted with lawyers and is pursuing legal action. Jones has also indicated that even if he loses control of InfoWars' current infrastructure, he and his team would continue broadcasting through what he described as an expanded "Alex Jones Network" and alternative websites already in development.

This represents The Onion's second attempt to gain control of InfoWars after a bankruptcy judge blocked their earlier auction bid in December 2024. A hearing on the licensing arrangement is scheduled for April 30 in Travis County, Texas, and Jones retains the right to appeal any unfavorable ruling.
