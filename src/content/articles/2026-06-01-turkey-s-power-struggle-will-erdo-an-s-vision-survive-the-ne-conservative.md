Turkey's political landscape is entering a critical phase as judicial proceedings against key opposition figures reveal the high stakes of upcoming elections. The arrest of Istanbul Mayor Ekrem Imamoglu on corruption charges in March 2025, combined with a court decision removing Ozgur Ozel from leadership of the opposition Republican People's Party (CHP), signals that Turkey's next electoral contest will be about more than routine politics—it will determine whether the nationalist system built over two decades survives.

Imamoglu's detention came at a strategically significant moment, just as the CHP prepared to name its presidential candidate. The Istanbul mayor had emerged as the opposition's strongest potential challenger to President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, capitalizing on urban discontent and demands for economic reform. Istanbul holds special importance in Turkish politics as the nation's economic engine and the city where Erdoğan himself launched his national political career.

The judicial removal of Ozel from CHP leadership—citing procedural violations at the party congress—and the restoration of previous leader Kemal Kilicdaroglu represents a setback for opposition renewal efforts. Ozel had taken over after Kilicdaroglu's 2023 presidential defeat and led the party to significant gains in 2024 municipal elections, demonstrating growing electoral strength.

For Turkey's current leadership, these moves reflect efforts to protect a strategic direction they view as essential to national interests. Over the past twenty years, Turkey has transformed itself into a more independent regional power, strengthening its defense industry, expanding military presence in neighboring regions, and pursuing a more autonomous foreign policy. The leadership sees the preservation of the presidential system, defense capabilities, and Turkey's assertive regional stance as dependent on electoral continuity. A change in power could reverse this entire trajectory, including Turkey's relationships with Russia, the West, and Middle Eastern partners.
