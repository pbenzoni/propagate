The Trump administration is reportedly considering a plan to purchase the Chagos Islands directly from Mauritius, bypassing the United Kingdom in negotiations over a strategically important military installation in the Indian Ocean, according to The Telegraph.

The move centers on Diego Garcia, a joint UK-US military base that houses approximately 2,500 American personnel. The facility has been used for bomber deployments and military operations in the Middle East. The islands' ownership has been disputed for decades, stemming from Britain's 1965 separation of the territory from Mauritius during decolonization—an act the International Court of Justice ruled unlawful in 2019, citing violations of the right to self-determination.

Last year, the UK agreed to transfer sovereignty of the islands to Mauritius while maintaining a 99-year lease on the base, a compromise that addressed the colonial legacy while preserving military operations. However, Trump—who initially supported the arrangement—reversed course in January, calling it "an act of great stupidity" and claiming it posed national security risks. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has reportedly presented the purchase proposal to Trump as one of several options under consideration.

The proposed deal would require Britain to first complete its handover to Mauritius, after which the US would negotiate a purchase with the Mauritian government. Mauritius has stated that its sovereignty over the archipelago is "non-negotiable," though it acknowledged awareness of the Telegraph report. The episode adds to growing tensions between the Trump administration and traditional allies, following similar proposals to acquire Greenland and repeated references to Canada as a potential "51st state."
