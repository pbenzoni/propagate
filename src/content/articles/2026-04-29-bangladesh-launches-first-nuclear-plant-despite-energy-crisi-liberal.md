Bangladesh has begun loading nuclear fuel at its first-ever nuclear power plant, the Rooppur facility constructed by Russia's state-owned Rosatom. The milestone marks the South Asian nation's entry into nuclear power generation, with the plant expected to supply 10% of the country's electricity needs once fully operational.

The $13 billion project—90% financed through a Russian state loan—will eventually house two reactors with a combined capacity of 2,400 megawatts. Bangladesh's Science and Technology Minister announced that the first reactor could begin commercial operations as early as August 2025, initially supplying approximately 300 megawatts to the national grid. Full-scale production is anticipated by late 2025 or early 2027.

The Rooppur project arrives as Bangladesh confronts a severe energy crisis exacerbated by global conflicts, including ongoing tensions in the Middle East. The country currently imports roughly 95% of its oil and gas from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Oman, and Iraq, while also receiving 15-17% of its electricity from neighboring India. Proponents argue the nuclear facility could reduce these dependencies and provide cleaner baseload power.

However, the plant's deep ties to Russia—including long-term contracts for fuel supply, technical maintenance, and spent fuel management—raise questions about energy sovereignty and geopolitical entanglements at a time when international alliances are increasingly scrutinized. Environmental and safety advocates have also expressed concerns about nuclear oversight in a region vulnerable to natural disasters and governance challenges, though Rosatom maintains the facility meets international safety standards.
