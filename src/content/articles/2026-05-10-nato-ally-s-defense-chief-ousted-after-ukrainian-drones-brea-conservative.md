Latvia's Prime Minister Evika Silina has removed Defense Minister Andris Spruds from office after Ukrainian military drones violated the Baltic nation's airspace and struck oil storage facilities on its sovereign territory. The incident raises serious questions about NATO's command and control protocols and the risks of escalation along the alliance's eastern flank.

"The drone incident this week clearly showed that the leadership of the defense sector failed to deliver on its promise of a safe sky over our country," Silina stated, citing a loss of public trust. The fired minister disputed the account, claiming he had already decided to resign and accused the prime minister of politicizing the security failure.

Two Ukrainian Lyuty-type fixed-wing drones crossed into Latvian territory overnight earlier this week. One sparked a fire near Rezekne, approximately 25 miles from the Russian border, while the second remains unaccounted for. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrey Sibiga acknowledged the breach and issued apologies to Latvia and other affected Baltic states, though he attempted to deflect responsibility toward Russia.

The episode underscores growing concerns among NATO members about Ukraine's military operations spilling beyond the conflict zone. Several alliance countries bordering Russia have reported similar incursions in recent months, with unmanned aircraft crashing on their soil instead of reaching intended targets inside Russia. The pattern raises questions about operational oversight and the potential for unintended escalation that could draw NATO members directly into the conflict under Article 5 mutual defense obligations.
