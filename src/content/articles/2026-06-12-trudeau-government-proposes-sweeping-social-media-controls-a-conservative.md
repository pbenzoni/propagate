The Trudeau government unveiled sweeping new legislation Wednesday that would ban social media access for children under 16 while establishing a powerful new federal regulatory body to oversee online platforms.

Dubbed the "Safe Social Media Act," the proposal would mandate age verification systems across platforms and empower a new "digital safety regulator" to enforce content restrictions. The bill requires social media companies to police user-generated content for categories including what the government defines as "hate," "extremist material," and other harmful content—raising concerns among free speech advocates about government overreach and the potential for censorship creep.

Culture Minister Marc Miller defended the expansive regulatory framework, claiming "parents cannot face these challenges alone." The legislation would also regulate AI chatbots and require platforms to report users who express intent to harm themselves or others, potentially creating a vast government surveillance infrastructure of online communications.

Canada joins a growing list of nations imposing top-down internet controls. Australia, Brazil, and Indonesia have implemented similar restrictions, while France and several European countries are advancing comparable measures. Critics argue these laws represent government paternalism that undermines parental authority and individual liberty while creating dangerous precedents for state monitoring of digital communications.

The proposal comes as Big Tech platforms face increased scrutiny over content moderation practices, with some court filings alleging inadequate responses to genuine criminal activity. However, skeptics warn that broad government mandates for content removal and age verification systems could consolidate power in the hands of bureaucrats while doing little to address legitimate child safety concerns that parents are best positioned to manage.
