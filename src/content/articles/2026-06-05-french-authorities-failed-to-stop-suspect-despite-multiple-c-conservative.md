A body discovered in southwestern France during the search for missing 11-year-old Lyhanna has exposed catastrophic failures in the French justice system. The 41-year-old suspect, a father of two, had faced multiple prior complaints involving the sexual abuse of minors—complaints that were either dropped or dismissed by authorities before the girl vanished.

Lyhanna disappeared on May 29 after witnesses reported seeing her enter the suspect's vehicle outside her school near Fleurance in the Gers region. Despite the man's acknowledgment that he gave the child a ride, his claim that he dropped her at a swimming pool was deemed "inconsistent" by prosecutors. He now faces formal investigation for kidnapping and confinement of a minor.

Prosecutors revealed a disturbing pattern of ignored warning signs. One complaint involving a teenager was dropped in 2018. Another alleging rape of a child under 15 was dismissed in 2024 for supposed lack of evidence. Most troubling, a complaint filed in August 2024 alleged the rape of a young girl—yet the suspect had not even been questioned when Lyhanna disappeared. "The system doesn't work," said Anne-Cecile Mailfert of the Women's Foundation, noting that a 10-year-old victim found the courage to report abuse but "nothing happened."

The case has ignited fury across France over the apparent prioritization of legal technicalities over child safety. Government spokeswoman Maud Bregeon called the emerging details "absolutely unbearable," questioning why victims' testimonies were given so little weight. The tragedy underscores broader concerns about soft-on-crime policies and bureaucratic failures that leave vulnerable children unprotected despite clear red flags and repeated cries for help from families and victims.
