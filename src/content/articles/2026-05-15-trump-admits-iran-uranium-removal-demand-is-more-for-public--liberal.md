President Donald Trump conceded in a Fox News interview Thursday that his administration's insistence on removing Iran's enriched uranium stockpile is "more for public relations than it is for anything else," an admission that calls into question the urgency behind a demand that has become a central obstacle in efforts to de-escalate tensions in the Middle East.

The president acknowledged that the United States maintains 24-hour surveillance on Iran's nuclear sites—where an estimated 400 kg of uranium enriched to 60% purity remains buried beneath rubble from last year's US-Israeli bombing campaign—saying "nobody's even gotten close to it." Despite this assurance, Trump indicated he would "just feel better" if the material were removed, while simultaneously threatening to "bomb it again" if negotiations fail to produce results on his timeline.

The zero-enrichment demand has deadlocked peace talks following a fragile ceasefire reached in April. Iran maintains its nuclear program serves solely peaceful purposes and views surrendering its uranium stockpile as an unacceptable infringement on its sovereignty. Notably, both US intelligence agencies and the International Atomic Energy Agency have previously stated they found no evidence Iran was actively developing nuclear weapons before the recent conflict—raising questions about whether diplomatic solutions focused on verification and transparency might prove more productive than maximalist demands that Tehran has consistently rejected.

Trump's public acknowledgment that the uranium removal issue is primarily about optics rather than immediate security concerns highlights a troubling pattern: the administration appears willing to perpetuate a military standoff over what the president himself characterizes as a symbolic matter, even as less confrontational pathways—such as Iran's reported offer to downblend its uranium to lower civilian-grade levels—remain unexplored.
