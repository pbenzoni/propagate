A German court has imposed a fine exceeding $2,000—equivalent to an average monthly salary—on a citizen for calling Chancellor Friedrich Merz a "lying Fritz" in an online comment. The case represents just one of 39 criminal proceedings launched by German authorities against Facebook users who criticized the chancellor, raising serious questions about government overreach and the erosion of free speech protections.

The prosecutions stem from October 2024, when local police issued a drone no-fly zone warning ahead of Merz's visit. Citizens responded with critical comments online, including calling him a liar, a "loose talker," and other unflattering terms. Authorities invoked Article 188 of the German Criminal Code, which criminalizes insults against people "engaged in public political life" if deemed likely to "significantly impede" their public activities. The court ruled that "lying Fritz" could "incite further negative prejudices or aggression among like-minded individuals."

Notably, Merz himself filed no complaints—these prosecutions were initiated by the state. When questioned, a government spokesman defended the actions as "normal legal procedures" that "must be protected," refusing further comment "out of respect for the judiciary." This official response has done little to calm concerns about state power being wielded to silence political dissent.

The crackdown comes as Merz ranks as the world's most unpopular political leader in recent polling, with reports suggesting his own party is considering replacing him due to historically low approval ratings. Critics warn these prosecutions fit a disturbing pattern: German regulators are reportedly planning to force social media platforms to elevate government-approved outlets in their algorithms, and even the European Union stated in April that Germany had misused hate-speech laws to restrict freedom of expression. For those who value constitutional protections and limited government, the message is clear: when the state can criminalize calling a politician a liar, no citizen's speech is truly free.
