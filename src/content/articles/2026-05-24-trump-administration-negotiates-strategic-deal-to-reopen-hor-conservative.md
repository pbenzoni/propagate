The Trump administration is finalizing a deal with Iran that would reopen the critical Strait of Hormuz to global shipping and temporarily lift restrictions on Iranian oil sales, according to American officials speaking with Axios. President Trump announced Saturday that a memorandum of understanding has been "largely negotiated" and is in final stages.

The proposed agreement would extend a fragile ceasefire by 60 days, during which the Strait of Hormuz—a chokepoint for roughly 25% of the world's crude oil—would reopen to all vessels without tolls. In exchange, the U.S. would remove its blockade on Iranian ports and permit unrestricted oil sales by Tehran. The two-month period would provide a window for negotiations on Iran's nuclear program, with the possibility of further extensions by mutual agreement.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei acknowledged the sides are moving toward a "convergence of views" but voiced Tehran's concerns about potential reversals by Washington. The current discussions "focus broadly on ending the war" and halting what Iran calls "US piracy and maritime banditry," Baghaei said in a television interview.

The temporary ceasefire follows a month of intense hostilities that began in early April, initiated by joint U.S. and Israeli action. According to American officials, the proposed deal would also include an end to Israel's military operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon. However, the lifting of broader economic sanctions and unfreezing of Iranian assets would only occur as part of a final, comprehensive agreement—not during the initial ceasefire period.
