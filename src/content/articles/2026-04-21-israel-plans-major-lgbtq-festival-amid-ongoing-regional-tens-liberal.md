Israel is promoting Pride Land, a four-day LGBTQ+ festival scheduled for July 2026 at the Dead Sea, as the "biggest LGBTQ+ festival ever in the Middle East." The event, set to take place in Ein Bokek, will feature performances, parties, exhibitions, and celebrations of queer culture, with organizers offering hotel-and-ticket packages for what they're marketing as a destination-style gathering.

The announcement comes against a complex backdrop of LGBTQ+ rights in Israel. While the country does not perform same-sex marriages domestically—marriage is administered through religious authorities rather than civil law—it does recognize same-sex marriages performed abroad. Israeli courts have extended recognition to same-sex couples in areas including adoption and family rights, making Israel relatively progressive on LGBTQ+ issues compared to neighboring countries in the region.

However, the festival's promotional framing as the "biggest ever" appears to be a marketing claim rather than an independently verified designation. Israel has previously promoted Tel Aviv Pride as one of the region's largest LGBTQ+ events, though that annual celebration was cancelled in both 2024 and 2025 due to the Gaza conflict and subsequent regional military operations.

The timing and scale of Pride Land raise questions about the intersection of LGBTQ+ celebration, tourism promotion, and regional politics. While advocates for LGBTQ+ rights may welcome expanded visibility and celebration, critics have historically pointed to concerns about "pinkwashing"—the strategic promotion of LGBTQ+-friendly policies to deflect attention from other contested policies and actions in the region.
