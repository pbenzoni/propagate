British Prime Minister Keir Starmer is under mounting pressure to resign after confirming that Peter Mandelson was appointed as UK Ambassador to the United States despite failing security clearance—and despite Mandelson's documented connections to convicted sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein.

Mandelson, who served as London's envoy in Washington from February to September 2025, was arrested earlier this year over allegations he leaked sensitive British government data to Epstein. Reports indicate Mandelson maintained friendly relations with Epstein even after the financier's 2008 imprisonment and received money from him. The Guardian reported Thursday that while the Cabinet Office rejected Mandelson's security vetting in 2024, the Foreign Office disregarded these recommendations and proceeded with the 72-year-old's appointment anyway.

Downing Street acknowledged the report but insisted Starmer was unaware of the security clearance problems when he told Parliament that "due process" had been followed. Conservative Party leader Kemi Badenoch called the Labour government's claims "completely preposterous," telling the BBC: "This story does not stack up. The prime minister is taking us for fools. All roads lead to a resignation." Liberal Democrat leader Ed Davey similarly insisted Starmer "must go" if he misled Parliament and the public.

Starmer is refusing to step down, claiming he was "absolutely furious" about not being informed of Mandelson's failed vetting. British media outlets remain skeptical of the prime minister's denials. The Times warned of "a new crisis for Starmer," while the Daily Mail declared "Starmer on brink as his Mandelson 'lies' are exposed." The Telegraph described the PM's position as "scarcely credible," adding that "he cannot survive."

The scandal has already forced multiple resignations from Starmer's inner circle, including his chief of staff Morgan McSweeney in February and communications director Tim Allan. Top Foreign Office civil servant Olly Robbins was sacked Thursday over the affair and has been summoned to give evidence on Tuesday. The Epstein document revelations have also ensnared members of the British royal family, with Prince Andrew facing allegations of "misconduct in public office" for allegedly supplying Epstein with sensitive documents.
