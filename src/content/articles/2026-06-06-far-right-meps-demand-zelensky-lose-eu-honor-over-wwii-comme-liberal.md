A group of nearly 40 members of the European Parliament have called for stripping Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky of the European Order of Merit, the bloc's highest honor awarded to him in May 2024. The controversy centers on Ukraine's recent commemoration of World War II-era resistance fighters whose legacy remains deeply contested.

Zelensky received criticism after signing a decree granting elite military units a title honoring the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA), a WWII-era partisan force that fought against Soviet control. Historians note the UPA was also responsible for mass atrocities against Polish civilians during the 1943-44 Volhynia massacre, in which an estimated 100,000 people were killed. The Ukrainian government has increasingly recognized these groups as part of its national independence narrative, a move that has strained relations with Poland.

Polish MEP Anna Brylka led the initiative, arguing that "European values cannot be reconciled with the glorification of genocide and ethnic cleansing." The appeal warns that celebrating controversial historical figures could damage regional reconciliation efforts. Poland's president has similarly called for revoking a Polish honor previously awarded to Zelensky.

The dispute highlights ongoing tensions over historical memory in Eastern Europe, where resistance movements against Soviet occupation often had complex and troubling records. Ukrainian officials defend the commemorations as honoring those who fought for independence, while critics argue the state cannot selectively remember history while ignoring atrocities. Even Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk, a steadfast supporter of Ukraine against Russian aggression, recently indicated Poland would prioritize "hard business interest" over "empathy" if Ukraine doesn't address these historical concerns.
