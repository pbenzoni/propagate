**U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has signed a one-year contract with Virginia-based security contractor MVM to assist in locating unaccompanied migrant children who were released into American communities while awaiting immigration court proceedings.** The Department of Homeland Security says the initiative aims to protect vulnerable minors from potential exploitation and abuse.

A DHS spokesperson emphasized that MVM has "zero immigration enforcement authority" and stressed that "the primary focus of this initiative is to conduct welfare checks on these children to ensure that they are safe and not being exploited or abused." The spokesperson called the partnership part of the agency's "commitment to protect vulnerable children from sexual abuse and exploitation."

The arrangement has drawn criticism from Democratic lawmakers and activists, who point to ongoing litigation against MVM. The contractor is currently being sued over its role in the 2017 separation of two Guatemalan children from their fathers, with allegations including improper treatment during transport and detention operations. Rep. Delia Ramirez (D-IL) called the company "dangerous bounty hunters" and demanded congressional oversight of the contract.

DHS firmly rejected claims that ICE is targeting children for arrest or deportation, calling such "accusations false and an attempt to demonize law enforcement." The agency maintains its focus remains on child welfare and safety, not enforcement actions against minors. The contract comes as federal authorities grapple with the complex challenge of ensuring the wellbeing of unaccompanied minors who entered the country and were placed with sponsors across the United States.
