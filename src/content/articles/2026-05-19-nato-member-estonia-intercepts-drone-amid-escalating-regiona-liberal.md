Estonia has intercepted an unmanned aerial vehicle over its territory for the first time, according to Defense Minister Hanno Pevkur, highlighting growing concerns about the regional security implications of the ongoing conflict between Russia and Ukraine.

The incident occurred over Lake Vortsjarv in southern Estonia after Latvian authorities provided advance warning about an incoming drone. A NATO Baltic Air Policing fighter jet—part of the alliance's mission to protect the airspace of Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania—successfully intercepted the aircraft. Pevkur indicated the drone was believed to be en route to Russian territory when it entered Estonian airspace.

Recent weeks have seen multiple incidents involving unmanned aircraft in the Baltic region, with several drones crashing in NATO member states including Latvia, Lithuania, and Finland. The pattern has raised alarm about the potential for unintended escalation and the safety risks posed by cross-border incursions. Estonian officials have emphasized the need for better coordination to prevent airspace violations that could destabilize the region.

The incident underscores the complex security challenges facing Baltic states as they balance their NATO commitments with the practical realities of their geographic proximity to an active conflict zone. Temporary flight restrictions were imposed at airports in the region following the incident, reflecting ongoing concerns about aviation safety and civilian protection.
