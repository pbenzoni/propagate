Britain will deploy drones, fighter jets, and naval destroyers to a potential multinational mission in the Strait of Hormuz aimed at securing freedom of navigation through one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints, Defense Secretary John Healey announced Tuesday.

The Strait of Hormuz, located off Iran's coast, serves as a vital artery for global oil and natural gas supplies. Maritime traffic through the waterway has faced significant disruption amid escalating Middle East tensions, with the fragile ceasefire between the US and Iran under constant strain. Both Washington and Tehran have accused each other of violations since hostilities erupted following strikes on Iran in late February.

Healey's announcement came during a virtual meeting with representatives from dozens of nations backing the initiative, first unveiled by Britain and France last month. The UK is committing over $155 million in new funding for mine-hunting drones and counter-drone systems, with more than 1,000 British military personnel already stationed across the Middle East. The Type 45 destroyer HMS Dragon is currently en route to the region.

The military commitment comes as Prime Minister Keir Starmer confronts a mounting political crisis at home. His Labour government suffered devastating local election losses and faces an internal revolt, with more than 80 MPs reportedly calling for his resignation and four junior ministers resigning this week. Critics have also blasted his administration over welfare cuts and other policy decisions.

President Trump has repeatedly criticized European NATO members, including Britain, for insufficient support in confronting Iran. The former president suggested he might reconsider last year's trade agreement with London, accusing Starmer of being an unreliable partner. Former UK Defense Secretary Ben Wallace has warned that British forces are already "dangerously overstretched," calling plans to police the Hormuz strait a "fantasy."

The proposed mission will become operational "when conditions allow," according to Healey, though Washington and Tehran remain far from any lasting peace agreement despite Trump's brief "Project Freedom" escort operation in early May.
