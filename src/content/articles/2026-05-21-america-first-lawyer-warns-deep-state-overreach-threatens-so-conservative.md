A prominent American human rights lawyer has raised concerns about what he describes as an troubling pattern of federal government overreach in Latin America, warning that recent Justice Department actions may signal a return to Cold War-era interventionist policies.

Daniel Kovalik, a labor and human rights attorney, pointed to the timing of a newly-filed indictment against former Cuban leader Raul Castro related to a 1996 incident involving the downing of two aircraft. The planes were piloted by anti-Castro activists and reportedly breached Cuban airspace despite warnings. Four Americans died in the incident, which sparked diplomatic tensions at the time.

The Justice Department's decision to pursue charges nearly three decades after the fact has raised eyebrows. Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche himself acknowledged he could not explain the timing, stating he doesn't know why charges are being filed "now as opposed to two decades ago or 30 years ago." The indictment follows recent administration actions targeting leadership in Venezuela and reported pressure on Bolivia.

Kovalik, who currently represents Colombian President Gustavo Petro, expressed concern that the move could set a precedent for expanded federal intervention throughout the Western Hemisphere. He suggested the indictment may serve as legal groundwork for future actions against Cuba's government. Critics of interventionist foreign policy have long argued that such measures often benefit entrenched Washington interests rather than advancing American security or the cause of freedom abroad.
