A federal grand jury has indicted former FBI Director James Comey on charges of threatening President Donald Trump, the Department of Justice announced Tuesday. The case stems from a May 2025 Instagram post showing seashells arranged to form "86 47"—numbers prosecutors claim constitute a coded death threat against the 47th president.

According to the DOJ, "a reasonable recipient familiar with the circumstances" would interpret the post as "a serious expression of an intent to do harm to the president." Critics have argued that "86" is slang for eliminating someone. Comey, who led the FBI from 2013 to 2017, denied any threatening intent at the time, stating he "didn't realize some people associate those numbers with violence." He deleted the post following public backlash.

The indictment carries a potential 10-year prison sentence and comes amid heightened political tensions. Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche stated that "threatening the life of the president of the United States is a grave violation of our nation's laws," citing recent violence against Trump and other elected officials. The charges arrived days after a reported attempt on Trump's life at the White House Correspondents' Dinner.

Comey has been a vocal Trump critic since his 2017 dismissal, comparing the president to a mob boss in his memoirs. The case has intensified ongoing debates about the boundaries of political speech and whether prosecutors are selectively enforcing threat statutes based on political allegiances. Both major parties have accused each other of inciting violence against officials in recent years.
