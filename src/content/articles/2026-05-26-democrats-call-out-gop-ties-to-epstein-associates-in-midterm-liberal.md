In Ohio's Senate race, incumbent Democrat Sherrod Brown has aired advertisements pointing out that his Republican challenger Jon Husted accepted over $116,000 from billionaire Leslie Wexner, described as "one of Epstein's closest friends and co-conspirators." The ad notes that Husted accepted a maximum donation from Wexner shortly before voting against releasing the Epstein files. Similar messaging has appeared in Maine, where Democratic candidate Graham Platner has criticized Republican Senator Susan Collins for allegedly prioritizing what he calls "the Epstein class" over constituents.

Senator Jon Ossoff has also characterized the Trump administration as representing a corrupt elite connected to the disgraced financier. "This is the Epstein class ruling our country," Ossoff stated at a February rally, echoing concerns about accountability and transparency in government.

The controversy has created tensions within both parties. The Trump administration has faced bipartisan criticism over heavily redacted materials released from the Epstein files, with critics from across the political spectrum accusing the Department of Justice of withholding key information. Even within the GOP, Representative Thomas Massie—who co-sponsored the Epstein Files Transparency Act with Democrat Ro Khanna—suggested his advocacy for disclosure created friction with Trump allies before losing his Kentucky primary to a Trump-endorsed challenger.
