The Pentagon has approached General Motors and Ford about converting civilian manufacturing capacity to produce weapons and military equipment, according to a Wall Street Journal report citing unnamed sources. The discussions, which involved senior executives from both automakers, centered on whether—and how quickly—existing factories could be redirected toward munitions production.

The push comes as the administration seeks to replenish weapons stockpiles depleted by support for Ukraine and ongoing military operations in the Middle East. Officials have framed the initiative as putting industry on a "wartime footing," evoking World War II-era mobilization when Detroit automakers suspended civilian vehicle production to manufacture military aircraft and equipment.

The reported conversations are part of a broader pattern of escalating defense spending. President Trump has requested approximately $1.5 trillion in military funding for fiscal year 2027—a 50% increase from the current year's nearly $1 trillion budget. The proposal includes over $1.1 trillion in base defense funding plus additional allocations tied to active military operations.

Critics have raised concerns about the pace and scale of weapons deployment. Military operations in Iran alone have reportedly consumed more than 850 Tomahawk cruise missiles in the first four weeks—a rate that has alarmed some Pentagon officials, according to the Washington Post. Independent trackers estimate the Iran conflict has cost nearly $48 billion since it began, with some former lawmakers claiming daily expenditures approach $2 billion.
