The Senate voted Tuesday to preserve President Donald Trump's constitutional authority as Commander-in-Chief, rejecting a Democrat-led effort to constrain his options for dealing with the communist dictatorship in Cuba. The 51-47 vote defeated a war powers resolution introduced by Virginia Senator Tim Kaine that would have required congressional approval before any military action against the island nation.

The Republican majority ruled the measure out of order, noting correctly that there are no active hostilities with Cuba. President Trump has pursued an aggressive "peace through strength" strategy toward hostile regimes in America's backyard, including tightening the decades-old embargo on Cuba's socialist government through an oil blockade announced in February. The administration has threatened sanctions against any country or company shipping crude to Havana, aiming to pressure the regime that has oppressed its people for over six decades.

Trump has made clear his commitment to freedom in the Western Hemisphere, pledging "a new dawn for Cuba" and suggesting the communist government could be "next" after concluding operations against Iran. The President pointed to what he called the "very, very successful" results of his tough approach, including the January operation that brought Venezuelan strongman Nicolas Maduro and his wife into custody.

Cuba has experienced nationwide blackouts and fuel shortages in recent months after Venezuela—once its primary oil supplier—halted shipments under American pressure. Russia has attempted to undermine the blockade, with a tanker reportedly delivering 100,000 tons of crude in late March. President Trump later acknowledged the delivery, saying Washington doesn't "mind having somebody get a boat load" to the island because "they need to survive," while maintaining maximum pressure on the regime itself.
