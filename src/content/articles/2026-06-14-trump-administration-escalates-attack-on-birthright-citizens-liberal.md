The State Department has announced a renewed crackdown on what it terms "illegal birth tourism," revoking hundreds of visas and targeting networks in Africa and Europe. The effort, which allows consular officers to deny visitor visas to pregnant travelers suspected of planning to give birth in the United States, represents an escalation of policies first introduced during Trump's previous term.

While officials frame the initiative as combating visa fraud, civil liberties advocates warn it threatens birthright citizenship—a constitutional right guaranteed by the 14th Amendment since 1868. That amendment, adopted to secure citizenship for formerly enslaved people and their descendants after the Civil War, grants automatic citizenship to nearly anyone born on U.S. soil, regardless of parental nationality.

Critics note that so-called "birth tourism" accounts for an estimated less than 1% of the 3.6 million births recorded annually in the United States. They argue the administration is using a statistically negligible phenomenon to justify unprecedented scrutiny of pregnant travelers and lay groundwork for broader attacks on birthright citizenship—a constitutional protection that has stood for more than 150 years.

The renewed enforcement comes amid the Trump administration's broader push for restrictive immigration policies and heightened visa screening. Civil rights organizations have raised concerns that empowering consular officers to determine travelers' "primary purpose" creates opportunities for discriminatory enforcement, particularly affecting women of color and those from countries the administration has targeted in its immigration crackdown.
