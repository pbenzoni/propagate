Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam has accused Israel of committing war crimes following the killing of journalist Amal Khalil in southern Lebanon on Wednesday. Khalil, a reporter for Lebanon's Al-Akhbar newspaper, died in an Israeli strike on a house in the village of al-Tiri where she had sought shelter after an earlier attack targeted her vehicle. Her colleague Zeinab Faraj was seriously wounded, and Israeli fire initially prevented rescue teams from recovering Khalil's body for hours.

In a statement on social media, Salam argued that such incidents could no longer be dismissed as isolated, saying "targeting journalists, obstructing access to them by relief teams, and even targeting their locations again after these teams arrive constitute clearly defined war crimes." He pledged that Lebanon would pursue accountability through international forums. Lebanese Information Minister Paul Morcos echoed these concerns, calling the killing "a flagrant violation of international and humanitarian law."

The incident occurred despite a ceasefire agreement, with Israeli forces continuing operations in southern Lebanon and maintaining an expanded security zone that prevents many residents from returning home. The Israel Defense Forces denied intentionally targeting journalists and said the incident was under review, claiming those in the vehicles posed an "imminent threat" to Israeli troops.

Khalil's death is part of a broader pattern of attacks on media workers in the region. At least nine journalists have been killed in Lebanon this year, according to the Associated Press, including three killed in a single strike on a clearly marked press vehicle in late March. Lebanese authorities report that more than 2,300 civilians have been killed since the latest escalation began, including at least 254 women and 168 children. Press freedom advocates have long raised concerns about the safety of journalists covering conflicts in the region.
