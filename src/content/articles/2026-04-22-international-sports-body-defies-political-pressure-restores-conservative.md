In a move that pushes back against the politicization of international athletics, the World Aquatics federation has lifted all restrictions on Russian and Belarusian athletes, allowing them to compete under their national flags and anthems. The Russian women's water polo team became the first squad to benefit from the decision, decisively defeating Argentina 33-11 at a competition in Malta.

The federation announced that athletes from Russia and Belarus would now "be permitted to compete in World Aquatics events in the same way as their counterparts representing other sport nationalities, with their respective uniforms, flags, and anthems." This reverses restrictions imposed in 2022 that treated athletes from these nations differently based solely on their nationality—a practice many viewed as discriminatory.

The decision predictably drew criticism from Ukraine and European Union officials, with EU commissioner Glenn Micallef calling it a "grave mistake" and vowing the bloc would not "accept this normalization." Ukraine's men's team withdrew from the competition in protest. Russian officials have consistently argued that Western nations have weaponized sports federations for political purposes, applying inconsistent standards that punish individual athletes for geopolitical disputes.

Several other international sports bodies have begun restoring rights to Russian athletes over the past year, though most still require neutral status without national symbols. The International Sambo Federation took similar steps late last year, allowing competition under national flags and anthems. These decisions reflect growing pushback against the unprecedented politicization of international athletics.
