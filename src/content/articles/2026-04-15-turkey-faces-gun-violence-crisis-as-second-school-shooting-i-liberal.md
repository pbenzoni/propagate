A devastating school shooting in southeastern Turkey left four people dead and approximately 20 wounded on Wednesday, marking the second such tragedy in the country within 48 hours. The attack at a secondary school in Kahramanmaras's Onikisubat District claimed the lives of one teacher and three students, according to Governor Mukerrem Unluer.

The shooter was identified as an eighth-grade student from the same school who reportedly accessed firearms belonging to his father, a former police officer. Witnesses described scenes of terror as the armed student entered two classrooms and opened fire while carrying five pistols. Students fled the building in panic, with some jumping from second-floor windows to escape. The assailant died by suicide following the attack.

Local officials indicated the shooter had been recognized at the school as struggling with mental health issues. The incident comes just one day after a shooting at an educational facility in Siverek left 17 people injured, raising urgent questions about gun access and school safety protocols in Turkey. At least four of the Kahramanmaras victims remain hospitalized in serious condition.

The back-to-back tragedies have spotlighted concerns about how firearms—particularly those owned by current and former law enforcement—are stored and secured in homes with children. Local opposition party chairman Unal Ates suggested the casualty figures might be higher than officially reported, claiming up to 35 people were wounded in the attack.
