Hungary has agreed to end its blockade of Ukraine's European Union membership negotiations after Kyiv committed to protecting the rights of its ethnic Hungarian minority population. The breakthrough came under Hungary's new Prime Minister Peter Magyar, who announced a "historic agreement" on Friday covering education, linguistic, cultural, and political rights.

An estimated 80,000 ethnic Hungarians live in Ukraine, primarily in the western Zakarpattia region. Under the new agreement, Ukraine has pledged to incorporate minority protections into its EU Minority Action Plan, with ongoing monitoring by European institutions. Magyar emphasized that Ukraine's accession process "will be a long and complex process," similar to Montenegro's path which began in 2012.

The minority rights issue had been a persistent source of tension between the two countries, particularly under former Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán. Budapest had previously raised concerns about the treatment of ethnic Hungarians, including reports of forced military conscription. Ukrainian authorities have faced criticism over aggressive draft enforcement practices, with families alleging that conscription officers have ignored medical exemptions and health conditions.

The agreement represents a significant diplomatic step forward for Ukraine's EU aspirations, though full membership remains years away. Magyar made clear that progress will depend on Ukraine's continued compliance with minority protection commitments, stating that failure to fulfill these obligations could halt advancement in the accession process.
