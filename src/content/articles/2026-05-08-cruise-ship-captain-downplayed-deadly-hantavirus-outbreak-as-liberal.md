Newly released video footage reveals that the captain of the cruise ship MV Hondius told passengers that a death aboard the vessel was from "natural causes" and "not infectious"—just before a deadly hantavirus outbreak was confirmed that ultimately claimed three lives.

The footage from April 12 shows Captain Jan Dobrogowski informing passengers that a man had died but assuring them the ship was safe. "Whatever health issues he was struggling with, I'm told by the doctor, were not infectious, so the ship is safe when it comes to that," he said. However, the victim—a 70-year-old Dutch man—had contracted the Andes strain of hantavirus, a rare pathogen that, unlike most hantaviruses, can spread between humans through close contact.

The outbreak aboard the Dutch-flagged vessel, which departed Argentina on April 1 with some 150 passengers from 23 countries, has now resulted in three confirmed deaths and at least eight suspected or confirmed cases. The first victim's wife died in a Johannesburg hospital on April 26, and a German woman succumbed to the disease on May 2. Health authorities are now urgently trying to locate approximately 29 passengers who disembarked at earlier ports before the outbreak was identified.

Investigators believe the initial infections occurred during a bird-watching excursion at a landfill site in Ushuaia, Argentina, where participants likely came into contact with infected rodent droppings. After being denied docking at Cape Verde and the Canary Islands due to health concerns, the MV Hondius has been permitted to proceed to Tenerife, where unaffected passengers will be transported home and Spanish nationals will be quarantined.
