Spain is pushing the European Union to stand up against the Trump administration's sanctions targeting the International Criminal Court, which issued arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu over alleged war crimes in Gaza.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez warned that "sanctioning those who defend international justice puts the entire human rights system at risk," calling on the European Commission to activate protective measures for the ICC and United Nations. The court, recognized by 125 countries worldwide, issued warrants in 2024 for Netanyahu and former Defense Minister Yoav Gallant related to alleged atrocities against Palestinians.

Trump imposed sanctions on the ICC starting in February 2025, attacking the Hague-based tribunal for what he called "illegitimate" actions against the US and Israel. Subsequent measures have restricted ICC judges and prosecutors from accessing the US financial system. Neither the United States nor Israel are signatories to the Rome Statute that established the court.

Spain has emerged as one of the EU's strongest voices criticizing Israeli military operations, with Sánchez describing recent airstrikes as "intolerable" and accusing Netanyahu of showing "contempt for life and international law." Madrid has called for suspending the EU's association agreement with Israel and denied Washington permission to use joint military bases for operations against Iran.

Despite Trump's threats to suspend all trade with Spain in retaliation, Sánchez has remained defiant. "We are not going to be complicit in something that is bad for the world simply out of fear of reprisals," the prime minister declared in March, positioning his country as a defender of international humanitarian law against authoritarian pressure.
