During President Trump's first state visit to China in nearly a decade, Secretary of State Marco Rubio emphasized that U.S. policy on Taiwan remains unchanged, pushing back against Chinese leader Xi Jinping's warnings about potential conflict over the island democracy.

In an interview with NBC News on Friday, Rubio made clear that any attempt by Beijing to forcibly seize Taiwan would constitute "a terrible mistake" with repercussions extending far beyond U.S.-China relations. The remarks came as Xi identified Taiwan as "the most important issue" in bilateral relations, cautioning that mishandling could lead the two nuclear powers toward confrontation.

The exchange highlights the delicate balance Washington seeks to maintain in the Indo-Pacific region. Rubio reaffirmed the U.S. commitment to "strategic ambiguity"—the longstanding policy of not explicitly stating whether America would militarily defend Taiwan—while simultaneously signaling strong opposition to any unilateral change in the status quo. He acknowledged that Beijing would prefer voluntary reunification through democratic means like a referendum, though noted the current policy framework spans multiple administrations.

While China considers Taiwan part of its territory, the self-governing island of 23 million people maintains its own democratic government and robust civil society. The Trump administration's visit agenda included discussions on trade, artificial intelligence rivalry, and the Iran conflict, though Rubio indicated that U.S. arms sales to Taiwan—a frequent point of contention—did not feature prominently in the talks. Any future weapons transfers, he noted, would require Congressional approval.
