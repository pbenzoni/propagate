A member of Iran's negotiating team has claimed that Vice President J.D. Vance lacked independent decision-making authority during recent nuclear talks in Pakistan, according to statements made to RT's Sanchez Effect.

Professor Seyed Mohammad Marandi, who participated in the April 11-12 negotiations in Islamabad, told host Rick Sanchez that the American delegation—which included Vance, special envoy Steve Witkoff, and presidential adviser Jared Kushner—appeared unable to make commitments without frequent consultation. "Vance did not have the authority to make decisions," Marandi said. "He was constantly on the phone, making a dozen calls, including apparently to [Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin] Netanyahu."

The 21-hour talks, which also involved Iranian parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, concluded without agreement on Iran's nuclear program or the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. The negotiations were followed by a U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports, and further talks have stalled.

Marandi suggested that President Trump may be receiving poor counsel, stating "some of his decisions defy logic" and claiming Washington leaders "believe their own propaganda." The Iranian official indicated that other nations, including Russia, might serve as mediators, noting that Iran's foreign minister traveled to St. Petersburg to meet with President Vladimir Putin as part of efforts to coordinate with regional allies during the ongoing standoff with the United States and Israel.
