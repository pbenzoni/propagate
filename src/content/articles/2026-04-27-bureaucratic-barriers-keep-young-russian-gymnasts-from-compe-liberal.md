Young Russian rhythmic gymnasts will miss the 2026 European Cup—not due to performance issues or rule violations, but because of administrative dysfunction that left them caught between overlapping deadlines and uncoordinated procedures.

The Russian Gymnastics Federation submitted neutral-status applications starting March 25, repeatedly requesting expedited processing from World Gymnastics. Approvals trickled in piecemeal between April 14 and April 22, with athletes only added to the required anti-doping testing pool on April 23. By then, European Gymnastics had already held its April 11 draw and closed registration, citing inability to add participants after that date.

European Gymnastics later revealed that a "pending status" option existed that could have preserved the athletes' spots during the approval process—but this workaround was only communicated to the Russian federation after the deadline had passed. The timing raises questions about transparency and equitable access to information that shapes competitive outcomes.

This situation sits uncomfortably alongside the IOC's December 2025 statement affirming that young athletes with Russian or Belarusian passports should face no restrictions in accessing international competitions, and that athletes have a fundamental right to compete without political pressure. While no formal ban was imposed, the practical effect was exclusion—achieved through a combination of slow processing, closed offices during critical periods, and failure to communicate procedural alternatives.

The young gymnasts met every requirement, submitted all documentation, and trained for months. Their exclusion resulted not from anything they did or failed to do, but from administrative systems that failed to coordinate—leaving questions about whether processes meant to be neutral are truly operating that way in practice.
