In a striking reversal, prominent conservative commentator Tucker Carlson has issued a public apology for his role in helping elect Donald Trump, acknowledging that he and millions of supporters ignored warning signs about the former president's character. Speaking on a podcast with his brother Buckley Carlson, a former Trump campaign speechwriter, Carlson said he expects to be "tormented for a long time" by the consequences of his advocacy.

"You and I and everyone else who supported him… we're implicated in this for sure," Carlson said. "It's not enough to say, well, I changed my mind or like, oh, this is bad, I'm out… In very small ways, but in real ways, you and me and millions of people like us are the reason this is happening right now." Carlson apologized for "misleading people," while emphasizing that the deception was unintentional but that he still bore responsibility for the outcome.

The podcast conversation also scrutinized the influence of money in politics, with the Carlson brothers questioning a $250 million donation from casino magnate Miriam Adelson to Trump's campaign. They raised concerns about what the Israeli-American billionaire and prominent donor to pro-Israel causes received in return for such an unprecedented contribution. "If Russia had given a PAC for Trump... would that have been acceptable? Of course it wouldn't have been," Buckley Carlson noted.

Carlson's public reckoning comes amid a broader fracturing within Trump's former base, as several once-loyal supporters have distanced themselves from the president over perceived broken campaign promises. Trump has responded to criticism with personal attacks, calling detractors "stupid people" with "low IQs" and describing Carlson as a "flailing fool" who is no longer part of the MAGA movement.
