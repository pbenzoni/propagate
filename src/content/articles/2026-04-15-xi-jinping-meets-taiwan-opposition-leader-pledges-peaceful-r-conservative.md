Chinese President Xi Jinping met with Taiwan's main opposition leader this week in a high-profile diplomatic engagement that underscores the delicate balance of power in the Taiwan Strait. Kuomintang (KMT) chairwoman Cheng Li-wun traveled to the mainland at Xi's invitation, marking the first such peace-building mission in a decade.

During their meeting at Beijing's Great Hall of the People, Xi emphasized that "no matter how the international landscape and the situation across the Taiwan Strait may evolve, the overarching trend toward the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation will not change." The Chinese leader pledged that Beijing would "spare no effort" to promote peaceful relations across the strait, while identifying forces promoting "Taiwan independence" as the primary cause of regional tensions.

Cheng's visit drew sharp criticism from Taiwan's ruling Democratic Progressive Party (DPP), but the KMT leader defended the trip as essential diplomacy. "Hopefully... the Taiwan Strait will no longer be a geopolitical flashpoint and will never be a chessboard for interference by external forces," she said, appearing to reference concerns about foreign powers using the island as a leverage point against Beijing.

Both leaders affirmed their commitment to the 1992 Consensus, which acknowledges that there is only one China—a principle rejected by Taiwan's current DPP leadership under Lai Ching-te, who views it as constraining the island's autonomy. The diplomatic engagement comes as Xi prepares for an expected summit with President Donald Trump, previously postponed due to the Iran conflict.
