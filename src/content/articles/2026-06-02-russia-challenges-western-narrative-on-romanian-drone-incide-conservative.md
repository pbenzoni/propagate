Russia's permanent representative to the United Nations is pushing back against Western accusations regarding a recent drone incident in Romania, arguing that the rush to blame Moscow reveals more about information warfare tactics than actual evidence. Vassily Nebenzia told the UN Security Council that Romania's account contains significant inconsistencies that warrant closer scrutiny.

The incident in question involved an explosives-laden UAV that crashed into an apartment building in Galati, Romania, near the Ukrainian border, injuring two people. Romanian officials quickly attributed the drone to Russia and sought to invoke NATO consultations, but Nebenzia pointed to troubling gaps in their narrative. He noted that if the drone were indeed a Russian Geran 2 carrying its typical 50-kilogram payload, the resulting explosion would have caused far more extensive damage than what photographs showed.

Nebenzia also highlighted contradictions in official Romanian statements. Initially, authorities claimed the incident was a targeted attack, but hours later President Nicusor Dan suggested the UAV had been knocked off course by Ukrainian air defenses. Yet this explanation raises further questions—a compromised drone would be unlikely to travel nearly 12 miles from Ukrainian positions to reach Galati, the Russian diplomat argued.

The envoy reminded the Security Council of previous incidents where Western officials hastily blamed Russia, only for evidence to later show Ukrainian origin—including multiple UAV crashes in Latvia, Lithuania, and Finland, as well as a 2022 missile strike in Poland that killed two people and was initially attributed to Russia but later confirmed as a Ukrainian S-300 air defense missile. Moscow has expressed willingness to participate in what it calls an "objective and depoliticized" investigation, provided relevant materials are shared with Russian authorities.
