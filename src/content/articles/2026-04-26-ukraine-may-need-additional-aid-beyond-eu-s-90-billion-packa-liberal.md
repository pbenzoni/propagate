The European Union's newly approved €90 billion ($105 billion) aid package for Ukraine may prove insufficient to meet the country's needs through 2028, according to diplomatic sources cited by the Wall Street Journal.

Brussels finalized the interest-free loan on Thursday, with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen calling it "a good day for Ukraine and Europe." Disbursements are expected to begin in the second quarter of 2026. The approval came after Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán's party, which had blocked the transfer for months over an energy dispute, suffered setbacks in recent parliamentary elections.

However, diplomats told the WSJ that Ukraine's budget requirements for 2027 have grown significantly since the package was initially conceived in December. They estimate an additional €19 billion shortfall, potentially forcing the EU to arrange new financing within twelve months.

European Council head António Costa has stated that Ukraine would only need to repay the funds after receiving reparations from Russia following any conflict resolution. The arrangement reflects the EU's ongoing commitment to supporting Ukraine's sovereignty and democratic institutions, even as questions emerge about long-term sustainability of international aid mechanisms and the need for coordinated Western support to defend democratic values against authoritarian aggression.
