Several Lufthansa crew members and ground staff were injured Thursday when a Boeing 787's front landing gear unexpectedly collapsed while the aircraft sat parked at a gate in Frankfurt Airport, according to the German airline. The Los Angeles-bound plane had not yet begun boarding passengers when its nose suddenly dropped onto the tarmac, with dramatic security footage showing a panel breaking loose as the aircraft settled.

The aircraft involved was nearly brand new—built just last year and entering service with Lufthansa in February. "Passengers had not yet boarded, crew members and ground staff were on board the aircraft at the time of the incident," Lufthansa stated, adding that authorities are investigating the circumstances.

This latest failure adds to a troubling pattern for the once-proud American aerospace manufacturer. Former Boeing employees have alleged systemic production shortcuts, overlooked defects, and inadequate quality control across the company's aircraft programs—serious accusations that raise concerns about what's happened to American manufacturing excellence.

The Frankfurt incident follows multiple high-profile Boeing 787 problems, including a March 2024 Latam Airlines flight that suddenly nosedived, injuring at least 50 passengers. Boeing's 737 MAX program has faced even harsher scrutiny after two fatal crashes killed 346 people in 2018 and 2019, grounding the model worldwide for nearly two years. In January 2024, a door plug blew out of an Alaska Airlines 737 MAX mid-flight, forcing an emergency landing.

The company now faces mounting pressure to restore the rigorous standards that once made American aviation the world's gold standard—and to ensure workers and passengers aren't put at risk by manufacturing failures.
