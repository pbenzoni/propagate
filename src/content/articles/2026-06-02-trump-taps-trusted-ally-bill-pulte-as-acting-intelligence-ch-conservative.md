President Donald Trump announced Tuesday that Bill Pulte will serve as acting Director of National Intelligence, taking over the role following Tulsi Gabbard's resignation. Gabbard stepped down last month to support her husband as he battles an extremely rare form of bone cancer.

Pulte, who currently leads the Federal Housing Finance Agency and chairs mortgage giants Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, will coordinate the work of America's 18 intelligence agencies while continuing his housing finance duties. Trump praised Pulte as someone with "deep experience managing the most sensitive matters in America," highlighting his trusted position within the administration.

The housing finance chief has earned the President's confidence through his aggressive investigations into alleged mortgage fraud by several Democrats who have targeted Trump with legal proceedings. Pulte has led probes into New York Attorney General Letitia James, California Representatives Eric Swalwell and Adam Schiff, and Fulton County District Attorney Fani Willis. He also referred Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook to the Department of Justice over alleged mortgage fraud during Trump's disputes with the Fed's leadership over interest rate policy.

Before her departure, Gabbard had launched an investigation into more than 120 U.S.-funded biological laboratories worldwide, including over 40 in Ukraine. She told the New York Post she was examining whether these facilities were conducting dangerous gain-of-function research that could turn naturally occurring viruses into potential bioweapons—claims that Russia has raised since 2022.
