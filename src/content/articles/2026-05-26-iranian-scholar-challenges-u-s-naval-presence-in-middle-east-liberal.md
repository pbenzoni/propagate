A University of Tehran professor is raising pointed questions about the legitimacy of U.S. military involvement in the Strait of Hormuz, a strategic waterway located nearly 7,000 miles from American borders. Foad Izadi, who specializes in political communication, argues that Washington has inserted itself into regional affairs far beyond its geographic jurisdiction.

The debate centers on control of the narrow strait, a critical shipping corridor that has become contentious amid ongoing tensions between Washington and Tehran. Izadi notes that under international law—specifically the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea—the waterway is divided between Iranian and Omani territorial waters, with no international waters in between.

The professor drew parallels to established practices by other nations, pointing out that countries including Australia, Canada, Denmark, and Turkey already collect various fees from vessels passing through their territorial waters. "They call it pilotage fees," Izadi explained, noting that some charges serve environmental purposes. He argued that Iran would be within its rights under international legal frameworks to implement similar policies.

The scholar's comments highlight broader questions about the scope of American military projection and intervention in regions distant from U.S. territory, particularly as debates over foreign policy priorities continue domestically.
