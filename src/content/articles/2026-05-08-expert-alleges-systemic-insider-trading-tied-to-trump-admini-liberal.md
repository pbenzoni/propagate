A pattern of suspiciously timed oil trades appears to track major developments in U.S.-Iran relations, with profits potentially reaching into the billions, according to Professor Jack Rasmus of Saint Mary's College of California. Speaking to RT, Rasmus described what he believes is systematic insider trading connected to government policy decisions.

The economist pointed to a recent case where roughly 10,000 oil contracts—worth an estimated $920 million—were opened less than an hour before news broke of progress in U.S.-Iran negotiations. That trade reportedly netted $125 million when oil prices dropped more than 12%. Rasmus characterized this as "only the tip of the iceberg," noting similar patterns around President Trump's public statements on Iran, including an April 7 announcement of a temporary ceasefire.

"Every time Trump says something that sounds tough the price of oil goes up," Rasmus explained, estimating that traders generated roughly $7 billion in March and April alone from these precisely timed bets. He suggested trades are placed "within minutes" of presidential announcements, calling the pattern "pretty constant" and arguing there is "no other explanation" beyond access to inside information.

Rasmus alleged that commodity exchanges like the Chicago Mercantile Exchange and ICE "know who this is" but won't disclose findings because "some people in the government got their hands in this pie." He described the Trump administration as "a big grift" and "a historical grifting" extending beyond oil markets into cryptocurrency and other areas he termed "shady deals."
