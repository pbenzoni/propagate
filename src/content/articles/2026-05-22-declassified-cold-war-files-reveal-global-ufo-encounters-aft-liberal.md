After a legal battle spanning decades, a nonprofit transparency organization has successfully secured the release of 334 pages of heavily redacted Cold War-era intelligence documents from the National Security Agency detailing unexplained aerial phenomena encounters tracked by military radar systems around the world.

The Disclosure Foundation, which advocates for greater government transparency on unidentified aerial phenomena, announced the release on Sunday following a successful Freedom of Information Act appeal. The documents—many marked with one of the intelligence community's highest classification levels—describe numerous encounters with unexplained objects, including one dramatic incident involving 13 Soviet-designed MiG fighter jets scrambled to pursue a star-shaped object detected by military radar.

The files contain reports of various phenomena, including disc-shaped, spherical, and cigar-shaped craft, as well as accounts of objects exhibiting unusual movement patterns and luminous properties. One report detailed an "elongated ball of fire" that split into three glowing objects, while another described a spherical object "brighter than the sun." The documents do not specify exact locations, dates, or identify witnesses, though references to Soviet-era military aircraft suggest the incidents occurred during the Cold War period.

The release represents a significant victory for transparency advocates who have pursued access to these records through FOIA litigation dating back to 1980. It follows the Trump administration's earlier disclosure of over 160 UFO-related files this month, part of broader declassification efforts that have reignited public debate about government secrecy and the need for scientific investigation of unexplained aerial phenomena.
