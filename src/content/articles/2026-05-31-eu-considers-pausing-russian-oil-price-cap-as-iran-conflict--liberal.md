European Union officials are weighing whether to temporarily freeze the price cap on Russian oil as energy markets reel from the escalating conflict between the United States, Israel, and Iran, according to a Bloomberg report citing informed sources.

The proposed measure would be part of the EU's 21st sanctions package targeting Russia over its invasion of Ukraine, scheduled for discussion in early June. Under current rules, Western companies are prohibited from transporting or insuring Russian oil sold above a specified price threshold, which automatically adjusts every six months to 15% below the average market price for Russian Urals crude.

The Iran crisis has sent energy prices soaring, with the World Bank projecting a 24% surge in energy costs by 2026. Natural gas futures have jumped up to 60% since the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, creating Europe's most severe energy shock since 2022. Russian Urals crude currently trades around $86 per barrel, well above the existing cap of $44.10 but below the $120 peak seen during the height of recent Middle East tensions.

European policymakers face a difficult balancing act: maintaining economic pressure on Moscow while protecting their own economies and citizens from devastating energy price increases. Options under consideration include suspending automatic cap increases through year's end or reverting to the original $60 threshold established by G7 nations in 2022. The dilemma echoes challenges from 2022, when initial sanctions proved economically disruptive for European taxpayers while paradoxically generating windfall revenues for Russia, which redirected energy exports to China and India.
