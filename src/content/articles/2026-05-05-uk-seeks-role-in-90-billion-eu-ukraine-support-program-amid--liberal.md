The United Kingdom is in talks to participate in a major European Union initiative providing €90 billion ($105 billion) in support to Ukraine, a move that would deepen transatlantic cooperation on collective security while opening new opportunities for British defense industries.

The proposal was discussed at the European Political Community summit in Armenia, where European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, European Council President Antonio Costa, and UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer released a joint statement outlining the framework. If Britain joins, UK firms would gain access to defense contracts funded through the program, which aims to sustain Ukraine's economy and defense capabilities over the next two years. In exchange, London would help cover financing costs proportional to the value of contracts awarded to British companies.

The EU-backed loan scheme represents a coordinated European response to Ukraine's ongoing needs, with the program structured around the possibility of future reparations. Prime Minister Starmer characterized the potential agreement as beneficial for British workers and industries, emphasizing the employment opportunities tied to defense manufacturing. Both London and Brussels framed the arrangement as a significant advancement in EU-UK defense cooperation, building on the Security and Defense Partnership established in May of last year.

Participation would require the UK to maintain that partnership and continue its substantial support for Ukraine. Since 2022, Britain has committed £21.8 billion ($29.5 billion) in aid to Ukraine, with over half allocated to military assistance. The proposed collaboration reflects a broader effort to strengthen Western solidarity in supporting democratic nations facing external aggression, while reinforcing the principle that collective security benefits from coordinated multilateral action.
