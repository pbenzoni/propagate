The youth wing of Germany's Social Democratic Party in Berlin has unveiled a radical proposal to abolish marriage entirely, dismissing the institution as a tool of "patriarchy" that perpetuates oppression. The motion, titled "Down with the patriarchy, even if it feels romantic," argues that marriage restricts freedom and serves what they call a "chauvinistic, capitalist nation-state."

Currently, marriage enjoys constitutional protection under Article 6 of Germany's Basic Law. The Young Socialists (Jusos) propose replacing it with temporary "communities of responsibility" that could be formed through a simple written application and dissolved at any time without the consent of the other party. According to the proposal, marriage enforces what the group terms "misogynistic, anti-queer, classist, and racist policies."

While the Berlin youth wing acknowledged that many Germans view marriage as a romantic bond and are happy within it, they insisted that true "emancipation" requires abolishing the institution along with all related laws and tax benefits. The motion is not expected to be discussed at the upcoming state party conference but may be postponed until 2027.

The Social Democrats' popularity has plummeted to just 13% in recent polling—their worst showing since 2019. Meanwhile, the conservative Alternative for Germany party (AfD), which champions traditional family values, has surged to a record 28% support. The contrast highlights a growing divide in German politics between those defending longstanding cultural institutions and progressive activists seeking to dismantle them in the name of social transformation.
