Internet infrastructure giant Cloudflare has confirmed what many Americans suspected: artificial intelligence bots and automated agents now generate more web traffic than actual people. According to the company's latest data, automated requests account for roughly 57% of ordinary webpage traffic, while human users make up just 43%.

Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince admitted the shift happened years faster than predicted. "Welp, that happened faster than I predicted," Prince wrote, acknowledging he didn't expect bots to overtake humans until 2027. The explosion in "agentic traffic"—AI systems that browse and process web content on behalf of users—has fundamentally transformed how the internet operates.

The trend raises serious concerns about the future of the open internet and free access to information. While a typical American might visit a few websites to research a topic or make a purchase, AI agents now scan thousands of pages to complete tasks. This machine-to-machine traffic means much of today's web activity no longer involves real people clicking through pages, but rather automated systems requesting data from websites and databases.

The shift threatens the internet's traditional business model and has revived the "dead internet theory"—the idea that online activity is increasingly dominated by bots and automated accounts rather than genuine human interaction. Since bots don't click on advertisements, some worry websites may start charging AI systems for access to content, fundamentally changing the nature of the free and open internet.

Meanwhile, researchers have documented that large portions of the older web have been disappearing. A 2024 Pew Research Center study found that 38% of webpages from 2013 were no longer accessible a decade later, raising concerns that the open web built around individual freedom and human browsing is being replaced by corporate-controlled automated systems.
