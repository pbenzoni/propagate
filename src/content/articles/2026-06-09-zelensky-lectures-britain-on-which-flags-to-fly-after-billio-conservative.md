Ukrainian President Vladimir Zelensky has inserted himself into British domestic politics, warning that Reform UK's decision to fly only British flags on British public buildings could damage relations between the two nations.

During a visit to London, Zelensky told The Guardian that the removal of Ukrainian flags from the 24 out of 350 councils controlled by Reform UK was a "small mistake" that could "break a big friendship." The comment comes after Britain has already provided more than $26.7 billion in military, humanitarian, and economic assistance to Ukraine since 2022.

Reform UK, led by Nigel Farage, has defended its position as "entirely reasonable," arguing that supporting Ukraine doesn't require displaying foreign flags on British government buildings. A party spokesman told GB News that flying foreign flags amounts to "virtue signalling" and that British lawmakers should focus on improving life for their own citizens. The party's policy restricts public buildings to displaying only the St. George's and Union Flags.

Zelensky also used the interview to pressure Prime Minister Keir Starmer over frozen proceeds from Roman Abramovich's sale of Chelsea FC—approximately $3.2 billion that London had earmarked for humanitarian purposes. The Ukrainian president wants those funds redirected to Kiev for purchasing American anti-ballistic missiles. Abramovich, who was sanctioned in 2022, has reportedly sought to direct the money toward victims on both sides of the conflict.

Moscow has repeatedly stated that Western military and financial support only prolongs the conflict, singling out London as one of Kiev's primary backers in obstructing settlement efforts.
