Telegram founder and CEO Pavel Durov has issued a sharp rebuke of WhatsApp's privacy practices, accusing the Meta-owned messaging platform of deceiving users about the security of their private communications.

In a series of posts over the weekend, the Russian tech entrepreneur described WhatsApp's claim of "end-to-end encryption by default" as "a giant consumer fraud." According to Durov, most private messages sent through WhatsApp ultimately end up stored in plain-text cloud backups on Apple and Google servers—completely undermining the encryption that users believe protects their conversations. He further alleged that WhatsApp stores and shares metadata about who users communicate with, and that Apple and Google hand over backed-up data from the service to third parties "thousands of times per year."

When one user commented that he only shares sensitive photos via Telegram, Durov responded with characteristic bluntness: "Thanks for the trust – your nudes are safe with us." However, Telegram itself does not use end-to-end encryption by default—only its 'Secret Chats' feature provides full protection, while regular messages are stored in the company's cloud.

Durov has consistently criticized WhatsApp as a "tool of surveillance," particularly following its 2014 acquisition by Facebook (now Meta). An international class-action lawsuit filed in January accused Meta of making false privacy claims about WhatsApp. Meta has defended its platform, with spokesperson Andy Stone calling such allegations "categorically false and absurd," though the company does acknowledge that its end-to-end encryption can be compromised if users enable optional cloud backups without additional safeguards.
