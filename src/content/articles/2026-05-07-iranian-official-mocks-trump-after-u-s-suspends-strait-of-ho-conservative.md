Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf openly mocked President Donald Trump on social media after the administration abruptly suspended a U.S. naval operation designed to escort commercial vessels through the Strait of Hormuz. The operation, dubbed 'Project Freedom,' was halted just two days after launch, stranding over 1,500 commercial ships in the Persian Gulf.

"Operation Trust Me Bro failed. Now back to routine with Operation Fauxios," Ghalibaf wrote on X, taking a jab at both the administration and American media reports about supposed diplomatic progress. The Iranian official's "Fauxios" reference targeted reporting by Axios, which had claimed the two nations were nearing agreement on a 14-point memorandum covering nuclear enrichment, sanctions relief, and maritime access.

President Trump cited "great progress" in negotiations and a request from Pakistan as reasons for suspending the operation. However, Iranian officials told the Tasnim news agency that Tehran has not officially responded to any American proposal, calling it riddled with "unacceptable clauses." The standoff centers on U.S. demands for complete dismantlement of Iran's nuclear program and surrender of enriched uranium stockpiles, while Tehran insists on guarantees against American military aggression and withdrawal of U.S. forces from the Gulf region.

The Strait of Hormuz previously accounted for 20% of global seaborne oil trade before recent military confrontations between the U.S., Israel, and Iran disrupted shipping lanes. Iran has consistently maintained it has no intention of building nuclear weapons, while American officials continue pressing for total nuclear capitulation as a precondition for any agreement.
