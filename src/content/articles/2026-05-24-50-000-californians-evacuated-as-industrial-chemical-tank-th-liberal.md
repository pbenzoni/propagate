Up to 50,000 residents across six Southern California cities have been forced from their homes after a pressurized industrial chemical tank began overheating at a GKN Aerospace facility in Orange County, raising fears of a catastrophic explosion and toxic release.

Governor Gavin Newsom declared a state of emergency as emergency crews worked around the clock to stabilize a 34,000-gallon tank containing 6,000 to 7,000 gallons of methyl methacrylate (MMA)—a highly flammable chemical used in plastic production. The crisis began Thursday when the tank started venting vapors at the Garden Grove facility, approximately 38 miles from Los Angeles.

What initially appeared to be a stabilized situation quickly deteriorated when responders discovered their drone temperature readings had only measured the tank's exterior shell. Manual readings revealed internal temperatures were still climbing—reaching around 90 degrees Fahrenheit by Saturday and rising roughly one degree per hour. Orange County Fire Authority Chief Craig Covey called it a "worst-case scenario," with damaged valves preventing crews from safely removing the chemical or relieving pressure.

Evacuation orders now span parts of Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park, and Westminster. If the container ruptures, the substance could ignite and release toxic fumes causing respiratory problems, neurological symptoms, and potential loss of consciousness. Emergency teams have established containment barriers while continuing efforts to cool the vessel and add neutralizing agents, though officials warn the threat of explosion remains ongoing with no immediate resolution in sight.
