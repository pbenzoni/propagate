President Donald Trump announced Wednesday he is reviewing a potential reduction of American forces stationed in Germany, signaling frustration with European allies who have declined to support U.S. military operations against Iran.

"The United States is studying and reviewing the possible reduction of troops in Germany, with a determination to be made in the near term," Trump wrote on Truth Social. The announcement follows public criticism from German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who questioned the administration's strategy in the conflict with Iran during remarks to students earlier this week.

More than 36,000 active-duty troops and 1,500 reservists are currently stationed at bases across Germany. Trump previously sought to withdraw approximately 12,000 service members near the end of his first term in 2020, though the Biden administration later increased troop levels in response to the Ukraine conflict.

The president has expressed disappointment with European NATO allies for refusing to back American-Israeli operations against Iran and declining to assist in reopening the Strait of Hormuz, which Iran closed to what it termed "hostile" shipping in February. Trump has renewed his longstanding criticism of the alliance, recently describing NATO as "a paper tiger."

Merz attempted to defuse tensions Wednesday, stating his personal relationship with the president "remains good" while defending his right to express concerns about the Iranian conflict. Oil prices climbed above $120 per barrel Wednesday—the highest since 2022—as uncertainty over U.S.-Iranian negotiations continues.
