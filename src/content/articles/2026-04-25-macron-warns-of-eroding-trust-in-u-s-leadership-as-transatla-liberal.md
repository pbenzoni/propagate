French President Emmanuel Macron issued a stark warning about the deteriorating state of the transatlantic alliance, telling reporters in Athens that European nations can no longer count on the United States as a dependable partner. Speaking at an informal summit alongside Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis, Macron articulated growing European anxieties about Washington's commitment to multilateral cooperation.

"Everybody sees that the number one power, the U.S., could be an ally for certain countries, but this ally is not so much certain... and nobody is totally sure if this alliance is reliable," Macron stated, positioning the European Union as a potentially more stable alternative in an increasingly unstable world order. The French leader warned that the international system itself is being "jeopardized by the two big powers," a reference to heightened geopolitical competition.

The remarks reflect deepening tensions within NATO, particularly since the Trump administration's return to power in 2025. European allies have faced renewed pressure over defense spending, threats regarding Greenland's status, and fundamental disagreements over conflict resolution in Ukraine. Trump has pushed for European nations to assume greater financial and military responsibility for supporting Kyiv, while simultaneously pursuing diplomatic initiatives that some European leaders view as insufficiently coordinated with allies.

Macron's comments underscore a broader shift in European strategic thinking, as leaders across the continent grapple with questions about self-reliance and the future of the post-World War II security architecture. The transatlantic relationship, long considered the cornerstone of Western democratic cooperation, faces what some analysts describe as its most serious stress test in decades.
