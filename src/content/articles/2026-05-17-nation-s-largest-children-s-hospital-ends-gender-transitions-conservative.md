The move comes as part of a settlement following a years-long investigation by the DOJ and Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton into the nation's largest children's hospital. The probe focused on allegations that the hospital submitted false billings to insurance companies to secure coverage for pediatric gender treatments. Under the settlement, Texas Children's agreed to permanently cease providing sex change services to minors, including administration of puberty blockers and cross-sex hormones.

The hospital will pay over $10 million in damages and "dedicate millions to the provision of medical care to children harmed by the provision of such procedures," the Justice Department stated. "The Justice Department will use every weapon at its disposal to end the destructive and discredited practice of so-called 'gender-affirming care' for children," Blanche stated. The settlement marks the first resolution in the DOJ's ongoing national investigation into transition care for minors.

While denying wrongdoing, the hospital said it chose to settle "to protect our resources from endless and costly litigation" and to focus on patient care.
