A State Department official is pushing FIFA to replace Iran with Italy at the upcoming World Cup, raising questions about political interference in international sports. Paolo Zampolli, who heads the Office of Global Partnerships, told Italian media he wants to "welcome [Italians] with open arms" by lobbying for the four-time World Cup champions to take Iran's spot at the tournament, which will be co-hosted by the United States.

The effort comes as ongoing tensions with the Iranian regime have created uncertainty around the Islamic Republic's participation. Iranian officials have sent mixed signals about whether their team would even play matches on American soil, with some calling for games to be moved to Mexico instead. FIFA President Gianni Infantino has maintained that Iran is still expected to compete.

Italy failed to qualify for the World Cup for the third consecutive time after losing to Bosnia and Herzegovina in a playoff—a stunning collapse for one of soccer's historic powerhouses that triggered widespread outrage and led to the resignation of the Italian football federation's head. Zampolli, an Italian-American, argues the traditional soccer power deserves the spot over a regime that remains hostile to Western interests.

According to reports, the lobbying effort may also serve domestic political purposes, potentially strengthening Republican support among Italian-American voters. The move comes amid broader U.S.-Italy tensions following disputes between the Trump administration and Italian officials, including criticism of Pope Leo and Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's subsequent rebuke.
