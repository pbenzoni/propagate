Iran is preparing legislation to charge ships from "hostile" nations—including the United States—fees to transit the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint through which roughly one-fifth of global oil supplies pass. The move comes after negotiations between Washington and Tehran collapsed in Islamabad, with Iranian officials saying America needs a deal "more than we do."

Ebrahim Azizi, head of the Iranian Parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Committee, told RT that the proposed bill would require countries that have imposed sanctions or caused economic damage to Iran to pay compensation before their vessels can pass through the strait. "You made a great mistake by blocking them," Azizi said, referring to frozen Iranian assets. "Now you also want to cross this waterway? That era is over."

President Trump has ordered a naval blockade and refused to rule out "limited" strikes against Iran while threatening to deny safe passage to ships complying with Tehran's new rules. The standoff represents a test of American resolve in defending freedom of navigation through international waters—a principle the U.S. Navy has upheld for decades. Azizi dismissed American credibility, questioning how a country could be trusted to honor agreements while maintaining what he called "arrogance and colonialism" as guiding principles.

The proposed Iranian law would establish a new "management and control system" for the strait, with specific toll amounts to be determined later by Tehran's cabinet rather than its parliament. Iranian officials claim many citizens opposed entering negotiations with Washington in the first place, suggesting domestic political pressure may limit Tehran's flexibility in any future talks.
