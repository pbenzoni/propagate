China's foreign ministry has firmly denied a Financial Times report claiming that President Xi Jinping launched into an intense criticism of Japan's military buildup during his recent summit with President Donald Trump in Beijing. The denial raises fresh questions about the reliability of mainstream media coverage of high-stakes diplomatic negotiations.

According to the British newspaper's account, Xi allegedly "castigated" Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi over her country's increased defense spending in what the outlet characterized as "an intense diatribe." The FT further claimed this was the most heated moment of the two-day summit, with Xi growing "visibly animated" in a way that supposedly caught American officials off guard.

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning pushed back firmly against the characterization, stating that accounts of Xi criticizing Takaichi during the summit "do not accord with the information available to the Chinese side." The denial comes as no surprise to critics of legacy media outlets, who have increasingly questioned their sourcing and framing of geopolitical events.

The summit itself marked President Trump's first visit to China by a sitting U.S. president in nearly a decade, underscoring the significance of renewed dialogue between Washington and Beijing. While the FT claimed Trump defended Tokyo's military buildup as necessary given threats from North Korea, details of the private conversations remain known only to the participants themselves—not to reporters relying on secondhand accounts from unnamed sources.

Japanese Prime Minister Takaichi, who spoke with President Trump immediately after his China trip concluded, told reporters she received a detailed briefing on his conversation with Xi but declined to comment further on specifics. The discretion stands in marked contrast to the sensationalized accounts published in the British press.
