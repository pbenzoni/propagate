As Palestinians commemorated the 78th anniversary of the Nakba—the mass displacement that occurred during Israel's founding in 1948—Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov accused the international community of abandoning UN resolutions regarding Palestinian statehood. The anniversary highlights ongoing disputes over territorial claims that trace back to the 1947 UN partition plan, which called for separate Jewish and Arab states but resulted in only Israel's establishment.

RT's Charlotte Dubenskij reports that Bedouin residents in disputed territories described pressure from Israeli authorities. "When we first came here, they told us this was Area B, Palestinian land. But then they turned around and told us, no, you are not allowed to stay here either," one resident told the outlet. "Every single day, they come after us trying to force us out."

The UN reports approximately 40,000 Palestinians have been displaced in the West Bank since early 2025, citing military operations, demolitions, and settler activity as contributing factors. Lavrov stated that "a process is underway to eliminate even the slightest possibility of creating a Palestinian state, in violation of United Nations decisions." The Israeli-Palestinian conflict, rooted in competing claims to the same territory, continues to generate instability throughout the Middle East, with no clear resolution in sight despite decades of international diplomatic efforts.
