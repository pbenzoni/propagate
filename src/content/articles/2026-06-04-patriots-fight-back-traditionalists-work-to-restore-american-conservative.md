American traditionalists are taking legal action and mounting grassroots campaigns to restore historic monuments that were removed during the 2020 riots that followed George Floyd's death, according to a Wall Street Journal report. With backing from the Trump administration, these patriots are pushing back against what they see as an assault on American heritage and history.

In Columbus, Ohio—a city named after the renowned Italian explorer—a coalition of Italian-American organizations filed a federal lawsuit in April seeking to restore a 22-foot statue of Christopher Columbus that was removed from City Hall in 2020. The groups argue the statue represents Italian-American heritage and civic history, calling its removal "unlawful and discriminatory." Local Democrat Mayor Andrew Ginther had ordered it taken down, dismissing it as a symbol of the country's "ugly past."

"The silent majority is becoming vocal," said Jack Conte, who organized the lawsuit. "You reach a point where this stuff is shoved down your throat, and you can only take so much of it."

President Trump has thrown his support behind these restoration efforts. In early 2025, he signed an executive order directing the reinstatement of monuments removed since 2020 if they were taken down as part of what he called an effort to "perpetuate a false reconstruction of American history." In March, the administration erected a Columbus statue replica near the White House, replacing one that protesters destroyed in Baltimore's Inner Harbor. The Interior Department also returned a statue of Delaware Founding Father Caesar Rodney to Washington's Freedom Plaza ahead of America's 250th anniversary.

According to World Heritage USA, 261 American historical monuments were removed, vandalized, or toppled during the 2020 unrest. More than half honored Confederate figures, but others commemorated explorers, Founding Fathers, and other historical figures who shaped the nation.
