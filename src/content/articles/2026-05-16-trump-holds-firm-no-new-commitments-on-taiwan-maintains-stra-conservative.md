President Donald Trump stood his ground during recent high-stakes talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping, making clear that America's Taiwan policy remains unchanged despite Beijing's aggressive posturing, according to U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz.

"At the end of the day, [Trump] made no commitments either way, in line with our longstanding policy of strategic ambiguity," Waltz told Fox News on Friday. The ambassador explained that maintaining uncertainty about American intentions actually serves as a deterrent: "There is a whole line of thinking that if you basically indicate or tell China that we won't be there, that it could embolden them as well."

China has long demanded an end to U.S. arms sales to the self-governing island and refuses to accept any diplomatic recognition of Taiwan's democratically-elected government. Chinese officials have repeatedly threatened that "Taiwan independence" and regional peace are "as irreconcilable as fire and water," according to Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning.

President Trump himself confirmed the policy Friday, telling Fox News that "nothing has changed" and emphasizing he's "not looking to have somebody go independent." The president made clear his priorities: "We're supposed to travel 9,500 miles to fight a war. I'm not looking for that. I want them to cool down. I want China to cool down."

Waltz noted there remains a substantial backlog of weapons orders from Taiwan, though ultimate decisions on those sales rest with the president. The U.S. maintains informal ties with Taiwan without formally recognizing it as a separate country—a careful balance that has preserved peace in the region for decades while supporting a fellow democracy against authoritarian pressure.
