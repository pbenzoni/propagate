Slovenia's newly elected Prime Minister Janez Jansa has restored normal relations with Israel, lifting an arms embargo and travel bans imposed by his left-wing predecessor. The move comes after a dramatic election cycle that saw a private intelligence firm expose alleged corruption within the previous socialist government.

Jansa's administration announced Thursday it would overturn the arms export ban and entry restrictions on senior Israeli officials including Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. "This will restore the conditions for a normal political dialog with Israel," the Slovenian Defense Ministry stated, adding the decision would "strengthen the role of the Republic of Slovenia in the efforts to achieve a lasting peace in the Middle East."

The reversal follows months of political upheaval triggered when covertly-recorded videos surfaced showing associates of former Prime Minister Robert Golob's Svoboda party discussing government corruption. The footage, filmed by Black Cube—an Israeli private intelligence firm staffed by former IDF veterans—emerged just before parliamentary elections. Though Golob's party narrowly won the popular vote by 0.67%, his coalition lost its majority and could not form a government, paving the way for Jansa's conservative coalition to take power.

Golob, who had recognized Palestine as a state and accused Israel of "genocide" in Gaza, had imposed sweeping anti-Israel measures including the arms embargo and a ban on goods from West Bank settlements. Slovenia's Intelligence and Security Agency determined Black Cube "deliberately attempted to influence democratic elections," though stopped short of directly accusing Jansa of commissioning the operation. Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar praised Jansa as "a bold leader and a true friend of Israel" for swiftly reversing what he called the previous government's "distorted anti-Israeli measures."
