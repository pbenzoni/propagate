German authorities have arrested a 33-year-old Polish citizen residing in Bavaria on suspicion of erecting Holocaust-themed structures outside a government building in the town of Eggenfelden. The man faces serious charges including incitement to hatred and use of prohibited symbols under Germany's strict anti-hate laws.

In late March, a large wooden replica of the Auschwitz concentration camp gates—complete with the infamous "Arbeit macht frei" inscription and multiple swastikas—appeared outside the local tax office. The replica included the inverted 'B' that marked the original gates. Weeks later, a second installation materialized: a black chimney structure resembling a crematorium oven, labeled "Zyklon B" and marked with SS runes, placed in a disabled parking space at the same location.

Investigators identified the suspect through fingerprint analysis and discovered additional Nazi symbols in his residence. Local Mayor Martin Biber condemned the installations as "brazen and disgusting" acts that constitute "an insult to society." A community coalition held a rally denouncing what organizers called the "instrumentalization of historical suffering."
