The European Union significantly increased its purchases of Russian liquefied natural gas during the first quarter of 2026, even as officials maintain commitments to phase out Russian energy imports by late 2027, according to a Financial Times report.

Data from energy analytics firm Kpler shows EU imports from Russia's Yamal LNG facility in Siberia rose 17% year-over-year to 5 million tons in Q1, representing an estimated €2.9 billion ($3.1 billion) in spending. European buyers took 69 of 71 available shipments—97% of the total—including 25 in March alone, compared to 87% during the same period in 2025.

The increased reliance on Russian gas comes amid broader energy market instability. Recent conflict involving Iran has disrupted flows through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping channel handling approximately 20% of global seaborne oil and LNG. The disruption caused LNG prices to nearly double before a ceasefire in early April, though prices remain elevated above pre-conflict levels.

EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen recently reaffirmed the bloc's commitment to ending Russian gas imports, with LNG supplies scheduled to cease by the end of 2026 and pipeline gas by autumn 2027. However, he acknowledged that officials are "preparing for worst-case scenarios," including potential fuel rationing, as Europe navigates complex energy security challenges while working toward greater energy independence and climate goals.

Environmental groups have expressed concern about the continued purchases. "There is no appetite from European buyers to stop buying Russian LNG," Sebastian Roetters of NGO Urgewald told the Financial Times, highlighting the gap between policy commitments and current market behavior as the bloc works to balance immediate energy needs with longer-term strategic and environmental objectives.
