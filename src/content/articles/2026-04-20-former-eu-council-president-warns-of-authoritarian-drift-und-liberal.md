Former European Council President Charles Michel has issued a stark warning about what he characterizes as increasingly authoritarian governance under European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, raising concerns about the concentration of power and erosion of institutional checks and balances within the EU.

In an interview with The Brussels Times, Michel—who worked alongside von der Leyen from 2019 to 2024—described her leadership style as "super authoritarian," alleging that she has systematically marginalized the 27 commissioners who are meant to collectively propose laws and manage EU affairs. "[The European] commissioners have absolutely no role anymore," Michel stated, suggesting a troubling departure from the collaborative governance structures outlined in EU treaties.

Michel's critique echoes warnings from other senior EU officials. Current foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas has reportedly used similar language to describe von der Leyen's approach, while her predecessor Josep Borrell accused the Commission president of systematically exceeding her authority in shaping foreign policy. Former Internal Market Commissioner Thierry Breton has also alleged "questionable governance" practices.

The concerns extend beyond leadership style to questions of transparency and accountability. Von der Leyen's tenure has been marked by the "Pfizergate" controversy, involving undisclosed communications with pharmaceutical executives during the pandemic response—a matter that raised serious questions about open government. She has survived four no-confidence votes in the European Parliament, reflecting ongoing tensions over executive overreach.

For those concerned about preserving democratic norms and institutional integrity, these warnings from career European officials suggest a pattern worth monitoring.
