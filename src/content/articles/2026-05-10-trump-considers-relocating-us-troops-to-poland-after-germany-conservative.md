President Donald Trump signaled Friday that American forces currently stationed in Germany could be relocated to Poland, reinforcing America's commitment to allies who stand with us. The move comes as the Pentagon prepares to withdraw approximately 5,000 of the roughly 36,000 service members from Germany over the next year.

The troop reduction follows German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's public criticism that Washington "obviously has no strategy" in the Iran conflict and claims the US "is being humiliated by the Iranian leadership." Trump responded decisively, warning the Pentagon would be "cutting a lot further than 5,000" troops from Germany.

"Well Poland would like that," Trump told journalists when asked about redeployment prospects. "We have a great relationship with Poland. I have a great relationship with the president. ... I like him a lot, so that's possible." Polish President Karol Nawrocki confirmed Wednesday that Poland stands ready to host American forces withdrawn from Germany and would personally advocate to Trump for the eastward redeployment.

The President has also raised concerns about troop levels in other NATO countries including Italy and Spain, both of which denied access to military facilities for operations against Iran. Spain closed its airspace to US aircraft and denied use of a naval base near Cadiz, while Italy blocked access to its air base in Sicily.

Trump has repeatedly criticized NATO as a "paper tiger" after allies refused to join the US-Israeli campaign in the Middle East, and has accused the bloc of failing to adequately support American efforts in Ukraine. The moves underscore the administration's willingness to reward allies who support American leadership while holding others accountable for undermining US interests.
