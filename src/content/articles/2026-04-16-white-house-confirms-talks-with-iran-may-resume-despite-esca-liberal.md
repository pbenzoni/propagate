The White House confirmed Wednesday that discussions are underway for a second round of diplomatic talks with Iran, following an initial meeting in Islamabad that failed to yield substantive progress. White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt stated that while talks are being considered, "nothing is official until you hear it from the White House."

The first direct meeting between the two nations took place over the weekend in Pakistan's capital, but ended without agreement. According to reports, Tehran attributed the breakdown to what it characterized as "unrealistic demands" from Washington. The failed negotiations were followed by a U.S. announcement of a blockade in the Strait of Hormuz targeting Iran-linked vessels, further escalating regional tensions.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian emphasized Wednesday that his country "is not seeking war and instability" and remains committed to "dialogue and constructive interaction with all countries of the world." However, he added a firm caveat: "any attempt to impose any will on the country or force it to surrender is doomed to fail."

The potential for renewed diplomacy comes against a backdrop of significant regional instability. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov, while welcoming the talks, urged the U.S. to "be realists" and avoid what he termed "unprovoked aggression" that affects the entire Middle East. Pakistan has positioned itself as a key mediator in the process, with officials indicating they remain committed to facilitating a diplomatic resolution despite the current impasse.
