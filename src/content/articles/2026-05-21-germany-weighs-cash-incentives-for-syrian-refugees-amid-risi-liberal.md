Germany's Interior Ministry is considering offering Syrian refugees up to $9,300 to voluntarily return home, according to Focus magazine—a nearly eightfold increase from current repatriation payments. The proposal emerges against a backdrop of surging support for the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party, which has made anti-immigrant sentiment central to its platform.

More than 951,000 Syrians currently live in Germany, many arriving during the 2014-2015 refugee crisis when then-Chancellor Angela Merkel adopted what critics and supporters alike called an "open-door" policy. Over half hold temporary residence permits tied to refugee or subsidiary protection status. Despite shifting political winds, voluntary returns have remained relatively low.

Hesse Interior Minister Roman Poseck defended the financial incentive by framing it as cost-effective compared to ongoing social support expenses. "Support payments in the four-figure range or sometimes even in the lower five-figure range would often still be a gain for the state when measured against the long-term costs of social benefits," he told Focus. Current voluntary return payments average around $1,163.

The proposal comes as Chancellor Friedrich Merz—recently rated Europe's most unpopular leader—faces mounting pressure from the right. Berlin now rejects 95% of new Syrian asylum applications, and Merz has suggested that up to 80% of Syrians in Germany could return within three years, though Syrian leader Ahmed al-Sharaa disputed making such a statement. The AfD has recently overtaken Merz's Christian Democratic Union in polling, despite being boycotted by mainstream parties and facing accusations of extremism.
