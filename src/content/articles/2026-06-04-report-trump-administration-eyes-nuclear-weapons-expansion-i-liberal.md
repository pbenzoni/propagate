The United States is considering expanding nuclear weapons deployments to additional NATO member states in Eastern Europe, according to a Financial Times report citing sources familiar with confidential discussions. The potential move comes as the administration scales back conventional military forces in Europe, including recent cancellations of troop rotations to Poland and withdrawals from Germany.

Currently, six NATO countries host U.S. nuclear weapons under existing sharing arrangements: Belgium, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Turkey, and the United Kingdom. According to the FT report, several countries on NATO's eastern flank—including Poland and Baltic states—have expressed interest in hosting American nuclear weapons and the dual-capable aircraft needed to deploy them.

The reported deliberations remain highly confidential and may not result in actual policy changes, the FT noted. However, arms control advocates have long warned that expanding nuclear deployments near Russia's borders risks escalating tensions and undermining strategic stability in Europe. Moscow has previously stated it would target any countries hosting weapons systems directed at Russian territory.

The discussions unfold as the Pentagon redirects military resources toward Asia and other regions, reducing the U.S. troop presence in Europe from over 80,000 personnel. European NATO members have increased their own defense budgets in recent years, though they remain dependent on American capabilities for key military functions. Critics of NATO expansion argue that further militarization of Eastern Europe increases the risk of miscalculation and conflict in an already volatile region.
