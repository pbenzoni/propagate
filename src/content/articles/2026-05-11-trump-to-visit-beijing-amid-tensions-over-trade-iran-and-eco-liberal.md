The Chinese Foreign Ministry has confirmed that President Donald Trump will travel to Beijing for a state visit from May 13 to 15, marking the first such diplomatic mission in nearly nine years. The summit, originally scheduled for late March, was delayed due to the escalating conflict involving the US and Israel against Iran, which disrupted global energy markets following the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

A principal deputy White House press secretary characterized the visit as carrying "tremendous symbolic significance," with discussions centered on "rebalancing the relationship with China and prioritizing reciprocity and fairness to restore American economic independence." President Trump is scheduled to hold bilateral talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping on Thursday, followed by a tea session and working lunch on Friday.

The visit comes against a backdrop of ongoing trade tensions. Last October, Trump imposed tariffs on Chinese goods that climbed as high as 145%, prompting Beijing to restrict exports of rare earth minerals critical to US industry and military production—a move that forced several American factories to suspend operations. A temporary trade truce was eventually reached in South Korea.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer indicated last month that Washington seeks to maintain stability in bilateral relations while securing continued access to rare earth minerals essential for American industry. However, senior US officials have suggested that Washington may also pressure Beijing over its purchases of Iranian oil and Tehran's acquisition of potential dual-use goods. China, which has rejected what it calls Washington's "unilateral" sanctions on Iran's oil sector, has repeatedly called for an end to the conflict. Trump's visit follows just a week after Beijing hosted Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi.
