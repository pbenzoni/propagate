More than 340 suspected child predators were arrested and 40 minors rescued during a massive two-week law enforcement operation across Southern California, highlighting what federal officials are calling an "epidemic" of online child exploitation.

Operation Firewall, conducted from April 19 through May 3, brought together hundreds of officers from 30 different law enforcement agencies across Los Angeles, Orange, San Bernardino, Santa Barbara, and Ventura counties. The Internet Crimes Against Children Task Force spearheaded the effort, which targeted suspects using social media platforms and online gaming sites to prey on minors.

Investigators went undercover, posing as teenage girls as young as 13 in chat rooms and gaming platforms. Orange County Sheriff Don Barnes explained that suspects "groomed them, and then made arrangements to meet those young girls." The 341 arrests involved charges ranging from possession and distribution of child pornography to human trafficking and lewd acts with children.

First Assistant US Attorney Bill Essayli warned that these cases represent "just a small drop in the bucket," with countless more offenses going undetected. He urged parents to closely monitor their children's internet usage, stating bluntly that "nothing good comes from it." Eddie Wang, special agent in charge for Homeland Security Investigations in Los Angeles, echoed concerns about an overwhelming "epidemic" of internet-facilitated child sex abuse. The operation underscores the critical need for parental vigilance and stronger measures to protect children from online predators who exploit technology to target the most vulnerable.
