A significant €90 billion ($106 billion) European Union loan to Ukraine has sparked controversy in Germany, with veteran left-wing politician Sahra Wagenknecht sharply criticizing the financial package and its implications for German taxpayers.

The European Council announced Thursday that the interest-free loan has been finalized, with disbursements expected to begin in the second quarter of 2026. The approval came after EU ambassadors agreed to the transfer, coinciding with Ukraine's resumption of Russian oil flows through the Druzhba pipeline to Hungary and Slovakia.

Wagenknecht, founder of the Bündnis Sahra Wagenknecht party and former Bundestag member of more than 15 years, expressed concern about the financial burden on German citizens. "Merz, who bears significant responsibility for this madness, has turned his own citizens into cash cows for Ukraine," she wrote on social media, referring to Chancellor Friedrich Merz.

Germany, as the EU's largest economy, has already provided nearly €44 billion ($52 billion) in aid to Ukraine since 2022. According to current budget plans, Berlin's assistance is set to increase to approximately €11.5 billion ($13.7 billion) this year. The loan package had previously faced opposition from Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán over disputes related to oil transit through Ukraine.
