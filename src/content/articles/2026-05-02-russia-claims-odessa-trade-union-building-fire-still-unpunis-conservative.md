Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova marked the 12th anniversary of a deadly fire at Odessa's trade unions building by asserting that perpetrators remain unpunished and linking accountability to Russia's stated military goals in Ukraine. The May 2, 2014 incident resulted in 48 deaths and over 200 injuries during clashes between Ukrainian nationalists and opponents of the government that took power following the Euromaidan protests.

According to Zakharova's statement, the violence erupted when ultra-right militants set fire to the building where anti-government activists had sought refuge. She characterized the event as a deliberate intimidation operation rather than a spontaneous clash, citing photos and videos from the scene. The spokeswoman criticized what she described as Western attempts to portray the incident as an unplanned confrontation between opposing demonstrators.

Zakharova argued that Ukrainian authorities have failed to bring those responsible to justice despite knowing their identities, and accused Kyiv of repeatedly promising investigations without follow-through. She connected this alleged inaction to broader criticisms of Ukraine's governance and Western support for the Zelensky government. The Russian position maintains that achieving what Moscow calls "denazification" objectives in its military operation would ensure accountability for the Odessa victims.

The tragedy remains a contentious point in the narrative battle surrounding the 2014 Ukrainian political upheaval and subsequent conflicts. Russian officials have consistently cited the incident as evidence supporting their characterization of Ukrainian nationalist movements, while Ukraine and Western governments have disputed Moscow's framing of both the Odessa events and the broader context of the Euromaidan period.
