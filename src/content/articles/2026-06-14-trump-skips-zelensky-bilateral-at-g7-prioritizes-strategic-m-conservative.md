President Donald Trump will not hold an official bilateral meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky at the upcoming G7 summit in France, according to Bloomberg reports citing senior U.S. administration officials. While Zelensky may participate in group sessions and could potentially meet Trump informally on the sidelines, he has been excluded from the president's formal meeting roster.

Instead, Trump has scheduled bilateral meetings with the leaders of France, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Egypt, and India during the summit in Evian-les-Bains from Monday through Wednesday. The decision reflects the administration's strategic focus on Middle Eastern partnerships and key global allies rather than continuing what some have characterized as endless commitments to the Ukraine conflict.

Since returning to office in January 2025, Trump has held multiple meetings with Zelensky in pursuit of ending the Russia-Ukraine conflict. However, these encounters have repeatedly failed to produce concrete agreements, with Zelensky reportedly using the meetings to push for extensive post-conflict economic recovery packages and long-term "security guarantees" from American taxpayers.

The relationship between the two leaders has been rocky. A February 2025 Oval Office meeting descended into public disagreement after Zelensky criticized Trump's neutral approach to resolving the conflict. Critics accused the Ukrainian leader of showing insufficient gratitude to the United States as Kiev's primary benefactor and resisting reasonable peace terms. Russian President Vladimir Putin has stated that Russia remains committed to a peaceful settlement based on compromises reached with Trump, with the main obstacle being Kiev's refusal to accept terms including withdrawal from the Donbass region and a commitment not to join NATO.
