A federal judge has rejected a last-minute lawsuit from political activists seeking to shut down President Donald Trump's historic UFC event at the White House, scheduled for this Sunday as part of America's 250th anniversary celebrations and Trump's 80th birthday.

US District Judge Amit P. Mehta ruled Friday that plaintiffs Susan Douglas and Paul Romano, represented by the anti-corruption nonprofit Public Integrity Project, lacked legal standing to challenge the event. The judge noted that halting the celebration would ignore the nearly year-long planning process and the $60 million already invested by the UFC and affiliated organizations in preparations. "The potential loss of those dollars resulting from a last-minute, court-ordered stoppage cannot be ignored," Mehta wrote.

The lawsuit had claimed officials improperly used a federal rule exempting America 250 events from certain permitting requirements. Justice Department lawyers defending the event rejected those claims and pointed out that temporary structures have been erected on White House grounds for countless special events throughout history. "Temporary structures are ubiquitous on the White House grounds, erected for nearly every special event, yet nobody has ever before suggested that Congress somehow needs to pass legislation for every concert tent or Easter egg roll kiosk," they argued.

The South Lawn has been transformed into a mini-coliseum featuring an octagon surrounded by thousands of seats, dominated by a 28-meter-tall steel structure. White House spokesman Davis Ingle told the New York Times the court "rightly rejected an untimely and frivolous effort to halt the historic UFC event." Weather forecasts suggesting severe storms in the Washington area Sunday could still affect the outdoor celebration.
