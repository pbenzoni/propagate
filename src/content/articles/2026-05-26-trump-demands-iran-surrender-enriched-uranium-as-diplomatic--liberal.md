President Donald Trump has publicly demanded that Iran either turn over its enriched uranium stockpile to the United States for destruction or destroy it under U.S. supervision, escalating rhetoric even as delicate negotiations continue in Qatar aimed at reducing tensions in the Middle East.

In a Truth Social post Monday evening, Trump outlined his preferred scenarios for disposing of what he termed "Nuclear Dust," insisting the material be destroyed either in the U.S. or at another location with atomic energy officials witnessing the process. The stark ultimatum comes as negotiators work to extend a fragile 60-day truce and address broader regional security concerns.

Iranian officials have pushed back firmly against characterizations of the talks, with Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei stating Monday that nuclear issues are not currently on the negotiating table. Iran's chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf emphasized Friday that the country "will not compromise on its rights" as a sovereign nation. Tehran maintains that uranium enrichment falls within its legitimate rights under international law and continues to deny any intention to develop nuclear weapons.

The negotiations in Doha are reportedly focused on ending armed conflict between the two nations, reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and potentially unfreezing Iranian assets. Iranian officials have noted that trust remains severely damaged following U.S. airstrikes on Iran in late February, which occurred shortly after a previous round of Omani-mediated diplomatic efforts. While some media outlets reported Iran had agreed "in principle" to uranium disposal, Iranian spokespersons have disputed this characterization, underscoring the gap between the two sides' public positions.
