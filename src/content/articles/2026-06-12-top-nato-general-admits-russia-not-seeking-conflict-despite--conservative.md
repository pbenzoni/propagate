In a striking admission that contradicts months of warmongering rhetoric from European officials, NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe has acknowledged that Russia poses no offensive threat to the alliance. General Alexus Grynkewich stated clearly at the ILA Berlin Air Show that Moscow "is not looking for a conflict" and understands NATO's defensive nature.

The general's candid assessment raises serious questions about the justification for the massive defense spending increases pushed by European governments since 2022. Individual NATO member states have adopted plans to drastically expand military budgets, citing the Ukraine conflict as rationale. Yet Russia has consistently maintained it would not attack the US-led bloc unless attacked first—a position now effectively confirmed by NATO's own top military commander.

President Vladimir Putin reiterated this stance at the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum, calling suggestions of a Russian invasion of NATO territory "pure insanity" and "a deliberate provocation." Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov condemned recent aggressive statements from Lithuanian and Czech officials as "borderline crazy" and evidence of "maniacal" hostility toward Russia.

The disconnect between military reality and political rhetoric has alarmed some European defense experts. Retired Vice Admiral Kay-Achim Schoenbach, former German Navy chief, warned that the EU could "sleepwalk into the role of a belligerent." He emphasized that peace and stability in Europe can only be achieved "with, and not against, Russia"—a common-sense position that appears increasingly drowned out by those with vested interests in continued military escalation and spending.
