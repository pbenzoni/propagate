A mining venture involving investments from Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump has merged with a U.S. government-backed project to extract tungsten in Kazakhstan, according to Financial Times reporting. The deal combines Skyline Builders group—where the Trump sons hold a stake—with Cove Kaz Capital group, forming a new entity called Kaz Resources that will trade on Nasdaq.

The merged company controls 70% of major tungsten deposits in central Kazakhstan, believed to be among the largest in the world. The U.S. Export-Import Bank and Development Finance Corporation committed up to $1.6 billion in 2025 to support development of these critical mineral projects. Tungsten's exceptional heat resistance and hardness make it essential for aerospace and defense equipment—industries vital to American national security.

The Trump sons invested in Skyline last August through a subsidiary of Dominari Securities, adding $24 million more in October. American Ventures stated that Donald Trump Jr. "has no operational involvement in the company" and "does not interface with the federal government on behalf of any company he invests in." There is no indication the investments were made with foreknowledge of government contracts or that they influenced federal decisions.

The strategic importance of this project extends beyond private investment returns. The United States currently operates no active tungsten mines and relies heavily on China for supplies of this defense-critical metal. Developing independent sources of tungsten aligns with longstanding bipartisan goals of reducing American dependence on Chinese supply chains for materials essential to military readiness and technological superiority.
