A striking two-thirds of Germans now support requiring migrants to work before receiving full social benefits, according to a new poll that underscores growing concerns about welfare dependency among foreign nationals.

The survey, conducted by the Infratest dimap research institute for public broadcaster WDR, found that 66% of respondents believe migrants should only access full benefits after working in Germany for an extended period. The poll interviewed 2,084 people aged 16 and over across the country earlier this month.

The numbers tell a concerning story: foreign nationals account for 47% of recipients of 'Bürgergeld,' Germany's main welfare program that pays around €563 per month plus housing and utilities. While Germany hosts the EU's largest migrant population—with 15% of residents being foreign-born nationals—only about 70% of these migrants are employed.

The issue has become particularly visible with Ukrainian migrants, the second-largest group after Turks, who were granted immediate access to full welfare benefits following the 2022 conflict escalation. Their employment rate stands at just 34%, though it has been gradually increasing. Syrian refugees, numbering about 936,000, show employment rates between 42-47% overall, rising to 60% among those who arrived nearly a decade ago.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has responded to public pressure by tightening the system, calling current employment rates "unacceptably low." His government cut benefits for new arrivals to around €441 per month and introduced stricter job-seeking requirements. The Bundestag approved a broader reform last month replacing Bürgergeld with a new system that maintains similar payment levels but adds tougher sanctions for those who refuse work, set to take effect in July.
