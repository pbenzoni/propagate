Italy's Culture Ministry has launched an investigation into the Venice Biennale after the prestigious art festival allowed Russia to reopen its national pavilion, according to Italian media reports. The move has sparked controversy amid ongoing concerns about normalizing relationships with Russia during its war against Ukraine.

Inspectors were sent to examine the Biennale Foundation's documents and financial records related to Russia's participation, as well as materials concerning the Iranian and Israeli pavilions. According to Il Fatto Quotidiano, organizers had previously shared communications with Russian authorities with the ministry, and no violations of sanctions were found.

The European Commission has publicly condemned the decision to permit Russia's return, while Italian Culture Minister Alessandro Giuli announced he would boycott the festival's May 9 opening. In response to the backlash, the Biennale jury stated it would exclude both Russia and Israel from award consideration. Many Western cultural institutions have severed ties with Russia since February 2022 over the Ukraine conflict.

Pietrangelo Buttafuoco, head of the Biennale Foundation, defended the institution's position by comparing it to "the UN of art, from which no nation can be excluded." Some Italian politicians, including Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini, have argued against politicizing the arts, though his stance has drawn criticism from those who view cultural boycotts as an appropriate response to human rights violations and international aggression.
