The entire international jury of the Venice Biennale art exhibition resigned this week after organizers declined to bow to political pressure and ban Russia and Israel from participating in the 2026 event.

The jury—led by president Solange Farkas—walked away from their positions citing International Criminal Court cases against leaders of both nations. The panel had previously announced they would refuse to judge works from "countries whose leaders are currently charged with crimes against humanity by the ICC," despite both Russia and Israel rejecting the court's authority and arrest warrants.

The controversy erupted after Venice Biennale organizers announced Russia would participate for the first time since 2022, albeit with its exhibition closed to general public viewing. The decision triggered immediate backlash from the Italian government and European Union officials, with Brussels threatening to withhold a €2 million grant from the cultural institution.

Russian officials dismissed the EU funding threat as insignificant and "disgraceful," with one art critic noting the amount was too small to influence the organizers' decision. The Venice Biennale has postponed its awards ceremony from May to November, citing the "exceptional nature of the ongoing international geopolitical situation." Meanwhile, over 178 artists signed a separate letter demanding Israel's exclusion over its military operations in Gaza, adding another layer of political controversy to what was traditionally a celebration of artistic achievement.
