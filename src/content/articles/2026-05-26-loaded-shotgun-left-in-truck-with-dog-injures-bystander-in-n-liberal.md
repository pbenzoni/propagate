A woman was injured in Scottsbluff, Nebraska, on Saturday after a loaded shotgun left unsecured in a pickup truck was accidentally discharged by a dog moving in the backseat. The incident underscores ongoing concerns about responsible gun storage and the absence of comprehensive safe storage laws in many states.

According to local police, the truck's owner left his dog in the vehicle while entering a store, with a loaded shotgun accessible in the backseat. When the dog moved across the seat, its paw caught the trigger, firing the weapon through the truck's door. A pellet struck a woman who was sitting in her car at a nearby traffic light with her arm outside the window. She was treated at a hospital for non-life-threatening injuries.

The Scottsbluff Police Department noted that traveling with a loaded firearm in a vehicle is illegal under Nebraska law, though it remains unclear whether charges will be filed. Gun safety advocates have long called for universal safe storage requirements, pointing to tragic accidents like this one as preventable consequences of inadequate regulations.

This incident is part of a troubling pattern. Similar accidents involving unsecured firearms and dogs have occurred in Pennsylvania, Tennessee, and Kansas in recent years, with one Kansas incident proving fatal. Public health experts emphasize that secure storage—using gun safes or trigger locks—could prevent many unintentional shootings, protecting both community members and gun owners themselves from devastating outcomes.
