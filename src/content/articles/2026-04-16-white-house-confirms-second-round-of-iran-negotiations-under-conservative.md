The White House confirmed Wednesday that discussions are underway for a second round of direct talks with Iran, following an initial meeting in Islamabad that failed to produce a breakthrough. Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt emphasized that while conversations continue, nothing is finalized until officially announced.

"We feel good about the prospects of a deal," Leavitt stated during a Wednesday briefing, adding that "it's obviously in the best interest of Iran to meet the president's demands." The administration credited Pakistan for serving as an effective mediator in the process, with Leavitt praising their "friendship and efforts to bring this deal to a close."

The first direct meeting between the two nations took place over the weekend in Pakistan's capital, coming after a US-Israeli strike on Iran in late February. Tehran attributed the lack of progress to what it called Washington's "unrealistic demands," while the US responded by implementing a blockade of the Strait of Hormuz targeting Iran-linked vessels.

President Trump told the New York Post Tuesday that new discussions "could be happening over the next two days," and suggested in a Fox News interview that the conflict is "very close to being over." Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian stated his country remains open to dialogue but will not accept imposed terms or surrender to external pressure. Pakistani officials confirmed their continued commitment to facilitating a diplomatic resolution between the two parties.
