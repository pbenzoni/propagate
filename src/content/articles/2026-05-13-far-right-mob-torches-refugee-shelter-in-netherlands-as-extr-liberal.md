A disturbing scene of anti-immigrant violence unfolded in the Dutch town of Loosdrecht on Tuesday evening, when far-right protesters set fire to an emergency refugee shelter and attacked police officers attempting to maintain order.

According to Dutch broadcaster NOS, an angry mob threw firecrackers at a town hall building designated to provide temporary humanitarian shelter for asylum seekers. The attack came on the same day that the first group of 15 refugees—people fleeing violence and persecution in their home countries—arrived at the facility. Protesters also hurled objects at law enforcement and attempted to prevent firefighters from extinguishing the blaze, putting lives at risk.

The violence represents an escalation of weeks of anti-refugee demonstrations in Loosdrecht, a town of 8,600 residents. Right-wing activists had previously submitted a petition with 3,000 signatures opposing the shelter, which was intended to house 70 asylum seekers after original plans for 110 were scaled back due to community pressure. Several far-right politicians joined the protests, with one leader telling the crowd that refugees should "go back to their own country"—rhetoric that echoes dangerous nativist movements across Europe and beyond.

Justice Minister David van Weel suggested that organized extremist groups may have infiltrated the protests to deliberately incite violence. Pro-immigration lawmakers condemned the attack, with some noting that many protesters appeared to come from outside Loosdrecht itself. A court had previously ruled in favor of using the vacant town hall for humanitarian purposes, upholding the community's legal obligation to provide refuge for those seeking safety. Several arrests were made following the arson attack.
