A European law enforcement task force has arrested 280 individuals in its first year of operations targeting what officials call "violence as a service"—a disturbing trend where criminal networks use social media to recruit young people, including minors, to carry out assaults and murders for hire.

The unit, known as GRIMM, was established in April 2024 by Europol alongside police from multiple European nations including the UK, Norway, and Iceland. According to the agency's statement, more than 1,400 individuals connected to these criminal networks have been identified. "Violence is no longer confined to local dynamics. It is increasingly offered as a service: accessible, scalable and driven by online ecosystems that enable recruitment, coordination, and execution across borders," the statement warned.

Recent cases highlight the severity of the problem: teenagers as young as 14 have been recruited through platforms like Snapchat, Telegram, and TikTok, as well as gaming environments, to carry out shootings in Denmark and Sweden. In one case, Dutch authorities arrested a suspect who allegedly acted as a getaway driver for two minors involved in a series of explosions in Germany in 2025. Andy Kraag, head of Europol's European Serious Organized Crime Center, told Dutch television the phenomenon is "spreading like a wildfire across Europe."

The findings come as European governments increasingly debate restrictions on minors' social media access amid concerns about online safety, mental health impacts, and exploitative platform design. France has passed age-verification requirements for users under 15, while Germany debates restrictions for those under 14. Meanwhile, messaging platforms including Telegram face criminal investigations in France over alleged failures to prevent illegal content, and Russia has imposed restrictions on both Telegram and gaming platform Roblox, citing security concerns and attempts by adults to contact minors through chat features.
