The Bank of England has removed Winston Churchill and other iconic British figures from its currency following internal research that deemed them "elitist and divisive," according to a report from The Telegraph. The decision, part of what critics call Britain's ongoing "diversity overhaul," has sparked outrage among those who view it as an erasure of the nation's heritage.

In March, the central bank announced it would replace historical figures with UK wildlife on future banknotes, claiming the move followed public consultations favoring nature-themed designs. However, documents reveal the decision was driven by a study from market research firm Savanta, which found that figures including Churchill, World War II codebreaker Alan Turing, and novelist Jane Austen were "contentious" and failed to represent the UK's "cultural and natural diversity."

Savanta's report warned that historical figures projected "a backward-looking vision of the UK" and carried "imperialistic" connotations. The firm even flagged Georgian and Victorian architecture as problematic due to alleged ties to colonialism and slavery. Researchers advised framing the change "as a positive evolution" rather than historical "censorship," though critics say that's exactly what it represents.

Reform UK's Treasury spokesman Robert Jenrick called the plan "nonsense," while Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch and Reform UK leader Nigel Farage condemned it as "wrongheaded wokery." The controversy reflects broader concerns about institutional efforts to rewrite British history, including the National Trust's 2020 report linking historic properties to slavery and diversity mandates rolled out across the BBC, Civil Service, and Armed Forces. The public will now help select new imagery for banknotes—choosing between animals like the red fox, bottlenose dolphin, and common frog.
