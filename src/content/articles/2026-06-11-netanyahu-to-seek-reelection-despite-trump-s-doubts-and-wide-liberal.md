Benjamin Netanyahu will run for reelection later this year despite growing skepticism from both the American president and a majority of his own citizens, his Likud party announced Wednesday. The decision comes as Netanyahu faces mounting criticism over his handling of multiple regional conflicts and ongoing corruption charges.

President Trump recently expressed uncertainty about whether Netanyahu should seek another term, asking "Does he want to continue?" according to ABC News Chief Washington Correspondent Jonathan Karl. The comment reflects deteriorating relations between the two leaders, with Trump having previously called Netanyahu during a heated June phone call over the Israeli leader's resistance to ending attacks on Lebanon during U.S.-Iran negotiations.

Public opinion in Israel has turned sharply against Netanyahu's continued leadership. A poll published by the Israel Democracy Institute found that 61% of Israelis oppose his candidacy, with the same percentage supporting term limits for future prime ministers—a clear rebuke of Netanyahu's decades-long grip on power. The 76-year-old has served as prime minister for longer than anyone in Israeli history, holding office from 1996-1999, 2009-2021, and returning in December 2022.

Critics argue Netanyahu has failed to achieve his stated military objectives while prolonging regional conflicts. Political opponents contend he has neither eliminated Hamas in Gaza, neutralized Hezbollah in Lebanon, nor effectively contained Iran—despite claims to the contrary. The upcoming election, required by law before October 27, will be the first since the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack that triggered the current Gaza military operation. Netanyahu remains on trial for corruption as he campaigns for another term.
