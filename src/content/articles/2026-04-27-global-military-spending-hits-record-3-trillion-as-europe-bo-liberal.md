Global military expenditure reached an unprecedented $2.887 trillion in 2025, marking the 11th consecutive year of increases, according to a report released Monday by the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI). The surge reflects escalating geopolitical tensions and ongoing conflicts worldwide, with military spending now representing 2.5% of global GDP.

Europe drove the largest regional increase, with spending rising 14% to $864 billion. SIPRI analysts attribute this dramatic uptick to the urgent need to counter Russian aggression in Ukraine, strengthen collective defense capabilities, and reduce dependence on unpredictable U.S. security commitments. Germany increased its defense budget by 24% to $114 billion, while Spain's spending jumped 50% to $40.2 billion, as NATO members work to meet alliance commitments and ensure European security autonomy.

U.S. military spending fell 7.5% to $954 billion, largely due to the Trump administration's refusal to approve new aid packages for Ukraine—a sharp reversal from the previous $127 billion in authorized military support. This decline underscores growing concerns about American commitment to transatlantic security and the importance of European self-reliance in defense matters.

Russia's defense expenditure grew 5.9% to $190 billion as it continues its war of aggression in Ukraine, while Ukraine itself increased military spending by 20% to $84.1 billion—representing a staggering 40% of its GDP. Meanwhile, China expanded its military budget by 7.4% to $336 billion, part of broader authoritarian military buildups that democratic nations are working to counter through strengthened alliances and defense cooperation.
