The Department of Justice has accused Yale School of Medicine of discrimination following a year-long investigation into its admissions practices, marking the latest salvo in the Trump administration's systematic campaign against diversity, equity, and inclusion programs at American universities.

The DOJ claims that Yale's admissions process favors Black and Hispanic applicants over White and Asian candidates with similar test scores, alleging the school has sought ways to work around the Supreme Court's 2023 decision restricting affirmative action. The agency argues that institutions receiving federal funding must comply with anti-discrimination laws, though civil rights advocates note these same laws were originally designed to remedy historical exclusion.

The investigation is part of a broader pattern under the current administration, which has intensified scrutiny of equity initiatives at federally funded institutions. Last week, the DOJ sent a similar letter to UCLA's medical school. The administration has also suspended funding to multiple Ivy League universities, citing concerns over both DEI programs and alleged antisemitism on campus.

Civil rights organizations have raised concerns that dismantling diversity programs could reverse decades of progress in making medical education—and ultimately healthcare—more accessible and representative. Research has shown that diverse medical workforces improve health outcomes for underserved communities, a consideration not addressed in the DOJ's enforcement actions.

The controversy reflects ongoing tensions over how to achieve equity in higher education following the Supreme Court's curtailment of race-conscious admissions policies, with different interpretations of what constitutes fair and legal consideration of applicant backgrounds.
