Four people were arrested at Spain's Bilbao Airport on Saturday after police clashed with supporters welcoming back participants in the Global Sumud Flotilla, a humanitarian mission that had been intercepted and detained by Israeli forces.

The flotilla, carrying approximately 400 activists from about 40 countries, departed from Turkey earlier this month with the goal of challenging Israel's naval blockade of Gaza and delivering aid to the enclave. Israeli forces intercepted the vessels in international waters on May 18, and participants were subsequently deported.

Video footage from the airport terminal shows officers charging at activists and their supporters with batons. According to activist Diana Zomeno, "Our companions have been attacked by the police. They were beaten and thrown to the ground. Police officers pinned them down with their knees, including one companion who had a broken shoulder blade." Zomeno added that this treatment came immediately after the activists had "spent four days suffering constant torture, assaults, and violence" while in Israeli custody.

The incident has drawn sharp criticism from Spanish progressive lawmakers. Ione Belarra, leader of the left-wing Podemos party, described the confrontation as "intolerable," while the leftist coalition Sumar condemned what it characterized as "police repression" against the activists. Sumar stated it was unacceptable that people allegedly subjected to violence in Israeli detention would face police brutality, identity checks, and arrests upon their return home.

Organizers of the Global Sumud Flotilla have called for an independent international investigation into the airport incident. The Basque Security Department defended the police response, claiming that some people at the airport obstructed passenger flow and provoked the clash.
