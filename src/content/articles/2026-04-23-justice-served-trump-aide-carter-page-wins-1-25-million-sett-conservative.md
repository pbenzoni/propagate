The US Department of Justice has agreed to pay Carter Page, a foreign policy adviser to President Donald Trump's 2016 campaign, $1.25 million to settle his lawsuit over unlawful FBI surveillance during the discredited Russiagate investigation.

Page filed suit in 2020 after the FBI secretly spied on him based on applications to the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court that contained serious irregularities. The surveillance was partially justified using the notorious Steele dossier—a collection of unverified allegations about Trump's supposed Russia connections that was funded by Hillary Clinton's campaign and compiled by a foreign ex-spy. Page was never charged with any crime and has consistently denied any improper ties to Russia.

The settlement represents a significant acknowledgment of government overreach during an investigation that targeted Trump's campaign with what many conservatives have long called politically motivated surveillance. Page's case was initially dismissed by lower courts before the Trump administration reached this settlement, which covers his claims against the federal government though not against individual former FBI officials.

This payout follows a similar settlement in March with Michael Flynn, Trump's former national security adviser, who received approximately $1.2 million after being unfairly targeted in Special Counsel Robert Mueller's investigation. The DOJ characterized the Flynn settlement as correcting a "historic injustice" tied to Russiagate prosecutions. These settlements underscore what many Americans already knew: the Russia investigation was built on a foundation of political bias and procedural abuse that violated the civil liberties of innocent Americans.
