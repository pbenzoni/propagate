The U.S. Department of Energy announced Friday that it has successfully removed 13.5 kilograms of highly enriched uranium from a decommissioned research reactor in Venezuela, marking the completion of a longstanding nonproliferation effort.

The material, which dates back to the Cold War-era "Atoms for Peace" program that ran from the 1950s through the 1970s, had been housed at a civilian research facility near Caracas. The DOE coordinated the transfer with Venezuela's Ministry of Science and Technology, and the uranium will now be processed and repurposed in the United States.

Nonproliferation experts emphasize that such operations—while not typically linked to imminent threats—are critical for reducing global stockpiles of weapons-usable nuclear material that could potentially be diverted or stolen. The Venezuela transfer is similar to dozens of other uranium-removal missions conducted over the past three decades through U.S.- and International Atomic Energy Agency-backed programs targeting civilian research reactors worldwide.

The announcement comes amid broader nonproliferation challenges, including ongoing tensions with Iran over its significantly larger enriched uranium stockpile, estimated at roughly 440 kilograms. While the administration has sought Iran's cooperation in reducing its nuclear material through diplomatic channels, Tehran has maintained that its enrichment program falls within its sovereign rights under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.
