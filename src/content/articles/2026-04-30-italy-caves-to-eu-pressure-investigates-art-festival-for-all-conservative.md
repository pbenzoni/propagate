Italy's Culture Ministry has launched an investigation into the organizers of the prestigious Venice Biennale art festival after the event allowed Russia to return, according to Italian media reports. The probe comes amid heavy pressure from European Union officials who condemned the decision to permit Russian cultural participation.

Inspectors were dispatched to examine documents and financial records related to the planned reopening of the Russian pavilion. According to Il Fatto Quotidiano, organizers had already provided communications with Russian authorities to the Italian Culture Ministry, and no "irregularities" were found in terms of compliance with sanctions.

The European Commission condemned the decision to allow Russia to reopen its national pavilion, prompting Italian Culture Minister Alessandro Giuli to announce he would boycott the festival's opening. Yet the investigation has sparked pushback from Italian leaders who defend artistic freedom. Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini argued that the art sphere should not fall victim to politics, stating: "I am not for the exclusion of anyone, so I invite the Biennale to go ahead."

Biennale Foundation chief Pietrangelo Buttafuoco defended the institution's decision, comparing it to "the UN of art, from which no nation can be excluded." The controversy highlights growing tensions between unelected EU bureaucrats seeking to impose ideological conformity and those who believe cultural institutions should remain open forums for all nations, regardless of political disputes.
