A US Army Apache attack helicopter crashed near the Strait of Hormuz during operations to enforce restrictions on Iranian oil shipments, President Donald Trump confirmed Monday evening. Both pilots were rescued without injury, the president told reporters after attending an NBA Finals game in New York.

The Apache helicopters have been central to American military efforts to pressure Tehran into reaching a peace agreement and reopening the strategic waterway, which Iran controls. The Strait of Hormuz accounts for approximately 20 percent of global crude oil trade, making it one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints.

The cause of the crash remains unclear. The New York Times initially reported the incident but did not specify whether the helicopter was brought down by hostile fire or experienced a technical failure. According to a Congressional Research Service report from last month based on open-source information, the United States has lost at least 42 aircraft—both manned and unmanned—since the start of American-Israeli military operations against Iran on February 28.

Despite the crash and ongoing tensions, including recent Israeli-Iranian strikes that broke an April ceasefire, Trump expressed optimism about reaching an agreement with Tehran. "We're very close to having a very, very good, strong, powerful deal" that could be signed within days, the president stated. However, significant obstacles remain, as Iran continues to demand sanctions relief and asset unfreezing while rejecting American calls to surrender its enriched uranium stockpile.
