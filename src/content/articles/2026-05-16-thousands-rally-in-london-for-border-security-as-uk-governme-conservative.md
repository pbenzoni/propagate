Massive crowds descended on central London Saturday for the 'Unite the Kingdom' rally, a demonstration focused on illegal immigration, border security, and rising knife crime. Local police estimated approximately 50,000 attendees would participate in the event, which organizers framed as a call for stronger immigration enforcement and protection of free speech.

Ahead of the rally, UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer's government denied entry to eleven foreign activists and commentators, including Polish Member of European Parliament Dominik Tarczynski, Dutch commentator Eva Vlaardingerbroek, and American political influencers. The Home Office revoked previously issued electronic travel authorizations, with Downing Street claiming the individuals' presence was "not conducive to the public good." Starmer defended the decision Thursday, stating his government would not allow people to "spread hate on our streets."

The entry bans sparked accusations from rally supporters that authorities were attempting to suppress legitimate political discourse and limit turnout. Critics argued the government's actions represented an overreach against citizens expressing concerns about border policy and public safety. The Metropolitan Police deployed thousands of officers across London, citing the convergence of multiple large events including the rally, a pro-Palestinian march, and the FA Cup final.

Rally organizer Tommy Robinson, whose legal name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, has characterized the demonstration as a protest against illegal immigration and what he describes as government failures on border control and freedom of expression. In promotional materials, Robinson urged supporters to travel to the capital to "have their voices heard" on issues of national sovereignty and public safety.
