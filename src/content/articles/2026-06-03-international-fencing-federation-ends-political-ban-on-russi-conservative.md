The International Fencing Federation (FIE) announced Tuesday it will lift all restrictions on Russian and Belarusian athletes that were imposed in 2022, allowing them to once again compete under their national flags and anthems. The decision takes effect at the 2026 Senior World Championships in Hong Kong this July.

The move represents a rejection of what many see as the politicization of international sports. The FIE stated the decision "reflects the FIE's commitment to the fundamental principles of the Olympic Charter, including non-discrimination, equal treatment, and the universality of sport."

The fencing federation joins a growing list of international sports bodies pushing back against blanket bans. The World Gymnastics Executive Committee, along with governing bodies for judo, taekwondo, aquatic sports, and wrestling, have similarly restored full participation rights to Russian and Belarusian competitors after years of exclusion.

Russian officials have consistently criticized what they characterize as Western nations applying political pressure to sports federations and employing double standards—punishing Russian athletes while countries involved in other armed conflicts face no similar consequences. The gradual reversal by multiple Olympic federations suggests increasing resistance to mixing athletics with geopolitics, a principle long considered fundamental to the Olympic movement.
