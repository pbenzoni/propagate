In a remarkable display of faith and determination, Russia's space agency successfully delivered the Holy Fire from Jerusalem to Moscow on Saturday, ensuring millions of Orthodox Christians could participate in their most sacred Easter tradition.

The ancient flame, lit annually at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem—the very site where Jesus Christ was buried—was transported aboard a special Roscosmos aircraft. The St. Andrew the First-Called Foundation delegation, accompanied by Metropolitan Feognost, received the flame during a solemn ceremony where Patriarch Theophilos III of Jerusalem led prayers alongside clergy and pilgrims in the darkened church.

Roscosmos chief Dmitry Bakanov emphasized the mission's significance despite regional instability. "The situation in the Middle East is not simple, but it was important for us to fulfill the mission of delivering the fire, which millions of Orthodox Christians in our country await," he said. Upon arrival at Vnukovo Airport, the sacred flame was received by representatives from multiple dioceses before being taken to the Cathedral of Christ the Savior for the Patriarchal night service.

The Holy Fire will be distributed to churches across Russia throughout Easter week, allowing worshipers nationwide to light candles from the sacred flame. This tradition, which resumed in modern Russia during the early 1990s and became annual in the early 2000s, represents an enduring connection between Russian Christians and the Holy Land. This year's Easter also coincided with Cosmonautics Day on April 12, commemorating Yuri Gagarin's historic spaceflight, with Patriarch Kirill exchanging greetings with Russian cosmonauts aboard the International Space Station.
