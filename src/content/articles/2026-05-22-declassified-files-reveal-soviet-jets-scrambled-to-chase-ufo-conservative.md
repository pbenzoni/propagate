After a protracted legal battle spanning decades, the National Security Agency has finally released 334 pages of heavily redacted Cold War-era intelligence files documenting unexplained aerial phenomena tracked by military radar systems worldwide. The documents, obtained by The Disclosure Foundation through a Freedom of Information Act appeal, reveal extraordinary encounters that government bureaucrats fought hard to keep hidden from the American people.

Among the most striking incidents: 13 Soviet-designed MiG fighter jets were scrambled after military radar detected a single unidentified object. Other files describe six MiGs "attacking said UFO," while witnesses reported objects ranging from star-shaped to disc-like craft. One report detailed an "elongated ball of fire" that split into three glowing objects, while another described a spherical craft "brighter than the sun and about half the Moon's diameter."

The files—many marked "TOP SECRET UMBRA," one of the intelligence community's highest classifications—do not specify exact locations or dates of the incidents. However, repeated references to Soviet-made MiG fighters suggest these encounters occurred during the Cold War period, including reported incidents over China.

The NSA initially denied access to these records, extending a transparency fight that began with a 1980 FOIA lawsuit. The release follows the Trump administration's publication of more than 160 UFO-related files earlier this month, part of broader declassification efforts that included documents related to the assassinations of John F. Kennedy, Robert F. Kennedy, and Martin Luther King Jr. The disclosure comes amid renewed national debate after former President Barack Obama dismissed claims the US was concealing evidence of alien contact—a statement that has only intensified demands for government transparency on what our military actually knows about these mysterious encounters.
