President Donald Trump is considering resuming military strikes against Iran after peace negotiations reached an impasse, according to a Monday report from Axios citing three U.S. officials. The potential escalation comes as Trump rejected Iran's latest diplomatic proposals as "totally unacceptable" and described the month-old ceasefire as being "on massive life support."

Reporting indicates Trump met with his national security team Monday to discuss next steps, including potentially restarting airstrikes against remaining Pentagon-identified targets and resuming "Project Freedom"—an operation to escort commercial vessels through the Strait of Hormuz. However, the initiative faces significant obstacles: Saudi Arabia has reportedly refused to allow use of its bases and airspace for the mission, while a CIA assessment cited by the Washington Post suggests Iran has retained approximately 75% of its mobile launchers and could withstand a naval blockade for three to four months.

Iran's chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf emphasized Sunday that the U.S. has "no alternative" but to accept Tehran's terms, warning that delays would increase costs to American taxpayers. Iranian officials have called Trump's demand to dismantle the country's nuclear sites "a non-starter," while pressing their own conditions including sanctions relief, reparations, and a new framework recognizing Iranian sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz.

The standoff highlights the complexity of Middle East diplomacy and raises questions about the effectiveness of military pressure versus negotiated settlements. Regional allies' reluctance to support further military action—Pakistan has served as a mediator while Saudi Arabia has declined to provide logistical support—suggests growing concerns about escalation in an already volatile region.
