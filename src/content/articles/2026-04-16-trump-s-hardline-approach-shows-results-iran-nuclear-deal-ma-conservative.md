Diplomatic efforts to address Iran's nuclear ambitions are showing signs of progress, with Pakistan serving as intermediary between Washington and Tehran. According to Al Jazeera, the likelihood of reaching an agreement has improved significantly following renewed negotiations.

The talks come after President Trump announced a ceasefire on April 7, demonstrating his administration's commitment to projecting strength while pursuing diplomatic solutions. Vice President J.D. Vance led the initial round of discussions in Islamabad, facing off against Iran's Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf over the weekend. The key sticking points remain the duration of any uranium enrichment freeze and Iran's stockpile of 440 kilograms of highly enriched uranium.

Pakistani Army Chief Asim Munir arrived in Tehran on Wednesday carrying new proposals from the United States. Options under consideration include removing Iran's uranium stockpile from the country entirely or reducing enrichment levels to 3% or natural form—a significant concession that would limit Tehran's path to weaponization.

President Trump signaled confidence on Wednesday that the conflict with Iran is "close to being settled," though he made clear that a proposed 20-year moratorium on uranium enrichment falls short of what America's national security requires. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif of Pakistan has been conducting shuttle diplomacy across Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Turkey to build regional support for any potential agreement. While Pakistan's Foreign Ministry confirms no dates have been set for the next round of talks, the momentum suggests Trump's maximum pressure approach may be yielding results where previous administrations failed.
