A citizen-led movement in Alberta has delivered nearly 302,000 signatures to election authorities, far exceeding the threshold needed to trigger a referendum on whether Canada's most productive energy province should chart its own course. The "Stay Free Alberta" initiative, backed by the Alberta Prosperity Project, submitted its petition asking voters: "Do you agree that the Province of Alberta should cease to be part of Canada and become an independent state?"

The grassroots effort reflects deep frustration with federal policies many Albertans see as hostile to their energy sector and economic freedom. Alberta produces over 80% of Canada's crude oil and 60% of its natural gas, boasting the highest per-capita GDP among Canadian provinces. Yet residents say Ottawa's environmental regulations, taxation policies, and barriers to export markets have strangled the province's ability to capitalize on its natural resources.

Premier Danielle Smith has indicated that if the signatures are verified, her government will honor the citizen-initiative process and hold the referendum, though she personally opposes independence. Recent polling shows 29% of Albertans would vote to leave Canada, while 65% prefer to remain—suggesting the movement represents a significant and growing minority voice.

Even if a referendum passed, any path to independence would face substantial legal and political hurdles. Canada's Clarity Act requires the House of Commons to determine whether a vote represents genuine support for secession before negotiations could begin. Indigenous groups have also raised concerns about treaty rights. Still, the signature collection itself sends a powerful message: productive regions are increasingly unwilling to subsidize and submit to distant bureaucratic control that undermines their prosperity.
