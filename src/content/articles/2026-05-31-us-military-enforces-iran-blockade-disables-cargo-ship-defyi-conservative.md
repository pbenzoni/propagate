US Central Command confirmed Saturday that American forces disabled a cargo vessel attempting to breach the Iran blockade near the Strait of Hormuz after the ship's crew ignored more than 20 warnings. The Gambian-flagged M/V Lian Star was struck with a Hellfire missile targeting its engine room, halting its progress toward an Iranian port.

According to CENTCOM, the surgical strike came only after repeated attempts to communicate with the vessel failed. The ship remained adrift following the operation, with US forces maintaining surveillance without boarding. This marked the latest enforcement action in a region handling approximately 25% of global seaborne oil trade and 20% of liquefied natural gas shipments.

The military action occurred amid stalled peace negotiations with Tehran. Iran had previously closed the waterway to ships from what it termed "hostile nations" following US and Israeli airstrikes in late February. The United States subsequently imposed its own blockade on Iranian ports in April. CENTCOM reported that American forces have disabled five commercial vessels and redirected 116 others while maintaining the blockade.

President Trump has maintained pressure on Iran to accept proposed terms, warning of potential resumed military operations. Iranian military leadership has responded with defiant statements, with Admiral Habibollah Sayyari declaring that Iran's armed forces "stand firmly and resolutely against any hostile move by the enemy."
