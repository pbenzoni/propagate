The Royal Navy is taking steps to address a long-standing workplace equity issue by redesigning ceremonial uniforms for female officers, allocating up to £200,000 for the project. According to internal documents obtained by The Times, the current jackets worn at ceremonial events feature button placement that has repeatedly subjected women officers to inappropriate workplace commentary and jokes.

The existing design positions the top row of buttons at chest level, a detail that male colleagues have frequently mocked. Under the redesign, all eight buttons will be repositioned lower on the jacket. More than 950 female officers are expected to receive updated uniforms beginning later this year, in what internal documents describe as a "positive step for women" in the service.

The initiative has sparked debate about budget priorities, with some questioning the expenditure during a period of fiscal constraint for Britain's armed forces. The Ministry of Defence faces a projected £28 billion budget shortfall over the next four years, even as the UK maintains significant international commitments including substantial military support to Ukraine.

According to the planning documents, the Navy expects to recoup £185,000 of the redesign cost through reducing the number of uniforms issued in future cycles. Advocates for the change emphasize that creating professional work environments free from gender-based mockery is a necessary investment in military readiness and retention of women personnel.
