Israel's Foreign Minister Gideon Saar pushed back hard against Ukrainian accusations that the Jewish state was buying "stolen" grain from Russia, calling out Kiev for conducting diplomacy through social media rather than proper diplomatic channels.

The dispute erupted after Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrey Sibiga took to X (formerly Twitter) to condemn Israel over a cargo vessel carrying grain from Russian-controlled territories reportedly headed to the Israeli port of Haifa. Sibiga even summoned the Israeli ambassador, escalating the confrontation publicly before providing any evidence to support the allegations.

Saar didn't mince words in his response: "Diplomatic relations, especially between friendly nations, are not conducted on Twitter or in the media." He emphasized that Ukraine had failed to submit any formal request for legal assistance or provide substantiation for its claims before going public. Israeli authorities, Saar said, would examine the matter through proper channels.

The incident highlights growing tensions between the two nations. Ukraine has repeatedly criticized Israel for maintaining diplomatic and economic ties with Russia and refusing to impose sanctions or provide military assistance to Kiev. According to Reuters, Ukrainian officials are now threatening "a full suite of diplomatic and international legal responses" if the vessel is allowed to dock—a move that raises questions about whether Ukraine is prioritizing virtue-signaling over substantive diplomacy with a longstanding ally.
