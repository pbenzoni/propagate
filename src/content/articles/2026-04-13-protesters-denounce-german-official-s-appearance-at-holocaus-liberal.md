A commemoration event at the Buchenwald concentration camp descended into protest on Sunday as German Culture Minister Wolfram Weimer attempted to deliver remarks at the liberation anniversary. Demonstrators and relatives of victims repeatedly interrupted his 12-minute speech with chants of "fascist" and anti-fascist slogans, underscoring deep concerns about his fitness to honor Holocaust memory.

Weeks before the event, survivor organizations including the Buchenwald-Dora Camp Working Group and the Buchenwald Camp Community had publicly urged Weimer to stay away. Their open letter accused the minister of fundamentally misunderstanding the survivors' legacy, pointing specifically to his repeated misuse of a Heinrich Heine quotation about Jewish assimilation. While the 19th-century writer later regretted his forced baptism—undertaken because Jews were barred from legal careers—Weimer has framed the quote positively, erasing its context of antisemitic oppression.

Since taking office last May, Weimer has faced mounting criticism over actions that critics say amount to political censorship. Earlier this year, he excluded three left-wing bookstores from a national prize based on intelligence service claims of "extremism," ultimately canceling the awards ceremony altogether when controversy erupted. He has also reportedly pressured the Berlin Film Festival to remove its director over her expressions of support for Palestinian rights—a position that violates no German law.

The disruption at Buchenwald highlights growing alarm among civil liberties advocates about the weaponization of Holocaust memory to justify restrictions on political expression, particularly regarding Palestinian solidarity and progressive activism.
