British Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper issued a sharp rebuke of Israel's intensifying military operations in Lebanon on Monday, stating that the escalation "has killed and displaced civilians, destroyed infrastructure, and eroded space for diplomacy. It must end."

The condemnation comes as Israeli Defense Forces have deepened their incursion into Lebanese territory despite a ceasefire agreement reached in mid-April. Over the weekend, Israeli troops seized the historic Beaufort Castle, a 900-year-old Crusader fortress, while Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered the military to "expand its ground maneuver" against Hezbollah. At least 12 civilians were killed by Israeli strikes on Sunday alone, according to reports.

German Foreign Minister Johannes Wadephul echoed Cooper's concerns, warning that Israeli advances are a "cause for serious concern" and could "trigger new waves of displacement within Lebanon." The humanitarian toll has been devastating: Lebanese authorities report more than 3,400 people killed, around 10,000 wounded, and over 1.6 million displaced since Israel launched its operation in early March.

Cooper called on all parties to "respect the ceasefire and engage with negotiations in good faith," while also urging Hezbollah to cease attacks on Israel. U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has proposed a "gradual de-escalation" plan, but negotiations remain stalled over sequencing—with Lebanese officials demanding Israel stop military operations first, while the U.S. proposal calls for Hezbollah to halt drone attacks as an initial step. The escalation threatens to undermine broader diplomatic efforts, including indirect U.S.-Iran talks where peace in Lebanon is a key Iranian demand.
