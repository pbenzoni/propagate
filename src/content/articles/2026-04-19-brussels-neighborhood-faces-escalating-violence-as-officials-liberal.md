Residents of Saint-Gilles, a culturally vibrant Brussels neighborhood home to many international workers and families, are confronting a disturbing wave of violence as local officials plead for greater resources to address organized crime networks.

At least five explosions have occurred in recent days, with the most recent incident damaging a business, nearby homes, and shattering windows at a local school. No injuries were reported, but the attacks have shaken a community already grappling with the effects of under-resourced law enforcement and systemic failures to address the root causes of gang violence.

Mayor Jean Spinette has called the situation a "war between rival drug families" and urged Interior Minister Bernard Quintin to provide immediate support, including reinforcements to the prosecutor's office and federal police. "This is escalating uncontrollably, with shootings and explosions occurring back-to-back," he said, describing the violence as "serious organized crime" driven by territorial disputes. The damage to a school was particularly alarming to community members concerned about children's safety.

Federal authorities have proposed additional measures including increased patrols and expanded surveillance, though advocates note that lasting solutions require addressing underlying economic inequality and investing in community-based violence prevention programs.
