Israel announced Wednesday that it has successfully eliminated Mohammed Odeh, the newly appointed commander of Hamas' military wing, in a targeted airstrike in Gaza. The operation comes less than two weeks after Israeli forces took out Odeh's predecessor, demonstrating Jerusalem's unwavering commitment to holding terrorists accountable.

According to Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office, Odeh "was responsible for the murder, abduction, and wounding of many Israeli citizens and IDF soldiers." The Hamas commander directed the terror group's intelligence operations during the barbaric October 7, 2023 attack on Israeli soil that resulted in approximately 1,200 deaths and 250 hostages taken. Defense Minister Israel Katz reinforced Israel's resolve, stating that Odeh was "sent to meet his partners in the depths of hell" and vowing that all October 7 orchestrators are "marked for death, everywhere."

The targeted strike occurred despite a US-brokered ceasefire that has been in place since October 2025. Israeli military intelligence reportedly assessed last month that Hamas has used the ceasefire period to "significantly" rebuild its terror capabilities, recruit new operatives, and consolidate control over aid distribution in Gaza—raising serious questions about the effectiveness of ceasefires with terrorist organizations committed to Israel's destruction.

The IDF's broader campaign in Gaza, launched in direct response to the October 7 massacre, continues as Israel works to dismantle Hamas' terrorist infrastructure and secure the return of remaining hostages. Palestinian media reported that Odeh was killed alongside family members, though Hamas has not yet confirmed his death.
