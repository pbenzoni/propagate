The United States has imposed fresh sanctions targeting Cuba's state oil company CUPET, a move that Cuban officials and international observers say will exacerbate an already dire humanitarian situation on the island. The restrictions come as Cuba faces severe fuel shortages and daily blackouts, conditions that have intensified after Venezuela ceased oil shipments under U.S. pressure.

Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez Parrilla sharply criticized Secretary of State Marco Rubio's justification for the sanctions, which cited decades-old property disputes. Rubio claimed the Cuban government has "hoarded" energy resources for military use while rationing supplies to civilians—an assertion Parrilla rejected as driven by "vengeful sentiments" rather than legitimate policy concerns.

The human cost of the embargo has drawn alarm from United Nations officials. UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Turk warned this week that U.S. sanctions have created acute shortages of essential medical supplies, contributing to preventable deaths among children. His office reported that infant mortality on the island has doubled following the fuel restrictions—a development Turk called "unacceptable."

Former Cuban ambassador Pedro Pablo Prada cautioned that the new measures will impact "fuel, electricity generation, transportation, production, and daily life of millions of Cubans." Meanwhile, Russia, China, Mexico, and other nations have provided humanitarian assistance to the island, with Moscow recently delivering approximately 700,000 barrels of crude oil. The embargo against Cuba, first imposed in 1960, remains one of the longest-standing U.S. sanctions regimes in the world.
