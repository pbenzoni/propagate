A senior Israeli diplomat has sharply responded to France's call for an emergency UN Security Council meeting over Israeli military activities in Lebanon, suggesting Paris has more urgent problems to address at home.

Waleed Gadban, political advisor to Israel's permanent representative to the United Nations, posted footage on social media showing chaotic scenes from Paris Saint-Germain's Champions League victory celebrations. "Meanwhile in Paris," he wrote, drawing attention to the contrast between France's focus on Israeli operations and its own domestic turmoil.

The rioting following PSG's victory resulted in nearly 800 arrests across France, with more than 200 people injured and at least one person killed in a road accident. Fifty-seven law enforcement officers were also injured attempting to restore order.

France requested the emergency UN meeting after Israel intensified operations in Lebanon and advanced deeper into Lebanese territory, despite a ceasefire announced more than six weeks ago. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot declared that "nothing can justify the continuation of Israeli military operations in Lebanon and its ever-deepening occupation of Lebanese territory."

The Israeli response highlights a common conservative criticism of European nations—that they focus on policing other countries' security decisions while struggling to maintain order within their own borders. Israel maintains its operations are necessary for national security, while critics like France argue they violate ceasefire agreements.
