Iran's Foreign Minister announced Friday that the Strait of Hormuz will be fully reopened to commercial vessels for the duration of the ceasefire in Lebanon, potentially offering relief to global energy markets after months of disruption.

Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi stated the waterway would remain open "for the remaining period of ceasefire" following a 10-day truce between Israeli forces and Hezbollah in Lebanon. The strait has been closed since late February following military strikes on Iran by U.S. and Israeli forces. However, vessels will be required to follow "coordinated routes" set by Iran's maritime authorities, indicating Tehran maintains operational control over the passage.

The closure of this critical chokepoint has had severe economic consequences for American consumers and the global economy. The Strait of Hormuz handles approximately 20% of the world's crude oil exports, and its shutdown drove energy prices sharply higher over recent months. Oil markets responded immediately to the reopening announcement, with crude prices dropping more than 10% to just over $83 per barrel.

President Trump acknowledged the development on Truth Social, appearing to welcome Tehran's decision to reopen what he called the "strait of Iran." The announcement represents a potential thaw in regional tensions, though the temporary nature of the arrangement—tied directly to the Lebanon ceasefire—suggests stability remains fragile. Whether this reopening leads to lasting relief for energy markets will depend on the durability of the broader regional peace efforts.
