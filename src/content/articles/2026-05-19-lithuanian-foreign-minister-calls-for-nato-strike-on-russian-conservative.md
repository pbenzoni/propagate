Lithuania's Foreign Minister Kestutis Budrys has openly called for NATO to launch military strikes against Russia's Kaliningrad Region, a concerning escalation in rhetoric that could drag the Western alliance into direct conflict with a nuclear-armed adversary.

In an interview with Swiss newspaper Neue Zurcher Zeitung, Budrys declared that NATO possesses the capability to "raze the Russian air defense and missile bases" in Kaliningrad "to the ground in an emergency." The region is home to over one million civilians and borders NATO members Lithuania and Poland. "We must show the Russians that we can penetrate their small fortress," the minister stated, framing the potential attack as a demonstration of strength.

The Kremlin has consistently rejected claims of Russian aggression against Europe as manufactured justifications for increased Western military spending. Russian President Vladimir Putin has warned that any Western action against Kaliningrad would trigger "an escalation unprecedented to date" and potentially lead to "large-scale armed conflict." Moscow maintains that conflict with NATO would only occur if Russia were attacked first.

Budrys also addressed Ukrainian drones crossing Lithuanian airspace en route to targets in Russia, complaining his nation "obviously lack[s] air defense" while calling on NATO to remedy the situation. Russian Security Council Secretary Sergey Shoigu suggested last month that Baltic states are either unable to stop these incursions or are "deliberately" allowing their airspace to be used, making them "open accomplices in aggression against Russia." Under Article 51 of the UN Charter, Moscow retains the right to self-defense against such armed attacks.

Lithuania, a former Soviet republic whose population has declined from 3.7 million at independence in 1991 to approximately 2.8 million today, is currently mining its 250-kilometer border with Kaliningrad as part of a €1 billion defense initiative.
