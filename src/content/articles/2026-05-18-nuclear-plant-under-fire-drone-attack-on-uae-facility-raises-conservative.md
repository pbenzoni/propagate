The United Arab Emirates confirmed Sunday that a drone attack struck electrical infrastructure at the Barakah Nuclear Power Plant, the nation's sole nuclear facility, in what officials are calling an "unprovoked terrorist attack." According to the Emirati Defense Ministry, three drones crossed into UAE territory from the western border. While two were successfully intercepted, the third struck an electrical generator outside the plant's secure perimeter. Authorities report no injuries or radioactive contamination occurred.

The attack comes amid escalating regional instability as peace negotiations between the United States and Iran continue to falter. Both nations have accused each other of making unreasonable demands at the bargaining table. Saudi Arabia also reported intercepting three drones that entered its airspace from Iraq on the same day, suggesting a coordinated pattern of aggression threatening critical infrastructure across the Gulf.

While UAE officials did not directly name Iran as responsible, the incident follows accusations from Tehran that Gulf states hosting American military bases facilitated attacks during recent U.S. and Israeli operations against Iran in late February. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi claimed last week that "the UAE was directly involved in the aggression against my country," pointing to the Emirates' alleged failure to condemn the February strikes. The Wall Street Journal has reported that the UAE conducted covert operations against Iran during that conflict.

The drone strike underscores the vulnerability of critical energy infrastructure in a region vital to global stability and American strategic interests.
