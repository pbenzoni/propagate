Taiwan conducted live-fire defensive drills this week, launching approximately 36 US-supplied missiles into waters off its west coast in what officials described as preparations to repel a potential invasion by mainland China. The exercise marked the first time the democratic island has publicly tested HIMARS rocket systems in such proximity to the mainland.

The Wednesday drills come as Taiwan faces mounting pressure from Beijing, which claims the self-governing democracy of 23 million people as its territory despite never having controlled it. Chinese President Xi Jinping has refused to rule out using military force to achieve what he calls "reunification," even as Taiwan maintains its own government, military, and democratic institutions.

The exercise demonstrated the HIMARS system's "shoot-and-scoot" capability—designed to fire and quickly relocate to avoid counterattack. Taiwan has ordered 29 of these mobile launchers from the United States as part of a broader defense modernization effort that includes anti-ship and air defense systems. The systems have a range of up to 300 kilometers, sufficient to reach China's Fujian Province across the strait.

Beijing has condemned US arms sales to Taiwan, calling them interference in what it considers a domestic matter. However, Washington maintains that helping Taiwan defend itself aligns with longstanding US commitments under the Taiwan Relations Act, even as it does not formally recognize Taiwan as independent. During recent meetings, Xi warned that mishandling the Taiwan issue could push US-China relations into "a very dangerous situation."
