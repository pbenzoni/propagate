Iran's chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf sharply criticized what he characterized as a contradictory U.S.-Israel approach to Middle East diplomacy on Sunday, hours after an Israeli airstrike killed at least three people in southern Beirut. The strike came as Washington pushed forward with diplomatic efforts toward a regional agreement.

"The Zionists' invasion of Dahiyeh has once again shown that America either lacks the will to fulfill its commitments or the ability to do so," Ghalibaf wrote on social media. "The game of good cop and bad cop is outdated. If you do not have the will and ability to fulfill your obligations, it is not possible to talk about moving forward."

The Israeli strike, which the IDF said targeted a Hezbollah command center, injured 15 others according to Lebanon's state news agency. President Trump acknowledged the attack "should not have happened" and called on both Israel and Hezbollah to halt hostilities, stating the parties were "very close to a Deal that will bring peace to the region."

Tehran has long insisted that any agreement with Washington must include an end to Israeli military operations in Lebanon. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei indicated that while a deal might not materialize immediately, negotiations could conclude "in the coming days," with discussions focused on ending hostilities and "the issues of Lebanon" rather than Iran's nuclear program at this stage.

The diplomatic tensions highlight the challenge of pursuing peace agreements while military operations continue—a pattern that Iranian officials argue undermines trust and the credibility of negotiated commitments in the region.
