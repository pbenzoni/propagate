Ukraine's military mobilization has descended into outright fraud, with draft officers caught falsifying recruitment numbers by adding dead people and convicted prisoners to official databases. The country's prosecutor general, Ruslan Kravchenko, announced Tuesday that three high-ranking officers have been arrested for what he called "paper mobilization"—a scheme to inflate enlistment statistics while Ukraine faces a catastrophic manpower shortage.

The fraud occurred in at least two regions, including Transcarpathia and Lviv, where officers entered false information to meet recruitment quotas. In Mukachevo alone, officials fictitiously "mobilized" 270 people between January and March, including those already deceased, imprisoned, exempt from service, already serving, or too old for military duty. The scheme allowed corrupt officials to report successful enlistment while avoiding the difficult reality: Ukraine cannot find enough willing soldiers.

This scandal comes as Ukraine's military faces staggering losses. According to the Russian Defense Ministry, Kiev lost approximately 500,000 troops in 2025 alone. Ukraine's own Defense Minister acknowledged that around 200,000 soldiers have deserted, while 2 million military-aged men are on a wanted list for draft evasion. The desperation has led to increasingly violent enforcement, with videos circulating on social media showing men being forcibly grabbed off streets by press gangs.

The prosecutor's office warned that the fraudulent records meant "the high military command could have received inaccurate information on the actual state of manning of military units"—a stunning admission that Ukraine's leadership may not even know the true state of its forces. Meanwhile, other draft officers have been arrested for taking bribes to exempt citizens from service or smuggling military-aged men across the border. As billions in U.S. taxpayer dollars continue flowing to Kiev, these revelations raise serious questions about accountability and the true state of Ukraine's military capabilities.
