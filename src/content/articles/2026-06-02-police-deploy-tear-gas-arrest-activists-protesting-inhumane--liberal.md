At least 20 activists were arrested outside an immigrant detention center in Newark, New Jersey, on Sunday night after police enforced a newly imposed curfew and deployed tear gas against the crowd. The arrests at the Delaney Hall facility followed days of escalating confrontations as demonstrators gathered to support detainees reportedly on hunger strike over what advocates describe as inhumane conditions.

The Department of Homeland Security released footage of officers dragging protesters from barricade lines and escorting them onto sheriff's department buses, announcing on social media there would be "ZERO tolerance for rioters." Newark Mayor Ras Baraka condemned the federal response, stating that "the use of riot gear, flash bangs, tear gas, and similar tactics against lawfully assembled protesters is wrong and clearly untenable."

Delaney Hall has become a flashpoint for protests since late May, when lawyers for detainees reported that a hunger strike had begun over conditions inside the facility. Those being held have told human rights groups they were served expired food, denied adequate medical care, and subjected to abuse by authorities. Democratic officials, including New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill, have accused facility operators of subjecting immigrants to inhumane treatment and restricting access to visitors.

The arrests come amid a sweeping immigration enforcement campaign that has resulted in thousands of detentions nationwide. Public support for the crackdown has weakened in recent months, with many Americans expressing concern that federal tactics have become overly aggressive and, in some cases, resulted in violence and deaths. Mayor Baraka emphasized that the demonstrations were aimed at drawing attention to the "inhumane conditions" detainees face inside the center.
