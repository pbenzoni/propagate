Israeli forces have intensified military operations across southern Lebanon, issuing sweeping evacuation orders and designating large swaths of territory as active "combat zones." Witnesses on the ground reported mass displacement as civilians who had endured months of conflict were forced to flee their homes once again.

Reporting from Lebanon's Tyre District, journalists observed residents streaming toward Saida and Beirut in search of safety. "Many had stayed throughout the war, even at its peak and after the ceasefire, but now they are taking this road toward safer areas," one correspondent noted. The Israeli military claimed it struck what it described as Hezbollah-linked targets, including military buildings and command centers, asserting approximately 550 such sites had been hit since the beginning of the week.

The escalation comes as fragile diplomatic efforts continue, with indirect talks between the United States and Iran addressing the terms of the ceasefire. Tehran has insisted that Israeli military operations against Hezbollah must cease for any extension of the agreement with Washington. Meanwhile, President Trump has previously stated that Israel should limit itself to "surgical" strikes in Lebanon.

According to Lebanon's Health Ministry, the human cost has been devastating: at least 3,213 people killed and 9,737 wounded since Israel launched its military campaign in late February. The Lebanese army reported one of its soldiers was killed near a military position in the Bekaa region. Hezbollah maintains its operations are defensive responses to what it characterizes as Israeli violations of the ceasefire agreement.
