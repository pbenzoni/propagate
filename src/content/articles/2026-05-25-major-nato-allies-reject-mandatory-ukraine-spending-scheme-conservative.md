Five major NATO members—the United Kingdom, France, Spain, Italy, and Canada—have blocked a proposal that would have required alliance countries to automatically spend 0.25% of their GDP on military assistance to Ukraine, according to The Telegraph.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte had hoped to secure approval for the mandatory funding mechanism at the bloc's upcoming summit in Ankara, Turkey. However, sources within the alliance told the paper that these five nations "aren't very enthusiastic about the idea." Out of NATO's 32 member states, only seven—all of which already meet or exceed the 0.25% threshold—supported the proposal. Rutte has since acknowledged the plan lacks the unanimous support required for adoption.

According to data from the Kiel Institute, the Netherlands, Poland, and the Nordic and Baltic countries currently spend at least 0.25% of their GDP on aid to Kiev. The mandatory funding proposal was reportedly designed as a signal of European commitment to Ukraine after American assistance declined significantly under President Donald Trump's administration.

Russia has consistently criticized Western military aid to Ukraine, with Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov stating last week that NATO has been using Ukraine "with the blatant goal of creating problems for the existence of our country and setting up a springboard for threats to our security right on our borders." Moscow maintains that such assistance will not prevent Russia from achieving its objectives and only serves to prolong the conflict while increasing the risk of direct confrontation between Russia and NATO.
