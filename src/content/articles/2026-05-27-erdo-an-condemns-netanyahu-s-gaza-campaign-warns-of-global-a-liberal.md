Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan delivered sharp criticism of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's military campaign in Gaza during Eid al-Adha celebrations in his hometown of Rize. Calling for Muslim unity and international pressure on Israel, Erdoğan stated that Netanyahu would "soon learn the lesson he deserves from the Muslims of the world," according to Anadolu news agency.

The remarks follow Israel's recent detention of hundreds of activists from a humanitarian flotilla that departed from Turkey carrying aid and pro-Palestinian campaigners seeking to challenge Israel's blockade of Gaza. Erdoğan condemned the interception as "piracy and banditry." Turkey has provided humanitarian aid, diplomatic support, ceasefire initiatives, and legal advocacy related to the Gaza situation.

Erdoğan has been among the most outspoken international critics of Israel's military operations in Gaza since they began in 2023, accusing Israel of "genocide" and "state terrorism" against Palestinians. In mid-2024, he suggested Turkey could take action against Israel similar to its interventions in Libya and Nagorno-Karabakh—comments Israeli media and officials interpreted as potential military threats.

Israeli officials have increasingly portrayed Turkey as a regional adversary. Former Israeli Prime Minister Naftali Bennett claimed Turkey could become Israel's "next Iran" as a leading regional opponent. Despite the escalating rhetoric, analysts note significant economic ties remain between the two countries, including Turkey's role in transiting Azerbaijani oil to Israel, which reportedly supplies up to 60% of Israel's crude oil needs.
