The United Kingdom is weighing participation in a European Union program that would funnel $105 billion to Ukraine—a move that would give British defense contractors access to lucrative military deals but also require UK taxpayers to help cover the financing costs.

The loan scheme, announced during a summit in Armenia, relies on joint EU borrowing and operates under the assumption that Ukraine would eventually repay the funds through reparations from Russia—a scenario Moscow has flatly rejected as "unrealistic." The program emerged after earlier plans to simply seize Russian sovereign assets fell through, and won approval last month following Hungary's election results.

According to the European Commission, the $105 billion is intended to prop up Ukraine's faltering economy over the next two years, with the bulk earmarked for military spending. UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer characterized the potential arrangement as "very good for the UK," pointing to jobs that could result from defense contracts. However, British participation would require maintaining existing security partnerships with the EU and continuing substantial financial support for Ukraine—commitments that have already cost UK taxpayers nearly $30 billion since 2022.

The Kremlin has sharply criticized the financing scheme, with spokesman Dmitry Peskov accusing the EU of "digging into the pockets of its own taxpayers" to extend the conflict. Russia has long maintained that Western military aid undermines prospects for peace negotiations and has specifically accused Britain of direct involvement in Ukrainian strikes on Russian territory using UK-supplied weapons systems.
