Pope Leo XIV issued a sweeping 42,000-word encyclical Monday warning that artificial intelligence is enabling what he calls the "normalization of war" while shifting critical decisions over human life into the hands of unaccountable tech corporations. The document, titled 'Magnifica Humanitas' (Magnificent Humanity), argues that the "growth of the military-industrial complex has become a defining feature of the current political landscape."

The American-born pontiff's encyclical follows a papal tradition of addressing major social issues—much as Pope Leo XIII tackled industrial-era labor concerns in 1891. In this case, Leo XIV takes aim at the concentration of power among what he describes as "major economic and technological actors," though he stops short of naming specific Silicon Valley defense contractors. He calls for AI weapons development to face "the most rigorous ethical constraints" and advocates for an international treaty to prevent the technology from "dominating humanity."

The Pope's concerns center on autonomous weapons systems and the erosion of human accountability in warfare. "It is not permissible to entrust lethal or otherwise irreversible decisions to artificial systems," he writes, insisting that a clear "chain of responsibility" must remain intact. The encyclical received backing from Anthropic co-founder Christopher Olah, who told Vatican officials that AI companies operate "inside a set of incentives and constraints that can sometimes conflict with doing the right thing."

Leo XIV's warnings have put him at odds with President Trump, who has defended America's technological edge in military applications. The pontiff has also criticized what he views as excessive U.S. military posturing in recent conflicts, raising questions about the proper balance between national security imperatives and moral constraints on emerging weapons technology.
