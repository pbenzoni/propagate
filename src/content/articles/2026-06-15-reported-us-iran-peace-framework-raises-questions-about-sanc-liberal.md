The United States and Iran have reportedly reached a preliminary framework aimed at de-escalating hostilities in the Middle East, following weeks of negotiations mediated by Pakistan. A formal signing ceremony is scheduled for Friday in Geneva, though the full text of the agreement remains undisclosed and the two governments have issued contradictory statements about its contents.

According to media reports, the memorandum of understanding addresses multiple issues including ceasefire extensions, sanctions relief, unfrozen Iranian assets, reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, and commitments regarding non-interference. The agreement appears to extend a fragile ceasefire for 60 days to allow further negotiations, with both sides declaring an end to military operations across all fronts, including Lebanon—where Israel has conducted operations against Hezbollah. However, Israel, which is not party to the agreement, has indicated its forces will not withdraw.

Significant contradictions have emerged around financial provisions. Iranian media reports suggest the US agreed to unfreeze approximately $24 billion in Iranian assets and suspend sanctions on oil exports, along with presenting a $300 billion reconstruction plan. Vice President J.D. Vance, however, has denied that the deal includes any unfreezing of assets. The agreement also reportedly addresses Iran's nuclear program, with assurances that uranium enrichment would remain at civilian levels, though final terms on nuclear issues are expected within 60 days.

Concerns persist about the deal's enforceability and durability, particularly given ongoing tensions with Israel and the lack of transparency surrounding key provisions. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global energy supplies, is expected to reopen, though reports differ on whether transit fees will eventually be imposed. Critics have raised questions about whether the framework adequately addresses human rights concerns or provides sufficient verification mechanisms for nuclear commitments.
