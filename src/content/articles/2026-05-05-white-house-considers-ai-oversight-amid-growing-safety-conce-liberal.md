The White House is exploring new oversight mechanisms for artificial intelligence models before their public release, according to a New York Times report citing administration sources. The potential shift comes as public concern about AI safety—particularly its military applications—has reached new heights.

The proposed framework would establish a working group bringing together government officials and technology executives to evaluate advanced AI systems. While the mechanism might not grant authorities the power to block releases outright, it would provide early access to assess potential risks, including cybersecurity vulnerabilities that could be exploited or capabilities that might be useful for defense applications.

The deliberations reportedly gained momentum after Anthropic chose not to publicly release its Claude Mythos model, citing concerns that it could expose critical software vulnerabilities. The company has also been embroiled in a legal dispute with the Department of Defense after refusing to weaken safety protocols around surveillance and autonomous weapons systems—leading the Pentagon to label it a supply-chain risk.

Public sentiment reflects deep unease about AI's trajectory. Recent polling shows a majority of Americans are more concerned than excited about AI development, with that anxiety increasing significantly since 2021. Concerns are particularly acute around military applications: 79% of Americans believe humans should make final decisions on lethal force, and 75% say AI is not reliable enough for life-or-death scenarios. Two-thirds support restrictions on how technology companies' products can be used, even by government agencies.

Any oversight framework would likely be introduced through executive order, though no timeline has been established. The approach under consideration resembles the UK's AI Security Institute model, which evaluates advanced systems for risks and advises on appropriate safeguards. Such a mechanism would represent a notable evolution in AI policy, balancing innovation concerns with growing demands for accountability and safety in an era of rapid technological advancement.
