The Trump administration is actively working to reinstate monuments that were removed during the 2020 racial justice protests following George Floyd's murder, according to a Wall Street Journal report. An executive order signed by President Trump in early 2025 calls for the restoration of monuments taken down since 2020, framing their removal as a "false reconstruction of American history."

More than 260 historical monuments were removed, vandalized, or toppled during the 2020 protests, with over half commemorating Confederate figures. Communities across the country had removed statues honoring colonizers and enslavers as part of a broader reckoning with systemic racism and historical injustice.

Now, conservative organizations are filing lawsuits to reverse those decisions. In Columbus, Ohio—where the city removed a statue of Christopher Columbus in 2020 after its Democratic mayor called it a representation of the country's "ugly past"—Italian-American groups are suing for its restoration. The Trump administration has taken direct action as well, erecting a replica Columbus statue near the White House and returning a monument honoring Caesar Rodney, a Declaration of Independence signer who enslaved people, to Washington's Freedom Plaza.

Critics view these efforts as an attempt to erase the progress made during 2020's racial justice movement. The removals represented communities' decisions to stop honoring figures associated with genocide, colonization, and slavery—a step toward acknowledging historical harm and its ongoing impacts.
