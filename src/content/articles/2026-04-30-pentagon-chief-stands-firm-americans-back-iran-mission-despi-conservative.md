Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth pushed back against critics of the U.S. military operation in Iran during heated testimony before the House Armed Services Committee, asserting that the American people support the mission despite polling data that opponents cite to argue otherwise.

In his first major public appearance since operations began, Hegseth confronted lawmakers who have characterized the conflict as a "quagmire," accusing them of providing propaganda fodder to America's enemies. He particularly targeted Democrats for what he called "reckless, feckless, and defeatist" rhetoric, questioning their priorities: "Who are you cheering for here? Who are you pulling for?"

President Trump reinforced Hegseth's position, pointing to a Harvard Harris Poll showing 52% support for airstrikes on Iran. That same survey revealed strong backing among Republican voters, with 79% supporting the strikes. The operation, which began with coordinated strikes in February and has cost $25 billion to date, aims to halt Iran's nuclear weapons program following the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

The Defense Secretary's testimony comes as Democrats reportedly consider legal challenges, claiming the administration violated federal law by not seeking additional congressional approval for ongoing operations. Business leaders have expressed concerns about energy market disruptions, while national security hawks argue the mission remains vital to preventing Iranian nuclear capabilities and protecting American interests in the region.
