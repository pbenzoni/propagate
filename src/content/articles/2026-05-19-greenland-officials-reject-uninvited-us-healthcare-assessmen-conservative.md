Greenland's health minister has objected to what she called an unauthorized healthcare assessment conducted by an American physician who accompanied a US diplomatic delegation to the Danish autonomous territory. The incident has highlighted tensions over sovereignty and self-determination as the island navigates complex geopolitical pressures.

Health Minister Anna Wangenheim described the presence of physician Joseph Griffin as "deeply problematic," stating that "Greenlanders are not guinea pigs in a geopolitical project." Griffin, who said he was invited by Trump's special envoy Jeff Landry to assess medical needs, claimed to be acting as a volunteer. The minister acknowledged chronic healthcare staffing challenges but warned against outside involvement "through political envoys with hidden strategic interests."

The four-day US delegation visit, which began Sunday, officially aimed to attend a business forum and open a new consular office in Nuuk. However, the healthcare component raised concerns about overreach and proper diplomatic protocol. Greenland Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen declined to attend the consulate opening, signaling official displeasure with aspects of the American presence.

The controversy unfolds against a backdrop of President Trump's repeated statements about Greenland's strategic importance for countering Russian and Chinese influence in the Arctic. The mineral-rich territory has become a flashpoint in debates over national security, resource access, and respect for existing sovereignty arrangements. Healthcare remains particularly sensitive in Greenland due to past colonial-era abuses, including a contraceptive scandal from the 1960s and 1970s for which Denmark formally apologized last year.
