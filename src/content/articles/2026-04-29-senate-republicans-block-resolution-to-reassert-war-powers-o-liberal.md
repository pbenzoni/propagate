The Senate voted Tuesday to reject a war powers resolution that would have required President Donald Trump to seek congressional authorization before using military force against Cuba. The measure, introduced by Senator Tim Kaine of Virginia, was defeated 51-47 along party lines, with the Republican majority ruling it out of order on procedural grounds.

Kaine argued the resolution was necessary given Trump's increasingly aggressive posture toward the island nation, including an oil blockade imposed in February that has intensified the decades-long U.S. embargo. The senator cited humanitarian consequences of the blockade, including disruptions to medical care, clean water shortages, and rising food prices across Cuba. Trump has repeatedly suggested possible regime-change operations, stating Cuba is "next" after current Middle East conflicts and touting what he calls "peace through strength."

The blockade has been enforced through threats of sanctions against any country or company shipping crude oil to Cuba, effectively cutting off the island's primary energy supplies. Cuba has experienced widespread blackouts and severe fuel shortages after Venezuela, historically its main oil supplier, halted shipments under U.S. pressure. Cuban officials have condemned what they describe as a brutal economic assault on their country.

The vote represents the latest skirmish over executive war powers, with Democrats seeking to reinforce Congress's constitutional role in authorizing military action. Republicans argued the resolution was premature given the absence of active hostilities. Russia recently delivered fuel supplies to Cuba despite the U.S. blockade, with Moscow pledging continued support for the island nation.
