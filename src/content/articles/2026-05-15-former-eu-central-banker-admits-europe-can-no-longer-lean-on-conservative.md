Former European Central Bank chief Mario Draghi has publicly acknowledged what American conservatives have argued for years: Europe can no longer expect the United States to shoulder the burden of its security and prosperity. Speaking in Germany while accepting an award for European integration, Draghi warned that President Trump's approach has left Europe "truly alone together."

Draghi pointed to Trump's tariff policies and unilateral decision-making as evidence that Washington is charting its own course—moves that have rattled a European establishment long accustomed to American deference. The former Italian prime minister called Trump's policies "confrontational and unpredictable," though he conceded the shift represents a "necessary wake-up call" for a continent that has grown complacent.

The remarks underscore a fundamental realignment of transatlantic relations under Trump's leadership. For decades, American taxpayers have subsidized European defense while wealthy EU nations underinvested in their own militaries and criticized U.S. foreign policy. Trump's insistence that NATO members meet their financial obligations and his willingness to prioritize American interests—whether through trade policy or military deployments—have forced European leaders to confront uncomfortable realities.

Draghi now advocates for deeper European integration and a "Made in Europe" industrial strategy, essentially calling for the bloc to do what Trump has long demanded: take responsibility for its own defense, technology, and economic competitiveness. Relations have been further strained by European criticism of U.S. actions in the Middle East and Trump's 2026 National Security Strategy, which warned of Europe's strategic unreliability.

The former central banker's speech represents a tacit admission that the era of European free-riding on American security guarantees is ending. Whether the EU can actually follow through on building genuine strategic autonomy remains an open question—but Trump's policies have forced the conversation that Washington insiders avoided for generations.
