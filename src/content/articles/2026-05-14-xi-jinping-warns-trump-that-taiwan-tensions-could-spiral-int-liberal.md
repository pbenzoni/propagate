During a high-stakes meeting at the Great Hall of the People on Thursday, Chinese President Xi Jinping delivered a stark warning to President Donald Trump: mishandling the Taiwan situation could push the world's two largest economies toward a "very dangerous situation" that might even escalate into direct conflict.

Xi characterized Taiwan as "the most important issue in China-US relations," emphasizing that the self-governed island—which Beijing claims as part of its territory—represents the single most sensitive flashpoint between the nations. "If it is handled properly, the bilateral relationship will enjoy overall stability," Xi stated. "Otherwise, the two countries may clash or even come into conflict, pushing the entire China-US relationship into a very dangerous situation."

The meeting marks the first US presidential visit to China in nearly nine years and comes amid mounting concerns about potential arms deals and diplomatic concessions. Taiwan officials have expressed alarm at the summit, with cabinet spokesperson Michelle Lee pushing back against Xi's framing by asserting that "China's military threat is the sole source of insecurity in the Taiwan Strait and the broader Indo-Pacific region."

While Washington officially acknowledges the One China policy, successive administrations have maintained robust unofficial ties with Taiwan's democratic government, including tens of billions of dollars in defensive arms sales over recent decades. The Trump administration approved an $11 billion weapons package in December, though reports suggest an even larger $13 billion arms deal has been temporarily frozen ahead of this week's summit. Xi also invoked the concept of the 'Thucydides Trap'—a theory describing how conflict often emerges when a rising power challenges an established hegemon—questioning whether the two nations could forge a new model for major-power relations.
