Two prominent artificial intelligence executives have offered starkly contrasting assessments of AI's threat to American workers, even as major technology companies eliminate tens of thousands of jobs while investing billions in AI systems.

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman acknowledged this week that his earlier warnings about AI-driven job losses were premature. "I thought there would have been more impact on entry-level white-collar jobs being eliminated by now than has actually happened," Altman told an Australian banking executive. He suggested many roles retain an "irreplaceable human part" that AI cannot yet replicate.

But Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah painted a far grimmer picture during remarks at the Vatican, warning of "a real possibility that AI will displace human labor at very large scale." Olah called supporting displaced workers "a moral imperative of historic proportions" and raised concerns about AI development being concentrated in wealthy nations with no mechanism for sharing benefits globally.

The debate unfolds against a backdrop of massive tech industry layoffs. Meta and Microsoft are reportedly considering cuts of up to 16,000 employees combined, while Amazon has announced roughly 30,000 layoffs linked to AI restructuring. Oracle has also reportedly eliminated around 30,000 positions worldwide. Polling from March showed nearly 80% of Americans fear the government has no plan to protect workers from AI-related job displacement.

Senator Bernie Sanders has warned that AI could eliminate nearly 100 million jobs across all sectors in the coming decade. Pope Francis has also repeatedly cautioned that AI poses risks to "human dignity, justice, and labor," calling for strict controls on the technology.
