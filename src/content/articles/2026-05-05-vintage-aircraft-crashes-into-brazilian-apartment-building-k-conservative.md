A tragic aviation accident in Belo Horizonte, Brazil claimed three lives Monday when a small single-engine aircraft lost power shortly after departure and collided with a residential building. The 1979 Embraer 721C plane, carrying five occupants, took off from Pampulha Airport around midday before the pilot radioed the control tower reporting difficulties during the initial climb.

Dramatic footage shows the struggling aircraft flying dangerously low over the neighborhood, with the pilot apparently attempting to navigate between taller structures. The plane ultimately struck the stairwell area between the third and fourth floors of a four-story apartment building in the Silveira neighborhood, narrowly avoiding direct impact with occupied living spaces. Emergency responders arrived within minutes of the 12:25 PM crash.

Among the deceased were pilot Wellington Oliveira, 34, and Fernando Moreira Souto, 36, son of the mayor of Jequitinhonha. Two survivors were initially transported to area hospitals in serious condition, though one later died from injuries sustained in the crash. Remarkably, no residents of the building were harmed despite the aircraft's violent impact.

Brazilian aviation authorities, including the Center for Investigation and Prevention of Aeronautical Accidents, have launched a full investigation into the incident. The age of the aircraft—manufactured in 1979—and the pilot's reported mechanical difficulties immediately after takeoff will be central focuses of the inquiry. Local civil police are conducting a parallel investigation to determine the exact cause of this devastating accident.
