Iran's Foreign Ministry has contradicted President Donald Trump's assertion that Iranian leadership approved a preliminary peace agreement, calling recent reports mere "speculation." The denial comes after Trump claimed on Thursday that a deal could be signed as early as this weekend and that Iran's Supreme Leader had greenlit the terms.

Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei told Iranian media that "the relevant authorities must review every detail of the text," adding that "the contradictory positions of the United States have always caused turbulence and disruption in this process." While acknowledging that major portions of the text have been finalized, Baghaei emphasized that Iran would not compromise on its core principles.

The disputed framework would reportedly extend a truce, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and establish parameters for future nuclear program discussions. However, significant gaps remain: Tehran continues to reject Trump's demand for its enriched uranium stockpile, maintaining that its nuclear program serves peaceful purposes only.

This week saw a dangerous resumption of military strikes after two consecutive days of bombardment—the most serious escalation since an April ceasefire. The renewed hostilities triggered another spike in global oil prices, compounding inflation pressures on American families.
