The Trump administration is exploring a pragmatic approach to American energy independence by considering oil extraction from beneath U.S. military bases, Bloomberg reported. The move aims to refill the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR), an emergency stockpile created in the mid-1970s to cushion against oil supply disruptions and price spikes.

Energy Secretary Chris Wright outlined the common-sense rationale at a Wall Street Journal forum in mid-April. "We have military bases or facilities that are in the middle of oil fields, but there's no development under those resources. That's crazy. It's right there," Wright explained. "We need creative ways to fill the strategic petroleum reserve all the way up."

According to Bloomberg, extracting oil from Department of Defense sites would allow the federal government to directly own the crude rather than purchasing it from private producers—a more cost-effective approach to rebuilding reserves. While the strategy may not deliver immediate relief at the pump, it represents a long-term investment in national security and energy self-sufficiency.

The SPR currently holds approximately 415 million barrels—the lowest level since the mid-1980s—after the Biden administration drew down reserves following the 2022 Ukraine conflict escalation. Global oil prices recently climbed above $100 per barrel amid Middle East tensions, with gasoline averaging over $4.50 per gallon this week. In March, Trump authorized releasing 172 million barrels from the SPR through 2027 under a loan program requiring energy companies to return borrowed oil plus additional barrels as premium.

Last September, the administration sold drilling rights for oil and gas beneath nearly 2,000 acres at Louisiana's Barksdale Air Force Base, home to B-52 strategic bombers, signaling the administration's willingness to leverage untapped federal resources for American energy security.
