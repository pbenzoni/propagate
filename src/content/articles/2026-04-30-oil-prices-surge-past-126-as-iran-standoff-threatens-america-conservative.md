The price of Brent crude oil climbed above $126 per barrel on Thursday, marking its highest point since March 2022, as tensions escalate between the United States and Iran over control of vital energy shipping lanes. West Texas Intermediate crude also jumped more than 10% to reach $110.50 per barrel.

The price spike follows reports that President Donald Trump is prepared to maintain a blockade of Iranian ports for an extended period as leverage in ongoing negotiations with Tehran. Iranian authorities have responded by continuing to obstruct vessels from the U.S. and allied nations from transiting the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint that handles roughly 25% of the world's crude oil trade.

Direct negotiations between American and Iranian representatives have stalled since failed talks in Islamabad in mid-April. Iran's foreign minister reportedly declined to meet with Trump's envoys for a scheduled weekend session, though back-channel communications continue through Pakistani intermediaries. The confrontation began after a U.S.-Israeli military operation against Iran in late February.

The oil market faced additional uncertainty this week when the United Arab Emirates announced its departure from OPEC and the broader OPEC+ coalition effective May 1. Abu Dhabi characterized the move as a sovereign decision designed to provide greater flexibility in managing its oil production levels according to national interests.
