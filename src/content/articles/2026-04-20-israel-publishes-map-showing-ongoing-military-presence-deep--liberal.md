The Israeli military has released a map detailing what it calls a "forward defense line" several kilometers inside southern Lebanon, raising concerns about compliance with the recently announced ceasefire agreement. The deployment line stretches 5-10 kilometers beyond the internationally recognized border, encompassing dozens of villages that have been abandoned or destroyed during Israel's military operations.

The map's publication comes just days after a U.S.-brokered ten-day ceasefire took effect following direct talks between Israeli and Lebanese officials on April 14—the first such negotiations in decades. The ceasefire was intended to halt fighting between Israeli forces and Hezbollah that has devastated southern Lebanon. According to Lebanese authorities, Israeli operations have killed approximately 3,700 people across the country, with nearly 2,300 deaths occurring since early March alone. More than 7,500 have been wounded and over 1 million displaced.

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz stated Sunday that the military has been authorized to use "full force" in Lebanon, even during the ceasefire period, and ordered the demolition of structures along the border deemed security threats. The IDF currently has five divisions and naval forces operating in the area, saying the operations are necessary to prevent attacks on northern Israeli communities.

Hezbollah, which did not participate in the Washington negotiations, has indicated conditional support for the ceasefire while demanding a complete halt to attacks and full Israeli withdrawal from Lebanese territory. The group had fired rockets in support of Iran, which has made an end to Israeli operations in Lebanon a precondition for broader regional diplomatic agreements. International observers have expressed concern about the humanitarian crisis and the question of when—or whether—Israeli forces will fully withdraw from Lebanese territory.
