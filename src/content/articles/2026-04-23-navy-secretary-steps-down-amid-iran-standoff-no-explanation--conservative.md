US Navy Secretary John C. Phelan has stepped down from his post "effective immediately," the Pentagon announced Tuesday, offering no public explanation for the sudden departure during a tense naval standoff with Iran.

Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell confirmed that Undersecretary Hung Cao will assume the role of acting secretary. "On behalf of the Secretary of War and Deputy Secretary of War, we are grateful to Secretary Phelan for his service to the Department and the United States Navy," Parnell said in a brief statement that provided no details about the circumstances of Phelan's exit.

Phelan, a businessman and investor with no prior military service, was sworn in as the 79th secretary of the Navy in March 2025 after being nominated by President Donald Trump. He had overseen the Department of the Navy for just over a year, a period that included the implementation of a U.S. blockade on Iranian ports. The confrontation in and around the Strait of Hormuz—a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies—remains active despite an extended ceasefire and stalled diplomatic efforts.

While the Navy secretary does not directly authorize combat operations, the post oversees the Department of the Navy's readiness, deployments, and policy execution. That means Phelan would have been deeply involved in managing the operational tempo and strategic posture during the Iran crisis. His departure comes just weeks after Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth forced out Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George and two other senior Army generals in a separate shake-up at the Pentagon.
