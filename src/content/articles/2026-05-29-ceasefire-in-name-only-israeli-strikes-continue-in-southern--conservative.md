Despite an officially declared ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah, deadly airstrikes have continued to hit villages in southern Lebanon, according to reports from the region. The strikes on Thursday coincided with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's statements about expanding military operations in southern Lebanon to establish what Israel characterizes as a necessary "security zone" along the border.

Local officials in Burj al-Shamali reported that an Israeli strike killed 14 people, including six women and six children, when it hit a residential area. Witnesses described devastating scenes with families trapped beneath collapsed buildings and civilians searching through rubble for survivors. In nearby Maraki, rescuers reportedly pulled a young girl alive from debris after a strike destroyed her family home, though several family members were killed or wounded.

Israel maintains that its operations target Hezbollah positions and infrastructure. Netanyahu stated earlier this week that the IDF was "seizing dominant terrain" and would continue operations aimed at pushing Hezbollah forces farther from northern Israel. However, the continued clashes despite ceasefire arrangements have raised concerns about mounting civilian casualties.

According to the Lebanese Health Ministry, more than 3,269 people have been killed and over 9,800 injured since the conflict escalated on March 2. The Israeli campaign against Hezbollah began days after US-Israeli strikes on Iran, highlighting the broader regional tensions that continue to fuel the conflict along Israel's northern border.
