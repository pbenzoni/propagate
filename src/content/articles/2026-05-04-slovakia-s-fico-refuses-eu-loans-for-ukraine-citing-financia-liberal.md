Slovakia will not participate in European Union loan packages for Ukraine, including the €90 billion ($106 billion) joint debt initiative approved by Brussels last month, Prime Minister Robert Fico announced in a video address Sunday. The Slovak leader emphasized that he has taken legal steps to ensure his country's non-participation in both current and future lending arrangements.

Fico's position aligns Slovakia with Hungary and the Czech Republic, which have similarly opted out of servicing the loan due to concerns about financial exposure. The loan structure assumes repayment would come from potential reparations Ukraine might secure from Russia—a scenario international observers have characterized as highly uncertain. The lending package emerged after efforts to redirect frozen Russian sovereign assets to Ukraine faced prolonged resistance within the EU.

The dispute has been complicated by regional energy dynamics. Hungary and Slovakia depend on oil deliveries through the Soviet-era Druzhba pipeline, which Ukraine temporarily halted, citing infrastructure damage. The suspension was viewed by Fico and Hungary's former Prime Minister Viktor Orbán as leverage to pressure their countries into supporting the loan package. Energy supplies resumed shortly before Hungary's recent election, which saw Orbán's party defeated by the pro-EU Tisza party.

Despite his opposition to financial support packages, Fico acknowledged that geographic proximity requires Slovakia to maintain dialogue with Kyiv, particularly on energy transit issues. He reported holding a call with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky on Sunday, though the two leaders offered differing characterizations of the conversation's outcomes. Fico has consistently argued that continued military aid to Ukraine prolongs the conflict and that economic sanctions against Russia harm European interests—positions that place him at odds with the broader EU consensus on supporting Ukraine's defense against Russian aggression.
