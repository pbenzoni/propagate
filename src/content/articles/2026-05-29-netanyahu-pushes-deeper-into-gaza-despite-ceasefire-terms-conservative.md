Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has directed his military to expand control over Gaza to 70% of the territory, exceeding the 53% maximum established under a US-brokered ceasefire agreement. The move comes as implementation of the peace deal has stalled amid continued security challenges.

Speaking at the Jordan Valley Conference, Netanyahu acknowledged Israel currently controls 60% of Gaza and outlined plans for further expansion. "We now control 60% of the territory in the Strip. We were at 50, we moved to 60," Netanyahu stated. "My directive is to move to… first of all 70%. Let's start with that."

The 2025 ceasefire agreement, backed by the UN Security Council, was designed as a phased Israeli withdrawal in exchange for Hamas disarming and relinquishing territorial control. However, the terrorist organization has refused to surrender its weapons, according to Bulgarian diplomat Nikolay Mladenov, who oversees ceasefire implementation. Sporadic fighting has continued throughout the supposed peace period.

The conflict originated after Hamas-led terrorists attacked southern Israel on October 7, 2023, killing approximately 1,200 people and taking more than 250 hostages. Israel's subsequent military response has resulted in over 72,000 casualties in Gaza, according to local health authorities. An international reconstruction initiative launched by President Trump in January has largely stalled amid the broader regional conflict with Iran.
