Kentucky's Republican primary has become ground zero in the fight for the soul of the conservative movement, as Rep. Thomas Massie faces the most expensive primary challenge in American history. The 14-year congressman with an unblemished record on gun rights, border security, and limiting federal overreach now confronts over $15 million in outside spending aimed at ending his career.

Massie's conservative credentials are impeccable: he has opposed every gun control measure in Congress, fought to abolish the Department of Education, and voted to deny birthright citizenship to children of illegal immigrants. He also stood against COVID-era government overreach, opposing massive spending bills that ballooned the national debt. Yet these America First positions have put him at odds with establishment forces backing his challenger, Ed Gallrein, a military veteran whose campaign is funded almost entirely by pro-Israel lobbying organizations.

The battle highlights growing tensions within the conservative coalition. Massie represents the constitutional, limited-government wing that opposes endless Middle East conflicts and questions whether American taxpayer dollars should fund foreign wars. His opponent Gallrein—who left the Republican Party in 2016 and vowed not to return until it was rid of certain influences—now enjoys support from the same neoconservative establishment that once opposed populist conservative movements.

According to Massie's campaign, over 95% of Gallrein's funding comes from pro-Israel interest groups, including more than $4 million from AIPAC and nearly $8 million from a super PAC funded by major donors. The unprecedented spending raises questions about whether elected representatives answer to their constituents or to wealthy special interests. For many constitutional conservatives, the Kentucky race will determine whether the GOP remains committed to putting American interests first or returns to the interventionist foreign policy of previous administrations.
