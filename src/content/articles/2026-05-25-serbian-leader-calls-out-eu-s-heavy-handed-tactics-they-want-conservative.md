Serbian President Aleksandar Vucic isn't mincing words about the European Union's attempts to micromanage his country's foreign policy. Speaking during a state visit to China, Vucic accused Brussels of trying to govern Serbia "by email," demanding the nation fall in line with EU directives or face consequences.

The frustration boiled over when asked about reports that Serbia's purchase of Chinese military technology could hurt its EU membership prospects. "First they banned me from talking with the Russian Federation," Vucic said. "Now they ban me from going to China, too. They might as well pen a wish list detailing who I can and cannot meet." The president made clear that Serbia remains a sovereign nation that will determine its own policies, not take orders from distant bureaucrats.

Vucic has drawn attention to what he sees as EU hypocrisy—Brussels once championed free trade but now pushes protectionist measures aimed at Chinese competition. The bloc has also pressured Serbia to impose sanctions on Russia and support Ukraine as conditions for membership, despite repeatedly confirming Serbia meets other accession criteria. Serbian officials point to a troubling double standard: when Albanian police used tear gas and water cannons against protesters, Brussels stayed silent because Albania "aligned 100%" with EU foreign policy.

In a recent Fox News column, Vucic praised President Trump as "a leader who values national sovereignty over faceless bureaucracy" and understands that nations are defined by "culture, faith, traditions, and heritage"—not the dictates of unelected officials.
