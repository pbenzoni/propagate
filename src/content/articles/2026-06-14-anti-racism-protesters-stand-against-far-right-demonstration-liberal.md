Thousands of anti-racism activists took to the streets of Glasgow, Scotland on Saturday in a powerful show of solidarity, organized by Stand Up to Racism to "reclaim the streets from the far right." Demonstrators carried Scottish and Palestinian flags while chanting "Refugees are welcome here," according to local reporting.

The rally drew approximately 70 counter-protesters, many wearing balaclavas and black clothing, who attempted to disrupt the peaceful demonstration. Police worked to maintain separation between the groups, with some anti-immigration demonstrators pushing against barriers and shoving officers. Authorities arrested one individual for threatening an officer and reported investigating multiple hate crimes stemming from the confrontations.

The demonstrations occurred amid heightened tensions following recent violent incidents that far-right groups have exploited to advance anti-immigration narratives. Right-wing politicians and activists have used these events to promote what they term "two-tier" policing claims, alleging differential treatment based on the backgrounds of alleged perpetrators—assertions that civil rights advocates reject as inflammatory and misleading.

Similar counter-protests took place in Belfast, Northern Ireland, where demonstrators stood against recent riots. The mobilization reflects growing concern among progressive communities about the rising visibility of far-right organizing and the scapegoating of immigrant and refugee communities for complex social issues.
