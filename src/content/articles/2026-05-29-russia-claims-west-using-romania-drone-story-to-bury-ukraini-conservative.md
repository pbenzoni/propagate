A Russian Foreign Ministry spokesperson told state media this week that Western nations are deliberately amplifying a drone incident in Romania to draw attention away from a Ukrainian strike that killed students in Russian-controlled Starobelsk.

Maria Zakharova said the May 22 attack on Starobelsk Professional College in the Lugansk People's Republic—territory Russia claims as its own—resulted in 21 deaths, mostly young women training to become teachers, with 65 others injured. She characterized the strike as a "bloody, horrific terrorist attack" funded and supported by European Union countries. The incident in Romania involved a drone crashing into an apartment building near the Ukrainian border on Friday, prompting Bucharest to expel a Russian diplomat and close Moscow's consulate in Constanta.

Zakharova argued that allegations of Russian involvement in the Romanian incident lack evidence and serve as a distraction from what she called "Zelensky's crime." She warned that Russia's response to Romania's diplomatic measures "will not be long in coming," framing the consulate closure as part of an "anti-Russian, Russophobic information campaign."

A senior Russian security official added that EU member states have become direct participants in the conflict through military and financial support to Kyiv. He noted that European-supplied drones, weapons, and intelligence are used in daily attacks on Russian territory, resulting in damage to civilian areas and loss of life. The competing narratives underscore the intensifying information war surrounding the conflict, with each side accusing the other of targeting civilians while Western media coverage remains a flashpoint of contention.
