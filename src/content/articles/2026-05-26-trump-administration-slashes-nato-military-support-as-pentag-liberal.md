The Trump administration has informed NATO allies that it will significantly reduce American military assets available to the alliance, according to a report from Der Spiegel. The cuts—which include reducing available fighter jets by one third and eliminating U.S. submarines and drones from NATO commitments entirely—were delivered at a confidential Brussels meeting by Pentagon adviser Alexander Velez-Green.

The announcement reportedly stunned European defense officials, who had anticipated more gradual reductions. Instead, they now face a July deadline at NATO's upcoming summit in Turkey to present plans for compensating for the American withdrawal. The move represents the latest step in what observers describe as Trump's systematic disengagement from the transatlantic alliance that has anchored Western security architecture since World War II.

Pentagon sources justify the reductions as necessary for refocusing on potential conflict with China in the Indo-Pacific region. Secretary of State Marco Rubio told NATO foreign ministers that the U.S. needs to position forces for "a two-front conflict," citing obligations across multiple regions. However, Trump has explicitly politicized troop deployments, recently withdrawing 5,000 service members from Germany while simultaneously sending the same number to Poland—a decision he linked to the "successful election" of a conservative Polish president.

The cuts come as more than 80,000 U.S. troops remain stationed in Europe, a presence maintained continuously since 1945. Critics worry that undermining collective defense commitments emboldens adversaries while weakening the democratic alliance system that has prevented major-power conflict for eight decades.
