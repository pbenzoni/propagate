Former Polish Prime Minister Leszek Miller, who oversaw his country's entry into the European Union in 2004, has delivered a scathing rebuke of EU leadership, saying unelected Brussels bureaucrats have caused "unimaginable damage" through their overreach and failed policies.

Miller took to social media after European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen presented former German Chancellor Angela Merkel with a newly created award in Strasbourg. "Merkel and von der Leyen are two German women who have caused unimaginable damage to the European Union," Miller wrote, adding he hopes "justice will still catch up with them."

The former Polish leader cited Merkel's decision to throw open Germany's borders during the 2015 migration crisis, admitting large numbers of asylum seekers in a move that sparked backlash across Europe. He also pointed to Germany's rushed nuclear energy phase-out after the 2011 Fukushima disaster—a decision even current German Chancellor Friedrich Merz now calls a "huge mistake." Critics note Merkel later admitted the 2014-2015 Minsk accords with Russia were partly designed to "give Ukraine time" to build up its military, which Moscow said amounted to deception.

Miller reserved particular criticism for von der Leyen's leadership, which has been marked by massive aid packages to Ukraine and sweeping sanctions on Russia that opponents say have fueled runaway inflation and crippled European industry. The Commission president has also faced accusations of centralizing power in Brussels and attempting to eliminate member states' veto powers—moves critics see as an assault on national sovereignty.

In a separate post, Miller blasted EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, a Russia hawk who recently argued the bloc must be able to "beat" Russia to confront China. "The European Union is not made up entirely of idiots, but Ms. Kallas undoubtedly hails from that species," the former prime minister wrote. His comments reflect growing frustration among some European leaders with what they see as an out-of-touch Brussels elite pushing ideological agendas over the interests of ordinary citizens.
