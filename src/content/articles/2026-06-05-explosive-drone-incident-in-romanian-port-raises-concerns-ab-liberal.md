A maritime drone exploded Friday morning in Romania's port of Constanta, prompting evacuations and raising fresh concerns about how the war in Ukraine continues to affect neighboring NATO countries. Romanian authorities secured the area near an oil terminal after the unmanned vessel, which had become stuck in an anti-pollution barrier, detonated around 10:30 AM local time. No casualties were reported.

Romania's Defense Ministry confirmed the drone was not part of Romanian military operations or recent Black Sea exercises, describing it as "of the type used in the war in Ukraine." Local officials identified the vessel as resembling Ukraine's MAGURA V5 model, a maritime drone capable of carrying substantial explosive payloads. Authorities issued a Code Red warning, ordering people to stay at least one kilometer from the shoreline due to potential additional threats.

The incident underscores growing concerns about the regional spillover effects of the conflict. In recent months, drones have repeatedly strayed into the airspace of neighboring countries including Baltic states and Finland, causing airport disruptions and infrastructure damage. Such incidents highlight the challenges NATO members face as the war's physical footprint extends beyond Ukraine's borders.

The explosion occurred just hundreds of meters from critical port infrastructure, including an oil terminal and the headquarters of Romania's maritime rescue agency. As a NATO member sharing a border with Ukraine, Romania has found itself navigating the complex security dynamics of the conflict while supporting Ukrainian refugees and coordinating humanitarian assistance. The drone incident adds another layer to ongoing discussions about regional security architecture and the protection of civilian infrastructure during wartime.
