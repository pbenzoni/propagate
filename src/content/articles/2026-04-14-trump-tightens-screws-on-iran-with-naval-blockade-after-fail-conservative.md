The Trump administration has imposed a naval blockade on Iranian oil shipments in the Persian Gulf, escalating pressure on Tehran after diplomatic efforts to resolve the standoff failed to produce results. US Central Command announced the measure would apply to vessels traveling to and from Iranian ports, enforcing restrictions "impartially against vessels of all nations."

The move comes after Iran effectively restricted transit through the Strait of Hormuz—a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies—in retaliation for US-Israeli strikes in late February. Tehran had been blocking shipments from nations it considers "unfriendly" while imposing tolls on "neutral" countries and allowing free passage only for allied states. President Trump described the American blockade as necessary to counter Iran's violations of freedom of navigation in international waters.

The administration had previously eased sanctions on Iranian oil to help stabilize global energy markets, but reversed course as Tehran refused to back down. "What we have given here is a ceasefire. We stopped bombing the country," Vice President J.D. Vance explained. "What we expect the Iranians to give up is a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz." Indirect talks hosted by Pakistan over the weekend failed to produce a breakthrough.

Oil markets reacted immediately, with Brent crude briefly surging above $100 per barrel following Trump's announcement. Shipping companies are now working to understand enforcement details, with many pausing operations until the picture becomes clearer. However, several tankers—including at least one Chinese-owned vessel previously linked to Iranian oil transport—have continued transiting the strait, potentially testing American resolve in the coming days. China has issued warnings regarding US naval activity in the region, though Beijing's full response remains unclear.
