A troubling video has emerged showing the captain of cruise ship MV Hondius telling passengers that a death aboard was from "natural causes" and "not infectious"—just before a deadly hantavirus outbreak was confirmed that has now claimed three lives.

The footage from April 12 shows Captain Jan Dobrogowski assuring the 150 passengers from 23 countries that "the ship is safe," despite a 70-year-old Dutch passenger having died the day before. "Whatever health issues he was struggling with, I'm told by the doctor, were not infectious," the captain stated. Within weeks, his widow and a German woman would also succumb to the rare Andes strain of hantavirus.

Health authorities now believe the elderly Dutch couple contracted the deadly rodent-borne pathogen during a bird-watching excursion at a landfill in Ushuaia, Argentina, before boarding the Dutch-flagged vessel on April 1. The virus, which spreads through contact with infected rodent droppings and can transmit between humans through close contact, has infected at least eight people connected to the cruise.

The ship was initially denied docking at Cape Verde and the Canary Islands as the outbreak unfolded, raising questions about transparency and passenger safety protocols. Health officials are now scrambling to locate approximately 29 passengers who disembarked at earlier ports before the danger was known. The vessel has finally been permitted to dock in Tenerife, where unaffected passengers will be sent home while Spanish nationals face quarantine.
