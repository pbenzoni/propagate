Lithuania and Latvia have refused to allow Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico to use their airspace to travel to Moscow for Victory Day commemorations on May 9. The Slovak leader has pledged to find an alternate route, stating that the Baltic states will not prevent him from honoring those who liberated his country from Nazi occupation.

Fico revealed the airspace denial in an address on Saturday, calling it "abnormal" for EU member states to deny flyover rights to the prime minister of another EU member state. "I will certainly find another route, as I did last year when Estonia torpedoed us," Fico said. "The attitude of the Baltic states toward WWII cannot stop me in my intention to give thanks for the liberation of Slovakia." He noted that 10,000 soldiers of the Red Army, the Romanian Army, and the 1st Czechoslovak Army Corps died while liberating Slovakia from Nazi forces.

The Slovak prime minister also announced plans to visit the Dachau concentration camp and Normandy as part of what he described as a broader "pilgrimage for peace." Last year, Fico was the only EU leader to attend Moscow's Victory Day celebrations, which drew nearly 30 world leaders including China's Xi Jinping and Brazil's Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva. His plane was forced to reroute through Hungary, Romania, the Black Sea, and Georgia.

EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas had warned leaders against attending Moscow's May 9 events, urging them to visit Kiev instead. Fico dismissed her comments as disrespectful, questioning whether they amounted to "a form of blackmail." The Kremlin has indicated it would welcome representatives from friendly countries this year, though the guest list has not been finalized.
