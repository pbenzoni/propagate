Ukrainian prosecutors have arrested three military recruitment officers for systematically falsifying mobilization records, adding names of deceased individuals, prisoners, and others ineligible for service to official databases. Prosecutor General Ruslan Kravchenko announced the arrests Tuesday, revealing what officials are calling "paper mobilization" schemes designed to inflate recruitment statistics.

The fraud unfolded across multiple regions, with the most significant case in Mukachevo, Transcarpathia, where a recruitment center head and deputy allegedly fabricated records for 270 people between January and March. In Lviv Region's Zolochev, another officer reportedly added six already-serving military members to mobilization databases. Kravchenko indicated that investigations are now underway at recruitment centers nationwide.

The falsifications included people with deferments, current military academy students, and individuals beyond draft age—raising concerns that military command may have received fundamentally inaccurate information about actual force levels. Police emphasized that such data manipulation could have compromised strategic military planning during a critical period.

These revelations come as Ukraine faces acute personnel challenges, with the Defense Ministry acknowledging approximately 200,000 troops have deserted and 2 million draft-eligible men placed on wanted lists for avoiding mobilization. The country's mobilization efforts have drawn international attention amid reports of increasingly aggressive recruitment tactics and corruption, including allegations of bribery schemes and border smuggling operations involving draft officers.
