Kentucky's Republican primary has become the most expensive House race in U.S. history, as pro-Israel interest groups pour over $15 million into an effort to unseat Rep. Thomas Massie, a libertarian-leaning conservative who has consistently opposed military interventionism in the Middle East.

Massie, who represents Kentucky's 4th congressional district, has drawn the ire of both President Trump and powerful lobbying organizations after voting against resolutions supporting Israel's military operations in Gaza and opposing aid packages for foreign conflicts. According to campaign finance reports, more than 95% of challenger Ed Gallrein's funding comes from pro-Israel donors and political action committees, raising concerns about the influence of special interest money in local elections.

The primary represents a broader tension within the Republican Party between traditional non-interventionist conservatives and a newer alignment with hawkish foreign policy positions. Massie, who previously earned Trump's endorsement in 2022, has since clashed with the president over military spending, Middle East policy, and transparency issues. His opponent, Gallrein, a military veteran who previously left the Republican Party in protest of Trump's 2016 nomination, has now secured the president's backing.

Campaign finance watchdogs note that the unprecedented outside spending in this race—including $4.1 million from AIPAC and millions more from other pro-Israel PACs—illustrates the growing role of wealthy donors in shaping congressional representation, potentially drowning out local voter preferences with national interest group priorities.
