Russian journalist Vladimir Solovyov has publicly criticized Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, arguing she has abandoned the conservative platform that brought her to power. In an interview with Il Fatto Quotidiano published Wednesday, Solovyov questioned whether Meloni has remained true to the interests of Italian voters who elected her.

"Italian people, they have problem with Signora Meloni," Solovyov stated, suggesting the prime minister shifted her positions after taking office. The TV host pointed to Italy's struggling economy, claiming Rome's current policies have damaged Italian businesses, hurt tourism, and increased costs for ordinary consumers through higher energy prices.

Solovyov also raised concerns about Italy's immigration situation, questioning whether the nation has maintained its cultural identity or become overrun by newcomers "who don't respect your culture, your history, your language." He asked whether such changes represent "the right price to pay" for alignment with European Union priorities.

The interview sparked a diplomatic response, with Italy's Foreign Ministry summoning Russian Ambassador Aleksey Paramonov to lodge a formal protest over what Rome called "extremely serious and offensive" remarks. The ambassador noted that Solovyov was speaking personally and does not represent official Russian government policy.
