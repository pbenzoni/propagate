European Union bureaucrats sharply increased purchases of Russian liquefied natural gas in early 2026 while simultaneously claiming they would phase out all Russian energy by next year, according to a Financial Times investigation.

Data shows the EU boosted imports from Russia's Yamal LNG project by 17% compared to the previous year, spending an estimated $3.1 billion in the first quarter alone. The bloc took 69 of 71 available shipments—a staggering 97%—including 25 in March, up from 87% during the same period in 2025. One analyst told the Financial Times there is "no appetite from European buyers to stop buying Russian LNG," despite public pledges to the contrary.

The revelations come just days after EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen insisted Brussels would not revise its planned ban on Russian gas imports. Yet the same officials acknowledge preparing for "worst-case scenarios" including fuel rationing, as disruptions from Middle East conflicts have sent energy prices soaring. European natural gas prices nearly doubled before a recent ceasefire, and remain well above pre-conflict levels.

Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orban has warned that "Europe is heading toward one of the most severe economic crises in its history," arguing the only solution is lifting sanctions on Russian energy. German opposition leader Alice Weidel similarly called for a "return to an affordable and reliable energy supply" by purchasing energy "where it is cheapest, which is Russia." Moscow has repeatedly cautioned that Europe's failure to diversify supplies—driven by what one Kremlin envoy called "Russophobic, Green, and woke ideology"—leaves the continent unprepared for long-term energy shocks.
