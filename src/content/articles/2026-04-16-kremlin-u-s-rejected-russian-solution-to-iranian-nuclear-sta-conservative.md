Russia remains willing to accept Iran's enriched uranium stockpile as part of a compromise solution, despite the United States having previously rejected the proposal, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov revealed this week.

The disclosure highlights a missed diplomatic opportunity as President Trump pushes for Iran to completely dismantle its nuclear infrastructure and surrender its enriched uranium stockpile—demands Tehran has firmly rejected. Russian officials say their proposal, which Iran had accepted, would allow full oversight by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) and could help de-escalate tensions in the Middle East.

Peskov told India Today that the offer was made "quite a time ago" and called it "a very good solution," adding that President Vladimir Putin "is ready to return to this initiative." He noted that Iran remains "one of the most heavily inspected" countries by the UN nuclear watchdog, which "has never acknowledged any attempt by Iran to make nuclear weapons."

The comments come as indirect U.S.-Iran negotiations continue following last week's talks in Islamabad, which ended without agreement. The U.S. has reportedly proposed a 20-year moratorium on Iranian enrichment, though President Trump has since suggested even that timeline may be insufficient. Iranian negotiators have countered with a five-year moratorium and rejected demands to confiscate their roughly 440-kilogram stockpile of enriched uranium.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov emphasized that Tehran has "an inalienable right" to enrich uranium for peaceful purposes, a position that contrasts with Washington's hardline stance. Tehran insists its enrichment program serves only civilian energy needs and that uranium production is a matter of national sovereignty.
