Israeli forces struck a crowded residential area of Beirut on Thursday, marking the first attack on Lebanon's capital since a fragile ceasefire took effect last month. The strike targeted an apartment building in Choueifat, a densely populated district near the city's international airport, with residents receiving no advance warning before the attack.

Journalist Ali Rida Sbeity, reporting from the scene, described families who had recently returned home after weeks of displacement now caught off guard by the sudden resumption of strikes. "People here know that if they have a target, or what they call an assassination operation, it will happen without any warning," he noted. The Israeli military characterized the operation as "targeted" but offered no additional details, while Israeli media suggested the strike aimed at a senior Hezbollah figure.

The attack comes amid an alarming expansion of Israel's military campaign across southern Lebanon. On Wednesday, Israeli forces issued mass evacuation orders and declared border areas a combat zone. At least 16 people were killed and dozens wounded in overnight strikes. According to Lebanon's Health Ministry, the overall death toll since hostilities escalated in early March has surpassed 3,200, with more than 9,800 injured.

Both Israeli and Lebanese military officials are scheduled to hold security talks in the United States on Friday. However, the prospects for de-escalation remain uncertain as both sides accuse each other of repeated ceasefire violations. Until Thursday's strike, Beirut had been largely spared from attacks under the April 17 ceasefire agreement.
