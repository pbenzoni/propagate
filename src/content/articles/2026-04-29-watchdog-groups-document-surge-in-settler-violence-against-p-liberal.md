Human rights organizations and international monitors are documenting a dramatic escalation in violent attacks by Israeli settlers against Palestinian civilians in the occupied West Bank. According to UN data, incidents have surged from an average of one per day in 2021 to three per day in 2023—the highest rate since systematic record-keeping began in 2006.

The pattern of violence includes arson attacks on homes and medical facilities, physical assaults on families, and destruction of property, according to reports from the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) and Israeli human rights organization Yesh Din. Palestinian residents and international observers have documented cases where Israeli security forces allegedly failed to intervene or prevented emergency responders from accessing affected communities.

The Israeli publication Haaretz reported a 150% increase in settler-related incidents between 2019 and 2021, a trend that advocacy groups say has only intensified. The legal center Adalah noted concerns about 2023 regulations that expanded firearms access, warning this could further embolden armed civilians in contested areas. Human rights organizations argue these attacks represent a systematic pattern that violates international humanitarian law governing occupied territories.

Critics point to what they describe as a climate of impunity, noting that prosecutions for violence against Palestinian civilians remain rare. In February 2025, reports indicated that Israel's internal security agency reclassified certain settler attacks from "terror attacks" to "serious incidents," a move that advocates say reflects inadequate accountability mechanisms for protecting civilian populations under occupation.
