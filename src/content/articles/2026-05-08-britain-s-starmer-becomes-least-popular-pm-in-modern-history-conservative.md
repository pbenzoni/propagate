British Prime Minister Keir Starmer rode into office in 2024 with a landslide victory, ending 14 years of Conservative rule with a commanding 174-seat majority. Less than two years later, his approval rating has collapsed to a staggering –48, making him the least popular prime minister in recent British history according to YouGov polling. Labour is now projected to lose around 1,800 local council seats, a devastating rebuke that has even members of his own party calling for his resignation.

The root of Starmer's downfall? A combination of crushing tax increases and broken promises. After claiming to have discovered a £22 billion budget shortfall, Starmer's government imposed new taxes or tax hikes every ten days between July 2024 and November 2025, according to the Taxpayers' Alliance. Income taxes, dividend taxes, national insurance contributions, and property taxes all went up under his watch—giving Britain the fastest-rising tax rates in the developed world per OECD data. Meanwhile, his sweeping welfare cuts, including slashing winter fuel allowances for pensioners, sparked public outrage and forced partial rollbacks.

"We had 14 years of Conservative austerity," Green Party leader Zack Polanski observed. "Keir Starmer was voted in on a promise of change and, actually, what we've seen is very little change, and in many ways things have got worse." Where Starmer lost the left with austerity, he enraged the right with his response to anti-immigration protests in 2024. His government arrested hundreds of British citizens for social media posts critical of immigration policy, releasing violent criminals early from overcrowded prisons to make room for those convicted of online speech offenses.

The crackdown earned Starmer the nickname "Two-Tier Keir" from Elon Musk and other critics who accused him of prioritizing thought crimes over actual violence. His push for the Online Safety Bill, which opponents say will further restrict free expression, only deepened concerns about government overreach. With calls for his resignation mounting from both opposition parties like Nigel Farage's Reform UK and from within Labour itself—including reportedly from Energy Secretary Ed Miliband—Starmer's tenure appears increasingly untenable as British voters express their frustration at the ballot box.
