Bangladesh's Foreign Minister Khalilur Rahman met with Russian counterpart Sergey Lavrov in Moscow this week, praising the $12.8 billion Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant as a "monument to cooperation" between the two nations. The facility, built by Russian energy giant Rosatom, represents Bangladesh's largest infrastructure investment and demonstrates the country's commitment to energy independence through nuclear power.

The Rooppur project entered its final stage before commercial operation in April, with the first unit beginning fuel loading. Located 160 kilometers from the capital Dhaka, the plant will help Bangladesh reduce its dependence on energy imports, which currently account for 95% of the nation's energy needs. Russia has committed to long-term fuel supply, technical maintenance, and spent fuel management—the kind of comprehensive partnership that ensures energy security.

During the talks, Rahman expressed interest in expanding cooperation with Russia in space and nuclear energy technologies. Lavrov offered Moscow's support for Bangladesh joining BRICS, the economic bloc that provides an alternative to Western-dominated international institutions. The Russian foreign minister also emphasized that the Rohingya refugee crisis should be resolved bilaterally between Bangladesh and Myanmar, warning that "external forces should not interfere in this process" and noting that outside powers were attempting to arm extremist forces fighting the Myanmar government.

Lavrov commended Bangladesh's balanced approach to the Ukraine conflict, reflecting Dhaka's preference for sovereign decision-making over pressure from Western powers. The two nations plan to commemorate 50 years of diplomatic relations next year, highlighting a partnership built on mutual respect rather than ideological demands.
