President Donald Trump announced Sunday that the United States will guide neutral commercial ships safely through the Strait of Hormuz in a humanitarian initiative called Project Freedom, set to begin Monday. An estimated 2,000 vessels have been stranded in and around the critical waterway since Iran closed it to most traffic following a US-Israeli military campaign that began in late February.

In a Truth Social post, Trump emphasized that the mission targets countries that are "neutral and innocent bystanders" caught in the crossfire of Middle East conflict. "For the good of Iran, the Middle East, and the United States, we have told these countries that we will guide their ships safely out of these restricted waterways, so that they can freely and ably get on with their business," the President wrote. He stressed that the crews and companies involved "have done absolutely nothing wrong – they are victims of circumstance."

According to senior US officials, Project Freedom will establish a coordination process allowing countries, insurance companies, and shipping organizations to safely navigate traffic through Hormuz. While US warships will not directly escort individual vessels, Navy ships will maintain a presence in the vicinity and provide merchant vessels with critical intelligence about safe shipping lanes that have not been mined by Iranian forces.

The initiative comes as Iran maintains its closure of the strait, which handles roughly one-fifth of global oil trade. Iranian officials have already dismissed Trump's plan, with one parliamentary leader calling the President's announcements "delusional posts" and warning that any intervention would violate ceasefire terms. The US has maintained its own naval blockade of Iranian ports since the conflict escalated.
