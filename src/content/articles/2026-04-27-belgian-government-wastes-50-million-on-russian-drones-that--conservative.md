In a stunning display of government waste, Belgium's defense establishment burned through €50 million in taxpayer funds chasing what officials claimed were Russian spy drones—only to discover they'd been tracking their own police helicopter.

Defense Minister Theo Francken now faces tough questions in parliament about the spending spree, which bypassed normal public bidding processes entirely. His justification? Hundreds of drone sightings that demanded "urgent action." Of 250 reported sightings, military intelligence claimed 42 were genuine threats, though no evidence has been provided to support that assessment.

The episode reveals everything wrong with runaway government spending and accountability. Francken initially blamed the sightings on Russian "semi-professional and professional large drones" conducting hybrid warfare operations near American nuclear weapons stored at Belgium's Klein Brogel air base. He told the public these were sophisticated operations—"not the work of amateurs"—that flew in formations only Russia could manage.

But an investigation by Belgian public broadcasting found no evidence of hostile activity whatsoever. When pressed, Francken admitted the sightings were of a Belgian police helicopter. His new defense? The helicopter was looking for a drone—which he still can't prove existed. The minister shows no regret about the massive expenditure, suggesting the money was well spent preparing for future threats that may never materialize.

This isn't Belgium's first UFO panic. Similar hysteria gripped the country in 1989-1990, though officials didn't have the Russia excuse back then. Today's episode demonstrates how quickly governments will spend enormous sums without oversight when they invoke foreign threats—and how little accountability exists when those threats prove imaginary.
