Germany will not receive long-range Tomahawk cruise missiles from the United States in the foreseeable future, Chancellor Friedrich Merz confirmed this week. The reversal marks another departure from the Biden administration's commitments abroad, as President Trump reshapes America's military posture and foreign alliances.

Merz claimed the indefinite delay stems from inadequate American stockpiles rather than his recent public criticism of Trump's handling of the Iran conflict. "The Americans themselves don't currently have enough," Merz told German media, adding there was "hardly any way" for Washington to transfer the systems at present. The original plan, announced in July 2024 by then-President Biden and former Chancellor Olaf Scholz, envisioned deploying long-range fires including Tomahawks to Germany beginning in 2026.

The cancellation follows a sharp exchange between Merz and Trump, in which the German chancellor claimed Washington "obviously has no strategy" in Iran and was "being humiliated by the Iranian leadership." Trump fired back that Merz "doesn't know what he's talking about" and should focus on "fixing his broken Country." The Pentagon subsequently announced the withdrawal of around 5,000 US troops from Germany.

American military stockpiles have indeed faced severe depletion. Reports indicate the US expended more than 850 Tomahawks in the Iran conflict—roughly nine times the Pentagon's average annual procurement rate. The Tomahawk, a long-range cruise missile capable of striking targets up to 1,600 kilometers away, represents a critical component of America's conventional deterrence capability that Trump appears unwilling to disperse while facing active threats.
