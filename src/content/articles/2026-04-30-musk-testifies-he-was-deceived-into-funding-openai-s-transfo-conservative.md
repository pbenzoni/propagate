Elon Musk took the stand Wednesday in federal court, testifying that OpenAI CEO Sam Altman betrayed the company's founding mission by converting what was supposed to be a charitable nonprofit into a for-profit behemoth now valued at $85 billion.

"I was a fool who provided them free funding to create a startup," Musk told the Oakland court, revealing he contributed $38 million between December 2015 and May 2017 based on assurances from Altman that OpenAI would remain a nonprofit venture. The SpaceX and Tesla chief described how Altman and other OpenAI leadership secured his charitable donations under false pretenses, then restructured the organization to enrich themselves while maintaining a nonprofit "halo effect."

Musk's lawsuit seeks to reverse OpenAI's transformation, remove Altman and co-founder Greg Brockman from leadership, and secure damages for the nonprofit arm. "They can't have it both ways," Musk testified. "They can't have a nonprofit and free funding and the positive halo effect of being a nonprofit charity and also enrich themselves greatly." He characterized his view of OpenAI's leadership trajectory as moving from "enthusiastic support" to discovering "they're looting a nonprofit."

OpenAI's legal team has pushed back, claiming no permanent commitment to nonprofit status was ever made and suggesting Musk's litigation aims to hamper OpenAI's success while boosting his competing AI venture, xAI, which he launched in 2023. During cross-examination, Musk sparred with OpenAI attorney William Savitt, accusing him of asking questions "designed to trick me essentially" when pressed about emails discussing potential for-profit structures. The trial, which began Monday, is expected to continue for four weeks.
