Taiwan conducted a significant show of defensive strength this week, firing approximately 36 U.S.-supplied missiles into waters near mainland China during unprecedented live-fire exercises on the island's western coast. The drills featured High Mobility Artillery Rocket Systems (HIMARS)—the same American-made weapons that have proven effective in Ukraine—demonstrating the democratic island's commitment to deterring aggression from the Chinese Communist Party.

The Wednesday exercise simulated defense against a potential Chinese invasion, showcasing HIMARS' tactical "shoot-and-scoot" capability that allows forces to strike and quickly relocate before enemy counter-attacks. With a range of up to 300 kilometers, these systems could reach targets in China's Fujian Province across the Taiwan Strait. Taiwan has ordered 29 HIMARS launchers from the United States and continues expanding its anti-ship and air defense capabilities.

Beijing predictably condemned the drills and broader U.S. arms sales to Taiwan, claiming they violate the One-China policy and interfere in what the Communist regime considers its "internal affairs." During recent meetings with President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping called Taiwan the most critical issue in U.S.-China relations, warning that mishandling could create "a very dangerous situation." The United States maintains close unofficial ties with Taiwan and serves as its primary arms supplier, supporting the island's right to self-defense without officially recognizing it as independent.

The timing underscores growing tensions as Xi's government continues refusing to rule out military force against the self-governing democracy of 23 million people, despite claims of seeking "peaceful reunification."
