A U.S. Special Forces Master Sergeant has pleaded not guilty to charges that he used classified information about a military operation targeting Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro to earn more than $400,000 on prediction markets. The case marks the first federal prosecution linking insider trading to these unregulated betting platforms, raising serious questions about oversight and the commodification of national security.

Master Sergeant Gannon Ken Van Dyke allegedly placed approximately $33,000 in bets on Polymarket—a platform where users wager on real-world events ranging from elections to armed conflicts—using advance knowledge of a January raid aimed at Maduro. He has been released on bond with travel restrictions while awaiting trial on charges including fraud and unlawful use of confidential government information.

The case has drawn attention to the broader regulatory gaps surrounding prediction markets, which critics argue transform geopolitical crises and military actions into profit opportunities with minimal accountability. President Trump has criticized insider trading broadly, calling the proliferation of such behavior evidence that "the whole world… has become somewhat of a casino." However, his son Donald Trump Jr. holds financial stakes in Polymarket, illustrating the tangled interests at play.

Regulators and ethics advocates are increasingly concerned that these platforms create perverse incentives, particularly when individuals with access to classified or nonpublic information can exploit geopolitical instability for personal gain. As prediction markets grow in popularity, lawmakers face mounting pressure to establish clear rules preventing such abuses.
