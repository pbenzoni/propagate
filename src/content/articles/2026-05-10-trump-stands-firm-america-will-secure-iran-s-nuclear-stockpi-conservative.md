President Donald Trump reaffirmed his commitment to securing Iran's enriched uranium stockpile during a Sunday interview, emphasizing American resolve in ongoing negotiations with the Islamic Republic.

"Well, we'll get that at some point, whatever we want," Trump told journalist Sharyl Attkisson when asked about the status of securing Iran's fissile material. The President highlighted America's surveillance capabilities, crediting his own creation of the Space Force for monitoring the situation. "If anybody got near the place, we will know about it. And we'll blow them up," he stated.

The United States launched operations against Iranian targets in late February alongside Israel, aimed at preventing Tehran's potential development of nuclear weapons. Washington's latest proposal demands that Iran commit to never developing such weapons, cease all enrichment activities, and surrender its stockpile of highly enriched uranium. According to International Atomic Energy Agency estimates, Iran currently possesses more than 400 kg of uranium enriched to 60% purity—though weapons-grade levels typically require 90% enrichment or higher.

Russian President Vladimir Putin stated Saturday that Moscow has offered to assist in removing the nuclear material, citing Russia's previous involvement in 2015. However, Iran has consistently rejected demands to surrender its uranium stockpile or abandon its civilian nuclear program. Tehran maintains it has no plans to build nuclear weapons and is demanding guarantees of non-aggression from Washington along with removal of U.S. forces from the region.
