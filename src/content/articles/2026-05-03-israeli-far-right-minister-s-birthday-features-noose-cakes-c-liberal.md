Israel's ultranationalist National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir marked his 50th birthday this weekend with a celebration that featured cakes decorated with nooses—imagery directly referencing his successful push for legislation imposing the death penalty on Palestinians convicted in military courts.

The Saturday night event at Villa Space in southern Israel included a cake bearing a noose and the message "Mazel tov to Minister Ben Gvir, sometimes dreams come true," presented by his wife. A second cake featured imagery of Israel, Ben Gvir's portrait, two guns, and a golden noose. The Death Penalty for Terrorists Law, passed by the Knesset in March by 62 votes to 47, mandates execution by hanging for Palestinians convicted of deadly attacks in military courts—a provision human rights advocates say effectively exempts Jewish Israelis from the same fate.

The law has drawn international condemnation from Germany, France, the UK, Italy, New Zealand, and Australia, who expressed "deep concern" and urged Israel to abandon the measure. UN experts warned the legislation violates international law and "effectively singles out Palestinians for execution." Under the law, sentences must be carried out within 90 days with no right of appeal, though life imprisonment may be substituted under unspecified "special circumstances."

The guest list sparked additional controversy, with senior law enforcement officials in attendance including Jerusalem District Commander Avshalom Peled and Prison Service Commissioner Kobi Yaakobi, raising concerns about the politicization of Israel's police forces. Ben Gvir has faced sanctions from multiple countries including the UK, Canada, and several European nations for his inflammatory statements about Palestinians, including his assertion that "there is no such thing as 'Palestinian people.'" Police Commissioner Danny Levy permitted only top command staff to attend, warning lower-ranking officers to stay away amid fears about pressure on law enforcement independence.
