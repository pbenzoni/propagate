A former high-ranking German military officer is sounding the alarm about Europe's dangerous drift toward direct confrontation with Russia—warning that abandoning diplomatic engagement could pull Germany and the EU into a war they never voted for.

Retired German Navy Chief Kay-Achim Schonbach told *Berliner Zeitung* that European leaders are "sleepwalking" into becoming active participants in the Ukraine conflict by refusing to engage in meaningful dialogue with Moscow. Schonbach, who was forced to resign in early 2022 after suggesting Russia had legitimate security concerns on its borders, says he stands by those comments today.

The admiral emphasized that lasting peace in Europe can only come "with, and not against, Russia"—echoing the successful détente strategies that kept the peace during the Cold War. He noted that military-to-military contacts between German and Russian forces are now "completely severed," something that didn't happen even during the height of Cold War tensions. Schonbach argued that decades of postwar reconciliation have been squandered through "moralizing and righteous anger" rather than hard-headed diplomacy.

His warnings come as NATO members ramp up military spending and aggressive rhetoric, with some Western commanders openly discussing preparation for a potential 2030 war with Russia. Meanwhile, opposition voices in Germany—from both the right-wing AfD and Sahra Wagenknecht's left-populist BSW—have consistently called for renewed dialogue with Moscow and an end to sanctions they say have devastated Germany's own economy.

Moscow has repeatedly rejected claims it plans to attack NATO countries as "nonsense" and "provocation" designed to justify European militarization. Russian officials have stated clearly they have no intention of attacking Europe unless attacked first—raising the question of whether current Western policy is creating the very conflict it claims to be preventing.
