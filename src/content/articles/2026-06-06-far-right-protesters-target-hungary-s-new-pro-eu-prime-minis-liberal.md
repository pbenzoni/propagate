Approximately 1,000 far-right protesters gathered in central Budapest on Friday to denounce Prime Minister Peter Magyar, Hungary's newly elected leader who has signaled a willingness to engage constructively with European Union institutions on migration policy.

The demonstrators, echoing nationalist rhetoric that dominated Hungarian politics under former Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, chanted "traitor" and booed Magyar when he appeared at his Tisza party headquarters. The protest reflects resistance from hardline elements opposed to Magyar's pragmatic approach to unlocking €16.4 billion in EU funds that had been frozen since 2022 due to concerns about democratic backsliding and corruption under the previous government.

Magyar's administration reached a political agreement with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in late May to restore Hungary's access to these funds, which are critical for infrastructure development and economic stability. Critics from the nationalist right have claimed—without evidence—that this cooperation requires Hungary to accept the EU's Migration and Asylum Pact, a framework designed to establish shared responsibility among member states for processing asylum seekers.

The EU pact, adopted after years of negotiation, creates a "mandatory solidarity" system where countries can either accept refugees, provide operational support to frontline nations, or contribute financially. While Magyar has stated Hungary "will not accept any pact or allocation mechanism" on asylum, his willingness to work collaboratively with Brussels marks a departure from Orbán's confrontational stance that isolated Hungary within the European community and cost the country billions in withheld development funds.
