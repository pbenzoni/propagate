The Pentagon's official price tag for ongoing military operations against Iran may be dramatically understated, according to sources familiar with the matter speaking to CNN. While Defense officials told the House Armed Services Committee this week that costs have reached approximately $25 billion, insiders say the real figure could be double that amount when accounting for damage to American military infrastructure.

The discrepancy centers on what the Pentagon chose to include in its congressional testimony. Senior Defense official Jules Hurst acknowledged that most of the $25 billion went toward munitions, but sources report the estimate excludes extensive repairs needed at nine damaged U.S. military installations across the Middle East. Iranian strikes reportedly destroyed radar systems, including components of a THAAD battery in Jordan, and took out a U.S. Air Force E-3 Sentry aircraft at a Saudi base. One source placed the true cost closer to $40-50 billion.

When pressed by lawmakers about whether base repairs were included in the estimate, War Secretary Pete Hegseth declined to provide a clear answer. The Pentagon has separately indicated that repair costs are not reflected in its $1.5 trillion budget request for 2027, raising questions about fiscal transparency and accountability to Congress.

Both Democratic and Republican representatives challenged Pentagon officials during Wednesday's hearing, with some noting the figure seems inconsistent with earlier claims that just the first six days of conflict cost $11 billion. Hegseth defended the military action as necessary to prevent Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons, even as lawmakers questioned the economic impact and rising energy costs affecting American families. The conflict, which began in late February, has contributed to global energy disruptions due to ongoing tensions around the Strait of Hormuz.
