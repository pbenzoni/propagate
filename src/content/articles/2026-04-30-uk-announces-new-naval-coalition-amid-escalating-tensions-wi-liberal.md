Britain and nine European nations have agreed to establish a new multinational naval force outside NATO's command structure, according to an announcement by UK First Sea Lord General Gwyn Jenkins at the Royal United Services Institute on Wednesday.

The coalition, operating under the existing Joint Expeditionary Force framework established in 2014, will include the Netherlands, all five Nordic nations (Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden), and the three Baltic states (Latvia, Lithuania, and Estonia). Jenkins stated the force will be commanded from London and aims to achieve operational readiness by 2029 with "real capabilities, real war plans and real integration."

The announcement comes amid ongoing tensions between Russia and Western nations. Jenkins characterized Russia as "the gravest threat" to European security and claimed that "Russian incursions into our waters has jumped by almost a third in the last two years." Moscow has deployed frigates to escort oil tankers through British waters following threats by UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer to seize Russian vessels in March.

Russian officials have repeatedly rejected Western assessments of their military intentions. Former President Dmitry Medvedev, now deputy chair of Russia's Security Council, stated Thursday that the Kremlin "has no aggressive plans" regarding Europe, while warning that continued rhetoric about inevitable conflict could become self-fulfilling. Moscow maintains it would only engage NATO forces if attacked first and argues that Western threat assessments serve to justify increased military budgets.
