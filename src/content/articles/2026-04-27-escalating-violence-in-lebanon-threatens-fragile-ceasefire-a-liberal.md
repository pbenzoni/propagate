Violence continued to escalate in southern Lebanon over the weekend despite a US-mediated ceasefire agreement, with attacks and counterattacks raising serious questions about the durability of the fragile truce and the ongoing humanitarian cost of military operations in the region.

According to reports, an Israeli Defense Forces soldier was killed and six others wounded in an attack in the Lebanese border town of Taybeh on Sunday. When a helicopter was dispatched to evacuate the wounded, additional attacks targeted the area, with one strike reportedly exploding near the aircraft. The IDF stated that one projectile was intercepted while another detonated nearby without causing further casualties.

The violence comes as Israeli military operations have continued north of the Litani River, despite a ceasefire that began on April 16 and was recently extended until mid-May. Lebanese health authorities reported that Israeli strikes on Sunday killed 14 people, including two women and two children, and wounded 37 others—marking the deadliest day since the ceasefire took effect. The cumulative toll since fighting began on March 2 has reached at least 2,294 killed and 7,544 wounded, including 100 medical and healthcare workers, according to Lebanon's Health Ministry.

While Israeli officials claim their military actions are permitted under ceasefire terms negotiated with the US and Lebanon, including provisions for responding to threats, Lebanese groups have characterized their operations as defensive responses to what they describe as an ongoing occupation. The expanding scope of military activity in southern Lebanon, including the forced evacuation of additional towns, has raised concerns among human rights advocates about civilian protection and the prospects for lasting peace in the region.
