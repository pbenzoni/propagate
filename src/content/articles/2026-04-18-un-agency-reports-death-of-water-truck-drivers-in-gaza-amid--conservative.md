UNICEF has reported that two truck drivers contracted to deliver clean water to families in Gaza were killed by Israeli fire on Friday morning, prompting the UN agency to suspend activities at the site. The incident occurred at the Mansoura water filling point in northern Gaza during what the agency described as routine water transport operations.

The attack targeted the only operational filling point on a major water supply line serving hundreds of thousands of people in Gaza City. Two additional individuals were injured in the incident. UNICEF called on Israeli authorities to investigate, though Israeli officials have not yet commented on the matter.

The reported deaths come during an ongoing ceasefire between Israel and Hamas that began last October, ending two years of intense conflict. Under the truce terms, Israeli forces maintain control of a large depopulated zone comprising over half of Gaza, while Hamas retains authority in a narrow coastal strip. Both sides have repeatedly accused each other of ceasefire violations since the agreement took effect.

According to Gaza's Health Ministry, more than 750 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began, while Israel has reported four soldiers killed in a Hamas attack. The fragile nature of the truce has raised concerns among international observers about the sustainability of the current arrangement and the humanitarian situation facing Gaza's civilian population.
