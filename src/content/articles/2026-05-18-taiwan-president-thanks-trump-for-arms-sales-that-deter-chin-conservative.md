Taiwanese President Lai Ching-te has publicly defended his government's purchases of American military hardware, calling the arms sales "the most important deterrent" against potential conflict with Communist China. The statement comes after President Trump characterized weapons deliveries to the democratic island as leverage in negotiations with Beijing.

"We thank President Trump for his continued support for peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait since his first term, including the continuous increase in the scale and amount of arms sales to Taiwan," Lai wrote in a social media statement. He emphasized that Taiwan "will not provoke" conflict but would also refuse to "give up national sovereignty and dignity."

The backdrop involves Trump's strategic approach to China policy. During his recent visit to Beijing, Chinese President Xi Jinping pressed the Taiwan issue as central to US-China relations, with Communist authorities repeatedly condemning American arms sales as interference. Trump approved a record $11 billion weapons package for Taiwan in December, including missiles, drones, and artillery systems. However, he recently indicated he was holding a proposed $14 billion package "in abeyance," telling Fox News it represents "a very good negotiating chip."

China claims Taiwan as part of its territory—a position most nations accept under the "One-China policy." While the US does not officially recognize Taiwan as independent, it maintains informal ties with the democratic government in Taipei. Xi has stated Beijing prefers peaceful reunification but refuses to rule out force. Taiwan's 23 million people live under a separate democratic system, raising questions about sovereignty and self-determination that resonate with American principles of liberty.
