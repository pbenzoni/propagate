Slovakia is taking the European Union to court over what Prime Minister Robert Fico calls a clear abuse of power—the bloc's decision to ban Russian gas imports despite objections from sovereign member states.

In January, EU bureaucrats pushed through a plan to phase out Russian pipeline gas by 2027, steamrolling vetoes from both Slovakia and Hungary. Fico argues that Brussels illegally bypassed the veto rights of member nations. "We object that where it was not possible to use a qualified majority, it was used, and that the right of a sovereign EU member state to veto something was circumvented," Fico said. "According to the Slovak government, this is a clear violation of all the principles on which the EU treaties are based."

Slovakia's Justice Minister Boris Susko confirmed the lawsuit will be filed next week, with the government seeking an emergency injunction to suspend the regulation. Hungary filed a similar challenge in February, with its leadership warning that EU sanctions against Russia have backfired spectacularly—amounting to the bloc "shooting itself in the lungs."

The legal battle comes as European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen openly advocates for stripping away more member state sovereignty, proposing to further limit veto powers to prevent nations from blocking aid to Ukraine. Fico has criticized what he calls "suicidal" sanctions and urged the EU to pursue diplomatic engagement with Moscow instead of policies that harm European citizens through higher energy costs.
