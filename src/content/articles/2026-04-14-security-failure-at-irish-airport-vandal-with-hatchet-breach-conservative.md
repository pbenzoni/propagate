A man in his 40s was arrested Saturday after breaking through security at Shannon Airport in Ireland and attacking a U.S. Air Force C-130 Hercules transport aircraft with what witnesses described as a hatchet or hammer. The breach forced a temporary shutdown of airport operations and has renewed concerns about the security of American military equipment at the facility.

Footage circulating online shows the intruder climbing onto the parked Hercules and striking the aircraft repeatedly. Irish police confirmed the arrest under criminal damage statutes, with the response involving airport police, fire and rescue services, and Irish defense forces. Two departing flights were delayed and an incoming aircraft was forced into a holding pattern before normal operations resumed.

The incident marks the latest in a troubling pattern of security breaches targeting U.S. military assets at Shannon Airport, which serves as a refueling and transit point for American forces. Since 2003, multiple incidents have occurred, including attacks with axes and spray paint on U.S. military and Navy aircraft. Just months ago in November 2025, activists drove a van into a restricted area and vandalized a U.S. Navy Reserve Boeing 737.

Authorities have not yet disclosed the suspect's motive or whether the attack was connected to recent U.S. military operations.
