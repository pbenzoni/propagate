A devastating conflict with Iran has severely damaged American credibility and left President Trump politically weakened at home, according to Trita Parsi, co-founder of the Quincy Institute for Responsible Statecraft. The expert argues that the military campaign, which he says was driven by pressure from Israel rather than genuine US security interests, has proven to be a strategic miscalculation with far-reaching consequences.

The conflict, which began with a US-Israeli bombing campaign on February 28 ostensibly aimed at Iran's nuclear program, has extended far beyond Trump's initial projection of "two to three weeks." The strikes have resulted in significant civilian casualties—more than 1,300 according to reports—as well as the deaths of senior Iranian officials including Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. Iran has responded with strikes on US military installations in Gulf states, exercising what it characterizes as legitimate self-defense.

The escalation has triggered a humanitarian and economic crisis, with the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz—a vital maritime passage handling approximately 20% of global oil supplies—creating an energy shortage with worldwide repercussions. Parsi told RT that Trump's focus on "the optics of winning rather than actually making sure the deal [with Iran] is secured" has backfired dramatically. He cautioned that coercive tactics like the naval blockade "will only backfire and is really jeopardizing the diplomacy at this stage."

"This has been a disaster for the United States," Parsi stated, noting that the administration has "lost a tremendous amount of credibility." He observed that even segments of Trump's political base have grown disillusioned as the US has become entangled in another military intervention that appears to serve Israeli strategic goals more than American ones. US military installations in the Gulf region have transformed from deterrents into "a source of insecurity" for host nations, according to the analyst.

Parsi emphasized the divergent objectives between Washington and Tel Aviv, suggesting Israel was content to see regional instability and American setbacks as long as Iran faced military pressure. Had Trump heeded advisers urging restraint, Parsi concluded, "he would probably not have gone down this path."
