The European Union has reached an agreement on new rules designed to accelerate the deportation of illegal migrants whose asylum claims have been rejected, marking a significant shift after years of lax enforcement that allowed millions to enter and remain in Europe illegally.

Under the new regulations agreed upon Monday, EU countries would gain authority to transfer rejected asylum seekers to third countries when they cannot be returned to their countries of origin. The rules also introduce stronger enforcement measures for dealing with illegal migrants, particularly those deemed security risks, including extended detention periods from six months to two and a half years, home searches, welfare cuts, and document confiscation. Entry bans would increase from five to ten years in most cases, with lifetime bans possible for serious offenders.

"For years, Europe sent the worst possible message: even if you had no right to stay, chances were high that nothing would happen. That era is ending," French MEP Francois-Xavier Bellamy told Politico. "If you have no right to stay in Europe, you will have to leave."

The move comes as Europe grapples with the consequences of a decade-long migration crisis. The EU migrant population reached a record 64.2 million in 2025, including approximately 46.7 million people born outside the bloc, according to recent data. Multiple EU nations, including Austria, Denmark, the Netherlands, and Greece, have already moved to tighten asylum rules and have advocated for establishing return centers outside EU territory.

While left-wing lawmakers and activist groups have opposed the measures, EU Migration Commissioner Magnus Brunner welcomed the deal, stating the bloc will now have "more control over who can come to the EU, who can stay and who needs to leave." The agreement still requires formal approval by EU governments and the European Parliament before taking effect.
