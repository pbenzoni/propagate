A significant fire erupted at RAF Fairford in western England early Sunday morning, just hours after anti-war protesters gathered outside the Royal Air Force base. The facility serves as a crucial hub for US Air Force operations in Europe, currently housing B-52 and B-1 bombers.

According to Gloucestershire Fire and Rescue Service, crews responded to the scene shortly after midnight Sunday. The blaze involved a single-story industrial storage building and was brought under control with no reported casualties. Video footage circulating online showed substantial flames and a large plume of smoke rising into the night sky from what witnesses identified as a commissary building.

The incident occurred approximately one day after hundreds of demonstrators rallied outside the base on Saturday, calling for the UK to close military facilities being used for American operations overseas. British authorities indicated preliminary findings suggest the fire was accidental, though investigations continue.

The timing has drawn attention given recent security concerns at military installations. Last week in neighboring Ireland, a man was arrested after breaking into Shannon Airport and attacking a US Air Force C-130 Hercules transport aircraft with a hatchet. He faces criminal damage charges, though authorities have not yet disclosed a motive for that separate incident.
