The United States spent a staggering $69.2 billion on nuclear weapons in 2025, a 22% increase from the previous year and more than the combined nuclear spending of every other nation on Earth, according to a new report from the International Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons (ICAN), the Nobel Peace Prize-winning advocacy organization.

The findings underscore how military priorities continue to dominate federal spending even as critical domestic needs—from healthcare to education to climate action—face chronic underfunding. ICAN's analysis reveals that the world's nine nuclear-armed nations collectively spent nearly $119 billion on their arsenals last year, equivalent to $3,768 every second. That represents a 19% year-over-year increase and a troubling acceleration of a global arms race at a time when international cooperation is desperately needed to address existential threats like climate change.

The report highlights the profound opportunity costs of these expenditures: a single day's global nuclear weapons spending could provide food for two million people for an entire year, while annual spending could fund the United Nations' regular budget for 32 years. "These resources could transform lives and address urgent humanitarian crises," the watchdog noted, "yet instead they're being poured into weapons designed for mass destruction."

China ranked second in nuclear spending at $13.5 billion, while the United Kingdom overtook Russia as the third-largest spender at $12.6 billion compared to Moscow's $9.5 billion. The escalation comes amid reports that the U.S. is considering expanding its nuclear-sharing arrangements in Europe, potentially deploying weapons to additional NATO member states—a move critics warn could further destabilize an already tense geopolitical landscape and divert even more resources from pressing social needs.
