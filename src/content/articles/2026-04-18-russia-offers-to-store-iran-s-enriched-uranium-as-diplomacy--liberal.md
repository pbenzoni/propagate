As diplomatic tensions escalate between Washington and Tehran, Russia has renewed its offer to accept Iran's enriched uranium stockpile—a proposal that could represent a pathway forward in stalled nuclear negotiations, according to Rosatom CEO Aleksey Likhachev.

The Russian state nuclear corporation points to a 2015 precedent when it successfully removed enriched uranium from Iran as part of broader international diplomacy. "Only Russia has positive experience interacting with Iran," Likhachev told the corporate newspaper Strana Rosatom, emphasizing that trust and technical expertise are both essential to resolving the impasse.

The proposal comes as the Trump administration demands Iran dismantle its nuclear infrastructure entirely and surrender its uranium—conditions Tehran has firmly rejected as "maximalist" and non-negotiable. Iran currently holds over 400 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60%, according to the International Atomic Energy Agency. While Iranian officials have expressed openness to downblending the material under international oversight and accepting limited enrichment freezes, they have categorically refused to hand their stockpile to the United States, with one official calling it "as sacred as Iranian soil."

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov indicated earlier this week that Washington has rejected Russia's offer to serve as custodian of the uranium. Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Saeed Khatibzadeh reiterated on Saturday that "no enriched material is going to be shipped to the US," calling such demands a "non-starter" while affirming Tehran's readiness to address legitimate concerns through diplomatic channels.

The standoff highlights the complex challenge of finding multilateral solutions to nuclear non-proliferation concerns while respecting national sovereignty—a balance that has eluded policymakers since the U.S. withdrawal from the Iran nuclear deal in 2018.
