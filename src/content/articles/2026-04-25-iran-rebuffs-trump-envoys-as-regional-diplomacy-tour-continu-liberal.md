Iran has firmly rejected reports that its foreign minister would meet with Trump administration envoys during a regional diplomatic tour, pushing back against what Tehran characterizes as premature claims of renewed negotiations. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi is visiting Pakistan, Oman, and Russia to coordinate with regional partners on bilateral issues and consult on what Iranian officials describe as escalating tensions with the United States and Israel.

Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei clarified on social media that "no meeting is planned to take place between Iran and the US" during the visit, though some reports suggest indirect discussions through Pakistani mediation remain a possibility. The White House had announced that envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner would travel to Islamabad for talks, with Vice President J.D. Vance on standby if negotiations advance.

The diplomatic maneuvering comes as Washington has extended a ceasefire with Iran while maintaining what Tehran calls an illegal blockade of Iranian ports—a measure that has contributed to global oil prices surging above $100 per barrel. Iranian officials have argued that meaningful dialogue cannot proceed while economic sanctions and military pressure continue, with Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf accusing the administration of attempting to turn negotiations into "a table of surrender."

Trump has escalated rhetoric around the standoff, reportedly ordering the Navy to use lethal force against Iranian vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global shipping chokepoint. Iran's ambassador to Moscow told Russian media that Tehran remains open to diplomacy but insists Washington must abandon coercive tactics. Iranian officials frame their position as defensive, arguing that the blockade violates the terms of the ceasefire and demonstrates the administration's lack of genuine commitment to de-escalation.
