A Turkish fishing boat sank in the Black Sea near Crimea after coming under attack Friday, killing one crew member and injuring four others, according to Turkey's Coast Guard. The incident underscores the growing dangers to civilian vessels operating in waters near the Russian-occupied peninsula.

The trawler DURU 67 was struck near Sevastopol, the port city that serves as headquarters for Russia's Black Sea Fleet. A nearby fishing boat rescued five wounded crew members and headed toward Turkey, but one sailor in critical condition died during the journey. The survivors, who suffered primarily shrapnel wounds, were transferred to a Turkish Coast Guard vessel and taken to a hospital in Kastamonu.

While Turkish authorities did not specify who carried out the attack, Ukraine has conducted extensive operations targeting military and logistical infrastructure around Crimea since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022. The peninsula was seized and annexed by Russia in 2014 in a move widely condemned by the international community and never recognized by the United Nations.

Ukrainian forces have increasingly struck Russian oil depots, supply routes, and port facilities in Crimea using naval drones and long-range weapons as part of efforts to disrupt Moscow's war effort. Turkey has previously expressed concern about attacks on vessels in its exclusive economic zone, warning of risks to maritime safety. The incident highlights the complex challenges facing neutral shipping in a conflict zone where military and civilian operations increasingly overlap.
