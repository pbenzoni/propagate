An Iranian-American digital artist who creates viral content about Middle East conflicts told Russian state media outlet RT that social media has fundamentally changed how war narratives spread, making it harder for any single government to control public perception.

Mahdi Hemmat, who produces Lego-style animated videos about regional conflicts, argued that platforms now allow "raw, unfiltered truth" to reach audiences instantly, bypassing traditional media gatekeepers. His AI-assisted animations—part of what some analysts call "defensive memetic warfare"—have garnered millions of views by depicting various incidents from ongoing hostilities, often set to rap music.

The phenomenon highlights ongoing debates about information warfare, media literacy, and the democratization of narrative-building in the digital age. While Hemmat frames his work as countering official U.S. messaging, media researchers note that all parties to modern conflicts now deploy sophisticated social media strategies to shape public opinion. The viral spread of user-generated content, regardless of its origin or accuracy, raises questions about verification, propaganda, and the public's ability to discern fact from persuasion in an era of algorithmic amplification.
