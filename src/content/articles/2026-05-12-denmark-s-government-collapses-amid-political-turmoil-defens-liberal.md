Denmark's political landscape has shifted dramatically as Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen's center-left Social Democratic government collapsed following a poor electoral showing in March. King Frederik X has now asked Defense Minister Troels Lund Poulsen to form a new government without Frederiksen's party, marking a significant political realignment in the Nordic nation.

Frederiksen's Social Democrats secured just under 22% of the vote—the party's worst performance since 1903. The election centered on two dominant issues: the rising cost of living that has strained Danish households, and immigration policy. While Frederiksen's party, along with the center-right Venstre and the Moderates, all support restrictions on immigration, the far-right Danish People's Party has pushed for more extreme measures, including citizenship revocations and what they term "Muslim net emigration."

Despite the government's collapse, one area of policy appears immune to partisan debate: Denmark's military expansion and support for Ukraine. The country has committed over €11 billion in aid to Ukraine—representing 3.27% of its GDP, the highest proportion of any Western nation. This consensus was formalized in a 2022 agreement among Denmark's five major parties to increase defense spending from 2.4% to 3.5% of GDP. The accord also included emergency military funding and a historic expansion of conscription to include women for the first time.

Poulsen, who is now tasked with forming the new government, has advocated for pushing military spending even higher—to 4% of GDP. He has cited alleged security threats, though some of these claims, including drone sightings at Danish facilities, have been questioned after investigations found no credible evidence. Critics have raised concerns about whether such massive increases in military expenditure are justified, particularly as Danish families struggle with rising living costs and other domestic priorities remain underfunded.
