Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam has accused Israel of committing war crimes following the death of journalist Amal Khalil, a reporter for Lebanon's Al-Akhbar newspaper. Khalil was killed Wednesday when an Israeli strike hit a house in the southern village of al-Tiri where she had sought shelter after an earlier attack targeted her vehicle. Her colleague Zeinab Faraj was seriously wounded in the incident.

The strike occurred despite a ceasefire agreement, raising questions about Israel's compliance with the truce terms. Israeli forces continue operating in occupied parts of southern Lebanon, maintaining an expanded security zone and preventing many residents from returning to their homes. The Israel Defense Forces denied deliberately targeting journalists and stated the incident is under review, claiming the individuals posed an "imminent threat" to Israeli troops after crossing a unilaterally declared security line.

"Targeting journalists, obstructing access to them by relief teams, and even targeting their locations again after these teams arrive constitute clearly defined war crimes," Salam wrote, pledging that "Lebanon will spare no effort in pursuing these crimes before the competent international forums." Lebanese Information Minister Paul Morcos called the killing "a crime and a flagrant violation of international and humanitarian law."

This incident marks the latest in a series of attacks on media workers in the region. According to the Associated Press, Khalil's death brought the number of journalists killed in Lebanon this year to at least nine. The escalating conflict has raised concerns about press freedom and the protection of civilians in conflict zones, with Lebanese authorities reporting more than 2,300 civilian deaths since the latest escalation began.
