A disturbing incident in northern Israel has drawn widespread condemnation after far-right lawmaker Zvi Sukkot was filmed wielding an electric saw outside a locked school gate in the predominantly Bedouin Arab town of Tuba-Zangariyye. Sukkot, who chairs the Knesset Education Committee and represents the Religious Zionism party, arrived under heavy police escort despite explicit objections from community leaders.

Local officials characterized the visit as a racist political stunt rather than any genuine educational inquiry. Tuba-Zangariyye council head Muayyad Haib told media that residents locked school gates to prevent entry, citing Sukkot's history of inflammatory statements toward their community. "What we least expected was for a member of the Israeli Knesset and the chairman of Israel's Education Committee to behave in such a way," Haib said, adding that some children were left "terrified" by the confrontation.

Rather than retreating when denied entry, Sukkot escalated the situation by using power tools at the locked gate—an act captured on video that quickly circulated online. In the aftermath, the lawmaker has doubled down, threatening to push for cuts to state funding for schools in Arab communities and arguing that Israel should not invest "even one shekel" in such educational institutions.

Critics across the political spectrum have accused Sukkot of deliberately seeking confrontation for political gain, raising concerns about the treatment of Arab communities within Israel and the weaponization of education policy along ethnic and religious lines. The incident highlights ongoing tensions between Israel's far-right government coalition and Palestinian Arab citizens, who comprise roughly 20% of Israel's population.
