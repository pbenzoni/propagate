President Donald Trump defended his Middle East ceasefire framework this week, telling reporters that the conventional definition of a truce doesn't always apply in that volatile region. Speaking candidly at a White House press conference Wednesday, Trump pushed back on media criticism of recent military exchanges, arguing that what counts as peace in the Middle East looks different than it does elsewhere.

"It's a different part of the world," Trump explained. "I'd say in that part of the world, ceasefire is when you're shooting in a more moderate manner." The remarks came after US forces reportedly targeted an Iranian tanker, prompting retaliatory Iranian strikes on US bases in Kuwait that damaged the country's international airport terminal. The administration described subsequent US airstrikes on Iran's Qeshm Island as "self-defense" measures.

Trump downplayed the Iranian attacks, characterizing Tehran's actions as responses to prior US military operations and maintaining that the armistice remains intact. The president first announced a truce with Iran on April 7, later extending it conditionally on April 21 and again for 60 days on May 8. He also announced a separate ceasefire in Lebanon on June 3.

The Lebanon situation remains complicated, with over 3,500 civilians reportedly killed by Israeli strikes since March and more than 1,100 since the April ceasefire was announced. A separate truce between the US, Israel, and Lebanon's government has been reaffirmed this week, though Hezbollah—the primary combatant—was not party to those negotiations and has accused Israel of multiple violations. Trump's frank acknowledgment of Middle Eastern realities reflects his transactional, pragmatic approach to a region where traditional diplomatic frameworks often fall short.
