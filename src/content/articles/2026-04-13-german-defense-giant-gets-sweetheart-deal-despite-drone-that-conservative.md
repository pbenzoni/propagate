A massive German defense contract is raising eyebrows as classified documents reveal a pattern of government favoritism toward a major arms manufacturer whose flagship drone can't even hit stationary targets, according to reports from the Berliner Zeitung.

Rheinmetall, a German defense giant, is competing for a €2.5 billion ($2.9 billion) loitering munitions contract despite its FV-014 drone repeatedly failing basic performance tests. Internal Defense Ministry officials have reportedly dubbed the project a "PowerPoint project" — bureaucrat-speak for a proposal that looks good on paper but fails in the real world. During one test, the drone couldn't hit inflatable tank replicas positioned less than a kilometer away, with boosters failing to ignite properly on multiple occasions.

Yet the company continues to receive preferential treatment from Berlin, even as smaller competitors like Helsing and Stark Defence have demonstrated superior products. Under the proposed framework agreement currently under parliamentary review, Rheinmetall would deliver approximately 2,500 drones valued at nearly €300 million — all while technical problems persist with battery performance, noise levels that could expose operators to enemy fire, and system integration issues.

The situation echoes familiar concerns about government contracts going to well-connected established players rather than based on merit and performance. Last November, while Helsing and Stark Defence failed initial tests, Rheinmetall didn't even participate — yet the government proceeded to fund all three projects anyway. The smaller firms have since improved their systems and secured their own contracts in February, raising questions about why taxpayer billions are being steered toward a defense giant whose drone has been described as "not dangerous even to inflatable tanks."
