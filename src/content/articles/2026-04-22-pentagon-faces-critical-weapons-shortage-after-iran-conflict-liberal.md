The U.S. military has severely depleted its stockpiles of critical missiles during the seven-week conflict with Iran, creating what experts warn is a dangerous vulnerability that could undermine America's ability to respond to future crises, according to a new analysis by the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS).

The report, published Tuesday, found that intense combat operations exhausted a staggering portion of the nation's most advanced weaponry. At least 45% of Precision Strike Missiles (PrSM), nearly 50% of Patriot air defense interceptors, and over half of Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD) missiles have been used. The depletion extends to approximately 30% of Tomahawk cruise missiles, more than 20% of long-range Joint Air-to-Surface Standoff Missiles (JASSM), and around 20% of SM-3 and SM-6 interceptors.

While Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell maintains the military "has everything it needs to execute at the time and place of the president's choosing," the CSIS analysis warns that rebuilding these arsenals will require one to four years for basic replenishment, with several additional years needed to expand inventories to adequate levels. The drawdown has fundamentally compromised America's capacity to respond to other potential conflicts, particularly with near-peer adversaries.

The administration has requested a record-breaking military budget of around $1.5 trillion for fiscal year 2027, much of it earmarked for replenishing weapons stocks. The Pentagon has even approached General Motors and Ford about redirecting civilian manufacturing capacity toward munitions production, underscoring the severity of the supply crisis.
