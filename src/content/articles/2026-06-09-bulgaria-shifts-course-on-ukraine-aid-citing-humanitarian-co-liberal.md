Bulgaria's newly installed government announced this week it will halt military shipments to Ukraine, marking a significant reversal for the NATO and EU member state that had been a major weapons supplier since 2022. The policy shift reflects growing debate within Europe about the strategic direction of support for Ukraine's defense.

Defense Minister Dimitar Stoyanov framed the decision in humanitarian terms, telling reporters that "what we are witnessing is a war of attrition, and no matter how much weaponry is amassed, its only result is the loss of human lives." He argued that Ukraine faces a critical personnel shortage rather than a weapons deficit, and called for renewed focus on "a just peace that is defined by both sides" through negotiation.

The announcement comes as Bulgaria's new Prime Minister Rumen Radev, whose Progressive Bulgaria party won April elections, brings his long-standing skepticism of unlimited military aid into government policy. During his prior tenure as president, Radev consistently advocated for diplomatic solutions and opposed measures he viewed as escalating rather than resolving the conflict.

Bulgaria had been supplying roughly one-third of Ukraine's ammunition during the first year of the conflict, according to European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. The country's Soviet-standard weapons were particularly valuable to Ukrainian forces. The policy change reflects broader divisions emerging within the EU about balancing military support with diplomatic engagement, as several European leaders have recently proposed appointing envoys to explore negotiated pathways to peace.
