A constitutional showdown is intensifying on Capitol Hill as Democratic lawmakers—joined by a growing number of Republicans—push back against the Trump administration's continued military operations against Iran without congressional authorization. The 60-day deadline mandated by the 1973 War Powers Resolution expired on May 1, yet U.S. forces remain engaged in enforcing a naval blockade on Iranian ports while Iran restricts traffic through the critical Strait of Hormuz.

The War Powers Resolution, passed in the wake of Vietnam to restore Congress's constitutional role in decisions of war and peace, requires presidents to obtain congressional approval within 60 days of introducing troops into hostilities. Critics across the aisle are now accusing the White House of flouting this law. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer rejected the administration's legal reasoning as "nonsense," writing on social media that "this is an illegal war" that endangers lives, increases costs for American families, and undermines democratic accountability.

In a Friday letter to congressional leaders, Trump claimed hostilities have been "terminated" since an April ceasefire, arguing he hasn't violated the 60-day limit. His Secretary of War Pete Hegseth doubled down on this position, suggesting the ceasefire "pauses" the clock—a novel legal interpretation that Senator Tim Kaine flatly rejected, citing "serious constitutional concerns." Even some Republicans are questioning the logic: Senator Todd Young asked pointedly whether a ceasefire can be considered valid "if fire could resume tomorrow."

Public sentiment has turned decisively against the conflict. A Washington Post poll found 61% of Americans now believe entering the war was a mistake, while other surveys show majority opposition and support cratering to around one-third. As the November midterms approach, Democrats are framing the fight as a defense of constitutional checks and balances against executive overreach—arguing that Republicans are enabling a president to wage unauthorized war while American families bear the costs.
