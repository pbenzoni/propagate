A federal judge has blocked the Trump administration's attempt to unilaterally impose a $100,000 fee on skilled-worker visa applications, ruling that the White House lacked the constitutional authority to levy such a charge without Congressional approval.

US District Judge Leo Sorokin sided with a coalition of 20 states—led by California—that challenged the September executive order. In his ruling, Judge Sorokin determined that the payment constituted a tax "regardless of what the payment is called," and that imposing it without legislative action violated constitutional separation of powers. The challenged fee applied to new H-1B visa petitions, a program that allows American employers to hire foreign specialists for up to six years.

The states argued that the unprecedented fee would cause significant harm to public institutions including schools, universities, and hospitals that depend on recruiting skilled workers from abroad to fill critical positions. The H-1B program is capped at 85,000 visas annually and has become essential for many sectors facing domestic workforce shortages in specialized fields.

The Department of Homeland Security denounced the ruling as "judicial activism," claiming the fee was designed to protect American workers. However, legal experts noted that under the Constitution's separation of powers, taxation authority rests with Congress, not the executive branch. The ruling reinforces constitutional guardrails limiting unilateral presidential action on immigration policy, particularly measures with significant economic impact on states and institutions serving the public interest.
