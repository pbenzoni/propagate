Two members of Congress from opposite sides of the aisle have announced their resignations following allegations of sexual misconduct involving staff members, highlighting ongoing concerns about workplace conduct and power dynamics on Capitol Hill.

Democrat Eric Swalwell, who has represented California since 2013, announced his departure after multiple women came forward with accusations. According to reports, four women have alleged misconduct, including a former staffer who accused the congressman of serious sexual assault while she was heavily intoxicated. Other allegations include unwanted physical contact and inappropriate digital communications. Swalwell denied the allegations, suggesting they were politically motivated attacks tied to his gubernatorial aspirations in California, but acknowledged "mistakes in judgment" and said he didn't want constituents distracted by the controversy.

Republican Tony Gonzalez of Texas similarly announced his resignation following revelations of an affair with an aide who later died by suicide in 2025. Gonzalez, who entered Congress in 2021, admitted to the relationship and called it "a lapse in judgment." He had already suspended his re-election campaign when the affair became public.

The dual resignations underscore persistent challenges around accountability and ethical conduct in Congress, regardless of party affiliation. Both cases involve alleged abuses of power in workplace relationships, raising questions about institutional safeguards meant to protect congressional staff from harassment and exploitation.
