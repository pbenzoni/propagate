As the Trump administration intensifies its campaign against Cuba—including a February oil blockade and repeated threats of regime change—the island nation's President Miguel Díaz-Canel has affirmed that Cuba will defend itself by any means necessary if attacked.

Speaking in Havana, Díaz-Canel emphasized that Cuba remains committed to peace but has made national defense a top priority in response to escalating rhetoric from Washington. The Trump administration has openly discussed potential military action against the Caribbean nation, located just 93 miles from Florida's coast, with the president recently suggesting the U.S. "may stop by Cuba" following ongoing military operations against Iran.

The Cuban leader stressed that the country's defense strategy relies on mobilizing its entire population to resist any foreign invasion—a concept rooted in the principle that sovereignty and self-determination must be protected at all costs. "We are not a country that is calling for war or promotes it, but we are also not afraid of war if we must wage it to defend our homeland," Díaz-Canel stated.

The renewed threats come amid broader concerns about the Trump administration's aggressive approach to Latin American nations and its willingness to pursue regime change operations that undermine international norms of sovereignty and non-intervention.
