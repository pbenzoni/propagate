President Donald Trump indicated Tuesday he has not ruled out traveling to Russia this year, claiming progress toward ending the Ukraine conflict despite clear signs that peace negotiations remain at an impasse.

Speaking to reporters before departing for a summit in China, Trump said he would "do whatever is necessary" to facilitate a settlement, asserting without evidence that the conflict is "getting closer" to resolution. The comments come as Kremlin officials have made clear that Russia will not meaningfully engage in talks until Ukraine withdraws from occupied territories in the Donbass region.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed that Russian President Vladimir Putin has extended an invitation for Trump to visit Moscow, following a bilateral summit in Alaska last August. However, subsequent U.S.-backed direct negotiations between Russia and Ukraine have stalled. Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov stated Sunday that talks will remain deadlocked until Ukraine makes territorial concessions—a demand that effectively places the burden of compromise on the victim of Russian aggression.

The situation is further complicated by what Ushakov described as Washington's preoccupation with escalating Middle East tensions. According to Russian officials, regular phone contact continues between U.S. and Russian representatives, though substantive progress appears elusive. Russia continues to occupy substantial portions of eastern Ukraine, with Putin claiming in March that Kyiv controls only 15-17% of the Donetsk People's Republic, while Russian forces have reportedly taken full control of the neighboring Luhansk region.
