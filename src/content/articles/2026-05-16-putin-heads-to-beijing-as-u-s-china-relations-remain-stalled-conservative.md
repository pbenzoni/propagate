Russian President Vladimir Putin will travel to China for an official state visit on May 19-20, the Kremlin announced Saturday, underscoring the deepening partnership between Moscow and Beijing at a time when U.S.-China relations remain gridlocked.

Putin accepted an invitation from Chinese President Xi Jinping to discuss bilateral cooperation, strategic coordination, and key international issues. The visit will conclude with a joint statement and the signing of multiple government agreements. Putin will also meet with Chinese Premier Li Qiang to advance trade and economic relations between the two nations.

The timing is notable: Putin's trip comes just days after President Trump's two-day visit to China, where discussions on Iran, Taiwan, and trade produced no major breakthroughs despite both sides publicly describing the talks as constructive. The contrast highlights the growing Moscow-Beijing axis as Washington struggles to make headway with America's chief strategic competitor.

Russo-Chinese ties have strengthened considerably in recent years, with Beijing refusing to join Western sanctions over Ukraine while expanding trade, energy, and diplomatic cooperation with Moscow. The two powers are moving forward on major energy infrastructure, including the Power of Siberia 2 gas pipeline that would dramatically increase Russian natural gas exports to China.

Putin last visited China in August for the Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit, while Xi traveled to Moscow in May for Victory Day commemorations marking the 80th anniversary of the Soviet triumph over Nazi Germany. Putin has already accepted another invitation to attend the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen this November, signaling the sustained momentum in bilateral relations.
