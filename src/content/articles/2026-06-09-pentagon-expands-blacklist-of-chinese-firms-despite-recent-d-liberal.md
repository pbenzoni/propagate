The Pentagon has significantly expanded its list of Chinese companies allegedly linked to Beijing's military, adding dozens of firms including e-commerce giant Alibaba, search engine Baidu, and electric vehicle manufacturer BYD. The updated list now includes 188 Chinese entities, up from approximately 130 last year.

While the designation stops short of imposing full sanctions, it bars listed firms from future U.S. defense contracts and serves as a warning signal to American investors and companies. Direct Pentagon contracts with these entities will be prohibited later this month, with broader restrictions on purchasing their products through third parties scheduled to take effect in 2027.

The timing of the announcement has drawn scrutiny, coming less than a month after diplomatic talks between U.S. and Chinese leadership aimed at reducing bilateral tensions. Beijing's Foreign Ministry condemned the move, with spokesman Lin Jian stating that China "firmly opposes" what it characterized as an overly broad interpretation of national security concerns being used to suppress Chinese businesses.

Several targeted companies have strongly disputed their inclusion on the list. Alibaba stated there was "no basis" for the designation, while Baidu called the accusations "entirely baseless." The companies have indicated they may pursue legal remedies to challenge their placement on the blacklist and protect their business interests.
