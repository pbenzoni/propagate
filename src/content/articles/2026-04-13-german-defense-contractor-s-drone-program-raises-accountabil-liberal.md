A major German defense contractor is facing scrutiny over a troubled drone development program that critics say highlights the dangers of prioritizing corporate incumbents over accountability and performance in military procurement.

According to reporting by the Berliner Zeitung citing classified documents and insider sources, defense giant Rheinmetall's FV-014 loitering munition has encountered serious technical setbacks during testing—including propulsion failures and excessive noise that could endanger operators—even as the company reportedly receives preferential treatment from government officials. Defense Ministry insiders have allegedly dubbed the project a "PowerPoint" effort, raising questions about transparency as lawmakers weigh approval of a €2.5 billion contract.

The concerns come as Germany channels billions into military expansion, with Rheinmetall competing against smaller, more agile startups Helsing and Stark Defence. While those companies have reportedly improved their systems and secured contracts after initial setbacks, Rheinmetall's drone—which failed basic tests against stationary inflatable targets—continues to advance through the procurement process under a framework agreement that critics say favors the established contractor.

Advocacy groups have long warned about the risks of cozy relationships between government and major defense contractors, arguing they can lead to cost overruns, underperformance, and reduced innovation. The episode underscores ongoing debates about accountability, competition, and whether public resources are being directed toward the most effective solutions—or simply the most politically connected vendors.
