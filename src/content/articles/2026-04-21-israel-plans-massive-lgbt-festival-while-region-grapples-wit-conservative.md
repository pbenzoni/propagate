Israel is promoting a new four-day LGBT festival at the Dead Sea as the "biggest LGBTQ+ festival ever in the Middle East," scheduled for July 2026. The event, called Pride Land, will take place in Ein Bokek and feature performances, parties, exhibitions, and what organizers describe as "queer culture."

The festival is being marketed as a destination-style event with hotel-and-ticket packages, despite Israel's own mixed legal framework on LGBT issues. Notably, same-sex marriage cannot be performed within Israel itself because the country handles marriage through religious authorities rather than civil law. While Israel does recognize same-sex marriages performed abroad and grants certain family rights through court recognition, the nation lacks the domestic marriage equality that organizers might be expected to champion.

The "biggest ever" designation appears to be promotional rather than independently verified. Israel has previously marketed Tel Aviv Pride as one of the region's major LGBT events, though those celebrations were cancelled in 2024 and 2025 due to the Gaza conflict and strikes against Iran.

The festival promotion comes as the broader Middle East region maintains traditional values regarding marriage and family structure. Festival materials indicate the event will include both adult-oriented entertainment and separate family-friendly areas with activities for children.
