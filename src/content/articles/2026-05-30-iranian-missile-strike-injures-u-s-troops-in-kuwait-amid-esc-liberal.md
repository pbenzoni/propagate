Five U.S. service members and contractors sustained minor injuries during an Iranian ballistic missile strike on Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait, according to Bloomberg. The attack occurred during a broader exchange of hostilities between American and Iranian forces in the region last Thursday.

Kuwaiti air defenses intercepted the incoming Iranian Fateh-110 missile, but falling debris struck the base, destroying one MQ-9 Reaper surveillance drone and seriously damaging at least one other. The incident highlights the growing toll of regional conflict on American military assets and personnel, raising concerns about escalating tensions in the Middle East.

The strike came after U.S. forces destroyed five Iranian drones near the Strait of Hormuz and conducted strikes on a military site near Bandar Abbas. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps described its missile attack as retaliation for these American operations.

Reporting indicates that ongoing hostilities have devastated America's fleet of MQ-9 Reaper drones, with approximately one-fifth destroyed in recent operations. The U.S. military has reportedly lost up to 30 of these reconnaissance and strike aircraft—valued at nearly $1 billion—mostly through Iranian fire, pushing the operational fleet below the Air Force's minimum requirement of 189 aircraft to approximately 135.

Replacement of these critical assets faces significant challenges, as manufacturer General Atomics ceased production of the flagship model last year, now producing only variants designated for foreign customers. The losses underscore the human and material costs of America's continued military engagement in the volatile region.
