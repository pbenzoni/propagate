Russian President Vladimir Putin will travel to Beijing this week for meetings with Chinese President Xi Jinping, the Kremlin announced Monday, in a visit that underscores the strengthening ties between two nations increasingly isolated from democratic alliances.

The May 19-20 visit, which coincides with the 25th anniversary of a friendship treaty between the two countries, will include expanded discussions on bilateral relations, trade, and what the Kremlin describes as "major international developments." Putin will be accompanied by a large delegation including senior ministers, top Kremlin officials, and heads of major Russian state corporations and banks, according to Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov.

The two leaders are expected to sign around 40 bilateral agreements, including a joint statement on deepening their comprehensive strategic partnership. Notably, they also plan to adopt a separate declaration promoting what they term a "multipolar world" and "a new type of international relations"—language that experts say represents a direct challenge to the democratic, rules-based international order established after World War II.

The timing of Putin's visit comes just days after former President Trump's trip to China last week, where discussions with Xi produced no major breakthroughs on key disagreements between Beijing and Washington. While Ushakov dismissed any connection between the two visits, the close timing highlights the complex geopolitical landscape as authoritarian governments seek to expand their influence on the world stage.
