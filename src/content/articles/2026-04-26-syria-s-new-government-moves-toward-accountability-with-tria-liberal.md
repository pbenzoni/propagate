Syria is preparing to launch its first public trials of prominent officials from the ousted government of former President Bashar Assad, according to an announcement from the country's Justice Ministry. The proceedings represent a significant step in what authorities are calling a transitional justice process aimed at addressing abuses committed during the devastating 13-year civil war.

The trials will begin with cases connected to Daraa, the city where the Syrian uprising began in 2011 after security forces arrested and allegedly tortured 15 students for writing anti-government graffiti during the Arab Spring. When protests erupted in response, security forces opened fire on demonstrators, igniting a conflict that would claim more than half a million lives and displace millions more.

Justice Minister Mazhar al-Wais emphasized the symbolic importance of beginning with Daraa-related cases, stating that "just as the beginning was in Daraa, the cradle of the revolution, justice demands that it also serve as the launchpad for the path of transitional justice." The proceedings are expected to include figures accused of ordering crackdowns on protesters, overseeing extrajudicial killings, and other human rights violations.

The trials come after a rebel coalition led by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham toppled the Assad government in late 2024, installing a transitional administration that has pledged to pursue accountability for civil war-era crimes. Media reports suggest the proceedings may be open to international legal observers and diplomats to ensure transparency, though this has not been officially confirmed. The new authorities have framed the trials as essential to rebuilding public trust and unifying a country fractured by years of conflict and foreign intervention.
