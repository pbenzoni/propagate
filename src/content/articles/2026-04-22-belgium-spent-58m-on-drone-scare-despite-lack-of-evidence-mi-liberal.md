Belgium's Defense Minister Theo Francken is defending his government's emergency response to last year's drone scare, despite acknowledging that most of the more than 550 reported sightings turned out to be false. The crisis prompted €50 million ($58.5 million) in emergency spending on anti-drone equipment, raising concerns about government accountability and transparency in crisis response.

An investigation by VRT News' program Pano revealed that several alleged drone sightings were actually police helicopters, and raised questions about procurement practices during the emergency. Contracts worth millions were reportedly awarded without standard public tender processes, with allegations of inflated pricing—a pattern that raises concerns about oversight and the potential for waste in emergency spending.

When questioned by Parliament's Defense Select Committee, Francken cited military intelligence findings claiming 42 out of 250 sightings over military facilities were "legitimate," though he acknowledged no physical evidence was collected. He offered what observers described as a qualified apology for personally circulating images of alleged drones that later proved to be a police helicopter. Critics argue the episode highlights the dangers of reactive policymaking without proper verification, and the need for stronger safeguards to prevent fear-driven emergency spending that bypasses normal accountability measures.

The controversy underscores broader questions about how governments respond to perceived security threats, the importance of evidence-based decision-making, and the need for transparency in emergency procurement processes to protect public resources.
