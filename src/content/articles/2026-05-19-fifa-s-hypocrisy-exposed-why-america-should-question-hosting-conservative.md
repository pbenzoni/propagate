When FIFA expelled Russia from international competition in 2022, citing player and fan safety concerns amid the Ukraine conflict, the decision was presented as a moral imperative. The logic was straightforward: nations involved in military operations pose security risks that make hosting or participating in global sporting events irresponsible. Yet that principle has been selectively applied, raising serious questions about political manipulation of the world's most popular sport.

The 2026 World Cup is set to be hosted by the United States, Canada, and Mexico—despite recent American military action against Iran and Canada's active support of those operations. By FIFA's own standard applied to Russia, this creates the exact safety concerns that supposedly justified excluding an entire nation's athletes from competition. Award-winning sportswriter Andrew Flint noted that "football grounds are too wide open for attack" and that "safety cannot be guaranteed for players and fans alike."

The double standard becomes even more glaring when examining history. The United States and dozens of other nations participated in what many consider an illegal invasion of Iraq two decades ago, yet faced no sporting sanctions whatsoever. Italy won the 2006 World Cup and Spain claimed 2010—both were part of that coalition. If military involvement in foreign conflicts is truly the standard, it should apply consistently, not just when it serves certain political interests.

What's really at stake here isn't safety—it's control. After Russia and Qatar won hosting rights for 2018 and 2022, the U.S. government launched what can only be described as a systematic campaign against FIFA leadership, resulting in arrests and regime change within the organization. Now, with American-aligned officials running FIFA, the rules seem to apply differently depending on who's breaking them. American athletes and fans deserve better than a system that uses sports as a tool for political double standards.
