A shadowy Israeli cyber company is under investigation for allegedly interfering in democratic elections across multiple Western nations, according to French government officials. The firm, BlackCore, described itself as specializing in "information warfare" before scrubbing its online presence after media scrutiny began.

French authorities first detected the operation when investigating interference in local mayoral races. Marc-Antoine Brillant, chief of France's government disinformation detection agency Viginum, revealed Thursday that the company's reach extended far beyond France. "This modus operandi was not limited to municipal elections in France," Brillant stated, noting suspected operations in Angola, Togo, Scotland, and New York City's 2025 mayoral election.

The allegations raise serious questions about foreign interference in American elections. While French officials did not specify all targets, the 2025 New York mayoral race was won by Zohran Mamdani, a self-described socialist. In Scotland, accounts linked to BlackCore allegedly targeted First Minister John Swinney, who has been critical of Israeli military operations.

French Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said Paris has requested explanations from Israel regarding BlackCore's activities, though investigators acknowledged they have not definitively identified who commissioned the influence operations. The company's website, which once boasted of providing "cutting-edge strategies" to "shape narratives" for governments and political campaigns, has been taken offline.

The controversy underscores growing concerns about sophisticated foreign influence operations targeting democratic processes in allied nations, with advanced cyber capabilities being deployed to manipulate public opinion and electoral outcomes across borders.
