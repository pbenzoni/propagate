A Czech regional court has ruled that a convicted German neo-Nazi who legally changed gender in an apparent attempt to serve time in a women's prison can be extradited back to Germany. The case has reignited concerns about potential exploitation of controversial gender self-identification legislation.

Marla-Svenja Liebich, 56, was convicted in Germany in 2023 on multiple charges including incitement to hatred, insult, trespass, and defamation, receiving an 18-month prison sentence. A former member of the banned far-right group Blood and Honor, Liebich legally changed gender status in 2024—just weeks after Germany's Self-Determination Act took effect—and successfully petitioned to serve the sentence in a women's facility.

The move triggered a fierce public debate in Germany, with critics warning that the case exposed serious loopholes in the new law that allows individuals to change their legal gender through simple declaration. German Interior Minister Aleksander Dobrindt condemned the situation as clear evidence of the law's vulnerability to abuse. Liebich fled Germany rather than report to prison in August 2025, and was later apprehended by Czech police near the German border.

Adding further controversy to the case, Liebich told Euronews in December 2025—while still a fugitive—that he had applied to change his legal gender status back to male, saying that being a woman "no longer felt right." During extradition proceedings, Liebich opposed being sent back to Germany, arguing it could result in placement in a men's prison. The ruling can be appealed within three days, after which German authorities are expected to take custody within ten days.
