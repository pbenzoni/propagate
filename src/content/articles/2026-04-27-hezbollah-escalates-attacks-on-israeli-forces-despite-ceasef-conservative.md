Hezbollah militants killed an Israeli Defense Forces soldier and launched drone attacks targeting a medical evacuation helicopter in southern Lebanon on Sunday, highlighting ongoing threats despite a U.S.-brokered ceasefire agreement.

The attack killed 19-year-old Sergeant Idan Fooks and wounded six other IDF soldiers, four of them seriously, according to Israeli military officials. When a helicopter arrived to evacuate the wounded troops, Hezbollah launched two additional drones at the evacuation site. The IDF intercepted one drone while another exploded near Israeli forces and the aircraft without causing additional casualties.

The incident occurred despite a ceasefire that began April 16 and was extended through mid-May. Israeli forces have maintained a security buffer zone approximately 10 kilometers into Lebanese territory to prevent attacks on Israeli communities. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stated that Israel is operating within the rules agreed upon with the United States and Lebanon, including maintaining "freedom of action" to respond to threats and neutralize emerging dangers.

Hezbollah, which receives substantial backing from Iran, has characterized its operations as a response to Israeli military presence in Lebanon. The Iranian-backed group has been designated a terrorist organization by the United States. According to Lebanon's Health Ministry, the conflict has resulted in significant casualties since fighting began on March 2, though these figures cannot be independently verified and may include combatants among the reported deaths.
