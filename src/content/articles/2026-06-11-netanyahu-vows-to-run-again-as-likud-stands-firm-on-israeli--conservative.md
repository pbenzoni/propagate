Benjamin Netanyahu will run for office again in Israel's upcoming election later this year, his Likud party announced Wednesday, reaffirming the veteran leader's commitment to continuing at the helm despite mounting political challenges.

The announcement came after ABC News Chief Washington Correspondent Jonathan Karl reported that President Donald Trump expressed uncertainty about whether Netanyahu should seek another term. "He's had an amazing career. Does he want to continue?" Trump reportedly said, describing Netanyahu as "a wartime prime minister" while expressing confidence that "we will very shortly win the war [against Iran] one way or the other."

Likud responded swiftly with a statement declaring that "Prime Minister Netanyahu will run in the upcoming elections – and with God's help, he will win." Netanyahu, 76, previously served as prime minister from 1996 to 1999 and from 2009 to 2021 before returning to office in December 2022. Israeli law requires the election to be held before October 27, marking the first vote since the nation's military response to Hamas's deadly attack on October 7, 2023.

A recent poll by the Israel Democracy Institute showed that 61% of Israelis oppose Netanyahu running again, with the same percentage favoring term limits for future prime ministers. Netanyahu, currently facing corruption charges, has weathered criticism over his handling of conflicts with Hamas in Gaza, Hezbollah in Lebanon, and Iran. The prime minister maintains he is achieving Israel's security objectives despite opposition claims to the contrary.
