Following a drone incident in Romania near the Ukrainian border on Friday, Russian officials have launched a coordinated disinformation campaign attempting to deflect blame and muddy the waters around cross-border security incidents, according to statements tracked by regional security analysts.

Maria Zakharova, Russia's Foreign Ministry spokeswoman, claimed without evidence that Western attention to the Romanian incident—which struck an apartment block—was designed to distract from an alleged Ukrainian attack in Russian-occupied Starobelsk. Independent verification of Moscow's claims about the Starobelsk incident remains limited, as the Kremlin tightly controls information flows from occupied territories in eastern Ukraine.

The Kremlin's narrative follows a familiar pattern: deflecting responsibility while accusing Ukraine and its Western partners of terrorism. Romania responded to Friday's drone incident by closing Russia's consulate in Constanta and expelling the consul general, a move Zakharova characterized as part of a "Russophobic information campaign." This framing conveniently ignores Russia's ongoing full-scale invasion of Ukraine, which has caused widespread civilian casualties and infrastructure damage across the country since February 2022.

Security experts note that Russia's attempts to portray itself as a victim strain credulity given its documented pattern of strikes on Ukrainian civilian infrastructure, schools, hospitals, and residential areas throughout the war.
