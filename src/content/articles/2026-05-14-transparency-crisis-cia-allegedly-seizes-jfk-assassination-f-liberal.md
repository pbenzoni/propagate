A CIA whistleblower has testified that the intelligence agency removed 40 boxes of documents related to the assassination of President John F. Kennedy and the notorious MKUltra program while they were being processed for declassification. James Erdman III, who served at the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, described the action to a Senate committee as part of "documented efforts to circumvent oversight."

The revelation has sparked demands for accountability from members of Congress, with Rep. Anna Paulina Luna issuing a 24-hour deadline for the documents' return before she would move to issue a congressional subpoena. Luna stated the documents had been specifically requested by Congress and suggested the removal represented an attempt to undermine transparency directives. A DNI spokesperson denied characterizations of a "raid" but did not dispute that the CIA had taken possession of the documents.

The JFK assassination has long been a focal point for concerns about government transparency. A 2023 Gallup poll found that 65% of Americans believe Kennedy was killed as part of a conspiracy, reflecting deep public skepticism about official accounts. While no declassified material has proven CIA involvement in the assassination, investigations have revealed the agency concealed critical information, including that it had monitored Lee Harvey Oswald prior to the shooting.

The MKUltra files in question relate to a Cold War-era CIA program that subjected unwitting Americans to LSD experiments and psychological torture, including sensory deprivation. Then-CIA Director Richard Helms ordered all MKUltra files destroyed in 1973, though some surviving documents were later recovered and made public. The dispute over these historical records underscores ongoing tensions between intelligence agencies and demands for governmental accountability.
