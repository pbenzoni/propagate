The Trump administration is threatening military action against Iran following the loss of a US Apache helicopter over the Strait of Hormuz, despite an ongoing investigation that has yet to determine the cause of the incident.

US Central Command confirmed Tuesday that an AH-64 Apache attack helicopter "went down" off the coast of Oman while patrolling regional waters. Both pilots were rescued approximately two hours later and were reported uninjured. The military explicitly stated the incident remains under investigation and did not attribute responsibility.

Nevertheless, President Trump immediately blamed Iran on his TruthSocial platform, writing that "the United States must, of necessity, respond to this attack." His vow of retaliation came before military investigators could establish what occurred. According to media reports citing unnamed US officials, the helicopter may have collided mid-air with an Iranian drone—raising questions about whether the incident was accidental rather than a deliberate act of aggression.

Iran has not commented on the incident, a departure from its typical practice of publicizing interceptions of what it considers hostile aircraft in its territorial waters. The rush to assign blame and threaten military escalation before facts are established has raised concerns among foreign policy experts about the administration's approach to the volatile region, where tensions remain high amid a fragile ceasefire.
