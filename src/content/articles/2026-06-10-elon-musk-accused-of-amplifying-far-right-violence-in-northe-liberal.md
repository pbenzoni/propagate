British government officials have sharply criticized Elon Musk for using his social media platform to encourage protests that preceded violent anti-immigrant riots in Belfast. The unrest erupted following a knife attack allegedly carried out by a Sudanese asylum seeker, which left one victim with severe injuries.

Before the violence broke out, Musk posted on X that "only by protesting REPEATEDLY and LOUDLY will there be any change," and amplified content from far-right activist Tommy Robinson promoting dozens of protest locations across the UK. The riots resulted in masked gangs attacking homes, torching vehicles, and clashing with police in Northern Ireland's capital.

Labour Party Chair Anna Turley condemned the tech mogul's intervention, telling LBC that "anyone seeking to drive and exploit a situation like this to drive their own political agenda is grievously wrong and doing damage." She emphasized that Musk, commenting from thousands of miles away, doesn't have to live with the consequences of the unrest he helps fuel.

Prime Minister Keir Starmer joined the criticism, warning that those inciting violence—whether online or in the streets—would face legal consequences. The incident is part of a broader pattern of Musk using his massive platform to inject himself into UK politics, amplifying far-right narratives about immigration and policing. Critics argue that wealthy tech executives wielding unchecked power over public discourse pose a growing threat to democratic stability and community safety.
