A California economics professor has raised concerns about suspicious trading patterns in oil markets that appear to align closely with major developments in the Iran situation. Professor Jack Rasmus from Saint Mary's College of California points to what he describes as a troubling pattern of trades placed shortly before or after key announcements.

According to reports, roughly 10,000 oil contracts worth approximately $920 million were opened on Wednesday, less than an hour before news emerged of progress in negotiations between Washington and Tehran. The timing resulted in oil prices dropping more than 12 percent and generated substantial profits for those holding the positions. Rasmus suggests similar patterns have occurred repeatedly, with trades placed "within minutes" of presidential statements about Iran.

The professor estimates that such trading activity may have generated around $7 billion in March and April alone. He alleges that commodity exchanges like the Chicago Mercantile Exchange and ICE likely have information about the traders involved but claims they have not made findings public. "There's no other explanation" for the pattern other than access to non-public information, Rasmus stated.

These allegations raise serious questions about market integrity and the potential misuse of government information for private profit. The pattern of suspicious trades coinciding with policy announcements—whether oil prices rise on tough rhetoric or fall on diplomatic progress—suggests a breakdown in the proper handling of sensitive information that affects markets.
