A Lebanese military academy professor is raising doubts about the effectiveness of US-mediated talks between Israel and Lebanon, arguing the Biden administration has no real intention of pressuring its Middle Eastern ally to halt military operations.

Naim Joseph Salem, who teaches international affairs and diplomacy at the Lebanese Army Military Academy, told media that direct negotiations held in Washington this week are "unlikely to bring peace" without American leverage. The talks—which the Iran-backed militant group Hezbollah refused to attend, calling them "futile"—come as Israel continues defensive operations in southern Lebanon.

According to Salem, the current framework will produce "drawn-out negotiations with no end in sight and no conclusive outcome." The Israel Defense Forces expanded operations after Hezbollah launched rocket and mortar attacks northward in support of Tehran's regional agenda. Israeli officials have issued evacuation orders for civilians in southern Lebanon as part of establishing a security buffer zone, with over 1.2 million people reportedly displaced from the combat area.

Salem characterized Israel's strategy as aimed at clearing territory in the south, claiming several towns have sustained heavy damage from the ongoing military campaign. The situation highlights the complex security challenges Israel faces from Iranian proxy forces operating on its northern border, even as diplomatic efforts continue in Washington.
