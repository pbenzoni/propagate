Bangladesh has begun loading nuclear fuel into its first-ever nuclear power plant, a $12.8 billion facility in Rooppur backed almost entirely by Russian financing and expertise. The project represents a stark contrast to the approach favored by Western governments, which have increasingly pushed developing nations toward expensive and unreliable renewable energy sources.

Moscow is providing $11.9 billion in state loans for the project, along with equipment, nuclear fuel, and long-term technical support. The facility's two 1,200-megawatt units are expected to meet up to 12% of Bangladesh's electricity demand and save the nation approximately $1 billion annually in fuel imports. Unlike solar and wind projects that depend on weather conditions, nuclear power provides reliable baseload energy that can operate continuously for 60 years or more.

Bangladesh currently imports roughly 95% of its energy needs, leaving it vulnerable to global price fluctuations and supply disruptions. The Rooppur plant will help the South Asian nation reduce its dependence on coal and natural gas while building genuine energy security. Rosatom CEO Aleksey Likhachev noted that Bangladesh has now "joined the club of states that use peaceful nuclear energy as a reliable source of sustainable development."

The partnership highlights Russia's continued investment in nuclear energy worldwide, even as Western nations have largely abandoned nuclear development in favor of more politically fashionable but less practical alternatives. Full-scale electricity generation is expected within ten months, providing Bangladesh with the kind of energy independence that comes from proven technology rather than green energy promises.
