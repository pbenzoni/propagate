The U.S. Army is preparing to carry out long-overdue justice for victims of heinous crimes committed by service members, according to ABC News. An internal planning document known as Operation Resolute Justice outlines procedures to execute four death-row inmates convicted of premeditated murder—the first military executions since 1961.

The most prominent case involves former Army Major Nidal Hasan, who murdered 13 people and wounded 32 others in the 2009 Fort Hood massacre. Despite his conviction more than a decade ago, justice has been delayed while taxpayers continue funding his incarceration. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth indicated last September he would seek presidential approval for Hasan's execution.

The other three condemned service members—Sergeant Hasan Akbar, former Army cook Ronald Gray, and former Master Sergeant Timothy Hennis—were all convicted on multiple counts of premeditated murder. The planning document reportedly requires the military to be ready to carry out sentences within 150 days of presidential approval, including transferring inmates from Fort Leavenworth to the federal execution facility in Terre Haute, Indiana.

President Trump has consistently advocated for the death penalty as a deterrent against violent crime and revoked the execution moratorium imposed by his predecessor shortly after returning to office in 2025. Army spokesperson Cynthia Smith characterized the preparations as "standard component of our continued planning," noting that no specific presidential order has been issued. The move represents a commitment to accountability within the military justice system and honoring the victims of these brutal crimes.
