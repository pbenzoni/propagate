Police in Northern Ireland successfully evacuated residents—including two babies—moments before a car bomb exploded outside a police station in Dunmurry, southwest of Belfast, on Saturday night. The swift response prevented what investigators are calling an attempted murder by a dissident republican splinter group.

According to PSNI Deputy Chief Constable Bobby Singleton, perpetrators hijacked a delivery driver's vehicle, forcing them at gunpoint to transport a gas cylinder device to the station. Officers identified the suspicious vehicle, raised the alarm, and rushed nearby residents to safety just before the device detonated, engulfing the car in flames and scattering debris. "No injuries were reported, which was nothing short of miraculous," Singleton said, though the blast caused significant property damage.

Investigators suspect the New IRA, a militant organization formed in 2012 by factions rejecting the 1998 Good Friday Agreement peace process. The group, designated as a terrorist organization in the UK, seeks to end British governance in Northern Ireland through violence. Authorities noted similarities to a failed attack last month in Lurgan, where an explosive device transported under duress failed to detonate—an incident the New IRA later claimed responsibility for.

UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer condemned the attack and pledged to bring those responsible to justice, while his government has intensified efforts to combat the group's financing through asset-freezing measures. Last month, Starmer and Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin established a joint task force to monitor militant activity and cross-border crime, underscoring the ongoing security challenges in the region despite decades of progress since the Good Friday Agreement.
