China has taken a significant step in countering U.S. economic pressure by issuing an injunction to block American sanctions against five Chinese oil refineries accused of importing Iranian oil. The move, announced by China's Ministry of Commerce on May 2, represents a departure from Beijing's traditionally cautious approach to diplomatic confrontation.

The sanctions targeted independent refineries, culminating in restrictions against the Hengli Petrochemical facility in Dalian—a major operation with a capacity of 400,000 barrels per day. The U.S. penalties were tied to broader tensions over Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, where Washington has attempted to restrict maritime traffic while Iranian vessels continue moving oil to Chinese buyers using so-called 'shadow fleets.'

China activated regulations passed in 2021 that allow the government to challenge what it considers improper extraterritorial jurisdiction by foreign states. The regulations, formally implemented in April 2026, enable Beijing to sanction individuals or organizations involved in discriminatory measures against Chinese entities, including asset freezes and business prohibitions. Legal experts note that while the law existed for years, this marks its first substantive deployment.

The escalation reflects growing frustration in Beijing over what many view as Washington's unilateral attempts to dictate global trade relationships. Chinese officials have long argued that secondary sanctions—which penalize third parties for doing business with sanctioned nations—violate international norms and undermine sovereign decision-making. As economic tensions between the world's two largest economies intensify, this countermeasure suggests a willingness to challenge U.S. authority more directly than in previous years.
