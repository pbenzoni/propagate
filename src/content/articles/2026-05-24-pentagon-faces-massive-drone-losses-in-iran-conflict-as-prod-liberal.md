The U.S. military has lost approximately 30 MQ-9 Reaper drones—representing nearly one-fifth of its pre-war fleet—since conflict with Iran began, according to a Bloomberg report citing defense sources. The losses, valued at close to $1 billion, come as production of the reconnaissance and strike aircraft has already been discontinued.

The Pentagon now operates roughly 135 Reapers, falling significantly below the Air Force's long-established minimum threshold of 189 aircraft, according to Lt. Gen. David Tabor, the Pentagon's deputy chief of staff for plans and programs. Each drone costs more than $30 million, and manufacturer General Atomics halted production last year, though variants continue to be built for international customers.

While many drones were destroyed by Iranian air defenses, others were lost in ground-based missile strikes and accidents, Bloomberg reported. The Congressional Research Service, Congress's nonpartisan research arm, issued a report this month estimating total U.S. aircraft losses at 42, including fighter jets, refueling planes, and a helicopter, alongside the unmanned systems.

The mounting equipment losses have contributed to escalating costs for the military operation. Acting Pentagon Comptroller Jules Hurst told the House Appropriations defense subcommittee that projected expenses have risen from $25 billion to $29 billion, driven partly by "updated repair and replacement of equipment costs." The figures raise concerns about resource management and the sustainability of current military commitments.
