A decade-long effort to develop a European alternative to American-made fighter jets has officially collapsed, underscoring the continent's continued reliance on U.S. defense systems despite repeated calls for strategic independence.

The Future Combat Air System (FCAS), a €100 billion ($116 billion) project launched in 2017 by French President Emmanuel Macron and then-German Chancellor Angela Merkel, was intended to produce a sixth-generation fighter aircraft by the 2040s. The initiative aimed to reduce NATO members' dependence on U.S. military hardware while strengthening Europe's domestic defense industrial base—a goal that has grown more urgent amid rising global tensions.

Yet the ambitious collaboration between France's Dassault Aviation and Germany-based Airbus Defence and Space ultimately fractured over disputes about profit-sharing, management structure, and intellectual property rights. German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius acknowledged the failure with resignation: "It was an ambitious, large European project that has now shattered against reality."

The collapse represents a significant setback for proponents of European strategic autonomy and multilateral cooperation on major infrastructure projects. While officials suggest some components—including drone systems and information-sharing networks—may be salvaged for future national programs, the core vision of a fully European combat aircraft has been abandoned. For Macron, who personally championed the project as part of his vision for a more independent Europe, the failure marks a notable blow to his foreign policy legacy.

The episode joins a growing list of stalled European defense initiatives, raising questions about the continent's ability to translate ambitious policy goals into operational reality—particularly when corporate interests and national pride complicate cross-border collaboration.
