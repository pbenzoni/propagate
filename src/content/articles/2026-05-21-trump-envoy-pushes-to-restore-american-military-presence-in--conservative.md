The United States should restore its military footprint in Greenland to counter growing threats from China and Russia in the Arctic, according to US special envoy Jeff Landry. The Louisiana governor, appointed by President Trump, visited the autonomous Danish territory this week to discuss expanding America's strategic presence on the mineral-rich island.

"I think it's time for the US to put its footprint back on Greenland," Landry told reporters during his visit to Nuuk. The Trump administration is exploring options to reopen former military installations that were shuttered after the Cold War, when the US operated 17 bases across the island. Currently, only the Pituffik base in northern Greenland remains as an American military outpost.

The push to expand America's Arctic presence comes amid growing concerns about adversarial nations gaining influence in the strategically vital region. Declassified documents reveal the US has viewed Greenland as essential to national security since 1946, even offering Denmark $100 million to purchase the island. Under a 1951 defense agreement, updated in 2004, Washington already has the right to expand military operations on Greenland with proper notification to Danish and local authorities.

Reports suggest the administration is in talks about establishing three new bases in southern Greenland. Landry emphasized that an expanded US military presence would strengthen security for both America and its allies, dismissing concerns about sovereignty while noting Washington's long history of respecting self-determination "even in places where we have had to go in and liberate." The initiative reflects the administration's commitment to protecting American interests and countering the ambitions of China and Russia in the resource-rich Arctic region.
