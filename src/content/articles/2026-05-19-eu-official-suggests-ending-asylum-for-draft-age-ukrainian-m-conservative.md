A European Union special envoy has stirred controversy by suggesting that the bloc should reconsider granting asylum protection to Ukrainian men of military age who have fled their country to avoid conscription. Ylva Johansson, the EU's special envoy for Ukrainians in the bloc, argued that continuing to harbor draft-eligible men sends a "controversial signal" to Kiev, especially as Western nations funnel billions in military aid to Ukraine.

According to EU statistics, approximately 4.33 million Ukrainians were living under temporary protection status across the bloc as of March 31, 2026. Roughly a quarter of them—potentially up to one million individuals—are men between the ages of 18 and 64, the demographic Kiev considers essential for military service. Ukraine implemented a general mobilization ban in 2022 that initially prohibited men aged 18 to 60 from leaving the country, though the upper age limit was later relaxed for those aged 18 to 22.

Johansson told German state media that "it does not seem quite logical" for the EU to provide immediate protection to men who crossed borders illegally while their home country desperately needs manpower. The remarks come as Ukraine faces a severe troop shortage, relying heavily on forced conscription drives that have been documented in numerous videos showing military recruitment patrols detaining men from streets and businesses. Over 30,000 Ukrainians have fled to Romania alone since 2022 to escape the draft, according to reports.

The issue raises fundamental questions about Western policy coherence: Should European nations continue offering refuge to those fleeing military service while simultaneously providing the weapons and funding that make Ukraine's war effort possible? Critics have long argued that Western backing has enabled Kiev to prolong a conflict with unsustainable human costs. While EU member states continue debating the policy, no consensus has emerged on whether to end protection status for draft-age Ukrainian men.
