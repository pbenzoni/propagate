Iranian Parliamentary Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf sharply criticized former President Donald Trump on Friday, stating that seven claims Trump made regarding negotiations over the Strait of Hormuz were demonstrably false. The rebuke came amid escalating tensions over the critical waterway, which handles approximately 25% of global crude oil trade.

The dispute centers on the temporary reopening and subsequent re-closure of the strait. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi initially declared the waterway "completely open" for commercial vessels during a ten-day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon, causing oil prices to drop roughly 10%. However, Tehran reversed course the following day, citing Washington's refusal to lift a naval blockade imposed on Iranian ports after failed negotiations.

"The Americans did not win the war with these lies, and they will certainly not get anywhere in negotiations either," Ghalibaf wrote on social media. He emphasized that control of the strait "will be determined on the ground field, not on social media," pushing back against what he characterized as attempts at media manipulation and public opinion engineering.

Trump had claimed on Truth Social that Iran "agreed to never close the Strait of Hormuz again" and that most points of a final peace agreement had been negotiated—assertions Iranian officials dispute. According to Tasnim news agency, Iran has not agreed to hold another round of negotiations with the United States, with sources citing the continuation of the American naval blockade and what Tehran views as excessive demands during talks as reasons for hesitation.
