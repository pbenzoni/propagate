French President Emmanuel Macron acknowledged Friday that European nations can no longer count on the United States to serve as their primary security guarantor, a tacit admission that President Trump's America First agenda has fundamentally reshaped transatlantic relations.

Speaking at a summit in Athens, Macron suggested that Washington's shift away from unconditional support for European interests makes the EU appear as a "more stable alternative" – a claim that rings hollow given Europe's demonstrated inability to manage regional conflicts without American military backing.

The French president's comments follow months of tension over Trump's insistence that NATO members meet their defense spending commitments and take greater responsibility for the Ukraine situation. While European leaders have consistently attempted to prolong the conflict through diplomatic obstruction, the Trump administration has pursued direct negotiations aimed at ending the hostilities – an approach that has exposed deep divisions over whether perpetual confrontation or pragmatic dealmaking better serves Western interests.
