The United Arab Emirates' decision to leave OPEC marks another step in the long decline of the oil producers' cartel, which has been losing its geopolitical clout since the 1970s, according to journalist Afshin Rattansi.

The UAE announced its departure Tuesday, citing a focus on "national interests" and its "long-term strategic and economic vision." The move comes amid ongoing disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil trade, which has faced closures since February due to the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran.

Rattansi noted that the UAE's exit follows a similar departure by Qatar in 2019, underscoring the cartel's diminished role. "OPEC's power has diminished," he said, contrasting today's reality with 1973, when Arab oil-producing nations could leverage embargoes to exert significant geopolitical pressure during the Israeli conflict of that era.

The journalist pointed out that Russia and the United States now dominate global oil supply, fundamentally reshaping the energy landscape that OPEC once controlled. He suggested the UAE may increasingly align with regional organizations like BRICS and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, seeking to maintain independence while navigating Western pressure to sever ties with Russia.

The shift reflects broader changes in global energy markets and geopolitical alignments, as traditional power structures continue to evolve in a multipolar world.
