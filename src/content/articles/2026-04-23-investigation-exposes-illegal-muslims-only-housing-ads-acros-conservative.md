An investigation by The Telegraph has revealed that landlords throughout London and southeast England have been posting rental advertisements explicitly restricted to Muslim tenants, in clear violation of Britain's Equality Act of 2010. The illegal listings were found on Facebook and Telegram, featuring language such as "Muslim only," "only for Muslims," and requirements for specific ethnic and linguistic backgrounds including Punjabi and Gujarati speakers.

When contacted by reporters, the landlords involved refused to rent to non-Muslim applicants. One landlord advertising a room for £850 per month simply told investigators to "go away." Under British law, such discrimination based on religion or race can result in civil action and fines up to £7,000, yet enforcement appears lacking.

Reform UK's economic spokesman Robert Jenrick condemned the practice, calling the advertisements "disgusting and anti-British." He emphasized a critical point about equal application of the law: "It goes without saying that there would be a national outrage if the tables were turned. All forms of racism are unacceptable, and no religious group should get a special exemption to discriminate in this way."

While a government spokesperson acknowledged that discrimination in the rental sector is illegal and violators should face consequences, authorities have not announced any concrete enforcement action. The controversy highlights growing concerns about the uneven application of anti-discrimination laws as Britain's Muslim population has reached approximately 4 million, representing 6.5% of the total population, with some London neighborhoods exceeding 40% Muslim residents.
