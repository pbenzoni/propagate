A Jewish educator in Israel says police detained him and forcibly cut a Palestinian flag from his religious head covering—a troubling sign of the country's rightward shift and increasing suppression of peaceful dissent.

Dr. Alex Sinclair, a lecturer at Hebrew University of Jerusalem, had worn a kippah displaying both Israeli and Palestinian flags for two decades as an expression of his complex identity and hope for coexistence. But earlier this week in Modi'in, a fellow café patron reported him to police, claiming the symbol was illegal. When officers arrived, they immediately demanded he remove the kippah. After Sinclair refused to surrender the religiously significant garment, police detained him. Though quickly released, officers initially tried to confiscate the kippah entirely. When Sinclair insisted on its return, a female officer cut out the Palestinian flag before giving it back—despite such symbols being legal under Israeli law.

"It's hard not to say that this is the kind of thing that fascist regimes do," Sinclair wrote on social media, expressing devastation at the direction his country is taking. He has filed a complaint seeking compensation and assurances he can wear the symbol without harassment. The incident reflects a broader pattern: since October 2023, Israeli police powers have expanded dramatically, with numerous reports of officers removing Palestinian flags and symbols. Civil liberties advocates warn these actions represent an alarming erosion of free expression rights.

The crackdown on peaceful symbols comes as the humanitarian crisis in Gaza deepens. Palestinian health authorities report more than 72,000 people killed and over 172,000 injured in Israeli military operations over the past three years—a toll that underscores the urgency of the coexistence Sinclair's defaced kippah represented.
