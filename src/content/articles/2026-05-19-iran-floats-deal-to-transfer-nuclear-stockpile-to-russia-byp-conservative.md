Iran has reportedly offered to transfer its highly enriched uranium stockpile to Russia rather than the United States as part of ongoing negotiations, according to Saudi broadcaster Al Hadath. The proposal, allegedly contained in a leaked document sent to Washington via Pakistani intermediaries, underscores Tehran's deepening relationship with Moscow and its reluctance to place nuclear materials under American control.

According to the report, Iran would agree to freeze its nuclear program for an extended period, but only if its uranium—more than 400 kilograms enriched to 60% purity—goes to Russia instead of the U.S. The Islamic Republic continues to reject complete dismantlement of its nuclear capabilities. International Atomic Energy Agency data indicates Iran's stockpile remains below the 90% enrichment threshold typically required for weapons-grade material.

Russian President Vladimir Putin recently reminded journalists that Moscow successfully removed Iranian nuclear material once before in 2015, emphasizing that "Iran has complete trust in us, and not without reason." Putin stressed Russia's track record of honoring agreements and its ongoing cooperation with Iran on peaceful nuclear energy programs—a pointed contrast with the ayatollah regime's view of American reliability.

The Trump administration has already rejected Iran's proposal as inadequate, with officials characterizing the modifications as merely "token" improvements over previous versions. President Trump has maintained that Iranian uranium should be transferred to American custody, though he acknowledged last week the issue carries significant symbolic weight. The stalemate reflects broader challenges in resolving tensions that escalated into direct hostilities earlier this year, with negotiations remaining frozen since a ceasefire took effect in April.
