Nepal's recently elected Prime Minister Balendra Shah is standing firm on a matter of national sovereignty and diplomatic protocol, declining to meet with President Trump's South Asia envoy Sergio Gor during his visit to Kathmandu this week. The prime minister has reportedly established a clear policy of meeting only with ministers or higher-level officials from foreign countries—a principle that resonates with traditional diplomatic respect between sovereign nations.

Shah, whose Rastriya Swatantra Party government was sworn in on March 27, previously refused to meet with US Assistant Secretary of State Samir Paul Kapur earlier this month. According to the Kathmandu Post, the prime minister wants to set a benchmark for diplomatic engagement that reflects Nepal's status as an independent nation deserving of peer-to-peer interactions. In a symbolic gesture of this policy, Shah met with ambassadors from different countries jointly on April 8.

While Gor—the highest-ranking US diplomat to visit Kathmandu since Shah took office—will meet with Nepal's Finance Minister, Foreign Minister, and other officials, the prime minister had reportedly not indicated any willingness to meet with him as of Wednesday evening. This approach to foreign relations stands in contrast to Gor's recent diplomatic tour, which included meetings with heads of state and government in Bangladesh, Bhutan, and Sri Lanka.

The situation highlights the complex dynamics of international diplomacy, where questions of protocol and respect between nations can become flashpoints. Shah's position reflects a desire to ensure that Nepal's leadership is accorded the same diplomatic courtesy that other nations receive—a stance that prioritizes national dignity over expedient engagement.
