Bulgaria is staring down European Union sanctions for excessive budget deficits just months after joining the eurozone, with Prime Minister Rumen Radev pointing fingers at his pro-EU predecessors for what he calls financial manipulation to secure entry into the currency bloc.

Speaking at a cabinet meeting in Sofia, Radev—widely viewed as an EU skeptic—said the European Commission will formally launch an "excessive deficit procedure" against Bulgaria on June 3. The country's budget deficit hit 3.5% last year, breaching the EU's 3% ceiling, and is projected to climb to 4.1% this year and 4.3% in 2027. If Bulgaria fails to bring spending under control, the EU can freeze funding and impose fines of up to 0.05% of GDP every six months.

Radev laid the blame squarely on the previous center-right, pro-EU government led by Zhelyazkov, which collapsed in December 2025 amid mass anti-corruption protests. "They lied to push Bulgaria into the euro… The bubble has burst," Radev declared, accusing the former administration of "negligence, incompetence, voluntarism, populism, and financial misconduct." Critics had warned that the Zhelyazkov coalition projected unrealistic revenue growth and manipulated state-controlled prices to artificially lower inflation—a key criterion for eurozone entry.

A 2025 Politico report highlighted a sudden 82.8% cut in state-set hospital fees in April of that year, a move that conveniently helped Bulgaria's 12-month average inflation fall below the threshold. An unnamed former official told the outlet that "the only reason Bulgaria has qualified is… due to state-administered prices." The previous government also slashed rail fares by over 9% to massage the inflation numbers.

Radev, who has called for more pragmatic ties with Russia and opposed military aid to Ukraine, had sought a public referendum on eurozone membership—arguing that such a monumental decision should be made by the people, not elites in Brussels or Sofia. Parliament blocked his request, with opponents accusing him of trying to sabotage the process. Radev countered that Bulgarian citizens were being ignored by an elite "marching toward the eurozone" and that "the representatives of the people denied the people their right to choose."
