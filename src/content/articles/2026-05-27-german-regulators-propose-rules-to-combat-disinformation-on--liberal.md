German media regulators are developing a proposal that would require social media platforms to adjust their algorithms to give greater visibility to established news organizations, according to documents reviewed by Apollo News. The draft "Digital Media State Treaty" aims to counter what regulators describe as the spread of misleading and polarizing content on platforms including X and TikTok.

Under the proposed framework, platforms would be required to prioritize content based on journalistic standards, timeliness, and public interest value. The regulation specifically mentions Germany's public broadcasters ARD and ZDF as examples of outlets that would receive enhanced visibility. Determinations about content quality and public value would be made by media regulators appointed through state parliamentary processes.

Thorsten Schmiege, who chairs the Conference of Directors of the State Media Authorities, told the Frankfurter Rundschau that the changes are necessary because content moderation standards deteriorated after Elon Musk acquired X in 2022. The proposal reflects ongoing tensions between European regulators and American tech platforms over content moderation practices.

The draft treaty has drawn criticism from U.S. officials, who view it as foreign interference with American companies. Under Secretary of State Sarah Rogers suggested the State Department may respond to the proposal, though she did not specify what form that response might take. The initiative comes as European authorities continue to enforce digital regulations, including a recent €120 million fine against X under the EU's Digital Services Act for issues related to its verification system.
