The Department of Justice announced Friday it will restore multiple proven methods for carrying out federal capital punishment, including firing squads, electrocution, and gas asphyxiation. The decision marks a return to law-and-order principles after the previous administration's pause on federal executions.

The DOJ is readopting lethal injection protocols used during President Trump's first term while expanding options to include additional execution methods. The department also announced it will streamline internal processes to expedite death penalty cases once convicted criminals have exhausted all appeals.

This policy shift fulfills President Trump's commitment to "vigorously pursue" the death penalty to protect Americans from "violent rapists, murderers, and monsters." The move reverses Joe Biden's approach, which included commuting the sentences of 37 federal death row inmates to life without parole in December 2024, leaving only three federal inmates facing execution.

According to the Associated Press, this represents the first time the federal government has explicitly authorized firing squads, though a 2020 rule already permitted using any execution method legal in the sentencing state.
