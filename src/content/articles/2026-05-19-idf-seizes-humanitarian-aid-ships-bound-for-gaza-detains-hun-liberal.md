Israeli military forces have intercepted and seized at least 39 of 51 vessels from the Global Sumud Flotilla, which was carrying humanitarian aid intended for Gaza's displaced population. The flotilla, consisting of yachts with more than 420 activists from 39 countries, had departed from Marmaris, Turkey, with the stated mission of challenging Israel's naval blockade of the Palestinian territory.

The interception occurred approximately 250 nautical miles from Gaza, off the coast of Cyprus, in international waters. Among those detained was Dr. Margaret Connolly, sister of Irish President Catherine Connolly. Organizers described the seizure as "illegal and violent," stating that Israeli forces "abducted our volunteers" in what they characterized as an act of aggression against a peaceful humanitarian mission.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu defended the operation, claiming it thwarted what he called "a malicious plan" to break the isolation imposed on Hamas. However, the interception has drawn sharp international criticism. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan condemned the action as "piracy and banditry," demanding the immediate release of detainees, including more than 40 Turkish citizens. Cyprus confirmed that Israel had not informed them of the operation beforehand.

Nearly 2 million Palestinians in Gaza have been displaced since the October 2023 outbreak of conflict. While Israel disputes claims that it is withholding humanitarian supplies, aid organizations and international observers have documented severe shortages of food, medicine, and basic necessities throughout the territory. This is not the first such interception; Israel detained flotilla participants including climate activist Greta Thunberg in a similar operation in October 2025.
