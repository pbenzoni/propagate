Spain is pressuring the European Union to actively resist U.S. sanctions designed to protect Israel from what many conservatives view as a politicized International Criminal Court prosecution. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez called on Brussels to "activate the Blocking Statute" to shield the ICC from American pressure over its war crimes case against Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The ICC issued arrest warrants in 2024 against Netanyahu and former Defense Minister Yoav Gallant over alleged actions by the Israel Defense Forces in Gaza. The court's jurisdiction is recognized by 125 countries, but notably, neither the United States nor Israel are signatories to the Rome Statute that created the tribunal. President Trump imposed sanctions on the ICC in February 2025, condemning what he described as "illegitimate and baseless actions targeting America and our close ally Israel."

Spain has emerged as one of the European Union's most aggressive critics of Israel's military operations. Sanchez has accused Netanyahu of showing "contempt for life and international law" and called Israeli defensive strikes "intolerable." Madrid has also urged the EU to suspend its association agreement with Israel and refused to allow the United States to use joint military bases for operations related to the conflict with Iran.

The Spanish prime minister's defiant stance has escalated tensions with Washington, with Trump threatening to suspend trade with Spain over its refusal to support allied operations. Sanchez responded by stating Spain would "not be complicit in something that is bad for the world simply out of fear of reprisals," signaling a willingness to break with traditional Western security partnerships over the issue.
