Slovenia's newly installed Prime Minister Janez Janša has lifted his country's arms embargo on Israel and reversed other pro-Palestinian policies, just weeks after taking office amid allegations that an Israeli private intelligence firm interfered in the nation's recent election.

The Slovenian Defense Ministry announced Thursday that the government would overturn the arms export ban and lift entry restrictions on senior Israeli officials, including Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The move reverses policies enacted by former Prime Minister Robert Golob, who had recognized Palestinian statehood, imposed the arms embargo, and characterized Israel's military campaign in Gaza as "genocide."

According to Slovenia's Intelligence and Security Agency (SOVA), the Israeli firm Black Cube—founded by former intelligence operatives—released covertly recorded videos showing alleged corruption discussions among associates of Golob's Svoboda party. The videos surfaced just months before parliamentary elections, following a December meeting between Janša and Black Cube executives. While Golob's party narrowly won the popular vote, his coalition lost its majority and couldn't form a government, enabling Janša to build a right-wing coalition.

SOVA concluded that Black Cube "deliberately attempted to influence democratic elections" and that the interference was "most likely commissioned from within Slovenia," though it stopped short of directly accusing Janša. Black Cube has acknowledged filming the videos. Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar praised Janša's policy reversal, calling him "a true friend of Israel" and commending his decision to lift what Sa'ar termed "distorted anti-Israeli measures."
