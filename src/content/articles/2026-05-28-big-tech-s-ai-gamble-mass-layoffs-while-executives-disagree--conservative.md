The leaders of two major artificial intelligence companies are offering starkly different predictions about AI's impact on American workers, even as Big Tech firms continue slashing jobs by the tens of thousands while ramping up AI spending.

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman admitted Tuesday he was "wrong" about earlier predictions of widespread white-collar job elimination, telling an Australian banking executive he's "delighted" that entry-level office jobs haven't disappeared as quickly as anticipated. Altman claimed many positions retain an "irreplaceable human part"—a notable retreat from Silicon Valley's earlier hype about AI capabilities.

But Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah struck a far different tone at the Vatican this week, warning of "a real possibility that AI will displace human labor at very large scale." Olah called supporting displaced workers "a moral imperative of historic proportions" and noted AI development remains concentrated among wealthy nations with no plan to share benefits globally. Pope Leo XIV has repeatedly cautioned that AI threatens "human dignity, justice, and labor."

The mixed messaging comes as Big Tech hemorrhages American jobs. Meta and Microsoft are reportedly preparing to cut up to 16,000 employees combined due to soaring AI costs, while Amazon has already announced roughly 30,000 layoffs across two rounds tied to AI restructuring. Oracle has reportedly eliminated around 30,000 positions worldwide.

March polling showed nearly 80% of Americans fear the federal government has no plan to protect workers from AI-related job displacement. Senator Bernie Sanders predicted last year that AI could eliminate nearly 100 million American jobs across all sectors within the next decade—a forecast that appears increasingly plausible as corporations prioritize automation over their existing workforce.
