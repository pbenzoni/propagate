A stunning new poll shows German voters are turning their backs on the political establishment in record numbers. The right-wing Alternative for Germany (AfD) has surged to an unprecedented 28% support, overtaking the ruling coalition led by Chancellor Friedrich Merz, according to a Bild/INSA survey published Saturday.

Merz's Christian Democratic bloc (CDU/CSU) has fallen to just 24% approval, while his coalition partner, the Social Democrats (SPD), languishes at 14%. The numbers reveal a crisis of confidence in Germany's traditional ruling class, with about 11% of voters supporting parties that wouldn't even clear the threshold to enter parliament—a sign of widespread frustration with available options.

The political upheaval comes as Germans grapple with economic stagnation under Merz's leadership. Germany's economy grew by a meager 0.3% in 2025 and suffered recessions in both 2023 and 2024. A separate YouGov poll found that 79% of Germans are dissatisfied with their government's performance—a devastating indictment of establishment policies.

Merz himself has become Europe's most unpopular leader, with a 76% disapproval rating according to Morning Consult. Even among his own CDU/CSU voters, only 34% express satisfaction with the coalition's performance. The poll of 1,203 respondents, conducted April 20-24, suggests that mainstream parties would need to form unprecedented alliances to govern—assuming they continue refusing to work with the AfD, despite its clear electoral mandate from German voters.
