Omar Artan, a trailblazing referee who made history as the first Somali national to officiate at a FIFA World Cup finals tournament, was turned away at Miami International Airport this week despite holding a valid U.S. visa. The denial, widely believed to stem from the Trump administration's sweeping immigration restrictions targeting Somali nationals, forced Artan to return to Istanbul, where he is based.

Ciise Aden Abshir, a senior adviser to Somalia's Ministry of Youth and Sports, condemned the decision as harmful not only to Artan personally but to the integrity of international sport itself. "Omar Artan is among Africa's most respected referees and deserves the support of the entire football community," Abshir said. "Denying him entry to the United States and preventing him from officiating scheduled matches harms not only him personally but also undermines football's commitment to fairness, merit, and the spirit of fair play."

The incident is part of a broader pattern of immigration enforcement affecting the 2026 World Cup, co-hosted by the U.S., Mexico, and Canada. In a separate incident, Senegal's national team—another country targeted by Trump's travel restrictions—was subjected to extensive on-tarmac searches immediately upon arrival, with players patted down and screened with metal detectors in footage that circulated widely online.

The disruptions extend beyond African nations. Scottish fans discovered their previously approved travel authorizations suddenly changed to "pending" or "not authorized" just days before the tournament, affecting dozens who had planned trips to see their national team compete for the first time in nearly three decades. Critics argue these policy shifts undermine both the spirit of international sport and America's commitment to welcoming the world.
