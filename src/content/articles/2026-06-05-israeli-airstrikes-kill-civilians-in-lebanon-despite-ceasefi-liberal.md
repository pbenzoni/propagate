Israeli strikes in southern and eastern Lebanon killed at least eight people and wounded 15 others on Thursday, according to Lebanon's Health Ministry, less than 24 hours after both countries reportedly agreed to a U.S.-mediated ceasefire proposal in Washington.

The continued violence underscores the fragility of diplomatic efforts to end hostilities that have devastated Lebanese communities. Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem rejected the Washington-backed arrangement, calling it a "shameless" attempt to legitimize Israeli military occupation rather than achieve genuine peace. The proposed deal would require Hezbollah fighters to withdraw from southern Lebanon while allowing Israeli forces to remain—a framework Qassem characterized as rewarding occupation.

The ceasefire proposal, announced by the State Department on Wednesday, calls for Hezbollah to cease fire and evacuate fighters from the South Litani region, while establishing "pilot zones" where Lebanese Armed Forces would assume exclusive control. However, Hezbollah—which was not included as a party in the Washington negotiations—argues that any arrangement permitting Israeli troops to remain on Lebanese soil while demanding their unilateral withdrawal amounts to coerced capitulation.

The latest strikes targeted the towns of Sohmor, Masaken, and Arab Al-Jalil, adding to weeks of Israeli military operations in Lebanon. President Trump claimed to have made "progress" on the issue, though his assertion that he had communicated with Hezbollah drew skepticism. The ongoing conflict has displaced thousands of Lebanese civilians and raised urgent humanitarian concerns about the protection of non-combatants in active conflict zones.
