President Donald Trump announced Tuesday that Bill Pulte, a housing finance executive with no national security background, will serve as acting Director of National Intelligence following Tulsi Gabbard's resignation.

Pulte, who currently heads the Federal Housing Finance Agency and chairs mortgage giants Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, will retain those positions while coordinating the work of the nation's 18 intelligence agencies and preparing the president's daily intelligence briefing. Trump praised Pulte as having "deep experience managing the most sensitive matters in America," though his career has been in residential construction and housing finance, not national security.

The appointment raises questions about qualifications and potential conflicts of interest. Pulte is considered a Trump loyalist, having led mortgage fraud investigations targeting several Democrats whom the president has accused of unfairly pursuing him legally—including New York Attorney General Letitia James, California Representative Eric Swalwell, Senator Adam Schiff, and Fulton County District Attorney Fani Willis. He also referred Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook to the Justice Department over alleged mortgage fraud during Trump's clash with then-Fed Chairman Jerome Powell.

Gabbard stepped down last month citing her husband's battle with a rare form of bone cancer. Her resignation came amid reports she had been sidelined by administration officials during planning for military operations in Venezuela and Iran—conflicts the former congresswoman had vocally opposed. Before leaving, Gabbard told reporters she was investigating over 120 U.S.-funded biological laboratories worldwide, including more than 40 in Ukraine, examining whether any conducted dangerous gain-of-function research.
