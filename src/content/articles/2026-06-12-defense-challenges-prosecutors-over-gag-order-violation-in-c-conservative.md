The legal team defending Tyler Robinson, accused of murdering conservative activist and Turning Point USA co-founder Charlie Kirk, is moving to hold state prosecutors in contempt of court for allegedly violating a gag order imposed in the high-profile case.

The controversy centers on disputed ballistics evidence that emerged in March when defense attorneys revealed forensic findings suggesting a bullet fragment recovered from Kirk's body could not be conclusively matched to the rifle allegedly used in the September 2025 killing. That disclosure sparked widespread media attention and raised questions about the prosecution's case. In April, prosecutors responded by speaking to reporters, characterizing the ATF findings as "inconclusive" rather than an outright non-match—comments Robinson's defense now argues constituted an improper "media tour" in violation of court-imposed restrictions on public discussion of the case.

Kirk, a prominent conservative voice and podcaster, was fatally shot in the neck during an event at a Utah college campus. Robinson was arrested two days later after investigators connected him to a modified Mauser Model 98 rifle found near the scene. Prosecutors cite DNA evidence on the weapon's trigger matching Robinson, as well as text messages allegedly exchanged with his transgender lover in which he purportedly confessed to the killing.

In a separate motion, Robinson's attorneys are seeking to delay proceedings while appealing a judge's decision to allow cameras in the courtroom, arguing that televised coverage of the upcoming preliminary hearing could compromise the ability to seat an impartial jury. The preliminary hearing is scheduled for July 6-10, when prosecutors will seek to advance the case toward trial. Prosecutors have indicated they intend to pursue the death penalty if Robinson is convicted. Robinson has not yet entered a plea.
