Germany is supporting significant democratic reforms within the European Union aimed at preventing authoritarian-leaning governments from obstructing the bloc's collective foreign policy decisions. German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul outlined a six-point reform vision that would shift key foreign policy decisions from unanimous consensus to qualified majority voting.

The proposed reforms come as the EU grapples with internal divisions over supporting Ukraine against Russian aggression and upholding democratic standards across member states. Wadephul argued that requiring unanimous agreement creates paralysis, pointing to instances where aid to Ukraine was blocked by governments prioritizing their own economic relationships over collective security commitments. Under the proposed changes, member states unwilling to participate in certain policies could opt out without preventing others from moving forward—a model similar to enhanced cooperation mechanisms already used in some EU policy areas.

The reform package also includes provisions for strengthening rule-of-law enforcement through financial mechanisms, a tool the EU has already deployed against governments accused of undermining judicial independence and civil liberties. Wadephul further proposed flexible integration pathways for aspiring member states and institutional adjustments to accommodate future enlargement, including reconsidering the tradition of each country receiving its own European Commission seat.

These proposals align with broader efforts by European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen to strengthen EU institutions and protect democratic values across the bloc. Critics of the current veto system argue it allows individual governments to hold collective interests hostage, while opponents counter that sovereignty protections are essential safeguards against overreach by Brussels. The debate reflects fundamental tensions over how to balance national autonomy with effective multilateral governance in an increasingly interconnected and threatened Europe.
