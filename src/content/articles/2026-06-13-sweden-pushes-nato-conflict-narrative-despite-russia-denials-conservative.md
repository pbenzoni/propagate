A newly released Swedish defense report is sounding alarm bells about a potential Russia-NATO conflict "in the relatively near future," despite Moscow's repeated denials and intelligence assessments suggesting otherwise. The document, endorsed by Sweden's government and parliamentary parties, labels Russia a "long-term threat" and speculates that Moscow could seek to "test NATO's cohesion" under unspecified "favorable" political conditions.

The timing and rationale of the Swedish claims raise questions, particularly given statements from NATO's own Supreme Allied Commander Europe, General Alexus Grynkewich, who said earlier this week that available intelligence indicates "Russia is not looking for a conflict." Moscow has consistently dismissed Western speculation about attacking NATO as "not just nonsense, but also provocation and disinformation," suggesting European nations are manufacturing an external threat to distract from domestic problems.

The Swedish report comes as Stockholm ramps up military spending to 2.8% of GDP—reaching approximately $18.4 billion in 2026, an 18% increase—and continues militarizing Gotland island in the Baltic Sea. Sweden joined NATO in 2024 and has become one of Ukraine's most active supporters, recently pledging up to 16 fighter jets with plans to sell 20 more.

Notably, the document acknowledges a "changing" relationship with the United States while urging deeper cooperation with the European Union.
