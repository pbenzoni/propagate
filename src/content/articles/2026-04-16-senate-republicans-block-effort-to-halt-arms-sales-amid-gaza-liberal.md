The U.S. Senate voted Wednesday to reject two resolutions that would have blocked arms sales to Israel, despite receiving support from approximately 75% of Senate Democrats. The measures, introduced by Senator Bernie Sanders (I-VT), sought to halt a $295 million sale of bulldozers and a $151.8 million sale of munitions.

The resolutions failed by votes of 59-40 and 63-36, with Senate Republicans voting nearly unanimously against the measures. The votes reflect a growing divide within Congress over U.S. military assistance to Israel as the humanitarian crisis in Gaza continues. More than 72,000 people have been killed and over 172,000 injured in Gaza over the past three years, according to Palestinian health authorities.

Senator Sanders argued the sales violated U.S. law governing foreign assistance and arms exports, citing the use of American-supplied weapons in strikes on Gaza and Lebanon, as well as the deployment of bulldozers in demolishing Palestinian and Lebanese homes. Following the vote, Sanders noted that "Democrats are beginning to listen to the average American who is sick and tired of spending billions of dollars to support Netanyahu's horrific wars when people in this country can't afford housing or health care."

Republican lawmakers opposed the resolutions, with Senate Foreign Relations Committee Chairman Jim Risch warning that blocking weapons deliveries could "send the message that the US is prepared to leave our ally Israel vulnerable." The vote marks a notable shift from previous years, when arms sales to Israel enjoyed strong bipartisan support. Israel's military operations have also extended into Lebanon, where the government in Beirut reports more than 2,100 civilian deaths.
