Armed criminals took approximately 25 people hostage during a brazen bank robbery in Naples, Italy on Thursday, highlighting the ongoing public safety crisis in the southern Italian city.

The attack unfolded shortly before noon when robbers stormed a Credit Agricole branch in the Vomero district, seizing customers and staff inside. Law enforcement quickly surrounded the building and sealed off the area as the suspects barricaded themselves inside, creating a dangerous standoff in one of the city's busiest neighborhoods.

After approximately two hours, police launched a coordinated raid with firefighters breaking through a reinforced window to allow officers to enter the building. All hostages were successfully freed, with at least six people treated for shock but no serious injuries reported. It remains unclear whether any arrests were made or if the suspects managed to escape.

Bank robberies involving hostage-taking have become a disturbing pattern in Naples, with criminals typically striking during business hours and threatening innocent workers and customers. In one incident last May, an armed robber held employees and clients hostage for around 40 minutes before escaping with more than $14,000.
