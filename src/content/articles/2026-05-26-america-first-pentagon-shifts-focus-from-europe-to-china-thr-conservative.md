The United States is reducing its military commitments to NATO as Pentagon strategists prioritize the growing threat from China, according to a report from Der Spiegel. At a confidential NATO meeting in Brussels last week, senior Pentagon adviser Alexander Velez-Green informed European allies that America will significantly scale back the forces and equipment it makes available to the alliance.

The cuts are substantial: fighter jet contributions will drop by one-third, while strategic bombers and warships will see major reductions. NATO will receive no American submarines or drones going forward, forcing European members to fill these capability gaps themselves. The announcement reportedly caught European officials off guard, who had anticipated more gradual adjustments.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio made clear at a meeting in Sweden that "there are going to be eventually less US troops in Europe than there have historically been." He explained that America faces obligations across multiple theaters—the Indo-Pacific, Middle East, and Western Hemisphere—and must position forces to handle "a two-front conflict" if necessary. With more than 80,000 US troops currently stationed in Europe, a presence dating back to World War II, Pentagon sources indicate a strategic pivot toward countering China in the Indo-Pacific region.

The shift comes as the administration has made clear that troop deployments will reflect which allies support American priorities. After withdrawing 5,000 troops from Germany, President Trump announced an additional 5,000 would deploy to Poland, citing the "successful election" of conservative President Karol Nawrocki. European NATO members now face a July deadline at the alliance's summit in Turkey to present plans for compensating for reduced American contributions.
