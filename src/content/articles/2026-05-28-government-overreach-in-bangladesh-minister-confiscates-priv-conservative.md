A Bangladeshi government minister has intervened to halt a family's private religious observance, confiscating their livestock after it gained internet notoriety for supposedly resembling President Donald Trump.

Interior Minister Salahuddin Ahmed ordered officials to stop a lawful sacrifice during the Muslim festival of Eid al-Adha, refund the buyer who had purchased the animal, and relocate the nearly 700-kg albino buffalo. The animal, owned by Ziauddin Mridha and his family, had become a social media sensation due to its cream-colored forelock, which some claimed resembled the former president's distinctive hairstyle.

The heavy-handed government action raises serious concerns about property rights and religious liberty. The buffalo had already been legally sold for ritual slaughter when authorities stepped in, effectively nullifying a private transaction. Moniruzzaman, who purchased the buffalo for the religious ceremony, was told he would be compensated or given another animal—but the fundamental principle of private property and religious practice had already been violated.

Footage showed the animal being led away with ropes and loaded onto a truck as disappointed residents looked on. Many had traveled with their families hoping to see the rare albino buffalo, only to find government officials had already removed it.
