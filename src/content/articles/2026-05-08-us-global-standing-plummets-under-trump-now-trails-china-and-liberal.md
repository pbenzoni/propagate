America's standing on the world stage has deteriorated sharply over the past two years, with the country now ranking below both China and Russia in a global perception survey, according to new research from the Alliance of Democracies Foundation.

The Denmark-based nonprofit's Democracy Perception Index shows the US has fallen to a rating of -16%, down dramatically from +22% just two years ago. The current score places America behind China (+7%) and Russia (-11%) in global perception. The survey, which polled more than 94,000 respondents across 98 nations between March and April, reflects growing international concern about the direction of US foreign policy.

Anders Fogh Rasmussen, former NATO Secretary General and founder of the Alliance of Democracies Foundation, described the findings as "saddening but not shocking." He pointed to multiple factors contributing to the decline, including aggressive trade policies, public threats against Greenland—a territory of NATO ally Denmark—and strained relationships with European partners. The transatlantic alliance, long considered a cornerstone of democratic cooperation, has faced unprecedented tensions.

The survey comes amid broader international unrest, including the US-backed Israeli military operations against Iran, which have contributed to global oil shortages particularly affecting European nations. Nordic countries—Sweden, Norway, and Denmark—topped the index, while Ukraine ranked near the bottom at 95th place with a -23% rating. The findings underscore growing questions about American leadership and democratic values on the international stage at a critical moment for global cooperation.
