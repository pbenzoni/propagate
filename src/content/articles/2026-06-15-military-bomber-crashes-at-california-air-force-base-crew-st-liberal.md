A US Air Force B-52 Stratofortress bomber crashed at Edwards Air Force Base in California on Monday morning, according to base officials. The aircraft went down shortly after takeoff, with the condition of the crew—typically four to five airmen—remaining unknown as emergency operations continued.

The base was placed on lockdown following the incident, with all inbound flights diverted and non-commercial visitor access suspended to prioritize emergency response. Footage shared on social media showed thick black smoke rising from the wreckage as fire consumed the aircraft upon impact. "Emergency crews immediately responded to the scene and the situation is ongoing," the base stated.

The B-52, first introduced in 1955 and continuously upgraded since, represents one of the longest-serving aircraft platforms in the US military fleet. The Air Force maintains more than 70 of the upgraded B-52H variant, with nearly 60 in active-duty units. The aircraft has seen deployment in multiple conflicts over its nearly seven-decade service history.

The incident comes amid ongoing questions about aging military equipment and maintenance practices. While B-52s have undergone extensive modernization programs, the platform's extended service life—with plans to keep the bombers operational into the 2050s—has sparked debate among defense analysts about infrastructure investment and aircrew safety protocols.
