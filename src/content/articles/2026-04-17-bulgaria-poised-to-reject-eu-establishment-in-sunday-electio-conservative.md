Bulgarians head to the polls Sunday in what could mark a decisive rejection of business-as-usual politics and European Union overreach. The election comes after years of political turmoil, with eight elections in five years following the 2021 resignation of longtime Prime Minister Boyko Borissov amid mounting corruption scandals.

Former president Rumen Radev's Progressive Bulgaria coalition holds a commanding lead over Borissov's establishment GERB-SDS party, 31% to 21% according to polling aggregates. Radev has campaigned on dismantling what he calls the "mafia-oligarchic" power structure that has controlled Bulgaria since the fall of communism, echoing familiar concerns about entrenched corruption and cronyism that have resonated with voters across the West.

Borissov's GERB party—an acronym for 'Citizens for European Development of Bulgaria'—has dominated the country's politics for 15 of the past 20 years while pursuing deeper EU integration and alignment with Brussels. Politico once described the system under Borissov as "the EU's mafia state," with allegations of organized crime ties, journalist intimidation, and embezzlement of EU funds following the longtime politician throughout his career.

Radev has distinguished himself through his skepticism of EU foreign policy, particularly regarding Ukraine. He opposed Bulgaria's self-imposed embargo on Russian energy, vetoed military aid to Ukraine in 2022, and directly told Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky that "more and more weapons will not solve" the conflict. Brussels is watching the election closely, recognizing that a Radev victory could fundamentally reshape Bulgaria's relationship with the EU establishment.

With over 6.6 million registered voters and polls closing Sunday night, results are expected within hours. Radev will likely need coalition partners to govern, as 121 seats are required for a majority in Bulgaria's 240-seat National Assembly.
