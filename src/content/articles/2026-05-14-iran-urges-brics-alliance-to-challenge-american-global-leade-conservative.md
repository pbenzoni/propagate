Iranian Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi called on BRICS member nations to unite against what he characterized as American "superiority and impunity" during a gathering of the economic bloc's foreign ministers in New Delhi on Thursday.

Speaking to representatives from Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa and other member states, Araghchi claimed Iran has faced U.S.-Israeli military actions twice in the past year. He argued that many BRICS countries have experienced what he termed "pressure and coercion" from Washington, framing American foreign policy as a "common and dangerous threat" to the group.

The Iranian diplomat advocated for BRICS to position itself as a counterweight to Western influence in shaping global affairs, promoting what he called a "fairer global order" with increased prominence for developing nations. Araghchi warned that established powers are "desperately" attempting to maintain their international standing as the world shifts toward multiple centers of influence rather than U.S.-led unipolarity.

While BRICS has historically focused on economic cooperation, recent discussions have touched on potential security collaboration. Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva recently suggested that BRICS nations should reduce dependence on foreign arms suppliers, raising questions about the bloc's future direction and its relationship with American global interests.
