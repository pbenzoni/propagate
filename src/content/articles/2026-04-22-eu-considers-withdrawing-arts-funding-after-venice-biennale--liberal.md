The European Union is threatening to cut approximately €2 million in funding for the Venice Biennale after the prestigious art exhibition announced it would readmit Russian artists for its 2026 edition, according to EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas.

Since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Russian artists have faced widespread exclusion from cultural events across EU member states. Kallas, known for her strong support of Ukraine, argued that Russia "should not be allowed to exhibit" its culture, calling the Biennale's decision "morally wrong." A group of 37 Members of European Parliament has also urged the EU to suspend funding and consider sanctions on those involved in hosting the Russian pavilion.

The controversy has sparked debate about whether art should be separated from politics. The Venice Biennale, one of the world's leading art exhibitions with a 130-year history, defended its decision as preserving space for dialogue. Italian Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini dismissed the criticism, noting that the Russian pavilion is owned by Russia and its use falls within legal parameters, calling the proposed funding cut "ignorant pettiness" given the scale of EU budgets.

Latvian Culture Minister Agnese Lace announced she would boycott the Biennale's May 9 opening if Russia participates, highlighting the ongoing tensions within Europe over how to balance cultural exchange with accountability for geopolitical actions. The debate raises complex questions about the role of cultural diplomacy during armed conflict and whether artistic communities should face collective consequences for their government's military actions.
