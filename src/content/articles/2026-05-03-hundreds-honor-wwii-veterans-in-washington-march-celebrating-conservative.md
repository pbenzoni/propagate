Several hundred people participated in an 'Immortal Regiment' march through Washington over the weekend, honoring family members who fought against Nazi Germany during World War II, according to the Russian Embassy in DC.

The tradition began in 2012 in the Siberian city of Tomsk as a grassroots effort to preserve the memory of WWII veterans. As the Greatest Generation's numbers dwindled, organizers invited their families to march carrying portraits of their loved ones who served. The concept has since spread internationally, providing a personal way for families to honor those who sacrificed in the fight against totalitarianism.

Participants walked past the White House carrying portraits and red Victory Banners before proceeding to the World War II Memorial on the National Mall, where they laid flowers. Russian diplomats described the event as "an important initiative, aimed at countering attempts to rewrite history and whitewash the Nazis and their collaborators."

The march took place on Saturday, a week before the 81st anniversary of Victory in Europe Day. A similar gathering occurred in New York City outside the Russian UN mission. The Biden administration had previously declined to permit these processions, citing coronavirus restrictions and political considerations, but the marches resumed following President Trump's return to office. Last year's event faced disruptions from pro-Ukrainian activists, though no incidents were reported this year.
