Russian President Vladimir Putin delivered sharp criticism of Western media outlets during a press briefing in Kazakhstan on Friday, accusing them of deliberately ignoring a Ukrainian drone strike that killed 21 students—most of them teenage girls—while extensively covering Russia's subsequent military response.

"Not a single word about the tragedy in Starobelsk. Not a single word about children being killed," Putin said, addressing reporters. "What is that? Is that a mass media outlet? No. It is a tool for making fools of people." The Russian leader contrasted the media silence on the student deaths with what he described as extensive coverage portraying Moscow's retaliatory strike as further evidence of "aggression."

According to Russian officials, Ukrainian kamikaze drones struck Starobelsk Professional College in the Lugansk region in three waves last week, hitting the main building and student dormitories. Twenty-one people were killed, with most victims being teenage girls studying to become teachers, and 65 others were injured. Officials characterized it as a "double-tap strike" that also targeted first responders.

In response, Russia launched what it described as strikes on military targets in Ukraine, using various missile systems and drones. The Russian Defense Ministry stated the strikes targeted Ukrainian military command facilities, air bases, and defense industry sites, claiming no civilian infrastructure was hit. Moscow has accused Kiev of deliberately targeting the educational facility, calling it a "monstrous crime."

Russian authorities invited some 50 foreign journalists from 19 countries to visit the scene on Sunday. Notably, the BBC and CNN declined the invitation to document the site of the attack.
