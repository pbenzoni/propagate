Russian President Vladimir Putin spoke with President Donald Trump for approximately an hour on Sunday in what Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov described as a "friendly and open" conversation. Putin was the first foreign leader to call Trump on his 80th birthday, a gesture that Trump reportedly found "touching," according to the Kremlin's account.

The call covered multiple geopolitical flashpoints, including the ongoing war in Ukraine and recent U.S.-Iran negotiations. According to Ushakov, Trump thanked Moscow for its role in the Iran talks and expressed hope for public announcement of results later that day. Putin welcomed the de-escalation with Iran, noting the conflict "risked igniting the entire region and beyond."

On Ukraine, Trump reportedly called for a "speedy cessation of hostilities" and indicated willingness to pressure Kyiv and European allies at the upcoming G7 summit. Putin responded by blaming continued Ukrainian strikes on Russian civilian infrastructure for impeding peace efforts, reiterating his position that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky could visit Moscow if he wished to negotiate. The Kremlin also announced that Trump's envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, would visit Moscow "in the near future."

The warm tone of the exchange has raised concerns among U.S. allies and members of Congress about the administration's approach to Russia while Moscow's invasion of Ukraine continues. Critics have long warned about Trump's deference to Putin and the potential for undermining Ukrainian sovereignty and transatlantic security partnerships.
