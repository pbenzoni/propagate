America has slipped into stagflation for the first time in decades, according to billionaire investor Ray Dalio, who famously predicted the 2008 financial crisis. The Bridgewater Associates founder made the assessment despite Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell's insistence that current economic conditions don't meet the technical definition of the 1970s-style crisis.

Dalio pointed to a dangerous combination of factors: annual inflation hit 3.3% in March—well above the Fed's 2% target—while real GDP growth collapsed to just 0.5% in the fourth quarter of 2025, down from 4.4% the previous quarter. The legendary investor warned Fed officials against cutting interest rates now, arguing "you will lose your credibility" if they back down from fighting inflation.

The warning comes as America grapples with fallout from the Iran war, which Dalio said is contributing to a broader breakdown in the global order. He noted the world is watching whether the United States has the resolve for sustained military operations and can meet its strategic objectives, with major implications for American power worldwide. "The more checks that are missing, particularly who controls the Strait of Hormuz, has big implications," Dalio said, adding that nations globally are reassessing the value of alignment with Washington.

Dalio, whose firm successfully anticipated both the 2008 financial crisis and the 2011 European debt crisis by analyzing historical patterns, also highlighted concerning shifts in the international system—including rising yuan transactions, deepening domestic political divisions, and the world moving away from rules-based governance toward "a might-is-right fighting order." He has previously warned of a potential U.S. "debt death spiral" within several years, driven by unsustainable interest payments and falling demand for American assets.
