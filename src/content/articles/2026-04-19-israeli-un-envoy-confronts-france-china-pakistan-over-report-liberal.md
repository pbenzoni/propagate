Israel's ambassador to the United Nations publicly challenged his French, Chinese, and Pakistani counterparts during a General Assembly session, alleging their countries negotiated safe passage deals with Iran through the blockaded Strait of Hormuz.

Ambassador Danny Danon directly questioned the French ambassador about financial arrangements, posting on social media: "How much money did you pay Iran to move ships safely through the Strait of Hormuz?" He claimed none of the three ambassadors provided answers to his inquiries.

The confrontation follows reports that commercial vessels from France, China, and Pakistan transited the strategic waterway during Iran's blockade, which Tehran imposed in response to US-Israeli military operations that began in late February. The Strait of Hormuz handles approximately 20% of global oil and liquefied natural gas flows, making its closure economically significant.

Iran has selectively permitted passage for vessels from certain nations, including China, India, Russia, Iraq, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka, according to March statements from Tehran. China remains Iran's largest oil customer, with most shipments passing through the strait. Tracking data cited by the Financial Times in April showed a French-owned container ship among vessels that successfully navigated the waterway.

At the UN, voting patterns have diverged: France supported resolutions condemning Iran's blockade, China either vetoed measures or opposed critical language, and Pakistan abstained from the votes.
