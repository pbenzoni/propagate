The U.S. Central Command has requested deployment of the long-delayed Dark Eagle hypersonic missile system to the Middle East, according to a Bloomberg report citing sources familiar with the matter. The weapon, officially known as the Long-Range Hypersonic Weapon (LRHW), has been in development since 2018 but has not yet been declared fully operational—raising serious questions about military readiness and the rush to deploy untested systems in volatile regions.

The Pentagon has invested more than $12 billion in the hypersonic program, which has consistently fallen behind schedule. The request for deployment reportedly stems from intelligence suggesting Iranian missile launchers have moved beyond the range of the Precision Strike Missile (PrSM), another system that was itself deployed without completing standard testing protocols. This pattern of fielding unproven weapons systems underscores growing concerns about oversight and accountability in military procurement.

The urgency around missile deployments comes amid broader questions about U.S. military engagement in the Middle East. Earlier this year, controversy erupted over a February 28 strike on a school and sports hall in the Iranian city of Lamerd that killed at least 21 people. While the Pentagon denied involvement, the New York Times reported that experts identified remnants consistent with a PrSM missile at the scene—highlighting the risks of deploying weapons systems without adequate testing, transparency, or public accountability.

Critics of expanding military presence in the region argue that rushing unproven weapons into deployment reflects a broader pattern of prioritizing military solutions over diplomatic engagement, while raising fundamental questions about civilian safety and the chain of command in authorizing strikes that can result in mass casualties.
