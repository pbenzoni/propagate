Secretary of State Marco Rubio has rejected Iran's recent proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil shipments, while postponing discussions on its nuclear program. Speaking on Fox News, Rubio characterized Iran's approach to the strait as an "economic nuclear weapon" being wielded against the international community.

According to multiple reports, Iran transmitted an offer to Washington that would include reopening the strategic chokepoint and ending current hostilities, but would defer negotiations on nuclear issues to a later stage. Rubio dismissed the proposal, arguing that any arrangement requiring international vessels to coordinate with or seek permission from Iran would not constitute genuine reopening of what should remain international waters.

"This is not the Suez Canal, this is not the Panama Canal, these are international waters," Rubio stated, warning that normalizing Iranian control over the strait would set a dangerous global precedent. He insisted that Iran's nuclear program remains the "core issue" and suggested the administration believes Tehran's leadership, which he characterized as uniformly hardline, will eventually pursue nuclear weapons.

The White House confirmed President Trump reviewed Iran's offer but was not satisfied with its terms. Tehran has consistently stated it does not seek nuclear weapons and has refused US demands to surrender its enriched uranium stockpile and dismantle its nuclear program entirely.
