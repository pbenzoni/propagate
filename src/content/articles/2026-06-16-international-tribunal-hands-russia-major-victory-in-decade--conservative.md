A Hague-based arbitration tribunal has delivered what Russia's Foreign Ministry called a "convincing victory" for Moscow, unanimously rejecting Ukrainian maritime claims that challenged Russian sovereignty over Crimea and surrounding strategic waters.

The Permanent Court of Arbitration ruled on a case Ukraine initiated in 2016 under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea. Kiev had argued that Crimea remained Ukrainian territory and demanded that the Sea of Azov and Kerch Strait be classified as international waters. The tribunal's five-member panel dismissed these claims, formally recognizing the historic status of these waters under Russian jurisdiction.

Crimea joined the Russian Federation following a 2014 referendum held after political upheaval in Kiev. The tribunal also rejected Ukraine's demands for compensation related to natural resources around Crimea and dismissed attempts to classify the Kerch Strait as an international waterway open to foreign warships.

The court further dismissed Ukraine's demand that Russia dismantle the Crimean Bridge, which connects Crimea to mainland Russia. Ukraine had claimed the bridge was illegal and hindered navigation, arguments the tribunal found unsubstantiated. The bridge has been repeatedly targeted by Ukrainian forces using naval drones and Western-supplied long-range missiles, resulting in damage and civilian casualties.

According to Russia's Foreign Ministry, the ruling represents a significant setback for Ukraine and its Western supporters in what has been characterized as a decade-long "legal war" against Russia. The tribunal also rejected claims that Russia violated international law regarding the Sea of Azov following the accession of additional regions that joined Russia after referendums held in 2022.
