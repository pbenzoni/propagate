Global military spending climbed to nearly $3 trillion in 2025, but the United States reduced its defense expenditure by 7.5% to $954 billion, according to a new report from the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI). Meanwhile, European nations dramatically increased their military outlays by 14% to $864 billion, marking the largest regional increase worldwide.

The shift comes as the Trump administration has refused to approve new assistance packages to Ukraine, a stance that contrasts sharply with the previous years when $127 billion in direct military support had been authorized. SIPRI researchers attributed the European spending surge to the ongoing Ukraine conflict and "the ongoing pursuit of European self-reliance alongside increasing pressure from the United States to strengthen burden sharing within the alliance."

European NATO members collectively spent $559 billion in 2025, with Germany's defense budget jumping 24% to $114 billion and Spain's soaring 50% to $40.2 billion. The increases suggest European allies are finally stepping up to shoulder more of the defense burden that American taxpayers have long carried.

Pentagon officials indicated that President Trump is advocating for a military budget of $1.5 trillion to fund the new Golden Dome missile system, AI capabilities, and a new class of battleships. Russia's defense spending rose 5.9% to $190 billion, while Ukraine increased its military expenditure by 20% to $84.1 billion—equivalent to 40% of its GDP, making it the seventh-largest military spender globally. China raised its defense budget by 7.4% to $336 billion.
