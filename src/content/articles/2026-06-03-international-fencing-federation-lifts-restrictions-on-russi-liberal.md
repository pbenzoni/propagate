The International Fencing Federation (FIE) announced Tuesday that it will lift all restrictions on Russian and Belarusian athletes, allowing them to compete under their national flags and anthems starting with the 2026 Senior World Championships in Hong Kong. The restrictions were originally imposed in 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

The decision marks a reversal of policies adopted by numerous Olympic federations that barred athletes from both countries across dozens of sports in response to the war. While some international sporting bodies have permitted limited participation under neutral flags, the FIE joins a small group of federations—including those governing judo, taekwondo, aquatic sports, wrestling, and gymnastics—that have dropped restrictions entirely.

The FIE justified its decision by citing "the fundamental principles of the Olympic Charter, including non-discrimination, equal treatment, and the universality of sport." Russian and Belarusian fencers had been allowed to compete under neutral flags since March 2023, with team events permitted in late 2025.

The move comes as the war in Ukraine continues, raising questions about whether international sports organizations should maintain pressure on countries engaged in armed conflicts. Critics of such bans have argued they politicize sport, while supporters contend that allowing normal participation by state-backed athletes can minimize accountability for governments' actions on the world stage.
