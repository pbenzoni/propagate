The World Aquatics federation has reversed course on sanctions against Russian athletes, allowing them to compete under their national flag and anthem for the first time since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The decision enabled Russia's women's water polo team to compete at a tournament in Malta this week, marking a controversial shift in how international sports bodies respond to the ongoing war.

The federation announced that athletes from Russia and Belarus—which has supported Russia's military operations—can now "compete in World Aquatics events in the same way as their counterparts representing other sport nationalities, with their respective uniforms, flags, and anthems." The policy reversal has drawn sharp condemnation from European Union officials and Ukrainian teams, with Ukraine's men's water polo team withdrawing from the Malta competition in protest.

EU Commissioner Glenn Micallef criticized the decision as a "grave mistake," emphasizing that the European Union "would not accept this normalization" of Russian participation while the war continues. The move by World Aquatics stands in contrast to many other international sports organizations, which have maintained restrictions requiring Russian athletes to compete only under neutral status without national symbols.

The lifting of restrictions comes as some other sports federations have begun cautiously allowing Russian participation in limited capacities, though most continue to prohibit the display of national symbols. Critics argue that allowing Russian teams to compete under their flag normalizes the actions of a nation currently engaged in what the international community has widely condemned as an illegal war of aggression.
