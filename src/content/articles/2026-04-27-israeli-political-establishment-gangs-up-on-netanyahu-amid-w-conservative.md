Former Israeli Prime Ministers Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid have announced a merger of their political parties ahead of elections scheduled for later this year, in a coordinated effort to unseat Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The unified list, titled 'Together, Led by Bennett,' combines Bennett's right-wing faction with Lapid's centrist Yesh Atid party.

Bennett and Lapid previously joined forces after the 2021 election to end Netanyahu's 12-year tenure, forming what they called a "government of change"—a fragile coalition that included left-wing parties and, controversially, the Arab party Ra'am. That alliance collapsed, and Netanyahu returned to power after winning the November 2022 election. This time, both leaders say they will only form a government with Zionist opposition parties, explicitly excluding Arab factions.

Netanyahu's leadership has faced mounting pressure on multiple fronts. He is currently on trial for bribery, fraud, and breach of trust in three separate cases—charges he has consistently denied as a political witch hunt. The International Criminal Court issued warrants for him in 2024 over alleged war crimes related to Israel's military response in Gaza, following the October 2023 Hamas attack that killed 1,200 Israelis and saw 250 taken hostage. Israeli military operations in Gaza have resulted in over 72,000 deaths, according to Palestinian health authorities.

Recent polling from Maariv shows Bennett's party tied with Netanyahu's Likud at 24 Knesset seats, while Lapid's Yesh Atid polls around six to seven seats. Bennett has pledged to advance universal conscription, halt funding for draft evasion, and introduce an eight-year term limit for prime ministers if his coalition takes power. The election must be held no later than the end of October.
