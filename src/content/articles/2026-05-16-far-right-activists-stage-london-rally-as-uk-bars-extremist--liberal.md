Tens of thousands of far-right demonstrators gathered in central London on Saturday for what organizers called a "Unite the Kingdom" rally, an anti-immigration event that drew scrutiny from authorities and counter-protesters alike. Police estimated approximately 50,000 attendees, a significant decline from a similar September rally that reportedly drew over 150,000 participants.

Ahead of the demonstration, Prime Minister Keir Starmer's government took the unusual step of blocking eleven foreign speakers from entering the country, citing concerns that their presence would not serve "the public good." Among those denied entry were Polish MEP Dominik Tarczynski, Dutch commentator Eva Vlaardingerbroek, and several American right-wing influencers. Starmer defended the decision, stating the government would not permit individuals to "spread hate on our streets."

The rally was organized by Tommy Robinson, whose legal name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, a controversial anti-immigration activist with a history of convictions. Metropolitan Police deployed thousands of officers across London to manage the event, which coincided with a pro-Palestinian march commemorating Nakba Day and the FA Cup final. At least one arrest was reported early in the day, with right-wing activist Ryan Bridge, co-founder of the "Raise the Colours" movement known for anti-migrant vigilantism, detained by police.

Critics of the rally pointed to its origins in far-right organizing and expressed concern about the rhetoric surrounding immigration, border security, and what Robinson characterized as threats to free speech. The government's decision to bar foreign speakers drew accusations from rally supporters of political suppression, though civil rights advocates generally supported measures to limit the platforming of extremist voices.
