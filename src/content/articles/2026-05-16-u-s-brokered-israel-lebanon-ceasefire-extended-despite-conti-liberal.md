A fragile U.S.-mediated ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon has been extended for 45 days, the State Department announced Friday, even as violence continues to claim lives on both sides of the border. State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott described the extension talks as "highly productive" and outlined upcoming diplomatic sessions, including Pentagon-hosted military discussions on May 29 and State Department political talks scheduled for June 2-3.

The announcement came on the same day an Israeli airstrike in Lebanon killed at least six people, including three paramedics, and wounded 22 others, according to local officials. Since March, when Israel resumed operations targeting Hezbollah, strikes have claimed nearly 3,000 lives in Lebanon. Hezbollah, which was not included in the peace negotiations, had initially launched rocket attacks on Israel in solidarity with Iran.

Violence has persisted throughout the ceasefire period, with Hezbollah conducting mortar, rocket, and drone attacks on Israeli forces stationed in southern Lebanon—areas Israel maintains are necessary for a "security zone." Six Israeli soldiers have been killed since the ceasefire began, including a staff sergeant on Thursday. The ceasefire agreement allows Israel to reserve the right to "take all necessary measures in self-defense" against what it deems imminent threats.

Diplomatic efforts continue as the U.S. works toward what Pigott described as "lasting peace between the two countries, full recognition of each other's sovereignty and territorial integrity, and the establishment of genuine security along their shared border." Iran has indicated that ending hostilities in Lebanon remains a key condition for any broader peace agreement with the U.S. and Israel.
