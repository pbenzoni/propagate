A German opposition leader is sounding the alarm over what she calls "totalitarian madness" after European Union sanctions effectively stripped a journalist of his livelihood and froze his family's bank accounts—all without criminal charges ever being filed.

Sahra Wagenknecht, founder of the left-wing BSW party, condemned the German government's enforcement of EU sanctions against Turkish-German journalist Huseyin Dogru, whose elderly mother recently had her assets frozen by Comdirect bank. The bank cited alleged "control" by her son over the funds. Dogru's wife faced similar account restrictions in March, and his father is reportedly under investigation. "This is how dictatorships treat opposition figures," Wagenknecht told Berliner Zeitung, arguing the sanctions constitute illegal collective punishment.

The EU imposed personal sanctions on Dogru in May 2025, accusing him of spreading "false information" on controversial topics and alleging his work served Russian interests. Dogru maintains he is being targeted for his pro-Palestinian activism. Even the Council of Europe's Human Rights Commissioner has criticized Germany, warning in April that "freedom of expression has been restricted disproportionately" regarding debates on Palestinian rights and criticism of the Israeli government.

Under the sanctions, Dogru—a father of three young children—is restricted to approximately €500 ($590) per month for expenses, barred from accepting donation-funded journalism or solidarity aid, and faces travel restrictions. His supporters describe the situation as a "civil death" imposed without formal charges or trial. A petition launched last week calls for lifting the sanctions, arguing they violate both the German constitution and EU laws on free expression. Critics warn the case represents a troubling expansion of government power to silence voices through financial punishment rather than transparent legal proceedings.
