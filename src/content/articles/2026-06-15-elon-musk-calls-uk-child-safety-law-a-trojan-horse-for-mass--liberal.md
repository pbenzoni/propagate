The UK government's plan to ban social media access for children under 16—announced by Prime Minister Keir Starmer as a child safety measure—is drawing fierce criticism from an unlikely coalition of privacy advocates and tech executives who warn the policy could usher in unprecedented government surveillance of all internet users.

Elon Musk, owner of X (formerly Twitter), has been particularly vocal, calling the legislation "a wolf in sheep's clothing" and claiming its "real goal is to enable the UK government to track everyone." Digital rights organizations including the Open Rights Group and Big Brother Watch have echoed these concerns, warning that adults will be forced to submit government IDs or biometric data to unregulated third-party companies simply to access social platforms.

The proposed ban would prohibit under-16s from using platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook, while imposing nightly online curfews on 16- and 17-year-olds. Messaging apps like WhatsApp and Signal would be exempt. To enforce the ban, the government says it will require age verification using a model similar to Australia's, where users must prove they are over 16 through government-issued ID or facial age scans—with platforms responsible for conducting the checks.

Civil liberties groups warn the policy represents a dangerous expansion of state power. "We will become one of the first democracies in the world to require IDs to access the internet," said Silkie Carlo, director of Big Brother Watch. "We are sleepwalking towards a total surveillance state." Critics note that Starmer previously advocated for mandatory digital ID systems, and that his government has already expanded facial recognition surveillance at protests and pressured tech companies to require ID for mobile phone use—raising questions about whether child safety is the true motivation behind the policy.
