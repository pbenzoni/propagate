Iran's Foreign Ministry has contradicted President Donald Trump's assertion that Iranian leadership had greenlit a preliminary peace agreement, underscoring the persistent challenges in reaching a stable accord with the Islamic Republic.

Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei characterized recent reports about an imminent deal as mere "speculation," emphasizing that Iranian authorities must carefully examine every provision. "The text has almost been finalized in its major parts," Baghaei acknowledged, but added a pointed criticism: "The problem is that the contradictory positions of the United States have always caused turbulence and disruption in this process." He made clear that Iran would not budge on what it calls its "red lines."

President Trump had announced Thursday that a settlement could be signed as soon as this weekend in Europe, claiming Iranian Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei had approved the terms. The President said he halted planned military strikes following two days of bombardment—the most serious escalation since an April truce. The proposed agreement would reportedly extend that ceasefire, reopen the strategic Strait of Hormuz, and establish a framework for future negotiations on Iran's controversial nuclear program.

Tehran has consistently rejected Trump's demand that it surrender its enriched uranium stockpile, maintaining its nuclear activities are strictly peaceful. Iran's chief negotiator warned that Washington's "wrong strategies and impulsive decisions" risk creating "an endless quagmire" in the Middle East.
