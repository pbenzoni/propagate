The Trump administration has approved a potential $373.6 million sale of precision-guided bomb conversion kits to Ukraine, following pressure from congressional Republicans over delayed arms deliveries. The State Department announced Tuesday that the deal would provide 1,532 JDAM-Extended Range tail kits to convert conventional bombs into GPS-guided munitions.

The approval came after Senate Defense Appropriations Subcommittee Chairman Mitch McConnell accused the Pentagon of freezing $400 million in congressionally authorized Ukraine assistance. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth subsequently confirmed the funds had been released. Boeing, based in St. Louis, Missouri, is listed as the primary contractor for the deal.

Notably, the sale differs from previous Ukraine aid packages in that it does not rely on direct U.S. taxpayer funding. Instead, the arms are being purchased through NATO's Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List (PURL), a mechanism where European nations and Canada finance the acquisition of American-made weapons. This approach aligns with President Trump's stated opposition to unconditional military support while maintaining America's defense industrial base.

The effectiveness of these systems remains uncertain, as Russian electronic warfare capabilities have increasingly jammed GPS signals, degrading precision-guided munitions according to Military.com. Moscow has repeatedly warned that Western arms deliveries prolong the conflict and make NATO a direct participant in the hostilities. The State Department claims the sale "will not alter the basic military balance in the region."
