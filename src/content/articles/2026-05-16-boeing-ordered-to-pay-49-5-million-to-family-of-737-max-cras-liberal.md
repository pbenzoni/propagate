A federal jury in Chicago has ordered Boeing to pay $49.5 million to the family of Samya Stumo, who died in the 2019 Ethiopian Airlines crash involving the company's 737 MAX airliner. The aircraft went down shortly after takeoff from Addis Ababa on March 10, 2019, killing all 157 passengers and crew on board.

The disaster was the second involving the 737 MAX within months. An earlier crash in Indonesia killed 189 people, prompting a 20-month worldwide grounding of the aircraft. Boeing later admitted that a design flaw in the flight control system was responsible for both tragedies. The jury awarded Stumo's family $21 million for pain and emotional suffering, $16.5 million for loss of companionship, and $12 million for grief.

Boeing's accountability has been limited despite the scale of the tragedies. In 2021, the company reached a deferred prosecution agreement with federal authorities, paying $2.5 billion to avoid criminal charges after admitting it deceived the Federal Aviation Administration about the 737 MAX's flaws. However, the Department of Justice found Boeing in breach of those settlement terms in 2024.

Under the Trump administration, the DOJ dropped its demand that Boeing plead guilty to criminal fraud conspiracy charges. A federal judge in Texas approved dismissal of the criminal case last November, a decision upheld by an appeals court in March. Boeing, a major defense contractor, has settled dozens of additional civil lawsuits through confidential pretrial agreements worth billions in compensation, though the Stumo family's attorneys intend to pursue punitive damages on appeal.
