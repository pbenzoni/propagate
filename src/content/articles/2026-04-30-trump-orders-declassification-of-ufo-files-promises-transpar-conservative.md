President Donald Trump announced this week that his administration will soon release long-classified government files on unidentified flying objects and aerial phenomena, fulfilling a promise he made in a February executive order directing the Department of War to disclose "any and all information" on the subject.

"I think we're going to be releasing as much as we can in the near future… some of it is going to be very interesting to people," Trump told reporters during a White House briefing Wednesday. The president noted that public interest in government transparency on UFOs has been building "for a long time," and said he personally interviewed military pilots who described witnessing unexplainable phenomena. "Very solid people," Trump emphasized, adding "they said they saw things that you wouldn't believe."

The move follows decades of government secrecy around UFO sightings, with the Pentagon currently operating an All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office to investigate reports. According to a late 2024 Pentagon report, the department received 757 UAP reports between May 2023 and June 2024, with 21 cases meriting further analysis due to "anomalous characteristics and/or behaviors." While the Pentagon has stated it found no proof of alien technology in a March 2024 assessment, many Americans remain skeptical of official denials.

Secretary of War Pete Hegseth said he will learn "alongside" the American people whether classified records contain evidence of extraterrestrial life. The Pentagon has indicated it is reviewing security concerns before releasing materials, but Trump's executive order represents a significant step toward government accountability on an issue that has sparked public curiosity and debate for generations.
