The European Commission has ordered Meta to provide competitors' AI assistants with free access to WhatsApp's business platform, threatening fines of up to 10% of the company's global revenue if it doesn't comply within five working days. The interim measure stems from an antitrust investigation launched after AI developers complained that Meta blocked their access to WhatsApp for Business—which serves over 3.3 billion users worldwide—while keeping its own Meta AI connected.

Meta had previously allowed external AI chatbots to access WhatsApp for a fee, but Brussels rejected this approach as economically unsustainable for competitors. EU antitrust chief Teresa Ribera claimed the measure would "preserve choice for citizens" and protect competition in the AI market. The commission argues it's safeguarding consumer interests and allowing AI companies to "innovate, scale up and reach their full potential."

Meta fired back, calling the decision "regulatory overreach" and announcing plans to appeal. A company spokesperson pointed out the absurdity of the situation: "The European Commission has decided that OpenAI and some of the largest companies in the world can use the paid-for WhatsApp Business product for free. This is regulatory overreach subsidized by the many European companies that pay." The company argues it's being forced to subsidize its largest competitors at the expense of paying customers.

This latest action adds to a growing pattern of EU penalties against American tech companies. Since early 2024, Brussels has extracted approximately $7 billion in fines from Meta, Google, and Apple for alleged antitrust and privacy violations. The Trump administration has accused the European Union of unfairly targeting U.S. firms, though the European Commission maintains it's simply protecting European consumers.
