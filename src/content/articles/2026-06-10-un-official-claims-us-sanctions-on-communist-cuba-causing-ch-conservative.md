UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Turk claimed this week that U.S. economic sanctions against communist Cuba are causing children to die due to medical supply shortages on the island. The statement comes as the Castro regime faces mounting pressure from Washington over its decades-long authoritarian rule.

Turk's office reported that infant mortality rates have risen to 9.9 per 1,000 births and childhood cancer survival rates have declined from 85% to 65%. The UN official characterized broad economic sanctions as "incompatible with basic principles of international human rights law," though critics note that Cuba's socialist government has systematically violated human rights for over sixty years.

The island nation has experienced severe fuel shortages after Venezuela—itself under the thumb of the authoritarian Maduro regime—stopped oil shipments. President Trump has made clear his administration's intent to address the communist threat just 90 miles from American shores, stating he intends to "take" Cuba "one way or another."

Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez Parrilla accused the United States of inflicting "collective punishment" on the Cuban people. However, defenders of the sanctions policy point out that the real source of Cuban suffering is the communist regime's failed economic system and authoritarian governance. Russia, China, and Mexico have provided humanitarian aid to prop up the faltering dictatorship, with Moscow sending approximately 700,000 barrels of crude oil in late March.
