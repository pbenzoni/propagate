President Donald Trump has removed Navy Secretary John Phelan from his position after the former billionaire investor failed to deliver on the administration's ambitious naval modernization plans, according to reports from the New York Times.

The dismissal came after Phelan struggled to meet the president's directive to build a new Trump-class battleship by 2028—a cutting-edge warship that would feature advanced American weapons systems including laser combat technology, railguns, and hypersonic missiles. The vessel, estimated to cost $17 billion, was intended to anchor what Trump called a new "Golden Fleet" for American naval dominance.

When Phelan realized the tight timeline would be difficult to meet with domestic shipbuilding capacity, he reportedly suggested turning to European shipyards for assistance. Trump rejected this proposal outright, insisting the warship be built on American soil with American steel—staying true to his America First principles.

The firing underscores ongoing challenges in U.S. naval shipbuilding. The troubled Zumwalt-class destroyer program, originally planned for dozens of vessels, was scaled back to just three ships after costs ballooned to $24.5 billion. Senior Pentagon officials have acknowledged that American shipyards currently lack the capacity to build technologically advanced battleships on accelerated timelines, highlighting the work needed to rebuild America's industrial base.

Phelan's departure also reflected internal Pentagon dynamics, with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and his team reportedly concerned that the Navy secretary had circumvented proper military chain of command by relying too heavily on his personal relationship with the president.
