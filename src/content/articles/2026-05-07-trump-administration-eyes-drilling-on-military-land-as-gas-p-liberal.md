The Trump administration is exploring plans to drill for oil directly beneath U.S. military bases in an effort to replenish the nation's Strategic Petroleum Reserve, according to a Bloomberg report. The move comes as gasoline prices have climbed above $4.50 per gallon for the first time since 2022, driven by global supply disruptions following escalating U.S.-Israeli military action against Iran.

The Strategic Petroleum Reserve, established in the 1970s as an emergency buffer against oil supply shocks, currently holds approximately 415 million barrels—its lowest level since the mid-1980s. Energy Secretary Chris Wright indicated in April that the administration is seeking "pragmatic" approaches to energy extraction on federal lands, pointing to military installations located in oil-rich areas. "We have military bases or facilities that are in the middle of oil fields, but there's no development under those resources," Wright said at a Wall Street Journal forum.

While drilling beneath defense sites is unlikely to immediately affect pump prices, the strategy would allow the federal government to own extracted oil outright rather than purchasing crude from private producers. Last September, the administration auctioned drilling rights for nearly 2,000 acres beneath Louisiana's Barksdale Air Force Base, home to B-52 strategic bombers. Critics may question the environmental and security implications of expanding fossil fuel extraction on sensitive military land.

The emergency reserve has been significantly drawn down in recent years. The Biden administration released substantial quantities in 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine and resulting price spikes. In March, Trump authorized the release of an additional 172 million barrels through 2027 under a loan program requiring energy companies to return borrowed oil with added premiums. Global crude prices have surged past $100 per barrel following Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz to what it terms "enemy ships"—a waterway that previously carried roughly 20% of global crude trade.
