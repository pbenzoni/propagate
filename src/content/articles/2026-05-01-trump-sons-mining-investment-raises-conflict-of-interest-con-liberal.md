A shell company in which Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump hold financial stakes has agreed to merge with a mining group that received up to $1.6 billion in U.S. government commitments in 2025, according to a Financial Times report raising fresh questions about potential conflicts of interest within the Trump administration.

The agreement between Skyline Builders group—in which the president's sons invested—and Cove Kaz Capital group was finalized Thursday. The merged entity will trade on Nasdaq as Kaz Resources. Cove Kaz controls 70% of major tungsten deposits in central Kazakhstan, with funding pledged by the federally-backed U.S. Export-Import Bank and the Development Finance Corporation.

According to the Financial Times, Trump's sons bought into Skyline last August through a special purpose vehicle, later increasing their investment by $24 million in October. While there is no evidence the brothers knew of the impending federal contract when they made their initial investment or that they influenced the contract award, the timing has drawn scrutiny from Democratic lawmakers who have repeatedly warned about conflicts of interest stemming from the Trump family's extensive business dealings.

The president's relatives reportedly generated more than $1 billion in pre-tax profits last year through investments in cryptocurrency, AI, drones, and critical minerals projects. American Ventures, associated with the investment vehicle, stated that Donald Trump Jr. "has no operational involvement" and "does not interface with the federal government" on behalf of companies he invests in. Eric Trump did not respond to requests for comment.

Tungsten is a critical mineral for aerospace and defense manufacturing due to its heat resistance and hardness. The United States currently operates no active tungsten mines and relies heavily on Chinese imports, making domestic sourcing a strategic priority that has attracted significant federal investment.
