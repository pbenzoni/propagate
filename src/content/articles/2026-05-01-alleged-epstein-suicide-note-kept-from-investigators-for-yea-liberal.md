A document described as Jeffrey Epstein's possible suicide note has been sealed in a New York courthouse for years, preventing investigators from accessing what could have been critical evidence in understanding the convicted sex offender's death, the *New York Times* reported Thursday.

According to the report, Epstein's former cellmate discovered the message in July 2019, shortly after Epstein was found unresponsive in his cell at the Metropolitan Correctional Center. The document allegedly expressed Epstein's frustration that investigators had "found nothing" despite months of scrutiny, and concluded with the words "Time to say goodbye."

The note was eventually sealed by a federal judge as part of the cellmate's separate criminal proceedings, keeping it out of reach of the Justice Department officials investigating Epstein's August 2019 death. A DOJ spokesperson confirmed to the *Times* that the agency never saw the document, and it was not included in the massive trove of Epstein files released earlier this year.

The revelation raises troubling questions about transparency and accountability in the handling of evidence related to Epstein's death. His case implicated numerous powerful individuals and sparked widespread public demands for a full accounting of his activities and connections. The *New York Times* has filed a petition seeking to have the note unsealed, arguing the public has a right to know what information has been withheld from investigators examining the death of one of the most notorious figures in a sprawling sexual abuse scandal.
