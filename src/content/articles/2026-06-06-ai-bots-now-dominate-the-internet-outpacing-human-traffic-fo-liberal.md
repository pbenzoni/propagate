Automated bots and AI agents now generate more internet traffic than actual humans for the first time in history, according to new data from Cloudflare, a major internet infrastructure company. The milestone marks a dramatic shift in how the web functions—and raises urgent questions about its future.

Cloudflare's recent analysis shows that automated bot requests account for approximately 57% of traffic to ordinary webpages, compared with just 43% generated by human users. CEO Matthew Prince acknowledged the development happened years ahead of his own projections. "Welp, that happened faster than I predicted," Prince wrote, noting he had expected this crossover point only in 2027.

The surge is driven primarily by AI agents—automated systems that browse and process vast amounts of web content on behalf of users. While a person might visit a few websites to research a topic or make a purchase, an AI agent can scan thousands of pages to generate answers or complete tasks. This means much of today's web activity consists of machine-to-machine traffic rather than people actually browsing pages.

The trend has revived concerns about the so-called "dead internet theory"—the idea that online activity is increasingly dominated by bots and automated systems interacting with machine-generated content rather than genuine human participation. It also threatens the advertising-based business model that has sustained much of the open web, since bots don't click on ads. Some experts worry websites may eventually need to charge AI agents for access, fundamentally changing the internet's accessibility.

Meanwhile, research shows large portions of the older web are vanishing. A 2024 Pew Research Center study found that 38% of webpages existing in 2013 were no longer accessible a decade later, fueling fears that the open internet—once built around human browsing and connection—is being transformed into something unrecognizable.
