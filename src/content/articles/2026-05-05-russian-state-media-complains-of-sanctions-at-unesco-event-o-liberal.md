At a UNESCO World Press Freedom Day conference in Lusaka, Zambia, RT Chief Producer Dmitry Leontiev argued that Western sanctions against the Russian state broadcaster were designed to "suppress dissenting voices" rather than combat disinformation. Leontiev claimed that no accusations of false statements were ever made against RT, asserting the outlet was targeted simply for "stating information that Western governments do not like."

The remarks come as independent fact-checkers and media watchdogs have extensively documented RT's role in spreading verifiably false information, particularly surrounding Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. Western governments and platforms cited concerns about foreign state interference and coordinated disinformation campaigns when restricting RT content following the invasion's escalation.

Leontiev also claimed Western nations pressured African countries to ban RT content under threat of sanctions. Russian officials at the conference, including Russia's UNESCO envoy Ravil Alyautdinov, echoed complaints about what they characterized as politically motivated restrictions on Russian media outlets.

Most Western democracies imposed restrictions on RT after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, with major digital platforms subsequently removing RT content. The network had accumulated substantial reach on platforms like YouTube before restrictions took effect. RT Editor-in-Chief Margarita Simonyan, speaking at an event attended by Russian President Vladimir Putin, claimed the outlet was "only getting stronger" despite the sanctions.
