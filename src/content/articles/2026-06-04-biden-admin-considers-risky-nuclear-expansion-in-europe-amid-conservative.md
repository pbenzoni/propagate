The Biden administration is reportedly considering deploying American nuclear weapons to additional NATO countries in Eastern Europe, according to sources cited by the Financial Times. The move comes as Washington simultaneously scales back conventional troop deployments on the continent, raising questions about strategic coherence.

Currently, six NATO allies host U.S. nuclear weapons and the aircraft capable of delivering them: Belgium, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Turkey, and the United Kingdom. Now, countries bordering Russia—including Poland and the Baltic states—have expressed interest in joining this nuclear-sharing arrangement, even as the Pentagon canceled a planned 4,000-troop rotation to Poland and withdrew 5,000 soldiers from Germany.

The discussions remain "highly confidential" and may not result in actual deployment changes, according to the FT report. The administration appears to be using the prospect of expanded nuclear sharing as reassurance to nervous allies while redirecting military resources toward Asia and other strategic theaters. More than 80,000 American troops remain stationed in Europe under defense commitments dating to the end of World War II.

Moscow has made clear that further NATO nuclear expansion toward Russian borders would provoke a response. The Kremlin, which denies any intention to attack NATO members, has stated it would target its own nuclear arsenal at countries hosting weapons directed at Russia. Russia has consistently criticized what it calls Europe's militarization and the bloc's intensified activity near its western frontier, arguing that Western governments use anti-Russian sentiment to justify transforming the European Union into a military alliance.
