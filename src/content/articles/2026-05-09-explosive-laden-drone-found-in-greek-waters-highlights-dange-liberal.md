Greek authorities are investigating a suspected Ukrainian-made kamikaze sea drone discovered by fishermen in a cave on the island of Lefkada in the Ionian Sea, according to Reuters reporting citing coast guard and police sources. The vessel, reportedly a MAGURA V3 drone found late Thursday, was preliminarily assessed to be fitted with at least three detonators, raising urgent questions about maritime security far from traditional conflict zones.

Bomb disposal experts and military divers have been deployed to dismantle the drone and examine the site. Investigators are working to determine the vessel's purpose and how it arrived in Greek waters—whether it was part of a larger shipment, drifted from elsewhere, or potentially even involved in illicit activities. Some sources have raised the possibility that given the reported lack of a confirmed warhead, the drone could have been repurposed for drug trafficking, as such vessels can cover vast distances while evading detection.

The discovery underscores growing concerns about the geographic expansion of maritime conflict tactics. While most incidents involving Ukrainian naval drones have occurred in the Black Sea, several strikes attributed to Ukraine have been reported in the Mediterranean in recent months, including an acknowledged December attack on an oil tanker off Libya and a March incident involving a liquefied natural gas tanker near Malta. Security experts warn that the proliferation of autonomous weapons systems poses significant risks to civilian shipping and regional stability, particularly as sophisticated drone technology becomes more accessible and conflict tactics evolve beyond traditional battlefields.
