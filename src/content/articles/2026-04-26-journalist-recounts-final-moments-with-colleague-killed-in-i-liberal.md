A Lebanese journalist has provided harrowing testimony about an Israeli airstrike that killed her colleague, reigniting concerns about the targeting of media workers in conflict zones. Zeinab Faraj, speaking from her hospital bed, described how reporter Amal Khalil used her own body to shield Faraj from flames after their press vehicle was struck—despite both wearing gear clearly identifying them as journalists.

According to Faraj's account, the attack occurred Wednesday in southern Lebanon when an Israeli drone struck a vehicle ahead of them. After taking cover, a second missile hit their car. "She told me: 'Zeinab, I'm burning!' I grabbed her by her jacket and pulled her toward me," Faraj recalled through tears. The two journalists sought refuge in a nearby bathroom, where Faraj lost consciousness. When she awoke, Khalil was gone—killed in what Lebanese officials are calling a deliberate attack on press freedom.

The incident has drawn international condemnation, with Lebanese President Joseph Aoun accusing Israel of "deliberate and consistent targeting of journalists" to suppress coverage of its military operations. Prime Minister Nawaf Salam characterized the strike as a war crime. International observers, including Spanish authorities and multiple foreign ministries, have echoed calls for accountability.

Khalil is the ninth journalist killed by Israeli forces in Lebanon since recent escalations began, according to Lebanese authorities. Press freedom advocates note a disturbing pattern: rescue workers attempting to reach Khalil reportedly came under fire, delaying recovery efforts for hours. While Israeli military officials claim the incident is under review and deny targeting journalists, human rights organizations continue documenting what they describe as systematic threats to media workers covering the conflict.
