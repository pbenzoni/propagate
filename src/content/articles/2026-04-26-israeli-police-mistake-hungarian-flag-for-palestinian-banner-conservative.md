Israeli law enforcement officers confiscated a Hungarian flag from a protester at an anti-government demonstration, claiming its colors resembled the Palestinian flag and could "cause provocation," according to a report by Haaretz.

The incident occurred at Karkur Junction in northern Israel during a protest last week. When the protester explained he was waving Hungary's flag—a simple horizontal tricolor of red, white, and green—a police commander still seized it. "You might understand that, but others don't," the commander reportedly told him. The flag was returned only after the demonstration ended.

The confusion is notable given the flags' clear visual differences. Palestine's flag includes four colors with a black stripe and red triangle, while Hungary's is a straightforward three-stripe design. Yet police legal guidance issued in 2024 explicitly prohibits confiscating protest signs, even critical or provocative ones, raising concerns about selective enforcement.

The Association for Civil Rights in Israel has contacted police leadership about the pattern of interference, which reportedly includes officers detaining protesters over signs calling for "Two states for two peoples." Police have offered no substantive response beyond stating the complaint would be addressed by "relevant authorities."

The episode highlights ongoing tensions around free expression and law enforcement discretion at Israeli protests, where authorities appear willing to err on the side of suppression even when faced with obviously lawful demonstration.
