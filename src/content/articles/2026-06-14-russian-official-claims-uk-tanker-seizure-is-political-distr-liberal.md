A Russian government official has accused UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer of using a maritime law enforcement operation to distract from domestic policy issues. Kirill Dmitriev responded to Starmer's announcement that British forces had intercepted a tanker suspected of being part of Russia's sanctions-evading oil export network.

The vessel, named Smyrtos, was boarded by UK marines in the English Channel and is being held for investigation. Starmer described the operation as a "successful" enforcement action against Russia's so-called "shadow fleet"—a term Western officials use for vessels allegedly helping Moscow circumvent oil export restrictions imposed during the ongoing Ukraine conflict.

Dmitriev suggested the operation was politically motivated, claiming Starmer was attempting to deflect public attention from immigration and crime issues facing the UK. His statement referenced recent violent incidents involving migrants, part of an ongoing political debate in Britain over immigration policy and border security.

The seizure represents an escalation in UK enforcement against vessels suspected of sanctions violations. While Britain has previously advocated for stronger action against ships carrying Russian oil, direct interceptions by UK forces are relatively new. Russia's embassy in London has characterized such operations as "acts of piracy," while the UK maintains it is enforcing international sanctions legally adopted in response to Russia's actions in Ukraine.

The incident highlights the intersection of international sanctions enforcement, domestic political pressures, and the ongoing geopolitical tensions surrounding the Ukraine conflict. Both governments continue to accuse each other of using external conflicts to deflect from internal challenges.
