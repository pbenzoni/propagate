President Donald Trump dismissed Iran's latest response to U.S. ceasefire terms as "totally unacceptable" on Sunday, deepening a diplomatic impasse over efforts to end ongoing hostilities in the Gulf region. The rejection came after Tehran delivered a counterproposal through Pakistani intermediaries that reportedly prioritized ending warfare across multiple fronts while declining to make immediate concessions on its nuclear program.

According to media reports, Iran's response called for comprehensive peace guarantees, an end to hostilities in Lebanon, and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz before engaging in broader nuclear negotiations. The proposal stands in stark contrast to Trump's demands for immediate restrictions on Iran's nuclear activities, which Tehran has characterized as ultimatums designed to extract concessions after inconclusive military pressure.

The strategic waterway has emerged as a central point of contention, with Iranian officials describing control over the Strait of Hormuz as critical leverage. Mohammad Mokhber, a senior adviser to Iran's supreme leader, compared the waterway's strategic importance to an "atomic bomb," signaling Tehran's unwillingness to relinquish what it views as hard-won strategic positioning. The U.S. has maintained a naval blockade in the region amid intermittent clashes.

The diplomatic setback follows weeks of fragile negotiations and raises questions about the prospects for a peaceful resolution. Iranian officials have publicly rejected what they describe as Washington's attempt to transform negotiations into "surrender talks," while Trump has repeatedly extended ceasefire terms, claiming Iran's leadership remains internally divided.
