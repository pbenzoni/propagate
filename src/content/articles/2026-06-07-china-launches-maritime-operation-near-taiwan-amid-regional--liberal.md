China deployed what it calls a "special maritime law enforcement operation" east of Taiwan on Saturday, intensifying regional tensions following an announcement of maritime border negotiations between Japan and the Philippines. The move underscores Beijing's increasingly aggressive posture toward the democratic island nation it claims as its own territory.

The operation, coordinated by China's Ministry of Transport, came in direct response to a May 28 summit where Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi and Philippines President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. announced plans to begin maritime delimitation talks concerning their exclusive economic zones. Chinese officials denounced the talks as violations of international law, asserting that Beijing must be involved in any negotiations concerning waters east of Taiwan—despite Taiwan's status as a self-governing democracy with its own territorial claims.

Taiwan's coast guard deployed at least five vessels in response, monitoring Chinese government ships departing from the mainland. The island's officials have also raised alarms about the Japan-Philippines negotiations, urging both nations to include Taipei in discussions that affect waters where Taiwan claims legitimate interests. This puts democratic Taiwan in the difficult position of defending its sovereignty against both Beijing's authoritarian claims and being excluded from regional negotiations.

The maritime operation represents the latest escalation in China's campaign of intimidation against Taiwan, which President Xi Jinping has refused to rule out taking by force. Tensions have been particularly high since November 2025, when Prime Minister Takaichi suggested a Chinese invasion of Taiwan could justify military intervention—a statement Beijing characterized as echoing "Japanese militarism." Japanese officials maintain that any agreement between Tokyo and Manila would not legally bind third parties, though this has done little to defuse the standoff.
