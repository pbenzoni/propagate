The U.S. attorney's office in Washington has filed a motion requesting that a federal appeals judge vacate convictions against members of the Oath Keepers and Proud Boys who were found guilty of seditious conspiracy related to the January 6, 2021 Capitol unrest.

President Donald Trump granted clemency to these individuals early in his second term, resulting in the release of 12 Oath Keepers and Proud Boys members from federal prison. However, unlike nearly 1,500 other January 6 defendants who received full presidential pardons, these militia members were not granted that complete legal relief. The group includes Stewart Rhodes, founder of the Oath Keepers, who had received an 18-year sentence—the second-longest among all January 6 cases.

The timing of the Department of Justice filing appears strategic. The defendants had challenged their convictions in appeals court, with a filing deadline approaching. Legal observers note the administration likely seeks to avoid courtroom proceedings that would require federal prosecutors to defend the Biden administration's seditious conspiracy charges. Those charges had alleged the groups acted as Trump's proxies, with prosecutors describing the Proud Boys as his "army" and accusing the Oath Keepers of attempting to obstruct the lawful transfer of power.

During the January 6 events, Trump supporters breached security barriers at the Capitol in an effort to disrupt certification of the 2020 election results. While some demonstrators remained peaceful, others engaged in property damage and confrontations with law enforcement. Trump faced impeachment on charges of "incitement of insurrection" but was acquitted. He has consistently maintained he neither coordinated nor bore responsibility for the violence that day.
