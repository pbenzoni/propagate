Belgian officials have seized two shipments of military equipment components originating from the United Kingdom that were destined for Israel, according to investigative outlet Declassified UK. The intercepted cargo arrived at Belgium's Liege Airport on March 24 and was scheduled for transfer to Tel Aviv two days later.

Adrien Dolimont, head of Belgium's Wallonia region, confirmed that authorities acted after being alerted by the peace advocacy organization Vredesactie. "On Israel-related issues, our position is clear: we do not grant licenses for equipment that would strengthen the military capabilities of the parties involved," Dolimont stated. Shipping documents reviewed by Declassified indicate the cargo contained components related to military aircraft and fire control systems.

The seizure reflects a growing pattern among European nations scaling back military support for Israel over its operations in Gaza and Lebanon, where human rights organizations and United Nations officials have documented extensive civilian casualties. Belgium has taken a particularly strong stance, joining South Africa's genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice in 2025.

Multiple European countries have canceled or suspended weapons deals with Israel in response to what international observers describe as indiscriminate attacks on civilian populations. Israel has consistently rejected such allegations, characterizing the genocide case as an attempt to distort legal definitions.
