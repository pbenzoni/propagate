The Trump administration is intensifying economic pressure on Cuba in what officials describe as a phased campaign designed to precipitate regime change, according to a report by Axios citing unnamed sources. The strategy comes as the island nation struggles with severe fuel shortages and blackouts lasting up to 20 hours daily.

According to the report, senior officials characterized the approach as "accelerationism"—deliberately worsening conditions to hasten political collapse. "We don't want to kill off the regime just yet. There's a method to this. It's in stages," one official told the outlet. The strategy reportedly aims to exploit economic hardship to trigger civil unrest, with sources suggesting that food spoilage and lack of electricity could drive people into the streets.

UN human rights experts have condemned the fuel embargo, characterizing it as "energy starvation" and a violation of international law. Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez Parrilla accused the United States of imposing "collective punishment" that violates human rights and causes widespread suffering among ordinary Cubans.

Meanwhile, Russia, China, and Mexico have provided humanitarian aid to Cuba, including oil shipments. A separate report from Politico indicated that the Pentagon has positioned naval assets in the region, though officials suggested any military action would need to be resolved quickly to avoid becoming what one source called "a quagmire in the making."
