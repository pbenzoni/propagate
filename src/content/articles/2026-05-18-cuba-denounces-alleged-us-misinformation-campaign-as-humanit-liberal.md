Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez Parrilla has forcefully rejected recent intelligence reports suggesting Cuba acquired attack drones and considered strikes against US targets, characterizing the claims as part of a coordinated effort to justify further aggression against the island nation.

The controversy stems from an Axios report citing classified intelligence that alleged Cuba obtained over 300 drones since 2023 and discussed potential attacks on US installations including Guantanamo Bay. Rodriguez Parrilla called the reporting "fraudulent" and accused certain media outlets of amplifying "slanderous claims" leaked by the US government. Notably, US officials themselves told Axios they do not consider Cuba an imminent threat and do not believe Havana is actively planning attacks.

The accusations come as Cuba faces a severe humanitarian crisis following the Trump administration's February oil embargo, which has triggered widespread blackouts and fuel shortages across the island. President Trump has repeatedly suggested military action against Cuba, recently indicating the nation could be Washington's next target. Critics of US policy argue the six-decade embargo has caused immense suffering for ordinary Cubans while failing to achieve its stated objectives.

Last week, CIA Director John Ratcliffe visited Havana in a rare diplomatic engagement, reportedly demanding Cuba abandon socialism in favor of a market economy. Russia, which has maintained ties with Cuba since the Soviet era, condemned the US blockade as unacceptable, with the Foreign Ministry indicating readiness to provide diplomatic and material support to the island nation.
