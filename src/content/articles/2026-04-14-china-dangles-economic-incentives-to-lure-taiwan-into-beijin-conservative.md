The Chinese Communist Party has rolled out a new economic incentives package targeting Taiwan, offering resumed flights, market access, and infrastructure projects in what critics see as an attempt to undermine the island's sovereignty. The ten-point plan was announced after Kuomintang opposition leader Cheng Li-wun met with Chinese President Xi Jinping during a controversial six-day visit to the mainland.

Beijing's Taiwan Affairs Office outlined measures including fast-tracking direct flights across the Taiwan Strait, facilitating access for Taiwanese food products to Chinese markets, and sharing energy and water resources with Taiwan-controlled islands near the mainland. The regime even proposed constructing a bridge to the islands of Matsu and Kinmen, which lie geographically closer to Communist China than to Taiwan proper.

Taiwan's democratically-elected government expressed deep skepticism about Beijing's overtures, noting the Communist regime's pattern of offering and then withdrawing similar incentives in the past. "Any exchanges should not be subject to political preconditions, nor should they be used as tools for political maneuvering or deals by specific parties," said Presidential Office spokesperson Karen Kuo, criticizing the opposition party for going around Taiwan's legitimate authorities.

The island's Mainland Affairs Council warned that previous measures had been "repeatedly rolled out and suspended by China," adding that the latest proposal lacks "any institutional safeguards for Taiwan's industries, farmers, fishers or the rights and interests of the public, making the measures highly risky." Taiwan has governed itself independently since 1949, when nationalist forces retreated there after losing the Chinese civil war to Communist forces.
