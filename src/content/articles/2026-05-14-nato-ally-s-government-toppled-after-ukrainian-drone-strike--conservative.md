Latvia's Prime Minister Evika Silina resigned Thursday after a government crisis triggered by Ukrainian kamikaze drones striking an oil depot near the Russian border. The incident, which saw two long-range Ukrainian drones hit a facility near Rezekne—approximately 40 kilometers from Russia—exposed serious questions about NATO airspace security and Ukrainian military accountability.

The crisis forced Defense Minister Andris Spruds to resign over the weekend, despite his previous support for Ukrainian strikes against Russia. Opposition parties prepared to advance a no-confidence vote that coalition leaders admitted would certainly succeed. Silina blamed "political jealousy and narrow party interests" for the government's collapse, but the underlying issue remains Ukraine's apparent disregard for allied sovereignty.

Moscow has accused NATO nations of tacitly permitting Ukraine to use their airspace for strikes on Russian targets, particularly oil facilities in northwestern regions. The charge gains credibility as multiple NATO members have reported similar incidents since mid-March. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo told Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky that such airspace violations are "unacceptable," while Estonia's Defense Minister urged Ukraine to "keep their drones away from our territory."

The Latvian government's downfall underscores growing frustration among Eastern European NATO members who support Ukraine's defense but increasingly question the wisdom of unlimited aid without meaningful oversight.
