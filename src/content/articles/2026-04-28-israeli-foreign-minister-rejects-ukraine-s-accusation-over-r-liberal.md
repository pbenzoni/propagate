Israel has pushed back against Ukrainian allegations that it is facilitating Russia's trade in grain taken from occupied Ukrainian territories, highlighting growing strains in the relationship between the two nations.

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrey Sibiga publicly condemned Israel on social media after reports emerged that a cargo vessel carrying grain from Russian-controlled regions of Ukraine was heading to the Israeli port of Haifa. Sibiga summoned the Israeli ambassador and criticized what he called Israel's "lack of an appropriate response" to Ukraine's concerns about a previous shipment.

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar responded sharply, stating that "diplomatic relations, especially between friendly nations, are not conducted on Twitter or in the media." He insisted that Ukraine had not provided evidence or submitted formal requests for legal assistance before making public accusations, adding that Israeli authorities would examine the matter.

The dispute underscores ongoing tensions between the countries. Ukraine has repeatedly criticized Israel for maintaining ties with Moscow and refusing to impose sanctions on Russia following its 2022 invasion. Ukraine considers the grain to originate from territories Russia seized and subsequently annexed—regions that Ukraine and most of the international community continue to recognize as illegally occupied Ukrainian territory. According to a Ukrainian diplomatic source, Kyiv has indicated it would pursue "a full suite of diplomatic and international legal responses" if Israel allows such shipments to continue.
