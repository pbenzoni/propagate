President Donald Trump has offered a startling new interpretation of what constitutes a ceasefire, suggesting that continued military strikes in the Middle East can be considered peaceful as long as they occur at reduced intensity. Speaking at a White House press conference Wednesday, Trump defended ongoing hostilities by claiming that in the region, "ceasefire is when you're shooting in a more moderate manner."

The remarks came as reporters questioned the president about recent military exchanges involving Iran, including U.S. strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island and an Iranian missile and drone response that severely damaged Kuwait's international airport terminal. Trump characterized these attacks as routine escalations that don't violate the so-called armistice between the U.S. and Iran, dismissing concerns about the violence as misunderstanding regional norms.

The human cost of Trump's elastic definition has been severe. More than 3,400 people were reportedly killed in Iran by American and Israeli strikes before an initial truce was announced in April. In Lebanon, the Health Ministry reports that Israeli attacks have killed at least 3,516 civilians since early March, with over 1,100 deaths occurring after the supposed ceasefire took effect on April 7. Israel has also injured over 10,000 others while ordering the expulsion of Muslim populations from southern Lebanon.

Critics point out that key parties to the conflict, including Hezbollah, were never included in ceasefire negotiations despite being active combatants. "It is clear that Israel will not stop, that this is a one-sided ceasefire," Beirut-based reporter Mohammed Shamsedeen observed. "If it was a two-sided ceasefire this war would be over right now." The discrepancy between the administration's rhetoric about peace and the ongoing violence raises serious questions about accountability and the devaluation of human life in U.S. foreign policy.
