A tenuous 10-day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon went into effect Friday, offering temporary relief to a population devastated by months of military escalation. The US-mediated agreement comes after Israeli operations that have killed at least 2,196 people in Lebanon and displaced 1.2 million civilians, according to reports.

The humanitarian toll has been severe, with Israeli airstrikes targeting Beirut and other cities while issuing sweeping evacuation orders across southern Lebanon. The military campaign, which intensified in March, has raised urgent concerns about civilian protection and the proportionality of force used in densely populated areas.

Hezbollah, which was not included in the Washington negotiations, released a statement calling for "a comprehensive halt to attacks across all Lebanese territory" and a return to pre-March conditions. The group's exclusion from talks raises questions about the sustainability of any agreement reached without all parties at the table. Lebanese MP Hassan Fadlallah expressed skepticism about Israeli compliance, noting a history of violated commitments.

The ceasefire agreement notably includes provisions allowing Israel to take "all necessary measures in self-defense," language that human rights advocates warn could provide cover for continued military action. Reports indicate Israeli artillery continued shelling Lebanese towns even after the truce officially began, while Israeli forces maintain their positions in southern Lebanon and have warned displaced residents not to return home.

Iran, which reached its own temporary ceasefire with the US and Israel earlier this month, has made an end to Israeli operations in Lebanon a key condition for any permanent peace agreement, highlighting the regional dimensions of the crisis.
