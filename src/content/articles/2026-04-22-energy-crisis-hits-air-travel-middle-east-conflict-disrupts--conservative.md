American travelers planning European vacations this summer face growing uncertainty as a deepening jet fuel shortage forces airlines to slash thousands of flights and raise ticket prices. The crisis stems from disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, where escalating conflict involving Iran has sharply reduced tanker traffic through one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints.

Around one-fifth of global oil supply passes through the narrow strait linking the Persian Gulf to world markets, making it vital to energy flows to Europe and Asia, according to the International Energy Agency. The agency's chief has warned this could become the worst energy crisis in history as the conflict escalates. Major carriers including Lufthansa have already announced cuts of 20,000 flights through October to conserve fuel, while airlines across Europe trim schedules as costs surge.

Europe's aviation sector is particularly vulnerable because the continent depends on Middle East imports for roughly 75% of its jet fuel, mostly from Kuwait. Unlike other petroleum products, jet fuel represents only about 10% of refined crude oil output, meaning supply cannot easily be increased even when demand spikes. The fuel crunch has already caused prices to roughly double since late February, forcing airlines to pass costs onto consumers through higher fares, baggage fees, and other surcharges.

The situation has sparked a noticeable shift in travel behavior, with British vacationers increasingly opting to holiday domestically due to uncertainty over international flights. Europe's largest holiday operator reported that customers are "demonstrating increased caution and booking closer to departure dates" as concerns mount about potential cancellations and disruptions. EU officials are now exploring emergency measures, including mandatory fuel-sharing between member states and importing supplies from alternative sources like the United States, though implementation timelines remain unclear.
