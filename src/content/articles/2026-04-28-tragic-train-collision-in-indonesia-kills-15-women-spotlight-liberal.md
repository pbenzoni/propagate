A devastating train collision on the outskirts of Jakarta has claimed the lives of 15 women and injured 84 others, underscoring urgent concerns about transportation infrastructure and passenger safety in Indonesia. The crash occurred Monday at Bekasi Timur station when a long-distance train struck the rear carriage of a stationary commuter train.

All of the victims were traveling in a women-only carriage, a protective measure commonly implemented on Indonesian trains to prevent sexual harassment—a policy that highlights both the progressive safety steps being taken for women passengers and the ongoing challenges they face in public spaces. Emergency responders worked through the night using specialized equipment to free survivors trapped in twisted metal, completing evacuations by Tuesday.

Preliminary investigations suggest the collision may have been triggered when the commuter train stopped after striking a stalled vehicle on the tracks, leaving it vulnerable to the approaching long-distance train. While all 240 passengers aboard the long-distance train were reported safe, the incident has renewed calls for improved rail infrastructure and safety protocols. Rail accidents remain a persistent concern in Indonesia, where aging infrastructure struggles to meet the demands of heavy passenger usage, raising questions about investment priorities and the protection of vulnerable commuters.
