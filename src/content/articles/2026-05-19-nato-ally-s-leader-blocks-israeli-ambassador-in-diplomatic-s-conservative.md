Croatian President Zoran Milanovic has taken the unusual step of refusing to approve Israel's proposed ambassador to Zagreb, standing firm against what his office called improper diplomatic pressure from the Israeli government.

The presidential office announced Monday that the proposed Israeli ambassador will not receive approval "due to the policies pursued by the current Israeli authorities." Officials noted that Israel broke with established diplomatic protocol by publicly announcing their ambassador pick before securing Croatian approval—a breach of traditional state-to-state courtesy that undermines host-country sovereignty.

The decision comes amid escalating tensions between Milanovic and outgoing Israeli Ambassador Gary Koren. The dispute intensified after Koren suggested that Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps might be conducting espionage from Tehran's embassy in Zagreb. Milanovic responded sharply, declaring Croatia wanted neither Iranian nor Israeli interference in the country's affairs—a stance that reflects growing concerns about foreign influence operations on European soil.

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar criticized Milanovic's rhetoric, but the Croatian president's office made clear that "public or political pressure, in this case from the Israeli side, will not alter" the decision. Israel's proposed envoy will now arrive as charge d'affaires instead, a lower-level position that bypasses presidential approval requirements. The standoff highlights how even close Western allies can clash over questions of national sovereignty and foreign policy independence.
