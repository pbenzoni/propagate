Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico is sounding the alarm about a threat many Western leaders seem determined to ignore: Ukrainian drones repeatedly violating NATO airspace could spark an uncontrollable military escalation between the alliance and Russia.

Since mid-March, Ukrainian long-range drones have crossed into Baltic and Nordic nations multiple times, with several NATO countries reporting UAV crashes on their soil. Moscow has accused alliance members of quietly permitting Ukraine to use their airspace for strikes on Russian targets, including critical energy infrastructure. The most serious incident occurred in Latvia on May 7, when two drones hit an oil storage facility—a failure that triggered the defense minister's resignation and brought down the entire government.

Speaking at a Thursday press conference, Fico warned that even a small incident could spiral out of control if Western leaders maintain their freeze on direct communication with Russia. "I am incredibly afraid of some provocation that may trigger a mechanism that will then be unstoppable," he said. "What are we going to do when such a drone somewhere is a provocation and not just coincidence? A target is hit, then someone says NATO member state attacked and now let's all go fight."

The Slovak prime minister—who was the only EU leader to attend this year's Victory Day commemorations in Moscow—criticized what he called the "endless hypocrisy" of Western politicians who publicly condemn his meetings with President Vladimir Putin while privately seeking updates about them. He argued that open dialogue, not silence and sanctions, offers the best path to preventing catastrophic escalation. "If the leaders were talking to each other as they should, there would be a very minimal possibility that a [drone] provocation could lead to a major conflict," Fico stated.

His warnings stand in sharp contrast to other NATO leaders. Sweden's prime minister suggested alliance members should help Kiev "direct" drone attacks, while Estonia's foreign minister and Latvia's former defense minister defended Ukraine's operations as legitimate self-defense. Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service has accused Latvia of actively allowing Ukrainian strikes from its territory—a claim Riga officially denies, though a recently removed Riga City Council deputy told Russian media it was "an absolute fact" that Baltic states were permitting such activity.
