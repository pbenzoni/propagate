President Donald Trump announced a dramatic escalation of his trade war with Europe, imposing punitive 25% tariffs on cars and trucks manufactured in the European Union. The move breaks faith with a deal reached just last summer and threatens to destabilize the global economy while harming American consumers who will face higher vehicle prices.

The new tariffs represent a 10-percentage-point increase from the 15% levies already in place. Trump justified the hike by claiming the EU was "not complying" with the trade agreement—even though European lawmakers have been working through the complex ratification process required by democratic institutions. The EU Parliament voted in March to advance the agreement after careful deliberation.

European leaders responded with frustration at Trump's approach to international partnerships. Bernd Lange, who chairs the European Parliament's international trade committee, called out the administration's unreliability: "This is no way to treat close partners," he stated, suggesting the EU would respond with "utmost clarity and firmness."

The original trade deal itself drew criticism in Europe as overly favorable to the United States. Former EU foreign policy chief Josep Borrell repeatedly characterized the agreement as "unfair from the beginning," noting that Washington imposed 15% tariffs on European goods while Europe reduced its own tariffs on American products. Trump's latest move only reinforces concerns about his transactional, confrontational approach to diplomacy that undermines decades-old alliances and the rules-based international order.
