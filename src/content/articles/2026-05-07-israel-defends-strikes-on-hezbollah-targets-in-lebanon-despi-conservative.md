Israeli military operations in Lebanon have targeted what officials describe as Hezbollah terrorist infrastructure in Beirut and southern Lebanon, even as debate continues over the terms and enforcement of a recently announced ceasefire agreement.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu personally authorized a strike on Beirut that he said targeted a commander in Hezbollah's elite Radwan Force, the militant group's most aggressive operational unit. The strikes damaged buildings in Beirut's southern suburbs and hit what Israel identified as a Hezbollah-linked commercial site in southern Lebanon. Local residents disputed Israeli characterizations of the targets, with one eyewitness claiming the site contained only civilian businesses.

The military action comes amid an unstable ceasefire arrangement brokered as part of broader U.S. efforts to de-escalate tensions with Iran. Israel has made clear it reserves the right to take "all necessary measures in self-defense" against Hezbollah, the Iranian-backed terrorist organization that maintains a powerful presence in Lebanon. Both sides have accused each other of violating ceasefire terms, and Israeli forces continue to maintain positions in parts of southern Lebanon.

The conflict escalation since late February has resulted in more than 2,700 deaths in Lebanon according to reports, though verifying casualty figures in areas controlled by Hezbollah remains difficult. The ongoing instability underscores the challenge of securing lasting peace when dealing with Iranian proxy forces that reject Israel's right to exist and maintain military operations from civilian areas.
