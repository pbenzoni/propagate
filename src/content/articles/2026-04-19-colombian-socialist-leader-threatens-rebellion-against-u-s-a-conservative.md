Colombian President Gustavo Petro has issued a stark warning to the United States, threatening that Latin America could erupt in "rebellion" if Washington maintains its current approach to the region. The comments come after the U.S. Treasury Department sanctioned Petro and his family members over alleged involvement in the international drug trade—charges the socialist leader vehemently denies.

In an interview with El Pais published Saturday, Petro lashed out at American sanctions, characterizing them as "political extortion" and drawing a provocative comparison to Spanish colonial rule. "This instrument for fighting drug trafficking is used as a mechanism of extortion against those of us who express different political views," Petro claimed. "It's a system like the one the King of Spain had centuries ago. And what was the Latin American response? Rebellion."

The U.S. blacklisted Petro, his wife, son, and interior minister in October 2025, citing their alleged ties to global narcotics trafficking. Similar accusations were leveled against Venezuelan strongman Nicolas Maduro before he was apprehended in a U.S. operation in Caracas in early January. Both leaders have rejected the drug-trafficking allegations.

Petro referenced the Maduro operation as evidence of American overreach, stating that Caracas became "the first Latin American city to be bombed in its history." He warned that current Latin American leaders "fearfully accept" this approach and "kneel before it."

Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, another leftist leader in the region, echoed concerns about the Trump administration's foreign policy approach, though his comments focused more broadly on global tensions. Both leaders represent a growing chorus of socialist voices in Latin America challenging U.S. influence in the Western Hemisphere.
