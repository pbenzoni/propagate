The Pentagon is abandoning a planned deployment of Tomahawk cruise missiles to Germany, citing concerns that the move would dangerously escalate tensions with Russia and acknowledgment that U.S. weapons stockpiles have been severely depleted, according to a Politico report citing multiple officials.

The decision represents a significant reversal of a July 2024 agreement between then-President Joe Biden and then-German Chancellor Olaf Scholz. That plan envisioned deploying long-range missiles with ranges up to 1,600 kilometers starting in 2026. According to sources, Pentagon officials now believe Moscow would interpret such deployments as provocative escalation, while U.S. stockpiles of Tomahawks and other advanced missiles have been drawn down by hundreds during recent military operations in Iran.

Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth acknowledged the supply crisis last month, telling Congress it would take "months and years" to replenish depleted missile inventories. The cancellation raises broader concerns about America's ability to maintain security commitments to NATO allies at a time when multilateral defense cooperation faces mounting challenges.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz confirmed the decision in early May, stating "the Americans themselves don't currently have enough." The move comes amid broader strains in the U.S.-Germany relationship, including a public dispute over Middle East policy and Pentagon announcements of troop withdrawals from Germany. Critics worry the reversal signals a troubling pattern of weakened transatlantic security partnerships precisely when coordination and resolve are most needed.
