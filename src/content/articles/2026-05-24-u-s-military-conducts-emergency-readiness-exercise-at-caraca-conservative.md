The United States military carried out an emergency response exercise at its embassy in Caracas, Venezuela on Saturday, marking the first such drill since the diplomatic mission recently reopened. The exercise was authorized by Venezuelan authorities and involved Marine Corps MV-22B Osprey aircraft and naval vessels operating in Caribbean waters.

According to the U.S. Embassy, the drill focused on ensuring rapid response capabilities for potential emergency evacuations. "Ensuring the army's rapid response capability is a key component of mission readiness, both here in Venezuela and around the world," the mission stated. The aircraft involved reportedly belonged to Marine Medium Tiltrotor Squadron 263, deployed aboard the USS Iwo Jima amphibious assault vessel currently in the region.

The exercise drew a small protest in Caracas, where several dozen activists gathered to voice opposition to the American military presence. Protesters displayed a Venezuelan flag bearing the message "No to the Yankee drill," describing the exercise as a national humiliation. The drill represents Washington's ongoing efforts to maintain force protection and emergency preparedness at diplomatic facilities worldwide, particularly in regions where American interests face potential threats.

The embassy evacuation exercise comes amid a complex period in U.S.-Venezuela relations. Former President Nicolas Maduro and his wife remain in U.S. custody facing drug trafficking charges, which Maduro has denied, calling himself a "prisoner of war." Venezuela's interim government has maintained cooperation with Washington following these developments.
