President Donald Trump has rejected a reported framework for limiting Iran's uranium enrichment program, saying a proposed 20-year moratorium is insufficient and explicitly stating he does not want Iranian negotiators to feel they have achieved a diplomatic victory.

According to reports, a U.S. delegation led by Vice President J.D. Vance proposed the 20-year pause during talks in Islamabad, while Iran countered with an offer of only five years. Iranian negotiators also declined to surrender the country's stockpile of enriched uranium, estimated at approximately 440 kilograms. "I've been saying they can't have nuclear weapons, so I don't like the 20 years," Trump told the New York Post on Tuesday.

When asked whether a shorter timeframe might provide Iranian leaders with a face-saving compromise necessary to reach an agreement, Trump was blunt: "I don't want them to feel like they have a win." The comments underscore the administration's hard-line approach to negotiations, even as Iranian officials express willingness to continue dialogue despite what they characterize as "deep mistrust" of Washington.

Iran has consistently maintained that its nuclear program is intended solely for peaceful energy purposes and has never pursued weapons development—a position Iran's consul general in Mumbai reiterated was verified through extensive international inspections. Russia's Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov also weighed in Wednesday, affirming Iran's "inalienable right" to enrich uranium for peaceful purposes and expressing hope that American negotiators "will be realists" rather than continuing what he termed "unprovoked aggression" against Tehran.
