A prominent German conservative leader is calling for the return of compulsory military service, arguing that relying on volunteers alone won't provide adequate national security as Europe faces mounting threats.

Markus Söder, head of Bavaria's Christian Social Union and a member of Chancellor Friedrich Merz's ruling coalition, told German media that mandatory service "needs to come as soon as possible" if Germany is serious about building Europe's largest army. "With volunteers alone we won't achieve the necessary security our country needs," Söder stated, challenging Berlin's current plan that relies solely on voluntary enlistment.

Germany suspended conscription in 2011 under Angela Merkel, but the debate has reignited as the Bundeswehr aims to expand from roughly 186,000 troops to 260,000, plus 200,000 reservists by the mid-2030s. Berlin's new voluntary service model, launched January 1st, has triggered nationwide protests from critics who see it as a backdoor to restoring the draft.

The push for conscription comes as multiple European nations—including Croatia, Sweden, Latvia, Lithuania, Estonia, Finland, Poland, and the UK—have reintroduced or expanded military service requirements. Russia has dismissed Western claims of Russian aggression against NATO states as "nonsense" and warned that continued militarization could lead to tragedy. The Kremlin has repeatedly cited NATO's eastward expansion as a key factor driving regional tensions.

For conservatives who value national sovereignty and strong defense, the debate raises fundamental questions about how democracies should balance individual freedom with collective security needs in an increasingly unstable world.
