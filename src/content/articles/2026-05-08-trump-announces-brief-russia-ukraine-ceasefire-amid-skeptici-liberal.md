President Donald Trump announced Friday a three-day ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine, scheduled for May 9-11, alongside a major prisoner exchange involving 1,000 POWs from each side. Trump characterized the temporary truce as potentially the "beginning of the end of a very long, deadly, and hard fought war."

The announcement, made via Trump's Truth Social platform, comes amid ongoing concerns about the administration's approach to the conflict that has devastated Ukraine since Russia's 2022 invasion. Critics have questioned whether short-term ceasefires without addressing underlying issues of sovereignty and territorial integrity can lead to meaningful progress toward a just and lasting peace.

While any reduction in violence and the return of prisoners of war represents a humanitarian step forward, foreign policy experts emphasize that sustainable peace requires addressing Russia's violation of international law and Ukraine's right to self-determination. The brief nature of the ceasefire and lack of details about broader diplomatic frameworks have prompted questions about whether this represents substantive progress or merely a symbolic gesture.
