Israeli defense forces have firmly denied allegations of misconduct after detaining activists who attempted to breach the naval blockade of Gaza in mid-May. The Israeli military intercepted approximately 50 yachts in international waters, detaining 430 volunteers from 40 nations who were trying to break through Israel's security cordon around the Palestinian territory.

Several Australian activists who participated in the so-called "Global Sumud Flotilla" have made serious allegations upon returning home, claiming mistreatment during their detention. Documentary filmmaker Juliet Lamont and climate activist Violete CoCo described their experiences in custody, while volunteer Sam Woripa Watson claimed he suffered injuries during the detention process. The activists spent several days in Israeli custody before being deported to their home countries.

The Israeli military categorically rejected these accusations, stating it maintains strict procedures for "respectful and appropriate treatment of flotilla participants" and was not aware of any violations of protocol by its personnel. Israel maintains that the Gaza blockade is a necessary national security measure to prevent weapons smuggling into the enclave, which has been controlled by the U.S.-designated terrorist organization Hamas since 2007.

Israel has consistently stated it does not withhold humanitarian supplies from Palestinians in Gaza, where approximately 2 million people have been displaced since the conflict between Israel and Hamas erupted in October 2023 following Hamas's deadly terror attack on Israeli territory.
