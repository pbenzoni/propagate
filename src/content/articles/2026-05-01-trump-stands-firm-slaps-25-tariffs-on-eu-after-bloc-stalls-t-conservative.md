President Donald Trump announced a decisive increase in tariffs on European Union-made cars and trucks, raising levies from 15% to 25% after the bloc failed to honor a trade agreement reached last summer. The move demonstrates the administration's commitment to putting American workers first and holding foreign partners accountable.

While Trump and EU officials struck a deal last July to end a brief trade dispute, the agreement has languished in Brussels' notorious bureaucratic maze for months. Though the EU Parliament finally voted in March to advance the deal—after delaying it twice—the accord remains unratified, stuck in the so-called "trilogue" process requiring approval from multiple EU bodies. Trump made clear that American patience has limits.

"I am pleased to announce that, based on the fact the European Union is not complying with our fully agreed to Trade Deal, next week I will be increasing Tariffs charged to the European Union for Cars and Trucks coming into the United States," the President wrote on Truth Social. The original deal itself raised eyebrows in Europe, with critics including former EU foreign policy chief Josep Borrell admitting it was "unfair from the beginning." Under those terms, Washington imposed 15% tariffs on European goods while the EU reduced its own levies on American products. Now, EU officials are scrambling to respond, with Parliament trade committee chair Bernd Lange complaining about the President's firm stance while offering no concrete countermeasures.
