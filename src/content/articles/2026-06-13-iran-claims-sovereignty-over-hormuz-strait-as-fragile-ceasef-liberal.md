Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi announced Friday that control of the strategically critical Strait of Hormuz—through which roughly a quarter of the world's seaborne oil and natural gas flows—will not revert to its previous international status following weeks of armed conflict with the United States and Israel.

The statement comes as negotiators work to finalize a ceasefire agreement after hostilities that began February 28 with joint U.S.-Israeli airstrikes on Iranian territory. Those attacks killed senior Iranian officials, including Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, and triggered a crisis that has disrupted global energy markets and raised fears of broader regional war.

According to Iranian media, Araghchi stated that Iran and Oman will issue a joint framework for administering the waterway, with Tehran planning to charge service fees to passing vessels. "The Strait of Hormuz lies under the sovereignty of Iran and Oman," he said, signaling a significant shift in the governance of the internationally vital chokepoint. The waterway has remained largely closed throughout the conflict, though the Trump administration claims to have escorted over 200 ships through the strait.

Araghchi also indicated that a pending memorandum of understanding would commit the United States to respecting Iranian sovereignty and non-interference in domestic affairs "for the first time in 47 years"—language that references decades of fraught relations dating to the 1979 revolution. The potential agreement would reportedly declare an end to hostilities across multiple fronts, including Lebanon, though significant questions remain about enforcement and regional stability in the agreement's wake.
