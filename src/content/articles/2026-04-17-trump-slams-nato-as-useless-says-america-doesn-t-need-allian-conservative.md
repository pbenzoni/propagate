President Donald Trump delivered a blistering critique of NATO on Friday, declaring the military alliance "useless when needed" and making clear America doesn't require its assistance in securing the Strait of Hormuz.

The rebuke came after Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi announced the critical oil and gas shipping corridor was "completely open" for commercial vessels. Trump confirmed the development on Truth Social, stating Tehran has agreed to "never close it again" while emphasizing that U.S. pressure on Iranian vessels would continue until Washington's demands are met.

For weeks, the President has criticized European NATO members for refusing to join American efforts against Iran after the regime blocked the strait to ships from nations it deemed hostile. Trump has suggested the United States could reconsider its participation in the alliance over European unwillingness to stand with America when it matters most.

NATO member France convened a meeting of European leaders in Paris on Friday to discuss the Hormuz situation. UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer and French President Emmanuel Macron announced plans for a "multi-national mission to protect freedom of navigation," though only after the immediate crisis had passed. Trump previously accused Starmer of being a fair-weather friend and has suggested reconsidering the trade deal that gave Britain relief from tariffs.

The episode underscores long-standing conservative concerns about NATO burden-sharing and whether European allies pull their weight when American interests are on the line.
