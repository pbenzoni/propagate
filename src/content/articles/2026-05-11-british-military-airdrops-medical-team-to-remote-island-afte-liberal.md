The British Royal Air Force has conducted an emergency airdrop operation to deliver medical personnel and supplies to Tristan da Cunha, one of the world's most remote inhabited islands, after a suspected hantavirus case emerged among passengers from a cruise vessel outbreak.

On Saturday, an RAF A400M aircraft deployed two medics and six paratroopers to the island, along with oxygen and critical medical equipment. The patient, a local resident who had been aboard the Dutch-flagged MV Hondius cruise ship, disembarked on April 14 and reported symptoms two weeks later. The individual is currently in stable condition on the small South Atlantic island, which has a population of fewer than 300 and no airstrip.

The World Health Organization has confirmed eight hantavirus cases linked to the cruise vessel, resulting in three deaths. The outbreak involved the Andes strain of hantavirus—the only variant known to spread between humans through close contact. Health authorities are working to trace contacts of passengers who disembarked at other ports, while most travelers have been repatriated and placed in quarantine.

WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, who oversaw the response operation when the vessel docked in Spain's Canary Islands, emphasized that while hantavirus is "serious," it does not pose the pandemic threat of COVID-19. The initial infection is believed to have originated from exposure to infected rodent droppings at a landfill in Argentina, where one passenger visited for birdwatching before boarding the ship.
