A federal jury in Chicago has ordered Boeing to pay $49.5 million to the family of Samya Stumo, who died in the March 10, 2019 Ethiopian Airlines crash that killed all 157 people aboard. The disaster, along with a similar Lion Air crash in Indonesia months earlier that killed 189, exposed serious design flaws in the 737 MAX flight control system that Boeing later admitted were responsible for both tragedies.

The jury awarded $21 million for pain and suffering, $16.5 million for loss of companionship, and $12 million for grief. While Boeing accepted liability in the case, the verdict highlights questions about government regulatory oversight that allowed the flawed aircraft to fly. In 2021, Boeing reached a deferred prosecution agreement with federal authorities and paid $2.5 billion after admitting it had deceived the Federal Aviation Administration about the 737 MAX's problems.

The case took an interesting turn under the Trump administration. After the Department of Justice initially found Boeing in breach of its 2021 settlement terms, the DOJ ultimately dropped its demand that the company plead guilty to criminal fraud conspiracy charges. Last November, a federal judge in Texas approved dismissing the criminal case against Boeing—a decision that was upheld on appeal in March of this year.

Boeing faced dozens of additional civil lawsuits from the disasters, most settled through confidential pretrial agreements worth billions in total compensation. Last November, another jury had ordered Boeing to pay $28.45 million to the family of Shikha Garg, another victim of the Ethiopian Airlines crash. The Stumo family's attorneys say they intend to appeal to reinstate punitive damage claims that were dismissed during trial.
