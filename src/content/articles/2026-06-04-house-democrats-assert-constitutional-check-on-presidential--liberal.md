The House of Representatives has passed a significant resolution requiring President Donald Trump to seek congressional approval before continuing military operations against Iran. The measure, which passed 215-208, marks a crucial reassertion of legislative oversight over executive war-making authority.

The administration launched military strikes alongside Israel in late February without consulting Congress. Under the War Powers Resolution of 1973—a post-Vietnam era law designed to prevent unchecked executive military action—presidents must withdraw forces after 60 days without congressional authorization. That deadline passed on May 1, though the administration claimed a temporary ceasefire reset the timeline.

New York Representative Gregory Meeks, who introduced the resolution, emphasized the House's constitutional role as "a check and a balance when the administration doesn't follow the Constitution." The measure drew support from all Democrats and four Republicans, including Thomas Massie of Kentucky and Brian Fitzpatrick of Pennsylvania, reflecting concerns that transcend party lines about proper constitutional process.

Michigan Republican Tom Barrett cited constituent concerns about the conflict's economic impact, noting Americans are "tired of $5 gallon gas and $6 gallon diesel." The resolution now moves to the Republican-controlled Senate, where its prospects remain uncertain. Recent military exchanges between Washington and Tehran underscore the urgency of establishing clear legislative guardrails around the use of military force.
