President Donald Trump arrived in Beijing on Thursday for a high-stakes meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping, marking the first presidential visit to China in seven years. The summit comes at a precarious moment, with a fragile ceasefire between Iran and Israel and an ongoing blockade of the Strait of Hormuz—a critical waterway that normally handles roughly 20% of global oil and liquefied natural gas trade.

During opening remarks, Trump offered effusive praise for the Chinese leader, calling Xi "a great leader" and saying he had "such respect for the job" Xi has done. "Sometimes people don't like me saying it, but I say it anyway because it's true," Trump added. The president declared that "the relationship between China and the US is going to be better than ever before," despite ongoing concerns about trade imbalances, human rights issues, and China's support for authoritarian regimes.

Xi used the occasion to frame the U.S.-China relationship in cooperative terms, stating that the two nations "have more common interests than differences" and arguing that "we should be partners, not rivals." The Chinese president emphasized that both countries "stand to gain from cooperation and lose from confrontation."

Secretary of State Marco Rubio indicated that the U.S. would seek to persuade China to play "a more active role" in negotiations with Iran—a noteworthy request given that Washington recently sanctioned Chinese refineries and satellite companies accused of aiding Tehran. China has denounced such restrictions as violations of international law.

Trump's delegation reportedly includes some of America's wealthiest corporate executives, with a combined net worth estimated at $1 trillion, including Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Apple CEO Tim Cook, and Meta President Dina Powell McCormick—raising questions about whether business interests may influence diplomatic priorities.
