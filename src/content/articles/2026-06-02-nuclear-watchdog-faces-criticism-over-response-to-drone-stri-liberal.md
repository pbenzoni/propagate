The International Atomic Energy Agency is under pressure to take a stronger stance after a drone strike damaged a building at the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant in Russian-occupied Ukraine over the weekend. The IAEA confirmed damage "consistent with the impact of a drone" at Europe's largest nuclear facility but has not attributed responsibility for the incident.

Rafael Grossi, the IAEA's Director General, called the strike "a serious incident that endangered key nuclear safety principles." The agency has maintained a presence of experts at the facility since Russia seized control of the plant in March 2022, navigating the challenging task of monitoring nuclear safety in an active conflict zone.

The Zaporizhzhia plant has become a flashpoint in the ongoing war, with the facility caught between Russian occupation forces and Ukrainian efforts to defend its sovereign territory. International concern has centered on the risks posed by military activity near critical nuclear infrastructure, particularly given the potential for catastrophic consequences that would extend far beyond Ukraine's borders.

The IAEA's cautious approach reflects the difficulty of making definitive attributions in a complex warzone while maintaining its role as a technical safety organization. The agency continues to call for the establishment of a nuclear safety and security protection zone around the plant, which has been under Russian military control for over two years.
