China has publicly rejected a *Financial Times* report alleging that President Xi Jinping sharply criticized Japan's military expansion during his recent meeting with President Donald Trump in Beijing. The May 13-15 summit marked the first visit by a U.S. president to China in nearly a decade.

According to the British newspaper's account, Xi reportedly grew animated when discussing Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's government and its increased defense spending, catching American officials off guard. The *FT* characterized the exchange as the most intense moment of the two-day talks. Trump allegedly responded by defending Japan's military buildup as necessary to counter threats from North Korea.

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning dismissed the reporting on Monday, stating that such accounts "do not accord with the information available to the Chinese side." Prime Minister Takaichi later confirmed she had received a briefing on Trump's conversations with Xi but declined to provide details.

Tensions between Beijing and Tokyo have escalated in recent years over Japan's growing defense budget—which increased 9.7% in 2025, its fourteenth consecutive annual rise—and disputes concerning Taiwan. China maintains its One-China policy, viewing Taiwan as part of its territory and seeking peaceful reunification while reserving the option to use force if the island declares formal independence. Japan has indicated that a Chinese attack on Taiwan could constitute an existential threat requiring a military response, a position that has drawn sharp criticism from Beijing.
