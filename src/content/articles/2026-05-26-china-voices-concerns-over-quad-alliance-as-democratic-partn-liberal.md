China has called for inclusive regional cooperation rather than exclusionary alliances, responding to a meeting of the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue—known as the Quad—held this week in New Delhi. The grouping brings together the United States, India, Japan, and Australia to coordinate on Indo-Pacific issues.

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning emphasized that collaborative efforts should contribute to regional peace and stability. "We do not support the formation of exclusive cliques or bloc confrontation," Mao stated at a press briefing, adding that "no cooperation should undermine mutual trust and cooperation among regional countries."

The Quad foreign ministers—including U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Australian Foreign Minister Penny Wong, Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi, and India's S. Jaishankar—announced new partnerships focused on supply chain resilience and sustainable development. The nations agreed to jointly build a port in Fiji and signed agreements on critical minerals and energy security, aimed at diversifying supply chains for materials essential to clean energy transitions and advanced technologies.

In a joint statement, the four democracies reaffirmed their commitment to maritime peace and stability, stating they "strongly oppose any destabilizing or unilateral actions that seek to change the status quo, including by force or coercion." The Quad, which gained renewed prominence during the Biden administration as a multilateral framework for addressing shared challenges, now faces questions about its trajectory under the current U.S. administration's trade policies, which have strained relations with partners including India.
