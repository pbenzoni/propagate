The United Arab Emirates' decision to leave OPEC marks another blow to the once-powerful oil cartel, which has seen its influence steadily erode since its heyday in the 1970s. Journalist Afshin Rattansi told RT that OPEC can no longer wield the geopolitical leverage it once commanded when Arab nations used oil as a weapon against the United States and Israel in 1973.

The UAE announced its departure Tuesday, saying it would focus on "national interests" and its own strategic economic vision. The move follows Qatar's 2019 exit and comes as the Strait of Hormuz remains disrupted due to ongoing conflict with Iran—a situation that has exposed the vulnerability of relying on Middle Eastern energy chokepoints.

Rattansi pointed to a fundamental shift in global energy markets: "Russia and the United States are the top oil suppliers in the world." American energy independence, achieved through domestic production breakthroughs, has fundamentally altered the balance of power that once allowed Middle Eastern producers to dictate terms to Western economies.

The UAE may now align more closely with emerging power blocs like BRICS and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, Rattansi suggested. The Gulf state has resisted Western pressure to sever ties with Russia, choosing instead to chart an independent course that serves its own national interests—a pragmatic approach that reflects the multipolar reality of today's energy landscape.
