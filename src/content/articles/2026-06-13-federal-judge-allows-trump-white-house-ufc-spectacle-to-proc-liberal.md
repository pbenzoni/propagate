A federal judge has declined to block a UFC championship event scheduled at the White House this weekend, despite legal challenges raising concerns about the commercialization of America's most symbolic public property. The ruling allows President Trump to proceed with what he calls "a birthday party the likes of which you have never seen" on the South Lawn.

U.S. District Judge Amit P. Mehta ruled that plaintiffs—including political activist Susan Douglas and Vietnam War veteran Paul Romano—lacked legal standing to challenge the event. Critically, the judge did not rule on whether the event itself is lawful, instead citing the $60 million already spent on preparations and nearly a year of planning as reasons not to halt it at the last minute.

The lawsuit, brought by the anti-corruption nonprofit Public Integrity Project, argued that officials improperly bypassed federal permitting requirements by claiming the event ties to the nation's 250th anniversary celebrations. Plaintiffs also raised conflict-of-interest concerns, noting the event is being run by private entities and could financially benefit Trump, who holds stock in UFC parent company TKO Holdings.

The South Lawn has been transformed into what organizers call a "mini-coliseum," featuring a 28-meter-tall steel structure and thousands of seats—raising questions among government transparency advocates about appropriate uses of national landmarks. Justice Department lawyers defended the setup by comparing it to routine "concert tents" and "Easter egg roll kiosks," though critics note the scale and commercial nature of this event are unprecedented.

Severe weather forecasts may still force cancellation of Sunday's event, potentially rendering the entire legal debate moot.
