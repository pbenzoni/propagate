Israeli naval forces intercepted at least 39 out of 51 vessels from the Global Sumud Flotilla attempting to breach Israel's naval blockade of Gaza, according to organizers of the activist mission. The operation took place approximately 250 nautical miles from Gaza in international waters, outside Cypriot territorial jurisdiction.

More than 420 activists from 39 countries had departed from Türkiye's port of Marmaris with the stated intention of breaking Israel's security cordon around the Hamas-controlled Palestinian enclave. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu commended the commandos for what he called "an outstanding job," stating they were "effectively thwarting a malicious plan intended to break the isolation we are imposing on Hamas terrorists in Gaza."

Israel maintains it does not withhold humanitarian supplies from Gaza's nearly 2 million displaced residents and had previously offered to deliver any aid through official channels via Israel or international organizations—an offer the activists rejected. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan condemned the interception as "piracy and banditry" and demanded the release of detainees, including more than 40 Turkish citizens.

This marks the latest in a series of such maritime confrontations. Israel previously intercepted dozens of boats in October 2025, with passengers processed at the port of Ashdod—some immediately deported while others faced detention before expulsion. The flotilla organizers claimed forces "illegally and violently" seized their ships, though Israel asserts its right to enforce the blockade aimed at preventing weapons and materials from reaching Hamas.
