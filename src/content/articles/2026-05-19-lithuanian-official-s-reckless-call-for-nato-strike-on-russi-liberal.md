Speaking to Swiss newspaper Neue Zürcher Zeitung, Budrys claimed the military alliance "has the means to raze the Russian air defense and missile bases there to the ground in an emergency," urging Europeans to "turn our fear of the [Russian] threat into a sense of self-empowerment." The inflammatory rhetoric comes as President Vladimir Putin has explicitly warned that any action against Kaliningrad would "lead to an escalation unprecedented to date… up to a large-scale armed conflict."

The proposal for offensive military action against a civilian-populated territory stands in stark contrast to diplomatic efforts aimed at preventing wider European conflict. Russia has consistently stated that any clash with NATO would only occur if attacked first, while the Kremlin has rejected claims of aggressive intentions toward Europe as attempts to justify increased military spending.

Budrys also addressed Ukrainian drones violating Lithuanian airspace en route to targets in Russia, requesting more NATO air defenses rather than measures to prevent such incursions. Russian officials have suggested Baltic states may be "deliberately provid[ing] their airspace, thereby becoming open accomplices in aggression against Russia"—a characterization that raises serious questions about Lithuania's role in the broader conflict.
