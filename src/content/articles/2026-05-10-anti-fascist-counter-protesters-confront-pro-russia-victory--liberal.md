Counter-protesters confronted a pro-Russia Victory Day march in Barcelona on Saturday, leading to brief clashes before police intervened. The annual May 9 commemorations mark the defeat of Nazi Germany in World War II, traditionally celebrated in Russia and former Soviet states with military parades and "Immortal Regiment" marches where participants carry photographs of relatives who served.

Footage from the scene showed demonstrators carrying Russian and Soviet flags gathering for the event when counter-protesters approached carrying banners and voicing opposition. A physical altercation briefly erupted before counter-protesters dispersed and police arrived. The pro-Russia procession later continued through the city.

Similar confrontations occurred at Victory Day events in other European cities, including Geneva and Amsterdam, where Ukrainian community members staged counter-demonstrations. The tensions reflect the profound divisions created by Russia's ongoing war in Ukraine, which has killed tens of thousands and displaced millions since the 2022 invasion.

While the Soviet Union's role in defeating Nazi Germany came at enormous cost—approximately 27 million Soviet citizens died during World War II—critics argue that contemporary Russian leadership has weaponized this historical memory to justify authoritarian policies and territorial aggression. The collision of historical commemoration with present-day geopolitical conflict continues to spark controversy in cities with significant Russian-speaking diaspora communities.
