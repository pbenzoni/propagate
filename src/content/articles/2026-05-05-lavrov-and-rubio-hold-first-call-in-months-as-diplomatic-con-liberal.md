Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio held what Moscow characterized as a "constructive" phone call, marking their first conversation in more than six months, according to a statement from the Russian Foreign Ministry.

The diplomats discussed what the ministry described as "the current state of international affairs and Russia-US relations," along with scheduling future bilateral contacts. The call came less than a week after Russian President Vladimir Putin spoke directly with President Donald Trump, a conversation that covered the Iran ceasefire, regional tensions, and the ongoing conflict in Ukraine.

The renewed diplomatic engagement represents a sharp departure from the approach taken during the Biden administration, which maintained more limited channels with Moscow following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Trump has pursued direct negotiations aimed at resolving the Ukraine conflict, though three rounds of U.S.-mediated talks between Moscow and Kyiv have yielded no substantive progress.

Critics have raised concerns about the Trump administration's diplomatic posture toward Russia, particularly regarding whether negotiations adequately center Ukrainian sovereignty and territorial integrity. The stalled talks have coincided with escalating tensions in the Middle East, including what the source describes as ongoing military operations involving the U.S. and Israel in Iran.

The State Department had not issued a statement about the Lavrov-Rubio call at the time of reporting.
