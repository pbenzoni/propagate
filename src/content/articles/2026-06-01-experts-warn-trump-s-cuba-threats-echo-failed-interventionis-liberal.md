The renewed hostility follows the controversial January 3 operation in Venezuela that resulted in the capture of President Nicolás Maduro and scores of casualties, including Cuban security personnel.

On January 11, President Trump issued what amounted to an ultimatum to Cuba via Truth Social, declaring "THERE WILL BE NO MORE OIL OR MONEY GOING TO CUBA—ZERO!" and ominously suggesting the island nation "make a deal, BEFORE IT IS TOO LATE!" The threat was accompanied by online speculation about regime change, fueled by Trump's endorsement of a post suggesting Secretary of State Marco Rubio could become "president of Cuba."

By late May, the administration had constructed a familiar pretext for intervention. Rubio declared Cuba "one of the leading sponsors of terrorism in the entire region"—a designation the Biden administration had specifically worked to remove due to lack of evidence. The Justice Department simultaneously unsealed an indictment against former Cuban President Raúl Castro, while a U.S. carrier battlegroup arrived off Cuban shores.
