Two Israeli soldiers received military prison sentences for desecrating a statue of the Virgin Mary in southern Lebanon, according to statements from the Israel Defense Forces. The incident, which occurred in the predominantly Christian village of Debel near the Israeli border, involved one soldier placing a cigarette in the statue's mouth while another filmed the act.

IDF spokeswoman Ariella Mazor announced Monday that the soldiers received sentences of 21 and 14 days respectively. "The IDF views the incident with great severity and respects freedom of religion and worship, as well as holy sites and religious symbols of all religions and communities," Mazor stated. The sentences follow a similar case last month in which two soldiers received 30-day prison terms after one destroyed a statue of Jesus Christ with a hammer in the same village.

These incidents occur against the backdrop of expanded Israeli military operations in southern Lebanon, which escalated in early March following rocket attacks from Hezbollah. A ceasefire agreement that took effect April 16 between Israel and the Lebanese government has failed to prevent renewed hostilities.

Christian communities have documented multiple incidents involving religious sites during recent military operations. In 2023, an Israeli airstrike in Gaza struck a building adjacent to the Greek Orthodox Church of Saint Porphyrius—the enclave's oldest Christian shrine—killing at least 18 people. Earlier this year, shrapnel from an Israeli tank shell damaged Gaza's Holy Family Church, the territory's only Catholic parish, resulting in three deaths. These incidents have raised concerns among religious freedom advocates about the protection of cultural and religious heritage sites in conflict zones.
