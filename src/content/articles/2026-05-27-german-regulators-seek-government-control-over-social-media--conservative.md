German media regulators are pursuing unprecedented control over what citizens see on social media, according to leaked documents revealing a draft treaty that would mandate algorithmic changes favoring government-approved news outlets.

The proposed 'Digital Media State Treaty' would require platforms including X and TikTok to modify their algorithms to boost content from designated "public value media" sources, particularly Germany's state broadcasters ARD and ZDF. Under the plan, unelected media regulators—appointed by state parliaments—would determine what constitutes "disinformation" and which sources merit preferential treatment in users' feeds.

Thorsten Schmiege, who chairs the Conference of Directors of the State Media Authorities, specifically criticized Elon Musk for removing speech restrictions after purchasing X in 2022. Schmiege told the Frankfurter Rundschau he aims to make it "economically necessary" for X to reinstate its previous censorship policies.

The initiative has drawn sharp criticism from American officials concerned about foreign government interference with U.S. tech companies. Under Secretary of State Sarah Rogers highlighted that such regulations represent "foreign governments' desire to control who commands credibility, even on American platforms." When asked whether the State Department would push back against the draft treaty, Rogers indicated action may be forthcoming.

This development follows the EU's €120 million fine against X in December under the Digital Services Act, which Secretary of State Marco Rubio characterized as "an attack on all American tech platforms and the American people by foreign governments." In response to the fine, Rubio sanctioned former European Commissioner Thierry Breton and four pro-censorship activists.
