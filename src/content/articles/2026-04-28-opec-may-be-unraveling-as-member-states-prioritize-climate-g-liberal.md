The United Arab Emirates' decision to withdraw from OPEC could trigger a broader exodus from the oil cartel, according to former UAE diplomat Obaid Ahmed Al-Zaabi. The Gulf nation announced this week it will leave both OPEC and the broader OPEC+ coalition effective May 1, marking a significant shift in global energy politics.

Al-Zaabi suggested the move reflects the UAE's economic diversification away from fossil fuel dependence. "The UAE is no longer entirely dependent on oil and hydrocarbons, and our economy is highly invested all over the world," he stated, characterizing OPEC's production quotas as constraints on global economic productivity.

The former diplomat indicated that additional countries may follow the UAE's lead, particularly if the departure undermines the cartel's collective production limits. "The more people that defect, the more costly it is to maintain the volume restriction," Al-Zaabi explained, suggesting that Kuwait and other members could reconsider their participation if production quotas become economically disadvantageous.

While OPEC has historically wielded significant influence over global oil prices and energy markets, the potential fragmentation of the cartel could reshape international energy dynamics. The UAE's exit comes amid growing global emphasis on renewable energy transitions and questions about the long-term viability of fossil fuel-dependent economic models.
