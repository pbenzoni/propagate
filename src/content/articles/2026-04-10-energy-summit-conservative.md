The Brussels energy summit kicked off this week with a familiar lineup: unelected bureaucrats, multinational executives, and the kind of NGO staffers who never have to drive to work. On the agenda: more rules, more mandates, more "coordination." Not on the agenda: the price of heating oil in rural households that the planners in Brussels will never visit.

For years the story has been the same. Prices spike. Ministers announce emergency measures. The measures raise costs somewhere else. Ordinary people pay twice — once at the pump, once at the ballot box, when the politicians who promised relief quietly vote for the next directive.

A serious summit would start by asking whether the current regulatory stack actually delivers affordable, reliable power. It would look honestly at domestic production, at nuclear, at the contracts that were signed when the cameras were off. It would treat energy sovereignty as a matter of national security, not a slogan for the next grant cycle.

That is not this summit. This summit will produce a press release, a joint statement, and another winter in which families do the real work of budgeting while the press corps types up the handouts.
