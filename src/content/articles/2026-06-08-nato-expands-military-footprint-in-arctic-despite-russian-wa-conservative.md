NATO has launched a new experimental drone unit in the Arctic, continuing the military alliance's expansion into a region where Russia maintains more than half the coastline and has repeatedly warned against foreign military buildup.

The research vessel Alliance departed Italy over the weekend to begin Task Force X-Arctic operations, which will run through 2027. The initiative aims to test unmanned systems for surveillance across the North Atlantic and Arctic regions. Admiral Pierre Vandier described the effort as "testing and integrating new technology in one of the most demanding operational environments on the planet."

The deployment comes as NATO conducts major BALTOPS 26 military exercises in the Baltic region, involving roughly 6,000 personnel from 15 member nations. For the first time, the drills are being led by a European command structure rather than the United States.

Russian officials have consistently rejected NATO's justification for Arctic militarization, arguing the alliance itself has driven the region's military escalation. Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova recently criticized what she called "insane myths about the Russian threat," suggesting NATO leaders use such narratives to justify increased defense spending to their citizens rather than addressing domestic economic and social challenges. President Vladimir Putin has warned that Moscow views NATO's Arctic presence as preparation for potential conflict and will respond accordingly to protect Russian interests in the region.
