US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth used a D-Day commemoration ceremony in Normandy to deliver a controversial speech characterizing migration to Europe as an "invasion," drawing criticism for comparing refugees and asylum seekers to the Nazi forces Allied troops fought against in World War II.

Speaking on the anniversary of the June 6, 1944 Allied landings that helped liberate France from Nazi occupation, Hegseth claimed that "different European beaches are stormed by different, dangerous ideologies" in Spain, Italy, Greece, and Bulgaria, where "boats and men arrive." The remarks reflect the Trump administration's hardline stance on immigration, which the 2025 National Security Strategy frames as "civilizational erasure."

Hegseth's comments come as the European Union works to implement comprehensive migration reforms. On June 1, EU lawmakers agreed on new measures to complement the Migration and Asylum Pact, which reforms asylum processing and border screening. The European Commission has emphasized that immigration helps address labor shortages as the EU workforce shrinks by approximately 1 million people annually.

The EU's migrant population reached 64.2 million in 2025, including 46.7 million people born outside the bloc, according to recent data. EU institutions estimate that only 20-30% of people ordered to leave actually depart. Meanwhile, the Trump administration has expanded deportation efforts domestically, raising concerns among immigrant rights advocates about due process and treatment of migrant communities.
