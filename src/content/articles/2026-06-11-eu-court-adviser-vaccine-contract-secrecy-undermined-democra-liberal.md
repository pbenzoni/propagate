A legal adviser to the European Union's highest court has issued a strongly worded opinion criticizing the European Commission's decision to keep its COVID-19 vaccine contract negotiations secret from the public. Advocate General Athanasios Rantos argued Thursday that the lack of transparency made it impossible to assess whether negotiators had conflicts of interest with pharmaceutical companies.

The Commission signed six advance purchase agreements worth a combined €71 billion ($82 billion) with companies including Pfizer, AstraZeneca, and Moderna between 2020 and 2021. When Green party members of the European Parliament and more than 3,000 citizens requested information about the negotiation process, the Commission heavily redacted documents, removing negotiators' names and many contract clauses. Commission lawyers defended the redactions as necessary to protect officials from harassment.

The opinion comes amid ongoing controversy over Commission President Ursula von der Leyen's personal text message negotiations with Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla over a €35 billion deal for 900 million vaccine doses. When the *New York Times* sued for access to those messages, the Commission claimed they had been lost. Last year, the EU court ruled the Commission "failed to provide credible explanations" for why the communications could not be produced—a decision that raised concerns about potential avoidance of transparency requirements.

While Rantos' opinion is not legally binding, it will inform the Court of Justice's final ruling on whether the Commission violated EU transparency standards. Advocates for government accountability argue that proper oversight of multi-billion euro contracts is essential to democratic governance, particularly when public funds are involved in pharmaceutical agreements affecting millions of Europeans.
