A British court convicted four pro-Palestinian activists Tuesday for a 2024 raid on a Bristol defense facility operated by Israeli firm Elbit Systems, which they believed was supplying weapons used in Israel's military campaign in Gaza—a campaign that has caused widespread destruction and thousands of civilian deaths.

The activists, members of the group Palestine Action, drove a decommissioned prison van through the gates of Elbit's Filton facility last August and used sledgehammers to destroy computers, drones, and other equipment, causing an estimated £1 million in damage. While acknowledging the destruction, defendants told jurors they aimed to "dismantle drones and weaponry" destined for use against Palestinian civilians, framing their actions as a moral imperative to save lives.

Charlotte Head, 30, Samuel Corner, 23, Leona Kamio, 30, and Fatema Zainab Rajwani, 21, were found guilty of criminal damage at Woolwich Crown Court; two others were acquitted. Corner was also convicted of inflicting grievous bodily harm on a police officer, though jurors cleared him of the more serious charge of intentional harm after he testified he acted in panic while being pepper-sprayed. The four convicted activists face sentencing in June, with maximum terms of up to ten years, though sentences for first-time offenders are typically far lower.

The case has intensified debate over Britain's role in enabling Israel's Gaza operations. While Elbit denies manufacturing or exporting weapons to the Israeli military, media reports suggest components from its UK subsidiaries have been used by Israeli forces. The British government designated Palestine Action a terrorist organization in 2025, though a High Court later ruled that ban unlawful—a decision still pending final judgment.
