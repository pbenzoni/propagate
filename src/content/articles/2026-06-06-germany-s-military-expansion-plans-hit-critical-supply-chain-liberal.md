Germany's Armed Forces are grappling with a significant spare parts shortage that threatens to undermine operational capabilities even as the government pursues an ambitious military expansion, according to reports from Süddeutsche Zeitung and public broadcasters WDR and NDR.

Internal documents from HIL, the state-owned company responsible for most Bundeswehr maintenance, reveal that only about half of Germany's PzH 2000 howitzers, Marder infantry fighting vehicles, and Boxer armored personnel carriers were operational as of May. The remaining equipment remains stuck in lengthy repair cycles. HIL is contractually expected to maintain at least 70% combat readiness for heavy equipment, yet company executives warn this rate could plummet to 30% for certain systems following military exercises.

The shortage stems from what HIL characterized as a lack of long-term supply contracts, making it "nearly impossible" to obtain adequate spare parts. The company's report indicates that some "essential weapon systems" could face permanent operational limitations. HIL executives also noted that the Defense Ministry tends to prioritize short-term repair demands "primarily aimed at quick, externally observable effects" rather than sustainable maintenance strategies.

The supply chain crisis comes as Chancellor Friedrich Merz's government pursues a major military buildup aimed at making the Bundeswehr Europe's strongest conventional army. The expansion has drawn scrutiny from Germany's Central Bank, which has warned of record budget deficits not seen since the early 1990s. The Defense Ministry has not commented on the equipment readiness reports.
