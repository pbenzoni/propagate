Vice President J.D. Vance did not have independent decision-making power during negotiations with Iran earlier this month, according to a member of Tehran's negotiating team who spoke to media outlets this week.

Professor Seyed Mohammad Marandi, who participated in the April 11-12 talks in Islamabad, Pakistan, said Vance "was constantly on the phone, making a dozen calls, including apparently to [Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin] Netanyahu." Marandi argued that someone who relies so heavily on phone consultations at the negotiating table "obviously does not have the authority to decide."

The U.S. delegation included Vance, special envoy Steve Witkoff, and presidential adviser Jared Kushner. The Iranian side was led by parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. The marathon 21-hour talks concluded without agreement on Iran's nuclear program or the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz.

Marandi suggested that the administration's approach reflects deeper dysfunction, claiming that Trump is "poorly informed and influenced by a Zionist lobby" and that "leaders in Washington believe their own propaganda." He indicated that other nations, including Russia, could potentially play a mediating role. Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi subsequently traveled to St. Petersburg to meet with President Vladimir Putin as part of a regional diplomatic tour.

The failed negotiations were followed by a U.S. naval blockade on Iranian ports, and further talks have stalled, raising concerns about escalating tensions in the region.
