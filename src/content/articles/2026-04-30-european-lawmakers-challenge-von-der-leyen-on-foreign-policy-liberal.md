European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen faced sharp criticism from members of the European Parliament during a Wednesday debate, with lawmakers from multiple political groups questioning the consistency of EU foreign policy and the bloc's response to rising energy costs.

Belgian MEP Kathleen Van Brempt raised concerns about what she characterized as selective enforcement of international norms, noting that the EU imposed sanctions on Russia over the Ukraine conflict while remaining comparatively quiet on actions in Gaza, the West Bank, and Lebanon. Portuguese MEP Joao Oliveira echoed these concerns, accusing the Commission president of hesitating to condemn military actions and displacement affecting over 1.2 million people in Lebanon.

On energy policy, multiple lawmakers expressed frustration with the EU's ongoing reliance on external energy suppliers. Austrian Green MEP Thomas Waitz warned that simply shifting dependence from one external source to another represents a "dead-end street" rather than a sustainable solution, arguing that such an approach harms both the economy and consumers. Oliveira called for stronger regulatory measures to control energy prices and support affected sectors, criticizing what he described as corporate influence over energy policy.

The parliamentary debate highlighted growing tensions within the EU over the direction of both its foreign policy stance and its approach to the ongoing energy security challenges facing member states.
