Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has sharply criticized European allies for their refusal to support military action against Iran, invoking Holocaust remembrance to question their moral standing.

Speaking on Holocaust Remembrance Day, Netanyahu accused European nations of "deep moral weakness" after the UK, France, and Germany rejected President Trump's call to help enforce a naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. Iran closed the strait to "enemy ships" following a U.S.-Israeli military attack on February 28.

Netanyahu argued that the U.S. and Israel were "defending Europe" through their military campaign, claiming Europeans have "forgotten so much since the Holocaust." Iran has condemned the conflict as unprovoked aggression and maintains it is defending its sovereign rights.

The escalation follows the collapse of Pakistan-mediated peace talks between the U.S. and Iran. President Trump subsequently ordered a naval blockade of the strait, a critical waterway for global oil shipping. European NATO members have declined to participate in the blockade, drawing Netanyahu's public rebuke.

Critics note the inflammatory nature of invoking the Holocaust to pressure allies into supporting military action, particularly when those same European nations have expressed concern about regional escalation and the humanitarian consequences of expanded conflict.
