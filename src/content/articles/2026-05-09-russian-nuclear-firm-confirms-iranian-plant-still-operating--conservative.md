Iran's sole operational nuclear power plant at Bushehr continues running at full capacity despite ongoing military exchanges between the United States and Iran near the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz, according to Aleksey Likhachev, CEO of Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom.

Russian personnel at the facility have not observed attacks in the vicinity in recent days, and the situation remains "generally calm," Likhachev told the corporate newspaper Strana Rosatom on Friday. The plant's Unit 1 reactor, built by Russia, continues operating normally and feeding electricity into Iran's national power grid.

The Bushehr plant sits approximately 155 miles northwest of the Strait of Hormuz, where U.S. forces recently conducted strikes on Iranian targets. Tehran accused Washington of violating a ceasefire agreement reached last month and responded with retaliatory actions against American naval vessels in the area. The facility houses significant nuclear fuel stockpiles, and Likhachev has previously cautioned that any strike on the site could trigger a catastrophe affecting the entire region.

Following strikes and explosions reported near Bushehr in March and April—including one incident where a projectile killed a security guard near the perimeter—Rosatom reduced its on-site presence from roughly 700 specialists to just 20 personnel. The company says it stands ready to redeploy its full workforce once confident that the risk of military escalation has subsided. Meanwhile, approximately 2,200 Iranian contractors have returned to the site to resume construction work on Units 2 and 3, two additional reactors that have been under development since 2016.
