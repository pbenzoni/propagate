The European Union is weighing an expansion of its existing naval operation to include the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway for global oil and natural gas shipments located off Iran's coast. The proposal, circulated by EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, would give the bloc's 'Operation Aspides' a primary role in mine-clearing operations in the strategic shipping lane.

Operation Aspides was launched in February 2024 to patrol the Red Sea, Gulf of Aden, and northwest Indian Ocean, where commercial vessels have faced threats from Houthi militants. Any expansion to the Strait of Hormuz would require unanimous approval from all 27 EU member states—a significant political hurdle given the bloc's existing reservations about deeper involvement in Middle East conflicts.

The timing of the proposal comes as tensions between the United States and Iran have escalated following strikes in late February and renewed exchanges this week that have threatened a fragile ceasefire reached in April. Maritime traffic through the strait has been severely disrupted, raising concerns about global energy security at a time when European nations are already grappling with significant energy challenges.

Kallas had previously stated that the EU had "no appetite" to expand its naval mission and emphasized that "this is not Europe's war," pushing back against pressure from the Trump administration for greater European military involvement in the region. The UK and France have since announced plans for their own multinational mission to protect freedom of navigation in the area when conditions permit.
