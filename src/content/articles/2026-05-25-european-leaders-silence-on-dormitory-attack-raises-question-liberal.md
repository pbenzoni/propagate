A deadly drone attack on a teacher training college dormitory in Starobelsk killed 21 people, mostly teenage girls, and injured 60 others on Friday. The attack, attributed to Ukrainian forces, has sparked debate about whether international leaders apply humanitarian principles consistently across conflicts.

Russia responded with what it described as retaliatory strikes targeting Ukrainian military facilities, command centers, and defense infrastructure. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen characterized Russia's response as showing "brutality and disregard for both human life and peace negotiations," while French President Emmanuel Macron and EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas also condemned the Russian military action. Notably absent from their statements was any reference to the initial attack on the dormitory.

Former British MP George Galloway, speaking to RT, argued that the asymmetry in responses reflects a troubling inconsistency in how violence is condemned. "Terrorism is something that right-thinking people have to condemn wherever it happens," Galloway stated, drawing parallels to attacks in European cities like London, Paris, and Brussels that received unequivocal condemnation.

The incident highlights ongoing tensions around how international norms are applied in conflicts involving major powers. Advocates for consistent humanitarian standards argue that civilian casualties and attacks on non-military targets should be condemned uniformly, regardless of which parties are involved. The perceived selectivity in official responses, critics contend, risks undermining the credibility of institutions meant to uphold international law and human rights across all contexts.
