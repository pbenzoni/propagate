American forces intercepted four Iranian attack drones over the Strait of Hormuz and subsequently destroyed Iranian coastal radar installations on Qeshm Island in what US Central Command described as defensive operations to protect vital shipping lanes.

The drones, launched toward the strategically critical strait, posed "an immediate threat to regional maritime traffic," according to CENTCOM's Friday statement. US forces then struck Iranian coastal surveillance radar sites in Goruk and on Qeshm Island "to defend against further attacks." The Strait of Hormuz serves as a crucial chokepoint for global oil and gas shipments, making its security vital to American energy and economic interests.

This marks the second US strike on Qeshm Island this week. Earlier, American forces targeted what CENTCOM described as "an Iranian military ground control station" in self-defense operations. The Islamic Revolution Guards Corps subsequently retaliated with ballistic missile strikes on US military installations in Kuwait and Bahrain, though the Pentagon reported all projectiles were either intercepted or missed their targets.

Shipping through the strait has been severely disrupted since recent US-Israeli military operations, with Iran threatening US-linked vessels while Washington has targeted Iranian-affiliated shipping. The escalation threatens a fragile ceasefire reached in April after over a month of hostilities. President Trump stated Wednesday the ceasefire remains in effect, telling reporters that in that region, a ceasefire means "shooting in a more moderate manner."
