The Canadian government unveiled sweeping legislation Wednesday aimed at protecting children from online harms, proposing restrictions on social media access for those under 16 while establishing stronger accountability measures for tech platforms.

The Safe Social Media Act would require social media companies to implement age verification systems and dramatically reduce children's exposure to harmful content, including child sexual exploitation, non-consensual intimate imagery, content promoting self-harm, bullying, hate speech, violence, and extremist material. The legislation would also regulate AI chatbots to mitigate harmful outputs and mandate better crisis reporting when users communicate intent to harm themselves or others.

"We have seen the very serious consequences that online harms can have," said Canadian Culture Minister Marc Miller. "As technologies evolve, we must ensure our laws keep pace, because parents cannot face these challenges alone." The bill would establish a new digital safety regulator to oversee enforcement, holding platforms accountable through meaningful oversight.

Canada's proposal joins a growing international movement to regulate children's online activity and address mounting concerns about Big Tech's impact on youth mental health and safety. Australia recently banned children under 16 from major platforms including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, while Brazil, Indonesia, and France have advanced similar measures. The UK, Austria, and Denmark are also developing protections.

The legislation comes as tech giants face intensifying scrutiny over allegations they deliberately designed addictive platforms targeting children. Court filings have alleged that Meta's Facebook failed to adequately police accounts involved in child sexual exploitation and trafficking, with some content reportedly remaining online through multiple violations. A landmark product liability trial in Los Angeles is examining whether major platforms bear responsibility for harm to young users.
