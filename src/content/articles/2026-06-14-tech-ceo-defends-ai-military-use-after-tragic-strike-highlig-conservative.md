The CEO of Anthropic has defended his company's role in providing AI technology to the U.S. military, even after a devastating strike on an Iranian school that killed nearly 160 people, mostly children, during the opening days of a conflict in February.

Dario Amodei told Bloomberg that the use of his company's Claude AI system in military operations "doesn't even violate our red lines," emphasizing that human operators maintain final decision-making authority. The Tomahawk missile strike on a girls' elementary school in Minab was reportedly based on flawed, outdated intelligence data processed by Palantir's surveillance software, which incorporates Anthropic's AI technology.

The tragic incident raises serious questions about the reliability of intelligence systems and the critical importance of human judgment in military operations. While AI can process vast amounts of data, this case demonstrates that technological tools are only as good as the information they're given—and that ultimate accountability must rest with human decision-makers, not machines.

The Pentagon has recently expanded partnerships with major tech companies including Google, Amazon, SpaceX, OpenAI, and Microsoft to integrate AI into military operations. Amodei stressed that his company opposes fully autonomous weapons systems, maintaining that human oversight remains essential. Critics warn, however, that increasing reliance on AI in warfare could lead to pressure for reduced oversight and accountability, potentially resulting in more civilian casualties when intelligence data proves inaccurate.
