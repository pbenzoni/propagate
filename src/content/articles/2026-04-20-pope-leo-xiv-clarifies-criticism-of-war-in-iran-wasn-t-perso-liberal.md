Pope Leo XIV sought to de-escalate tensions with the White House this weekend, telling reporters that his recent criticism of the war in Iran was not intended as a personal confrontation with President Donald Trump. Speaking aboard his flight to Angola on Saturday, the pontiff pushed back against media narratives suggesting he was deliberately challenging the president.

"There's been a certain narrative that has not been accurate in all of its aspects," the pope said, criticizing what he described as excessive commentary and speculation that inflated the dispute. He clarified that his remarks in Cameroon earlier this week—condemning leaders who spend billions on wars and describing the world as "ravaged by a handful of tyrants"—were not directed at Trump specifically. "It was looked at as if I was trying to debate the president, which is not my interest at all," he explained.

The pope has previously criticized Trump administration policies, including mass deportations and the president's characterization of Europe as "decaying." The war in the Middle East has further strained their relationship. Leo XIV called on all parties to "stop the spiral of violence" and condemned Trump's threat to destroy Iranian civilization as "unacceptable." He also stated that God "does not listen to the prayers of those who wage war," a pointed response after Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth urged Americans to pray "in the name of Jesus Christ" for military success.

Trump responded by calling the pope "weak on crime, and terrible for foreign policy," adding that he did not want "a pope who criticizes the President of the United States." Vice President J.D.
