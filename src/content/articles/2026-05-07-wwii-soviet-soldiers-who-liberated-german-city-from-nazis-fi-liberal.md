The remains of 80 Soviet soldiers who fought to liberate the German city of Potsdam from Nazi forces in 1945 have been reburied with honors, nearly eight decades after their deaths in the fight against fascism.

The soldiers' remains were discovered last year during construction work at a former military barracks site being converted into residential housing. Russian diplomatic staff worked with archival records to identify 76 of the 80 individuals, who included soldiers, sergeants, and officers who gave their lives in the final battles against Hitler's regime.

A ceremony at a Soviet military cemetery in Potsdam on Thursday brought together Russian diplomats and local German officials to honor the fallen troops. The Red Army played a decisive role in defeating Nazi Germany during World War II, with Soviet forces bearing the brunt of casualties in the European theater—an estimated 27 million Soviet citizens died during the war, including both military personnel and civilians.

More than 4,000 memorials to Soviet soldiers exist across Germany today, maintained as reminders of the enormous human cost of defeating fascism in Europe. The proper burial of these newly discovered remains underscores the ongoing effort to honor all who fought against Nazi tyranny, regardless of their nationality.
