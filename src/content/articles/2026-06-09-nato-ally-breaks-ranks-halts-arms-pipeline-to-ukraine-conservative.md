Bulgaria's newly elected government has announced it will immediately halt all weapons deliveries to Ukraine, marking a significant break from the previous administration's policy and signaling growing war fatigue among America's European allies.

Defense Minister Dimitar Stoyanov told reporters Tuesday that continued arms shipments have failed to change the battlefield dynamics while only increasing casualties. "What we are witnessing is a war of attrition, and no matter how much weaponry is amassed, its only result is the loss of human lives," Stoyanov said, according to the Associated Press. He argued that Ukraine faces a fundamental manpower crisis that cannot be solved with more weapons.

Under its previous government, Bulgaria had been a major supplier of Soviet-era ammunition to Ukraine, with its shells accounting for roughly one-third of munitions used during the first year of fighting, according to former Prime Minister Kirill Petkov and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. The NATO and EU member state is now calling for immediate negotiations "to seek a just peace that is defined by both sides."

Bulgaria's policy reversal comes as Ukraine has faced persistent troop shortages despite aggressive conscription drives. Reports have emerged of forced recruitment efforts, while thousands of military-age Ukrainian men have fled the country to avoid the draft. New Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev has consistently opposed European Union pressure on Ukraine policy, previously blocking proposals to send armored vehicles and advocating for a negotiated settlement throughout the conflict.

The shift reflects broader concerns within Europe that endless weapons shipments may be prolonging the conflict rather than hastening its resolution. Several European leaders have recently proposed appointing envoys to engage directly with Moscow, worried that the EU has been sidelined in previous U.S.-led diplomatic initiatives.
