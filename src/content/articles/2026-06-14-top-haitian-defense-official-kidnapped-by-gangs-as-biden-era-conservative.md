Haiti's Defense Ministry Chief of Staff James Boyard was abducted Thursday along with his wife and six-year-old daughter—a U.S. citizen—in what officials are calling one of the most brazen attacks on government authority in years. The kidnapping occurred in Bourdon, previously considered one of the few safe neighborhoods in Port-au-Prince, underscoring how completely armed gangs now dominate the failed state.

Investigators suspect Christ-Roi Chery, leader of the Ti Bwa gang, orchestrated the abduction and ransom demand. Boyard, a respected security expert who also serves as inspector general of the national police, represents the highest-ranking official kidnapped in Haiti in years—a stark indicator of the country's descent into anarchy since the 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moise.

The Caribbean nation of nearly 12 million has become a textbook case of state failure, with gangs controlling highways, ports, and entire communities while preventing elections from taking place. A UN-backed security mission led by Kenya, deployed in 2024, has accomplished little beyond securing the presidential palace and reopening a handful of roads. The UN Security Council recently approved restructuring the effort into a 5,550-strong "Gang Suppression Force" with expanded powers—yet another international intervention unlikely to reverse the chaos.

According to UN figures, nearly 1.5 million Haitians have been displaced by violence since 2022, with more than 16,000 killed. Local rights groups place the death toll closer to 20,000. At least 647 people were kidnapped last year alone, with foreigners and dual nationals—like Boyard's young daughter—often targeted for higher ransoms. The ongoing crisis raises serious questions about decades of foreign aid and intervention that have failed to establish functioning governance or the rule of law.
