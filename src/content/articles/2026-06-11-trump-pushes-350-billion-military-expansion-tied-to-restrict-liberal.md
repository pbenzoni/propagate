President Donald Trump is urging Congressional Republicans to fast-track a $350 billion military spending package that would simultaneously impose sweeping new restrictions on voting access nationwide. The proposal, which Trump has dubbed "Recon 3.0," aims to bring total U.S. military spending to $1.5 trillion while fundamentally reshaping how Americans cast their ballots.

The spending would fund expanded missile defense systems, new fighter aircraft, bombers, drones, and ammunition stockpiles—investments Trump claims are necessary after weapons supplies were depleted by military operations in Iran and years of support for Ukraine. The Center for Strategic and International Studies has noted it could take three or more years to replace certain advanced missiles.

Crucially, the package includes the so-called "Save America Act," which would mandate photo identification and proof of citizenship for all voters while largely eliminating mail-in voting except in narrow circumstances like illness or military service. Voting rights advocates have consistently warned that such requirements disproportionately impact eligible voters—including elderly citizens, people of color, and those in rural communities—making it harder for them to exercise their constitutional right to vote. The bill would also insert federal restrictions on transgender healthcare for minors and ban transgender athletes from women's sports.

The legislation faces significant obstacles even within Republican ranks. Senate Majority Leader John Thune has acknowledged the Save America Act lacks sufficient votes under normal procedures, while fiscal conservatives are demanding spending offsets for the massive military increase. Democrats are expected to unanimously oppose measures they view as voter suppression efforts disguised within defense policy.
