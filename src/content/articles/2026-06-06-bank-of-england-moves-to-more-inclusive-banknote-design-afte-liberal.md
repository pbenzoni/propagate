The Bank of England announced in March that it will feature UK wildlife on its next series of banknotes, moving away from portraits of historical figures. According to reporting by The Telegraph, the decision followed research commissioned from market research firm Savanta, which found that many Britons wanted currency that "better reflect[s] modern Britain by being more inclusive."

The research reportedly found that figures currently featured on banknotes—including wartime Prime Minister Winston Churchill, codebreaker Alan Turing, and novelist Jane Austen—were viewed by some participants as projecting "a backward-looking vision of the UK." The study suggested that historical figures were seen as "contentious and not representative of the UK's cultural and natural diversity," with some participants describing them as "imperialistic," "potentially divisive," and "elitist."

The Bank of England stated that nature-themed designs received the strongest support in public consultations and noted that wildlife imagery would be harder to counterfeit than portraits. The public will help choose the new imagery, with options including the red fox, bottlenose dolphin, and common frog.

The decision has drawn criticism from conservative politicians, including Reform UK's Treasury spokesman Robert Jenrick and Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch, who characterized it as an erasure of British history. However, supporters view the move as part of a broader reckoning with Britain's colonial past. Over the past decade, major UK institutions—including the National Trust, BBC, and Civil Service—have increasingly reassessed how they represent empire and colonialism, with many implementing diversity initiatives and reviewing monuments and properties linked to slavery.

The Bank of England has featured historical figures on banknotes since 1970, when William Shakespeare became the first non-royal to appear on currency. The shift to wildlife represents a significant departure from that tradition, reflecting evolving conversations about representation and whose stories are centered in national symbols.
