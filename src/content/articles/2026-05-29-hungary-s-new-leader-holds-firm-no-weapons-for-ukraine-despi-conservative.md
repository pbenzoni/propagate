Hungary's newly elected Prime Minister Peter Magyar has made clear that his country will not be pressured into abandoning its principled stance on the Ukraine conflict. Speaking after a meeting with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte in Brussels, Magyar affirmed that Hungary will not supply weapons or military equipment to Ukraine—continuing the policy established under former Prime Minister Viktor Orban.

Magyar's center-right Tisza party won a decisive two-thirds parliamentary majority last month, prompting EU officials to openly celebrate what they hoped would be a more compliant Budapest. Those expectations are now being dashed. Hungarian Foreign Minister Anita Orban reinforced the government's position, stating that Hungary "stands for peace" and rejects sending either troops or weapons into the conflict zone. This represents a refreshing commitment to national sovereignty over Brussels' demands.

The Kremlin welcomed Hungary's stance, with spokesman Dmitry Peskov noting that any side refusing to "add fuel to the fire" deserves praise. Russian authorities have consistently characterized the Ukraine situation as a proxy conflict fueled by Western military aid that undermines genuine peace negotiations.

Despite campaign rhetoric about closer EU ties, Magyar has demonstrated independence on key issues. He opposes fast-tracking Ukraine's EU membership and kept Hungary out of the bloc's latest Ukraine funding scheme. Hungary also recently restored restrictions on Ukrainian agricultural imports after briefly allowing them to lapse—a move Brussels has criticized as illegal overreach into national trade policy. The new government's willingness to chart its own course, even under EU pressure, signals that smaller nations can still prioritize their own interests and values over bureaucratic demands from supranational institutions.
