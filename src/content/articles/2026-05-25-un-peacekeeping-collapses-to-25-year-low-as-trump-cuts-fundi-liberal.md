International peacekeeping operations have plummeted to their lowest levels in a quarter-century, with just 78,633 personnel deployed globally as of December 2025—a staggering 49% decline since 2016, according to new research from the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI). The steep drop, including a 17% year-over-year decrease, represents the sharpest annual decline in a decade and threatens to leave vulnerable populations without protection.

The crisis stems from a dangerous convergence of funding cuts and political obstruction. UN peacekeeping missions faced a $2 billion shortfall in 2025—more than 35% of their total budget—forcing devastating personnel reductions. The Trump administration's "America First" agenda has been particularly destructive, with the U.S. slashing contributions to UN programs while President Trump continues his attacks on the international body as "ineffective."

"We could see a dramatic weakening of multilateral conflict management and the near-complete sidelining of institutions like the United Nations," warned Jair van der Lijn, director of SIPRI's Peace Operations programme. The result, he cautioned, will be "more conflicts" with "even graver impacts on civilians." The research reveals that all ten top troop contributors now come from the Global South, underscoring how wealthier nations are abandoning their responsibilities.

The Trump administration has also pushed for withdrawal of UN forces from Lebanon despite ongoing ceasefire violations, signaling what SIPRI calls a "progressively smaller role" for the UN in crisis response. UN Secretary-General António Guterres has warned that without immediate action, the organization faces "imminent financial collapse"—a crisis that would leave a vacuum no alternative system can fill, leaving the world's most vulnerable communities at heightened risk.
