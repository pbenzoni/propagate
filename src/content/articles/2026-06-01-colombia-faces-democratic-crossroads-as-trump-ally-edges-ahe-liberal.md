The contest represents a stark choice between two visions for the country's future relationship with the United States and its domestic social priorities.

De la Espriella has openly aligned himself with Donald Trump's worldview, praising what he calls Trump's "cultural battle against wokeism" and vowing to uphold what he describes as traditional gender norms. He has been compared to El Salvador's authoritarian President Nayib Bukele and welcomed Trump's return to the White House. In January, he publicly called for the Trump administration to "put [outgoing President Gustavo] Petro in his place" and supported the controversial U.S. military raid in Venezuela that resulted in the abduction of President Nicolás Maduro.

He has firmly rejected what he describes as imperial domination, stating: "We are neither a colony nor a protectorate of the United States. We will not submit to any form of imperial or authoritarian domination." In a recent interview, Cepeda condemned Trump's attempts to revive the 19th-century Monroe Doctrine, which treated Latin America as an exclusive sphere of U.S. influence.

The election comes at a critical juncture for Colombia, which shares a border with Venezuela and has historically been Washington's closest security and counternarcotics partner in the region. Relations between the U.S. and Colombia deteriorated under Petro, who criticized Trump's mass deportation policies and military interventions. The runoff will determine whether Colombia moves closer to Trump's vision for the hemisphere or continues on a path emphasizing sovereignty and regional cooperation.
