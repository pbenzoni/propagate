Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton is leading the charge against Meta with a groundbreaking lawsuit that accuses the tech giant of deliberately deceiving American consumers about WhatsApp's privacy protections. The suit alleges Meta falsely marketed WhatsApp as offering secure, encrypted messaging that supposedly keeps conversations private from everyone—including the company's own employees.

According to Paxton's office, Meta's promotional materials have misled millions of users into believing their communications are completely private through end-to-end encryption. However, the lawsuit cites media reports and whistleblower accounts revealing these privacy claims are "blatantly inaccurate" and represent a "complete and total misrepresentation" of Meta's actual data practices. The suit comes as multiple sources have raised serious questions about whether Meta can actually access user messages despite public assurances to the contrary.

The allegations gain credibility from WhatsApp co-founder Brian Acton's own 2018 admission that he "sold my users' privacy" when Facebook acquired the messaging platform for $22 billion in 2014. Telegram founder Pavel Durov has also challenged WhatsApp's security claims, stating users would have to be "braindead" to trust the platform's encryption in 2026. Federal authorities have reportedly been investigating similar concerns about Meta's data practices.

While Meta spokesperson Andy Stone insists the company will fight the lawsuit and maintains that WhatsApp cannot access encrypted communications, the case represents another example of a conservative state attorney general holding Big Tech accountable for potentially misleading business practices. The lawsuit highlights growing concerns about Silicon Valley companies making promises about privacy and data protection that they may not be keeping.
