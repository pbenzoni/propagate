Israel's ambassador to the United Nations publicly confronted representatives from France, China, and Pakistan this week, accusing their governments of cutting backroom deals with Iran to move commercial vessels through the blockaded Strait of Hormuz while other nations' shipping remained trapped.

Ambassador Danny Danon pressed his counterparts during a UN General Assembly session focused on Iran's chokehold over the strategic waterway, which handles roughly 20% of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas flows. "I asked the French ambassador: How much money did you pay Iran to move ships safely through the Strait of Hormuz?" Danon wrote on X after the confrontation. "Surprisingly, he had no answer. The ambassadors of China and Pakistan also had no answer."

The accusations stem from reports indicating that commercial vessels from all three countries managed to transit the strait during Tehran's blockade, in some cases with explicit Iranian authorization, even as broader restrictions crippled international shipping. Iran imposed the blockade in response to a US-Israeli bombing campaign that began in late February, briefly opening the waterway as part of ceasefire arrangements before closing it again the next day.

China, ranked as Iran's biggest oil customer, reportedly secured passage for its vessels as part of Tehran's March announcement that ships from select nations—including India, Russia, Iraq, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka—would be allowed through. Tracking data reported by the Financial Times in April showed a French-owned container ship sailing through the strait alongside other vessels. At the UN, France voted for resolutions condemning Iran's blockade, while China either vetoed measures or opposed critical language. Pakistan abstained from the votes, raising questions about whether diplomatic silence came at a price.
