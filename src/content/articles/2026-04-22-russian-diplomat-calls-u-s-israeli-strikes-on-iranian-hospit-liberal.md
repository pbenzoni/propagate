Russian Ambassador to Iran Aleksey Dedov has publicly condemned recent strikes on Iranian civilian infrastructure, including a hospital in Tehran, characterizing them as violations of international humanitarian law. During a visit to damaged sites on Monday, Dedov joined other foreign diplomats to assess the aftermath of attacks attributed to U.S. and Israeli forces.

According to RT correspondent Saman Kojouri, the medical facility was struck without advance warning while patients and medical staff were present. The full extent of casualties remains unclear. "It's a horrible thing to see," Dedov stated while examining the destroyed hospital. "This is evidence of an inhumane approach by Israel and the US… it's very clearly a war crime."

Tehran Province Governor Mohammad-Sadegh Motamedian organized the diplomatic tour to provide firsthand witness to the damage, expressing concern about what he characterized as inadequate Western attention to the humanitarian impact of military operations in the region. The incident highlights ongoing debates about proportionality and civilian protection under international law during armed conflict.

The strikes occur against a backdrop of escalating tensions in the Middle East, raising questions among humanitarian organizations and international observers about accountability mechanisms for protecting civilian infrastructure, particularly hospitals and medical facilities, which receive special protections under the Geneva Conventions.
