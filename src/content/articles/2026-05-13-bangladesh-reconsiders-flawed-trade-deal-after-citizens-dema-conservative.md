Bangladesh's newly elected government is reviewing a controversial trade agreement signed by its unelected interim administration just days before February's national elections. The deal has sparked widespread protests from citizens concerned about surrendering their nation's sovereignty to foreign powers.

Prime Minister Tarique Rahman's adviser on information and broadcasting, Zahed Ur Rahman, acknowledged Tuesday that the government is prepared to renegotiate problematic provisions. "We can identify the areas that are more problematic and potentially harmful to the state, and conduct an initial review," he stated, noting that scrapping the agreement entirely could affect bilateral relations and tariff arrangements.

The agreement, signed by an interim government led by Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus, contains provisions that limit Bangladesh's ability to make independent trade decisions. According to reports, the deal allows Washington to terminate the pact if Bangladesh enters trade agreements with countries the U.S. labels as "non-market" economies—a designation used for China and Russia. The arrangement also reportedly requires Dhaka to automatically comply with U.S. sanctions and trade policies, eliminating its ability to remain neutral in international disputes.

Left-leaning student organizations held a major rally in Dhaka demanding the deal's cancellation, highlighting concerns that unelected officials compromised the nation's economic independence. Critics argue the agreement's provisions undermine Bangladesh's sovereign right to pursue trade relationships that serve its own national interests, regardless of Washington's geopolitical preferences.
