American taxpayers footed the bill for Israel's defense while the ally conserved its own military resources during recent conflict with Iran, according to a Washington Post report citing U.S. officials. The revelations raise serious questions about whether America's interests are being properly served in the Middle East.

U.S. forces expended more than 200 THAAD interceptors—roughly half the entire national stockpile—plus over 100 additional SM-3 and SM-6 missiles, according to three officials who spoke to the outlet. Meanwhile, Israel reportedly used only 100 Arrow interceptors and 90 David's Sling interceptors, some against less sophisticated threats from Yemen and Lebanon. "The United States absorbed most of the missile defense mission while Israel conserved its own magazines," Kelly Grieco of the Stimson Center think tank told the newspaper.

The Pentagon claims direct spending of less than $30 billion, but analysts warn the true cost—including munitions replacement, lost assets, and long-term economic impact—could exceed $1 trillion. This comes as American defense production struggles to keep pace with demand, leaving our own stockpiles dangerously depleted.

Despite the massive expenditure of American resources, Iran allegedly retained around 70% of its mobile launchers and missile stockpile, and regained access to 90% of damaged underground facilities. President Trump has extended a fragile April ceasefire multiple times, with observers suggesting he's seeking an exit from the increasingly costly conflict. Israel, however, is reportedly pressuring Washington to continue the fight.

Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian has dismissed the notion that "forcing Iran to surrender through coercion" will succeed, while demanding the U.S. end actions against Iranian ships and release frozen funds.
