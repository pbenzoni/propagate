The Pentagon released 72 additional declassified documents on unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) on Friday, marking the third phase of a transparency initiative launched earlier this year. The release includes witness testimonies, photographs, and video evidence of unexplained aerial observations.

The disclosure effort began following sustained pressure from Congress and testimony from military personnel and whistleblowers who raised concerns about government secrecy surrounding objects observed near sensitive facilities. In February, a presidential directive mandated that relevant agencies disclose information related to UAPs, responding to growing demands for governmental accountability and transparency.

Among the newly released materials are reports describing unusual aerial objects observed by military personnel, including recurring sightings of luminous spheres and a 2008 CIA account of a rotating disc-like object reportedly seen above an international airport in Zimbabwe. The initial release in May included approximately 160 previously classified documents from multiple agencies including the Pentagon, FBI, and NASA.

Pentagon officials emphasize that the released files contain no confirmed evidence of extraterrestrial technology or systematic cover-ups. Many documented incidents have been attributed to conventional explanations including camera artifacts, aircraft signatures, military training exercises, weather phenomena, and classified test flights. A small portion of cases remain unresolved due to insufficient data for conclusive analysis.
