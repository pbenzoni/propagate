Tucker Carlson has called out what he described as an "attempted assassination" after an Israeli airstrike targeted RT correspondent Steve Sweeney and his cameraman in southern Lebanon last month. The attack raises serious questions about press freedom and the targeting of Western journalists in conflict zones.

Sweeney, a British citizen, and cameraman Ali Rida Sbeity were clearly wearing press gear when an Israeli F-16 fired a GBU-38 bomb at their position near the Al-Qasmiya Bridge. The camera captured the moment the munition struck less than 10 meters from Sweeney as he ducked for cover. According to Sweeney, the bomb passed through a hole in an already-destroyed bridge, suggesting "no military objective" for the strike.

In his interview with Carlson released Friday, Sweeney explained his decision to work for RT, saying that space for challenging official narratives in Western media has "completely disappeared," particularly regarding the Ukraine conflict. "I have complete freedom to report exactly what I want, and nobody tells me what to say," the former Morning Star reporter said. He noted the irony that RT is banned in the US and EU while Western broadcasters still operate freely inside Russia.

Sweeney also revealed that UK counterterrorism police detained and interrogated him at Heathrow Airport last July, and he is currently under investigation for potential terrorist activity "based on my journalism" alone. Despite the dangers, including the near-fatal Lebanese strike, Sweeney told Carlson he has "no intention of leaving" or stopping his reporting from conflict zones.
