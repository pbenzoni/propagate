The European Union's top court has delivered a decisive victory for LGBTQ+ rights, ruling that Hungary's controversial 2021 law restricting content about sexual orientation and gender identity violates fundamental EU principles. The Court of Justice found the legislation discriminatory and in breach of core freedoms protected under the bloc's Charter.

The law, enacted during Viktor Orban's tenure, banned what Hungarian authorities characterized as "promotion" of homosexuality and gender change in media accessible to minors. While Budapest claimed the measure aligned with child protection standards, the Court of Justice rejected this framing, determining that the law stigmatizes and marginalizes LGBTQ+ individuals while restricting freedom of expression and access to information.

The ruling comes at a pivotal moment, arriving just days after Orban's significant electoral defeat to reformist challenger Peter Magyar. The court emphasized that while EU member states retain some discretion in defining content standards for minors, such authority must be exercised consistent with the bloc's fundamental values of non-discrimination and human dignity.

Budapest now faces a critical decision: comply with the court's order to repeal the discriminatory law or risk substantial financial penalties. The ruling presents an early test for incoming leadership, balancing commitments to EU integration and human rights protections against domestic political pressures. Hungary has previously seen billions in EU funding frozen over rule-of-law concerns under the previous administration.
