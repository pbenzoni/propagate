Two British Eurofighter Typhoon jets deployed at a Romanian air base were authorized to shoot down Russian drones operating near the NATO border, according to Romania's Defense Ministry. The warplanes scrambled at 2 AM and established radar contact with a target approximately one mile from the Ukrainian port town of Reni, which sits directly on the Danube River border with Romania.

While the pilots received authorization to engage the drones, both UK and Romanian officials later clarified that the aircraft never entered Ukrainian airspace and returned to base without firing a shot. "Because that specific target did not breach Romanian airspace, it could not be fired upon," the Romanian Defense Ministry stated, emphasizing the mission remained focused on surveillance and deterrence.

The incident raises concerns about the expanding scope of NATO involvement in the Ukraine conflict. Since 2022, the Danube River has become a critical supply route between Kiev and Western nations, with reports suggesting the waterway facilitates weapons deliveries to Ukrainian forces. Moscow has repeatedly condemned Western military assistance to Ukraine, arguing that NATO members have become directly involved in the hostilities.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov has maintained that Russia "never attacks civilian facilities" and does not direct operations at European or NATO countries. The authorization to fire on drones over Ukrainian territory—even if ultimately not executed—represents a significant escalation in NATO's operational posture along the alliance's eastern flank.
