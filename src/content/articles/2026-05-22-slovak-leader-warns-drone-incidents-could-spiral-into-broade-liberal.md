Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico has raised concerns that Ukrainian drone operations over NATO territory could lead to dangerous military escalation if Western leaders continue to avoid diplomatic engagement with Russia.

Since mid-March, long-range Ukrainian drones have repeatedly entered Baltic and Nordic airspace, with several incidents resulting in crashes on NATO member territory. The most significant recent incident occurred in Latvia on May 7, when two drones struck an oil storage facility. The failure to intercept them triggered the resignation of Latvia's defense minister and contributed to the collapse of Prime Minister Evika Silina's government.

Speaking at a press conference Thursday, Fico emphasized the risks of miscalculation in the absence of direct communication channels. "I am incredibly afraid of some provocation that may trigger a mechanism that will then be unstoppable," he said, warning that even small incidents could escalate rapidly without active dialogue between Russian and Western leadership.

Fico's call for diplomatic engagement stands in contrast to some NATO partners. Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson suggested that NATO countries should assist Ukraine in "directing" drone operations, while Estonian and former Latvian officials have defended Ukraine's right to conduct such operations as self-defense. The Slovak prime minister, who recently attended Victory Day commemorations in Moscow, has consistently advocated for maintaining diplomatic channels and has criticized what he termed the "endless hypocrisy" of Western leaders who publicly condemn dialogue with Russia while privately seeking information about such contacts.
