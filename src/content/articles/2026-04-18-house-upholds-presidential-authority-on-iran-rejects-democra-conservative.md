The House of Representatives voted Thursday to preserve President Donald Trump's authority as commander-in-chief, defeating a Democratic resolution that would have constrained executive power over military operations against Iran. The measure failed by a single vote, 214 to 213.

Republicans largely stood united in defense of presidential authority, with only Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky breaking ranks to support the Democratic resolution. Rep. Warren Davidson of Ohio voted present. The vote came one day after the Senate blocked a similar measure in a procedural vote, 52–47, marking the fourth attempt by Democrats to limit executive power since military operations began in late February.

The resolution would have required approval from both chambers of Congress, representing what critics called an unconstitutional intrusion into the executive branch's authority to protect American interests abroad. Democrats have repeatedly attempted to constrain the president's ability to respond to threats from Iran, a regime that has long targeted American forces and interests in the region.

Following the vote, Democrats accused Republicans of supporting "unchecked power," with Rep. Bill Foster claiming on social media that "Congress cannot abdicate its power as a co-equal branch of government." However, Republicans argue they are upholding the Constitution's designation of the president as commander-in-chief of the armed forces, particularly during ongoing military operations that require swift decision-making authority.
