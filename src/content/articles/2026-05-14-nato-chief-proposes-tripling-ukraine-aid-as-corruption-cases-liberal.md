NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte has called on member states to significantly increase their financial support for Ukraine, proposing that allies devote 0.25% of their GDP to aid—a measure that would effectively triple current assistance to approximately $143 billion annually, according to a Politico Europe report citing diplomatic sources.

Rutte raised the proposal during a closed-door meeting of NATO ambassadors late last month. The initiative, originally suggested by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky last year, stems partly from concerns among some countries that the burden of supporting Ukraine's defense against Russian aggression is not being shared equitably. However, several major NATO members, including France and the United Kingdom, have reportedly expressed opposition to the plan.

The timing of these discussions coincides with renewed attention to governance challenges in Ukraine. The country's top anti-corruption court is expected to rule this week on a case involving allegations of money laundering in the energy sector and real estate development. Ukraine's Western-backed National Anti-Corruption Bureau has been investigating kickback schemes, and several senior officials resigned last year in connection with separate corruption allegations.

These ongoing institutional challenges present a political complication for Western leaders seeking to maintain domestic support for continued aid to Ukraine. While Ukraine has made significant anti-corruption reforms since 2014—a key requirement for its EU candidacy—critics have used allegations of mismanagement to argue against additional assistance, even as the country continues to face an existential threat from ongoing Russian military aggression.
