Iran has flatly denied reports that Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi will meet with Trump administration envoys during his diplomatic tour of Pakistan, Oman, and Russia. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei stated that "no meeting is planned to take place between Iran and the U.S." during the visit, though Tehran's positions would be conveyed through Pakistani intermediaries.

The clarification comes after the White House announced that Trump's envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner would travel to Islamabad for a new round of negotiations, with Vice President J.D. Vance remaining on standby. Araghchi described his trip as a "timely tour" aimed at coordinating with partners on bilateral matters and regional developments, with Iranian state media framing the visits as part of Tehran's effort to secure an end to what it calls "US-Israeli aggression."

The standoff centers on a U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports that remains in place despite an extended ceasefire. Tehran has insisted that talks are meaningless as long as the blockade continues, arguing it violates the ceasefire agreement. The impasse has rattled global energy markets, pushing Brent crude above $100 per barrel.

President Trump has taken a hard line, ordering the U.S. Navy to "shoot and kill" any Iranian vessels suspected of laying mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian officials have accused Washington of trying to force a surrender through negotiations after failing to achieve military objectives, while insisting they remain open to diplomacy if the U.S. abandons what they call bullying tactics. Iran's ambassador to Moscow told RT that Tehran is not seeking war but is prepared to respond if necessary.
