A Canadian tourist was killed and 13 others wounded Monday when a lone gunman opened fire at the Pyramid of the Moon in Teotihuacan, Mexico, a UNESCO-protected archaeological site that draws approximately 2 million visitors annually. The incident underscores ongoing security challenges south of the U.S. border.

According to Mexican authorities, the 27-year-old assailant armed with a handgun ordered a group of tourists to lie down atop the pyramid around 11:30 AM before firing multiple shots. Eyewitness videos show terrified visitors fleeing down the ancient structure, with some sustaining injuries from falls while others suffered gunshot wounds. The attacker reportedly threatened foreign tourists and referenced the pyramid's historical association with human sacrifice.

National Guard troops responding to the scene shot the gunman in the leg. Once surrounded, he took his own life with his revolver. Authorities recovered a handgun, ammunition, a knife, and identification from the scene. Reports indicate an AI-generated image linking the attacker to the perpetrators of the 1999 Columbine High School massacre was found at the crime scene. The shooting occurred on the 27th anniversary of that tragedy.

The wounded include visitors from Canada, the United States, Colombia, Brazil, the Netherlands, and Russia. Eight victims remained hospitalized as of Tuesday. The Canadian Embassy confirmed the death of one Canadian citizen and injury to another in what it called "a horrific act of gun violence." The incident highlights safety risks facing American tourists and other foreign nationals traveling in Mexico, where violence has become increasingly common in recent years.
