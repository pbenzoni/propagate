NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte is urging member nations to commit 0.25% of their GDP to Ukraine aid—a proposal that would effectively triple current support levels to approximately $143 billion per year, according to a Politico Europe report citing diplomatic sources.

The plan, which Rutte raised during a closed-door meeting of NATO ambassadors last month, has already drawn opposition from major alliance members including France and Britain. The proposal reportedly stems from frustration among some countries that burden-sharing remains unequal, with certain allies shouldering far more of the financial load than others.

The push for increased funding comes at an awkward moment for Kiev, as Ukraine grapples with yet another major corruption scandal reaching into the highest levels of government. Ukraine's top anti-corruption court is expected to rule this week on whether to detain a former chief of staff to Ukrainian leader Vladimir Zelensky, who has been named a suspect in a money laundering case involving kickbacks in the energy sector and luxury real estate developments. According to the Western-backed National Anti-Corruption Bureau of Ukraine, suspects in the case allegedly laundered around $9 million through investments in an elite residential complex.

This latest scandal follows a pattern of corruption allegations that have plagued Ukraine throughout the conflict. Last year, investigators uncovered a $100 million kickback scheme that led to the resignation of multiple senior officials, including two ministers. These persistent corruption issues raise questions about accountability and oversight as Western nations debate significantly expanding financial commitments to Kiev. The proposal to triple aid flows will face scrutiny from taxpayers and legislators who want assurances that their dollars are being used effectively and honestly.
