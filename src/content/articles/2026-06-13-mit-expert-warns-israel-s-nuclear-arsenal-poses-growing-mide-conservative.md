Professor Theodore Postol of MIT, who previously served as Science and Policy Adviser on Strategic Nuclear Issues to the Chief of Naval Operations, delivered the stark assessment in an interview with Russian state media outlet RT.

Postol's analysis centers on what he describes as Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's overreach in committing Israeli forces beyond their sustainable capacity. According to the MIT physicist, Israeli military commanders have privately warned Netanyahu that their forces are "on the ropes" and have reached operational limits, with the country experiencing significant troop casualties. The expert cited reports indicating that military leaders have told the Prime Minister they "cannot do any more."

While Israel has never officially confirmed or denied possessing nuclear weapons, the nation is widely understood to maintain an undeclared nuclear arsenal. Iran's nuclear program has frequently been invoked as justification for Israeli military actions, including strikes against Iranian targets earlier this year. However, Postol argues this framing misidentifies the true source of nuclear instability in the region.

In a notable aside, Postol offered cautiously optimistic comments about President Trump's approach to nuclear weapons, describing the President as "extremely horrified and afraid of nuclear weapons, which is good." The expert expressed confidence that Trump would receive strong counsel against any use of such capabilities, noting it would "open a box that none of us want to see opened."
