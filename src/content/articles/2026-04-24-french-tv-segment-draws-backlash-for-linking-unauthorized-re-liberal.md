A controversial segment on French television channel M6 has ignited debate after a sociologist drew connections between unauthorized Christian cross installations and far-right vandalism in the Nice area, according to religious publication Tribune Chretienne.

Sociologist Erwan Lecoeur appeared on a Wednesday broadcast discussing two crosses bearing the inscription "Ave Christus Rex" ("Hail Christ the King") that were recently placed without authorization on Baou de Saint-Jeannet hill, a protected natural area near Nice. Lecoeur contextualized the installations alongside nearby graffiti reading "Nice is Germany"—a slogan historically associated with Nazi occupation and contemporary far-right movements—suggesting the symbols formed part of a connected ideological landscape.

The comparison has drawn sharp criticism from religious commentators who argue it conflates legitimate religious expression with extremist ideology. Critics contend that while the crosses may have been installed illegally under French secularism laws prohibiting unauthorized religious symbols in public spaces, equating ancient Christian symbols with hate-based imagery represents a dangerous intellectual leap. Both crosses have since been removed—one by authorities, one apparently cut down—raising questions about how France balances its strict secular traditions with religious freedom.

The incident reflects broader tensions in French society over the intersection of religion, public space, and rising far-right activity. France's commitment to *laïcité*—the separation of church and state—has increasingly collided with debates over religious expression and the appropriation of traditional symbols by extremist movements.
