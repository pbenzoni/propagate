President Donald Trump threatened military action against the Gulf nation of Oman, a longstanding U.S. ally, if it attempts to participate in managing traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. The stunning remarks came during a cabinet meeting on Wednesday, representing a sharp escalation in rhetoric toward a country that has historically served as a diplomatic bridge between Washington and Tehran.

"It's international waters. And Oman will behave just like everybody else, or we'll have to blow them up," Trump told reporters, according to Reuters. The comments followed reports that Iran and Oman were negotiating a potential agreement to jointly manage maritime traffic through the strategic waterway, which has been effectively closed since U.S. and Israeli military strikes against Iran in February.

The Strait of Hormuz, positioned between Iran and Oman at its narrowest point, serves as a critical chokepoint for global energy markets, normally handling approximately 25% of seaborne oil trade and 20% of liquefied natural gas shipments worldwide. Iran closed the route to vessels from "hostile nations" following the airstrikes, while the U.S. declared its own blockade of Iranian ports in April.

Oman has played a crucial diplomatic role in the region, having mediated nuclear negotiations until the current crisis suspended those talks. The reported Iranian peace terms include demands for reparations, withdrawal of U.S. forces from the region, and an end to Israeli military operations in Lebanon and Gaza. Washington has insisted that Iran completely dismantle its nuclear program, which Tehran maintains exists solely for peaceful purposes.
