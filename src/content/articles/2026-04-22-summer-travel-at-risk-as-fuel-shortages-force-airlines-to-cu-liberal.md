Airlines across Europe are slashing flight schedules and raising ticket prices as a deepening jet fuel crisis threatens summer travel plans. Lufthansa announced Tuesday it will cancel 20,000 flights through October, while carriers including KLM, Ryanair, and Aer Lingus have similarly reduced service amid soaring costs and tightening supplies.

The shortage stems from disrupted tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint where roughly one-fifth of global oil supply passes from the Persian Gulf to world markets. The escalating U.S.-Israeli military conflict with Iran has sharply reduced shipments through the narrow waterway, creating what International Energy Agency chief Fatih Birol warned could become "the worst energy crisis in history."

Europe's aviation sector is particularly vulnerable because approximately 75% of the continent's jet fuel imports come from the Middle East, predominantly Kuwait. The EU produces some jet fuel domestically but not nearly enough to meet demand. Making matters worse, only about 10% of crude oil can be refined into jet fuel, meaning supplies cannot easily scale up even when needed.

Jet fuel prices have roughly doubled since late February, forcing airlines to pass costs onto travelers through higher fares, baggage fees, and fuel surcharges. The Guardian reports that more Britons are choosing domestic holidays due to "uncertainty over flights," while Europe's largest holiday operator Tui notes customers are "demonstrating increased caution and booking closer to departure dates."

EU Transport Commissioner Apostolos Tzitzikostas said Tuesday that member states may be asked to share emergency jet fuel reserves if shortages worsen. European transport ministers are also exploring alternative suppliers including the United States, though the crisis underscores the aviation industry's continued dependence on fossil fuels and vulnerable global supply chains.
