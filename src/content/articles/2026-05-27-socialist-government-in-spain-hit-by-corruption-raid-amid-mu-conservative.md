Spanish authorities raided the headquarters of Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez's Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE) on Wednesday as part of a corruption investigation into alleged influence-peddling and irregular party financing. The Civil Guard's elite Central Operative Unit entered the party's Madrid offices to gather evidence in a case centered on former PSOE member Leire Diez and several former party officials.

Diez stands accused of attempting to influence corruption investigations and undermine Spain's anti-corruption police unit—charges she denies. The Socialist Party claims it will cooperate fully with investigators, though Sanchez dismissed concerns about the probe as "fake news" while speaking to reporters in Rome.

The raid represents just the latest in a series of investigations circling Sanchez's inner circle. Separate probes are examining allegations involving the prime minister's wife and brother, while former Prime Minister Jose Luis Rodriguez Zapatero—a key Sanchez ally—recently came under investigation in connection with a controversial 2021 state bailout of Plus Ultra airline.

Sanchez has rejected opposition calls for snap elections, insisting he cannot call a vote based on "partisan interests." The embattled prime minister, who has led Spain since 2018, warned against what he characterized as the politicization of judicial investigations. Despite the mounting scandals, Sanchez has publicly backed Zapatero and maintained that his party has acted appropriately.

The corruption probes come as Sanchez has positioned himself as a vocal critic of Israel and strained relations with the United States by refusing to allow American forces to use Spanish bases for operations related to strikes on Iran.
