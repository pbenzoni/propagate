The Pentagon announced Friday it has secured agreements with major technology companies—including SpaceX, OpenAI, Google, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services, and Oracle—to deploy their artificial intelligence systems on classified military networks. The move comes despite mounting concerns from experts about AI's ability to operate within international laws of war and its potential for invasive civilian surveillance.

The AI systems will be integrated into the Defense Department's most sensitive networks to support military operations, with the Pentagon claiming the technology has accelerated certain tasks "from months to days." According to the statement, over 1.3 million military personnel have used the department's AI platform in recent months, generating tens of millions of prompts.

Notably absent from the partnership list is Anthropic, an AI startup that refused to weaken safety protections for its technology earlier this year. The company raised concerns that its AI could be used for domestic surveillance or autonomous weapons deployment without human oversight. In response, the Pentagon designated Anthropic a "supply-chain risk"—a label typically reserved for foreign adversaries—effectively blocking future contracts.

The escalating conflict reached a boiling point this week when Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth called Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei an "ideological lunatic" during Senate testimony, comparing the company's stance to "Boeing giving us airplanes and telling us who we can shoot at." Anthropic is currently challenging the Pentagon's designation in court, highlighting growing tensions between tech companies prioritizing ethical AI development and military demands for unfettered access to cutting-edge technology.
