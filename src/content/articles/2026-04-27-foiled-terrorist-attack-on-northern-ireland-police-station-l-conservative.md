Law enforcement in Northern Ireland thwarted a terrorist car bomb attack Saturday night after officers evacuated residents mere moments before a massive explosion outside a police station in Dunmurry, southwest of Belfast. Authorities believe the New Irish Republican Army (New IRA), a dangerous terrorist splinter group, orchestrated the attack.

The perpetrators hijacked a delivery driver at gunpoint, forcing him to transport a vehicle rigged with a gas cylinder device to the police station. Alert officers spotted the suspicious vehicle, raised the alarm, and immediately began evacuating nearby homes. "A number of residents, including two babies, were being taken to safety by officers when the device exploded, engulfing the vehicle in flames and sending debris in all directions," said PSNI Deputy Chief Constable Bobby Singleton, calling it a "cowardly attack."

Miraculously, no injuries were reported, though the blast caused significant damage. Police are treating the incident as "attempted murder," with a counterterrorism unit leading the investigation. The New IRA, designated a terrorist organization in the UK, is the most active dissident republican group in Northern Ireland. Formed in 2012 by factions rejecting peace agreements, the group seeks to end British rule through armed violence.

This attack follows a similar failed attempt last month in Lurgan, where terrorists forced a delivery driver at gunpoint to transport an explosive device that failed to detonate. The New IRA later claimed responsibility for that incident. UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer condemned the attack and pledged that "those responsible will be brought to justice," while his government has stepped up efforts to freeze assets and curb funding for the terrorist organization.
