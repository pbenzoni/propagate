Markus Söder, head of Bavaria's Christian Social Union and member of Germany's ruling coalition, is advocating for the return of compulsory military service to support Germany's military expansion plans. The call comes as Berlin faces criticism over its new voluntary service model that took effect January 1.

"If the Bundeswehr is to become Europe's largest army, military service is unavoidable," Söder told German media Sunday. "With volunteers alone we won't achieve the necessary security our country needs." Germany suspended conscription in 2011 under Chancellor Angela Merkel, but the voluntary approach has sparked nationwide protests, with critics concerned it could serve as a pathway to mandatory service.

Germany has been ramping up military recruitment since 2022, aiming to expand the Bundeswehr from approximately 186,000 troops to 260,000, plus 200,000 reservists by the mid-2030s. Berlin cites evolving security concerns in the region as justification for the buildup. However, Russia has dismissed claims of aggressive intentions toward NATO states and warned that Germany's military expansion could have serious consequences.

The push for conscription extends beyond Germany. Croatia, Sweden, Latvia, Lithuania, Estonia, Finland, Poland, and the UK have all either reintroduced mandatory service, increased recruitment targets, or raised age limits for military personnel and reservists. The head of the European Defense Agency recently suggested that compulsory service could return across the EU, with Germany likely following suit.
