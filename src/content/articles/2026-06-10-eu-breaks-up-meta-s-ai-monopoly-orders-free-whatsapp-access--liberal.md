The European Commission has ordered Meta to stop blocking rival AI assistants from accessing WhatsApp's massive user base, delivering a significant blow to the tech giant's attempts to dominate the growing AI market. The ruling, announced Tuesday, requires Meta to restore free access to WhatsApp for Business API—a gateway the company had locked behind prohibitively high fees to shut out competitors.

The investigation began after AI developers from the US, France, and Spain complained that Meta was abusing its control over WhatsApp's 3.3 billion users to unfairly advantage its own Meta AI assistant. While Meta briefly allowed paid access in March, European regulators determined the fees were designed to be economically unsustainable for smaller competitors—a classic anticompetitive tactic.

EU antitrust chief Teresa Ribera emphasized the consumer protection dimension of the decision, stating it would "preserve choice for citizens across Europe on the AI assistants they want to use with WhatsApp, without that decision being made for them." The ruling also aims to "safeguard competition in the growing market for AI assistants" by preventing Meta from using its platform dominance to stifle innovation.

Meta has five working days to comply or face fines of up to 10% of its global revenue. The company responded by claiming "regulatory overreach," arguing that Brussels was forcing it to subsidize competitors including OpenAI. The complaint echoes familiar Big Tech resistance to European consumer protection enforcement—regulations that have resulted in approximately $7 billion in fines against Meta, Google, and Apple since early 2024 for antitrust and privacy violations.
