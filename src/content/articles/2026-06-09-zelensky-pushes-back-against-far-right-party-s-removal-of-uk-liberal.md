Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky criticized the far-right Reform UK party during a weekend visit to London, warning that removing Ukrainian flags from public buildings could damage the strong alliance between the two nations. The comments came as Zelensky met with UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer, French President Emmanuel Macron, and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz to discuss continued support for Ukraine.

Reform UK, led by Nigel Farage, has ordered councils under its control—24 out of more than 350 across the UK—to remove all foreign flags from public buildings, dismissing such displays as "virtue signalling." The party insists only British flags should fly above government buildings, arguing lawmakers should focus on domestic concerns instead.

"Sometimes little, small mistakes can break a big friendship or huge contacts," Zelensky told The Guardian, urging the party to reverse course. He emphasized that Britain's support for Ukraine serves UK interests by strengthening European security. The UK has provided over $26 billion in military, humanitarian, and economic assistance to Ukraine since Russia's 2022 invasion.

During the same visit, Zelensky also pressed Starmer about frozen proceeds from sanctioned oligarch Roman Abramovich's sale of Chelsea Football Club, arguing the £2.4 billion ($3.2 billion) should be transferred to Ukraine for purchasing anti-ballistic missile systems. The funds remain in dispute, with Abramovich reportedly seeking to direct them toward victims on both sides of the conflict.
