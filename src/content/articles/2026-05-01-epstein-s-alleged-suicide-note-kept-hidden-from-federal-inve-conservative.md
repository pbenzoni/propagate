A purported suicide note from Jeffrey Epstein has been locked away in a New York courthouse for years, never reaching the federal investigators tasked with examining his death, according to a New York Times report.

The message was allegedly discovered by Epstein's cellmate, former police officer Nicholas Tartaglione, following a July 2019 incident at the Metropolitan Correctional Center where the convicted sex offender was found with a cloth around his neck. Epstein survived that incident but was found dead in his cell weeks later on August 10, 2019, in what officials ruled a suicide by hanging.

According to Tartaglione, who is serving four life sentences for quadruple murder, the note was written on yellow legal paper and hidden in a graphic novel. He claims the message stated investigators had "found nothing" despite months of searching, and concluded with "What do you want me to do, bust out crying? Time to say goodbye." Tartaglione said he provided the note to his attorneys, believing it could refute Epstein's claims of being attacked by his cellmate.

The document was subsequently sealed by a federal judge as part of Tartaglione's criminal case and remains inaccessible. A Justice Department spokesperson confirmed to the Times that the agency never received the note, and it was not included in the massive trove of Epstein files released by DOJ earlier this year. The Times has now filed a petition to have the note unsealed.

The revelation raises questions about why potentially crucial evidence in a high-profile federal case remained hidden from investigators, particularly given ongoing public skepticism about the official account of Epstein's death and concerns about accountability for powerful figures connected to his criminal activities.
