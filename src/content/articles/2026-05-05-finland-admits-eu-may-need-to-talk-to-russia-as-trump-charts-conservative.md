Finnish President Alexander Stubb has acknowledged what many observers have long suspected: the European Union may need to break its self-imposed silence with Russia, especially if American policy continues diverging from Brussels' approach to the Ukraine conflict.

In remarks to Finnish media, Stubb suggested the EU should prepare to resume diplomatic channels with Moscow "at some point," with timing likely dependent on whether Washington's strategy aligns with European priorities. "If the answer is that it may not, then we are probably getting close to the moment when some European leader should, in a coordinated way, engage with President Putin," he said, noting such discussions have been ongoing "for the past two years."

The admission comes after four years of Brussels largely refusing direct talks with Moscow—a strategy that has left Europe sidelined as President Trump pursues his own initiatives to resolve the conflict. While European leaders have spent years insisting Russia represents "the biggest security threat," Moscow has dismissed such characterizations as fear-mongering designed to justify massive military spending increases, including an €800 billion "ReArm Europe" plan and NATO commitments to raise defense spending to 5% of GDP.

Calls for dialogue are spreading across Europe. Estonian President Alar Karis recently suggested the bloc should prepare for talks if the conflict ends "quite suddenly," while other leaders worry about being left out of peace negotiations led by Washington. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov has emphasized Moscow never rejected contact with Western leaders, noting "they can just call President Putin"—though he cautioned discussions must have clear purpose rather than devolve into public relations exercises.
