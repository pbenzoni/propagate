A controversial visit by Trump administration envoy Jeff Landry to Greenland has reignited concerns about American respect for territorial sovereignty and Indigenous self-determination. Landry, the Republican governor of Louisiana, arrived in the autonomous Danish territory's capital Nuuk on Sunday to advocate for expanded U.S. military presence—despite reports that Greenlandic authorities had not officially invited him.

During his visit, Landry openly questioned whether Greenland currently possesses sovereignty at all, while simultaneously calling for the U.S. to "put its footprint back on Greenland" through reopening shuttered Cold War-era military bases. His comments come as the Trump administration seeks to expand operations beyond the single remaining U.S. military outpost at Pituffik base in the island's far north. The envoy's rhetoric about respecting sovereignty "even in places where we have had to go in and liberate" has raised alarms about the administration's view of Greenlandic self-rule.

Greenlandic Prime Minister Jens Frederik Nielsen firmly responded that the island's position "had not moved an inch" after meeting with Landry, emphasizing that Greenland's "red lines" on sovereignty remain unchanged. The controversy reflects growing tensions within NATO, as Denmark—a founding member—finds itself increasingly at odds with Washington over respect for its autonomous territory's right to self-determination.

The Trump administration has justified its push for greater control over the mineral-rich Arctic territory by invoking security concerns, claiming Greenland risks falling into the hands of China or Russia. However, critics point out that under existing 1951 defense agreements, the U.S. already possesses rights to expand military infrastructure on the island with proper notification—raising questions about whether the administration's aggressive rhetoric serves strategic purposes or represents overreach into a democratic ally's sovereign territory.
