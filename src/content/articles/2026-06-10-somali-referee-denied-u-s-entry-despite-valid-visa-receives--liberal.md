Omar Artan, a 34-year-old referee who was poised to become the first Somali official at a FIFA World Cup, returned home to a hero's welcome this week after U.S. immigration authorities denied him entry despite his valid visa and official FIFA documentation.

Artan was detained for approximately 11 hours at Miami International Airport before being sent back to Istanbul, even though he had completed all required paperwork for the 2026 World Cup, which the United States is co-hosting with Mexico and Canada. U.S. Customs and Border Protection cited vague "vetting concerns" without providing detailed explanation, though the *New York Times* reported that a Somali national with a similar name appears on a U.S. sanctions list.

The incident highlights ongoing concerns about the Trump administration's sweeping travel ban targeting citizens of 12 countries, including Somalia. The policy has raised questions about how such restrictions may undermine international sporting events and prevent qualified professionals from participating in global competitions on U.S. soil.

Upon his arrival in Mogadishu on Wednesday, hundreds of supporters—including government officials and representatives from the Somali Football Federation—greeted Artan with national flags and banners bearing his photograph. Named Africa's best referee of 2025 by the Confederation of African Football, Artan vowed to continue his career and officiate at future World Cups. "They might think they have broken him, but they have created a new hero," one supporter wrote on social media, celebrating his resilience in the face of what many view as discriminatory immigration enforcement.
