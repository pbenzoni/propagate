Germany is leading a push to fundamentally undermine the sovereignty of European Union member states that refuse to rubber-stamp Brussels' agenda, particularly on foreign policy matters. German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul outlined a six-point plan that would strip dissenting nations of their ability to block EU decisions—a direct assault on the principle that sovereign nations should control their own destinies.

Speaking at the Konrad Adenauer Foundation in Berlin, Wadephul endorsed proposals championed by European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen to abolish veto powers on foreign policy. The timing is revealing: von der Leyen explicitly cited the recent electoral defeat of Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orban—a staunch defender of national sovereignty—as "momentum" for consolidating power in Brussels. Wadephul specifically pointed to Hungary's blocking of aid to Ukraine after Kiev cut off Russian oil transit to Hungarian consumers, arguing that countries unwilling to follow Brussels should be sidelined.

The German plan goes beyond just foreign policy. Wadephul called for expanded use of financial sanctions against member states accused of violating so-called "rule of law" standards—a mechanism Brussels has already wielded against Hungary and Poland when those countries refused to comply with Western European priorities. He also advocated eliminating the principle that every member state receives its own European commissioner, a change that would further concentrate power in the hands of larger nations like Germany.

Critics have long warned that Brussels operates as an extension of German political interests, imposing Western European values on eastern member states. The EU's lockstep support for Ukraine, its abandonment of affordable Russian energy, and its controversial migration redistribution policies—all championed by Germany—have already triggered mass protests by farmers and fierce resistance from nations seeking to preserve their borders and economic interests. Now Germany wants to ensure dissenting voices can simply be overruled.
