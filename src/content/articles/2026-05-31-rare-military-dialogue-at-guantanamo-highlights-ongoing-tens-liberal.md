Senior military officials from the United States and Cuba held an uncommon face-to-face meeting Friday at Guantanamo Bay, the U.S. naval installation on Cuban soil that remains a flashpoint in relations between the two countries.

According to U.S. Southern Command, its commander met with Cuba's chief of the general staff for what was described as "a brief exchange on operational security matters." Cuba's Ministry of the Revolutionary Armed Forces characterized the discussion as "positive" and said both sides agreed to maintain communication between commands. The meeting comes as the Trump administration has imposed sweeping fuel restrictions on Cuba, contributing to widespread shortages and blackouts across the island.

The U.S. has controlled Guantanamo Bay since 1903 under a lease agreement that the Cuban government considers illegitimate. While Cuba retains formal sovereignty over the territory, Washington exercises full jurisdiction and control—an arrangement that has been a source of tension for decades. The base gained international notoriety as the site of a detention facility that became synonymous with post-9/11 controversies over indefinite detention and interrogation practices.

Tensions have escalated in recent weeks. The Pentagon recently announced the arrival of the USS Nimitz carrier strike group in the Caribbean, while the CIA Director made a rare visit to Havana earlier this month. Cuban President Miguel Díaz-Canel has condemned U.S. measures as violations of international law, while stating Cuba remains open to dialogue—but only on equal terms. The Trump administration has defended its policies by citing security concerns.
