Israeli forces have taken control of Beaufort Castle, a 900-year-old fortress in southern Lebanon, marking what Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called a "dramatic shift" in the country's military campaign—despite a ceasefire that was supposed to take effect more than six weeks ago.

The medieval castle, known locally as Qalaat al-Chakif, had previously served as an Israeli military base during the country's two-decade occupation of southern Lebanon that ended in 2000. Netanyahu announced he had directed the military "to expand its ground maneuver in Lebanon," signaling an escalation of operations that many observers say violates the terms of the April 17 truce.

The ongoing military action has been accompanied by intensified airstrikes on southern Lebanese cities, particularly around Nabatieh, located approximately 6 kilometers from the captured fortress. Footage from the area shows significant damage to residential neighborhoods, raising concerns about civilian casualties and the humanitarian impact of the continuing hostilities.

Both Israeli forces and Hezbollah have repeatedly accused each other of ceasefire violations since the truce nominally began. The broader regional conflict, which involves multiple parties including Iran, remains unresolved despite ongoing diplomatic efforts. Iran has reportedly made a comprehensive end to the Lebanon conflict a precondition in Pakistan-mediated negotiations with Washington, though those talks have yet to produce substantive results.
