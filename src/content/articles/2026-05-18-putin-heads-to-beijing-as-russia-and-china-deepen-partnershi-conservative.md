Russian President Vladimir Putin will travel to Beijing this week for talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping, bringing a delegation that includes senior ministers, top Kremlin officials, and heads of major Russian state corporations and banks. The May 19-20 visit marks the 25th anniversary of the Russia-China Treaty of Good-Neighborliness, Friendship and Cooperation.

Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov told reporters that Putin and Xi will hold both one-on-one and expanded-format discussions covering bilateral relations, trade, energy cooperation, and major international developments. Around 40 bilateral agreements are expected to be signed, including a joint statement on deepening the comprehensive strategic partnership between the two nations.

The two leaders are also set to adopt a separate declaration on promoting a "multipolar world" and "a new type of international relations," Ushakov said. He emphasized that Russia and China are "not friends against anyone," but are working toward "peace and universal prosperity." The Kremlin aide dismissed suggestions that Putin's trip was timed in response to recent U.S.-China contacts, stating preparations began shortly after Putin and Xi held a video call in February.

The visit comes as both nations seek alternatives to Western-dominated international institutions and economic frameworks. President Trump made a two-day visit to China last week to discuss Iran, Taiwan, trade relations and other issues with Xi, though the talks produced no major breakthroughs on key disagreements between Beijing and Washington.
