Texas Children's Hospital in Houston—the largest pediatric medical center in the United States—will cease providing gender-affirming care to minors and terminate five physicians following a settlement with the Department of Justice and Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton, federal officials announced Friday.

The agreement concludes a years-long investigation into alleged billing irregularities related to insurance coverage for pediatric transition-related treatments. Under the settlement terms, the hospital will pay over $10 million in damages, discontinue prescribing puberty blockers and hormone therapy to youth patients, and establish what officials are calling a "detransition clinic" for adolescents seeking to reverse previous gender-related medical interventions.

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche characterized the settlement as the first resolution in a broader national DOJ investigation targeting youth transition care. "The Justice Department will use every weapon at its disposal to end the destructive and discredited practice of so-called 'gender-affirming care' for children," Blanche stated.

Texas Children's Hospital denied wrongdoing but said it agreed to settle "to protect our resources from endless and costly litigation," emphasizing its commitment to patient care over political battles. LGBTQ advocacy organizations condemned the outcome. "It's deeply appalling to see [the hospital] capitulate to the relentless pressure campaigns of both AG Paxton and the Trump Administration," said Karen Loewy, senior counsel at Lambda Legal.

The settlement marks an escalation in state and federal efforts to restrict access to gender-affirming medical care for transgender youth, treatments endorsed by major medical organizations including the American Academy of Pediatrics and the Endocrine Society.
