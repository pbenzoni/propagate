A senior Kremlin investment official declared China to be Russia's "most powerful strategic partner" this week, outlining plans for the two authoritarian powers to collaborate on artificial intelligence, logistics, and infrastructure projects that could challenge Western technological dominance.

Speaking at Beijing's Tsinghua University on Tuesday, Kirill Dmitriev, who heads Russia's Direct Investment Fund, emphasized that Russia's vast natural resources and cheap energy make it an ideal partner for Chinese AI development. Dmitriev claimed Russia could fuel AI operations "ten times cheaper than any other source," positioning the Moscow-Beijing axis as a potential counterweight to American innovation.

The comments underscore growing concerns among democratic nations about authoritarian collaboration on emerging technologies. Dmitriev praised China's DeepSeek AI model—which has raised alarm bells in Washington over potential national security implications—for "breaking the monopoly" of countries that "tried to create technology monopoly." The remarks appear to frame democratic efforts to maintain technological leadership and protect sensitive technologies as exclusionary rather than security-driven.

Dmitriev's framing of China and the "Global South" as drivers of growth while criticizing unnamed nations unwilling to "give up constructs" reflects Russia's ongoing effort to position itself and Beijing as champions of a multipolar world order—a vision that runs counter to the rules-based international system championed by democracies.
