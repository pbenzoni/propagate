Cuba's foreign minister lashed out at Secretary of State Marco Rubio this week following new U.S. sanctions on the island's state-run oil company, CUPET. The State Department announced the restrictions Thursday, citing the company's control over assets that were "unlawfully expropriated from American owners years ago" following Castro's communist takeover.

Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez Parrilla attacked Rubio in heated terms, calling his statements "crude lies" and accusing him of harboring "ambitions of conquest." Parrilla suggested Rubio—whose family fled Cuba before Castro's revolution brought decades of authoritarian rule—was motivated by personal vendetta rather than legitimate policy concerns. Secretary Rubio had accused the Cuban regime of "weaponizing" energy resources by hoarding fuel for military use while rationing it to ordinary citizens.

The sanctions come as Cuba faces severe energy shortages, with daily blackouts plaguing the island in recent months. While Havana blames American pressure, the crisis also follows Venezuela's decision to halt oil shipments to Cuba. Both nations have been supported by Russia and China, with Moscow recently delivering 700,000 barrels of crude oil to prop up the failing socialist economy.

Cuba has operated under a U.S. embargo since 1960, a response to the Castro regime's seizure of American property and its alignment with the Soviet Union during the Cold War. President Trump has characterized regime change in Cuba as "a question of time," continuing a decades-long bipartisan policy of maintaining pressure on one of the Western Hemisphere's last remaining communist dictatorships. A former Cuban diplomat warned the sanctions would impact fuel availability and electricity generation across the island.
