Germany has dramatically reduced protections for Syrian asylum seekers, with approval rates dropping from approximately 90% during the 2014-2015 refugee crisis to just 5% today, according to government data released in response to a parliamentary inquiry.

The shift represents a stark departure from the humanitarian policies championed by former Chancellor Angela Merkel, who opened Germany's doors to refugees fleeing civil war in Syria. The country now hosts nearly one million Syrian residents, one of Europe's largest diaspora communities.

According to official figures, Germany's Federal Office for Migration and Refugees processed 3,134 Syrian asylum applications in October 2025, granting protection to only 26 individuals. The agency now conducts stricter case-by-case assessments rather than offering broad protections based on general country conditions. Some religious and ethnic minorities, including Yazidis, Christians, and Alawites, continue to receive higher approval rates.

The policy change follows the 2024 collapse of Bashar Assad's government and the rise of a new Syrian leadership. German officials argue that changing conditions in Syria no longer warrant automatic protection, though critics note the timing coincides with intensifying pressure from far-right political forces, including the Alternative for Germany (AfD) party, which has campaigned aggressively against immigration. Chancellor Friedrich Merz has suggested that up to 80% of Syrians in Germany could return home within three years—a figure he later walked back amid controversy.

Human rights advocates have expressed concern that the dramatic policy reversal prioritizes political expediency over genuine humanitarian need, particularly as Syria's long-term stability remains uncertain under its new government.
