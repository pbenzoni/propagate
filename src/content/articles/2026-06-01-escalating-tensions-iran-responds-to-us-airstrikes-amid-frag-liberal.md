The exchange has further endangered a fragile ceasefire agreement reached in April, which was designed to de-escalate tensions after more than a month of armed conflict.

US Central Command (CENTCOM) stated Monday that American forces conducted what it termed "measured and deliberate strikes" on Saturday and Sunday, targeting Iranian radar and drone command sites. The US justified the action as a response to Iran's downing of an American MQ-1 drone, which Washington claims was operating over international waters. Iran, however, maintains the unmanned aircraft violated its airspace over the Persian Gulf, necessitating defensive action.

The tit-for-tat strikes come at a critical juncture for diplomacy between Washington and Tehran. Both nations are currently attempting to negotiate a memorandum of understanding that would extend the April ceasefire for another 60 days and restart discussions on Iran's nuclear program. The IRGC warned that "if the aggression is repeated, the response will be completely different," signaling potential escalation.

According to Iran's Tasnim news agency, Tehran has been reviewing modifications to a draft agreement after reports emerged that the Trump administration sent a tougher proposal on Sunday. Iran's top negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf emphasized that his country "has no trust in promises made by the US" and will not approve any agreement without assurances that Iranian interests are protected.
