Russia is calling out the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) for its weak response to a Ukrainian drone strike on the Zaporozhye Nuclear Power Plant, Europe's largest nuclear facility. Rosatom CEO Aleksey Likhachev warned that the IAEA's failure to clearly identify who is responsible amounts to giving a "green light for further escalation."

The attack occurred Saturday when a Ukrainian fiber-optics-guided drone struck the machine hall of the plant's sixth power unit, puncturing the building. Likhachev described it as the "first targeted attack on an operating nuclear power unit in human history." While IAEA experts on-site confirmed damage "consistent with the impact of a drone," the international watchdog stopped short of holding Ukraine accountable—a pattern critics say undermines nuclear safety worldwide.

"Radiation knows no borders and does not recognize passports," Likhachev emphasized, warning that nuclear incidents threaten multiple countries for years to come. The facility, under Russian control since March 2022, has faced repeated Ukrainian attacks. In recent months, Kiev has also targeted civilian infrastructure in the nearby city of Energodar, including kindergartens, schools, and vehicles carrying humanitarian supplies.

The Russian government is demanding the IAEA take concrete action and clearly identify those responsible for endangering nuclear safety. Face-to-face consultations between Russian ministries and IAEA leadership are scheduled for early July to address the agency's inadequate response to these strikes.
