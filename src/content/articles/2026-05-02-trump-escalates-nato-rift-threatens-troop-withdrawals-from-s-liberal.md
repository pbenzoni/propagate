President Donald Trump announced Friday that he is considering withdrawing American troops from Spain and Italy, dramatically expanding his threats against NATO allies who have refused to support the US-led bombing campaign against Iran.

The move follows Trump's earlier threat to reduce forces in Germany, where the Pentagon has already announced plans to withdraw approximately 5,000 of the 36,000 active-duty service members currently stationed there. Spain denied the US use of a naval base near Cadiz for attacks on Iran and closed its airspace to American military aircraft involved in the campaign, while Italy similarly refused access to an air base in Sicily.

"Italy has not been of any help to us. And Spain has been horrible, absolutely horrible," Trump said from the Oval Office, questioning why he should maintain troop levels in countries that have declined to participate in the Middle East military operations. The president continued his criticism of NATO as a "paper tiger" and claimed the alliance has been ungrateful for American military support to Ukraine.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez has called the Iran conflict "illegal" and "a big mistake," while condemning what he termed "unilateral attempts by the United States to engineer regime change in Venezuela and now Iran—all without seeking even a veneer of international approval." Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni has similarly criticized the war for adding to Middle East instability and driving up energy prices, and called Trump's attacks on Pope Leo XIV over his opposition to the conflict "unacceptable."
