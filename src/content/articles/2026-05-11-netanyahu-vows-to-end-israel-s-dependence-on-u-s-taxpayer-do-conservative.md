Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has announced an ambitious goal to end his nation's reliance on U.S. financial assistance within the next ten years, a move that could reshape the decades-long special relationship between the two allies.

In a recent interview with CBS' 60 Minutes, Netanyahu stated he wants to "draw down to zero the American financial support" that Israel receives, specifically targeting the financial component of military cooperation. "I've said this to President Trump. I've said it to our own people. Their jaws drop," Netanyahu explained. Israel currently receives approximately $3.8 billion annually under a ten-year agreement signed in 2016, with American assistance accounting for roughly 16% of the country's military budget.

The Israeli leader acknowledged declining American support for Israel, citing recent polling showing six in ten Americans now hold unfavorable views of the nation—a nearly 20-point increase since 2022. Rather than attributing this shift to Israeli military operations, Netanyahu pointed to what he called foreign manipulation of social media platforms. "Israel is besieged on the media front, on the propaganda front," he said, claiming that "several countries" have used "bot farms with fake addresses, to break the American sympathy to Israel."

The announcement comes as Israel has received more than $300 billion in economic and military assistance from Washington since 1948, making it the largest cumulative recipient of U.S. foreign aid since World War II. Netanyahu's proposed timeline would begin immediately and conclude by the mid-2030s, potentially reducing the burden on American taxpayers while testing Israel's ability to maintain its defensive capabilities independently.
