Canada's Governor General has finally stripped a convicted child predator of the nation's highest civilian honor—but only after he spent years behind bars following his 2019 conviction. Peter Dalglish, co-founder of Street Kids International, was awarded the Order of Canada in 2016 for his supposed humanitarian work. Three years later, he was convicted in Nepal of raping two boys and sentenced to 16 years in prison.

The case raises serious questions about vetting procedures for national honors. Dalglish had worked since the late 1980s with vulnerable youth across Africa and Asia, including stints with UN agencies in Afghanistan and Liberia. Nepalese authorities arrested him in April 2018 after raiding his mountain villa near Kathmandu, where they found two boys, ages 11 and 14. Investigators alleged he lured children from poor families with false promises of education and opportunities.

The governor general's office called the April 15 revocation an "extraordinary measure," stating Dalglish "acted in a manner inconsistent with the standard of conduct expected." Yet the award wasn't rescinded until six years after it was granted and five years after his arrest. This follows a troubling pattern in Nepal, where multiple foreign nationals have been accused of exploiting their positions as aid workers to prey on vulnerable children.

Dalglish denies the charges, with his attorney claiming he was framed. However, a Nepalese court found him guilty in July 2019 and ordered him to pay approximately $10,000 to his victims in addition to his prison sentence. The delayed response from Canadian authorities highlights concerning gaps in accountability when prestigious national honors are awarded to individuals whose true character later comes to light.
