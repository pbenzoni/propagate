Tensions between the United States and Iran intensified sharply after peace negotiations in Islamabad ended without agreement, prompting President Donald Trump to order a naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz—one of the world's most vital energy shipping corridors.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian responded by stating that diplomatic progress remains possible only if "the American government abandons its totalitarianism," a reference to what Tehran views as Washington's refusal to negotiate on equal terms. The breakdown came after U.S. Vice President J.D. Vance led negotiations that Iranian sources say were undermined by inflexible American demands on uranium enrichment, transit fees through the strait, and Iran's peaceful nuclear energy program.

Trump announced the blockade on social media, threatening to intercept any commercial vessel that has paid transit fees to Iran and ordering minesweeping operations in international waters. The move risks disrupting global energy markets, with Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warning Americans to "enjoy the current pump figures" before fuel prices rise. Notably, the United Kingdom—a key NATO ally—has reportedly declined to participate in the blockade operation.

The escalation has prompted Israel to raise its military alert level and drawn international concern about the potential for broader conflict in a region that handles roughly one-fifth of the world's petroleum traffic. Iran has insisted it will meet any hostile naval operations in the strait with force, while maintaining that vessels require Iranian permission to transit the waterway safely.
