President Donald Trump announced plans to declassify government files on unidentified flying objects and unidentified aerial phenomena, describing some materials as "very interesting" during a White House briefing this week. The move follows an executive order signed in February directing the Department of Defense to disclose information related to UFO sightings.

The Pentagon has indicated it is reviewing and consolidating relevant records before any public release, citing security concerns. Trump referenced conversations with pilots who reported unusual sightings, though the Department of Defense has previously stated it found no evidence that unidentified aerial phenomena represent alien technology. A 2024 Pentagon report noted that most cases involved weather balloons, spy planes, satellites, or other conventional explanations.

The announcement comes amid ongoing questions about government transparency and the handling of classified information. Between May 2023 and June 2024, the Pentagon's All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office received 757 reports of unidentified aerial phenomena, with 21 cases flagged for further analysis due to "anomalous characteristics and/or behaviors." The office has been collecting such reports for decades, though previous investigations have not substantiated claims of extraterrestrial technology.

Critics have raised concerns about the process for declassifying sensitive national security materials and the potential implications for ongoing intelligence operations. The administration has not provided a specific timeline for the document release, saying only that it will occur "in the near future" to address what Trump characterized as "tremendous interest" from the American public.
