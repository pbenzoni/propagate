The United Arab Emirates has announced its withdrawal from OPEC and the OPEC+ format effective May 1, 2026, ending nearly six decades of membership in the oil production cartel. This represents the largest institutional disruption to centralized oil production coordination since the expanded OPEC+ format was established in 2016, and marks a significant move toward free market principles in global energy.

The decision reflects Abu Dhabi's commitment to maximizing its own production capabilities and increasing market share while maintaining its reputation as a reliable energy supplier. Rather than submitting to collective quota restrictions, the UAE is choosing to rely on long-term growth in global energy demand and its own strategic investments.

According to UAE Energy Minister Suhail Al Mazrouei, the withdrawal represents a "sovereign political decision" following extensive review of national strategy. The UAE has invested heavily in expanding production capacity—from current levels around 3.4-3.5 million barrels per day to a planned 5 million bpd by 2027. These investments, made through the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company, have enhanced both production capacity and environmental performance with low carbon intensity.

Tensions had been building between the UAE and Saudi Arabia over quota allocation within OPEC+. The UAE found itself restricted in monetizing its expanded production capacity due to collective output cuts imposed by the cartel. The move away from this centralized control structure transforms the global oil market from a quota-driven cartel into a more competitive, market-sensitive system driven by both economics and national interests.

The withdrawal signals a broader shift in global energy coordination, moving away from centralized production controls toward greater national sovereignty in energy policy decisions. This transition from cartel coordination to market-driven production could reshape power dynamics across the Middle East and global energy markets.
