A photograph showing an Israeli Defense Forces soldier placing a cigarette in the mouth of a Virgin Mary statue in southern Lebanon has ignited widespread condemnation online. The image, which surfaced this week, was reportedly taken in the predominantly Christian village of Dibil near the Israeli border and shared on social media by IDF personnel.

The IDF acknowledged the incident in a statement, calling it a matter of "utmost severity" and saying the soldier's conduct "completely deviates" from military values. The military said the photo was taken several weeks ago and that disciplinary measures would follow an investigation, emphasizing respect for religious symbols and holy sites.

This desecration follows a troubling pattern of incidents affecting Lebanon's Christian communities. In April, video emerged from the same village showing an Israeli soldier using a mallet to destroy the face of a Jesus Christ statue during ongoing ground operations. Two soldiers were subsequently jailed for 30 days after that footage went viral.

Christian communities have documented multiple incidents at religious sites in recent years. A 2023 Israeli airstrike in Gaza struck near the Greek Orthodox Church of Saint Porphyrius, the enclave's oldest Christian shrine, killing at least 18 people. Earlier this year, shrapnel from an Israeli tank damaged Gaza's Holy Family Church, the territory's only Catholic parish, killing three. Last month, a Catholic nun was assaulted in Jerusalem in what Church officials characterized as part of a broader pattern of harassment against Christians.

Lebanese authorities report that more than 2,700 people have been killed and approximately 1.2 million displaced since Israel launched combat operations in Lebanon in early March. Despite a ceasefire agreement that began in mid-April and was extended into May, Israeli forces have continued military operations and expanded a buffer zone stretching roughly 10 kilometers into Lebanese territory.
