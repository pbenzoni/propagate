A troubling pattern of social distress is emerging across the United States, revealing the consequences of policy choices that have prioritized corporate profits over community well-being. Despite decades of economic growth concentrated at the top, the material security and life prospects of working Americans have steadily deteriorated.

Since the 1970s, birth rates have declined precipitously—falling to 1.6 children per woman by 2023, with births returning to 1979 levels despite the population being a third larger. This decline cuts across racial and ethnic lines, affecting white, Black, and Latino communities alike. Meanwhile, rising mortality rates, particularly among working-class Americans, point to what researchers have termed "deaths of despair"—fatalities linked to substance abuse, suicide, and chronic health conditions exacerbated by economic insecurity and inadequate healthcare access.

The United States has become increasingly diverse through immigration, with communities of color growing as a share of the population. In major cities like New York, Los Angeles, and Chicago, no single racial group now constitutes a majority. This demographic evolution represents both the enduring appeal of America as a destination and a rebuke to nativist narratives—immigrants continue seeking opportunity here even as systemic failures leave too many longtime residents struggling.

Yet immigration alone cannot address the underlying crisis. The fundamental issue is not demographic change but rather the collapse of the social compact: stagnant wages, unaffordable healthcare, crushing student debt, and the erosion of communities devastated by deindustrialization and the opioid epidemic. These conditions reflect policy choices—deregulation, weakened labor protections, and disinvestment from public goods—that have concentrated wealth upward while leaving working families behind.

Addressing this crisis requires confronting its root causes: restoring worker power, guaranteeing healthcare as a right, investing in education and infrastructure, and building an economy that works for everyone, not just those at the top.
