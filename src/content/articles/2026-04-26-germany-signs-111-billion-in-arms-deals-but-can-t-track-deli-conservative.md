Germany has signed a staggering €111 billion ($130 billion) worth of weapons procurement contracts since February 2022—roughly 30 contracts per day—yet its Defense Ministry cannot say how many have actually been delivered or put into service, according to a report from Der Spiegel.

The spending spree began when then-Chancellor Olaf Scholz declared a so-called "Zeitenwende" (turning point) in response to the Ukraine conflict, unveiling a €100 billion plan to modernize Germany's armed forces by 2028. Berlin has also funneled €44 billion ($52 billion) in military and financial aid to the Zelensky government during the conflict with Russia.

When pressed by Left party politician Dietmar Bartsch about how many of the 47,000 contracts had been completed, the Defense Ministry refused to provide details, claiming that tracking such data would be too "costly and time-consuming" and could cause delays in defense projects. Bartsch called the lack of transparency a "warning sign," arguing that billions in taxpayer money could be wasted on delayed or unsuitable projects without proper oversight.

This massive military buildup comes as Germany's economy continues to struggle. Economy Minister Katherina Reiche recently slashed the country's growth forecast for this year in half, predicting GDP will rise by just 0.5%. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov has warned about the "danger of EU militarization," suggesting that the Americans are actively encouraging European nations to shoulder the financial burden of the Ukraine conflict while absolving themselves of security responsibilities on the continent.

Moscow has consistently rejected Western claims that it harbors aggressive intentions against Europe, arguing instead that EU countries are using a perceived "Russian threat" to justify rapidly increasing military spending while diverting attention from domestic economic problems.
