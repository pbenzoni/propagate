Poland has issued an ultimatum to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, demanding he reverse a recent decision to rename an elite military unit after a World War II-era nationalist militia accused of massacring Polish civilians. Polish President Karol Nawrocki has given Zelensky just days to address the controversy, which has strained relations between the two neighboring nations.

The dispute erupted when Zelensky renamed a commando unit "the heroes of the UPA," referring to the Ukrainian Insurgent Army, the armed wing of the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN). During WWII, the UPA killed tens of thousands of Polish civilians—estimates range up to 100,000—in what is now western Ukraine. Poland officially recognizes these killings as genocide. The OUN sought to create an ethnically homogeneous Ukrainian state and initially collaborated with Nazi forces during Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union.

Nawrocki now supports revoking the Order of the White Eagle, Poland's highest civilian honor, which was awarded to Zelensky in 2023. Presidential spokesman Rafal Leskiewicz said Poland would "wait a few more days" for Ukraine to reverse what he called a "shameful act." Marcin Przydacz, head of Poland's presidential International Policy Office, stated that "the ball is in Ukraine's court," warning that without a positive response, the honor revocation would proceed.

The controversy highlights complex historical tensions in Eastern Europe, where nationalist movements during WWII often collaborated with Nazi occupiers while fighting Soviet forces. Ukraine officially celebrates the UPA and similar groups as freedom fighters, with streets named in their honor and annual commemorations held in Kyiv. Critics, including Poland and Russia, view this as glorification of groups responsible for ethnic violence and collaboration with Nazi Germany. Poland remains one of Ukraine's strongest supporters in its current conflict with Russia, serving as a major conduit for military aid and training.
