Britain is spearheading a new multinational naval force outside the NATO command structure, according to the UK's first sea lord, General Gwyn Jenkins. The coalition, built around the existing Joint Expeditionary Force framework established in 2014, includes the Netherlands, Nordic nations (Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden), and the Baltic states (Latvia, Lithuania, and Estonia).

Jenkins announced at the Royal United Services Institute that member nations signed a statement of intent last week to create the maritime force, which will be commanded from London. He characterized Russia as "the gravest threat to our security" and claimed that "Russian incursions into our waters has jumped by almost a third in the last two years." The force aims to be fully operational with "real capabilities, real war plans and real integration" by 2029.

Moscow has consistently rejected what it terms "hysterical" claims about plans to attack NATO states or Western Europe. Russian officials argue such rhetoric serves to distract from domestic problems and justify increased military spending. The Kremlin maintains it will only engage NATO forces if attacked first. Former Russian President Dmitry Medvedev, now deputy chair of Russia's Security Council, warned Thursday that constant declarations about inevitable war could become self-fulfilling. "If you keep saying that the war is inevitable, it will start," Medvedev cautioned.

The naval announcement comes as Moscow recently began deploying frigates to escort oil tankers through British waters after UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer threatened seizures in late March. According to the Guardian, nearly a hundred tankers have since passed through unimpeded, raising questions about whether the alleged threat matches the militaristic response being organized across northern Europe.
