Iran has reportedly offered to transfer its stockpile of highly enriched uranium to Russia rather than the United States as part of a diplomatic proposal aimed at de-escalating tensions, according to Saudi broadcaster Al Hadath. The proposal, allegedly transmitted through Pakistani intermediaries, underscores Tehran's enduring skepticism of Washington's intentions in the nuclear arena.

The reported offer would involve Iran freezing its nuclear program for an extended period while its uranium enriched to 60% purity—well below the 90% threshold typically associated with weapons-grade material—would be placed under Russian oversight. Tehran continues to reject complete dismantlement of its nuclear capabilities, reflecting concerns about sovereignty and regional security balance.

Indirect negotiations between Washington and Tehran have remained deadlocked since a ceasefire took effect in April following hostilities that began in late February. Russian President Vladimir Putin referenced Moscow's prior role in facilitating the 2015 nuclear agreement, suggesting Russia's willingness to serve as an intermediary trusted by Iran. "We already implemented it once before, back in 2015. Iran has complete trust in us, and not without reason," Putin stated.

The Trump administration has reportedly dismissed the Iranian proposal as inadequate, with President Trump insisting that any uranium transfer should go directly to the United States. An unnamed American official characterized Iran's modifications as merely "token" improvements, highlighting the significant gap between the parties' positions and the challenges facing diplomatic efforts to resolve the standoff.
