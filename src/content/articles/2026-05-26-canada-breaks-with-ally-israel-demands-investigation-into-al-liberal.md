The Canadian government has called for an independent investigation into what Prime Minister Mark Carney described as Israel's "appalling" treatment of humanitarian activists aboard a Gaza-bound aid flotilla, marking a significant departure from Canada's historically close relationship with Israel.

The diplomatic confrontation follows Israel's interception of the flotilla in international waters on May 18-19, during which hundreds of activists attempting to deliver humanitarian supplies to Gaza were detained. Foreign Minister Anita Anand stated that Israel violated the Vienna Convention—an international treaty protecting detained foreign nationals' rights to consular access—and said Ottawa had provided evidence of abuse to Israeli authorities.

Activists have alleged they endured beatings, tasers, sexual assault, humiliation, stress positions, and denial of food and water during detention. Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir released footage showing detainees kneeling with hands restrained behind their backs while he mocked them on camera. Israel has denied the allegations of mistreatment.

Canada appears to be the first government to formally demand an independent investigation into the detentions. The move is particularly striking given Canada's decades-long support for Israel, including backing it diplomatically at the UN and maintaining extensive security and intelligence partnerships. The tensions come as nearly 2 million Palestinians remain displaced in Gaza since the conflict between Israel and Hamas began in October 2023.
