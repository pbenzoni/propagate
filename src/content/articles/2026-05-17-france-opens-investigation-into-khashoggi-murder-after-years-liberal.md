France has launched a judicial investigation into the 2018 murder of journalist Jamal Khashoggi, responding to complaints filed by human rights organizations accusing Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman of complicity in the killing.

Khashoggi, a 59-year-old Washington Post columnist and prominent critic of the Saudi government, was killed inside the Saudi consulate in Istanbul, Turkey. His body was believed to have been dismembered and was never recovered. The journalist had once been close to Saudi leadership before becoming an outspoken advocate for democratic reform and press freedom in the region.

In 2022, Democracy for the Arab World Now—Khashoggi's former organization—and Reporters Without Borders filed a legal complaint alleging the crown prince ordered the murder. France's anti-terrorism prosecutor initially opposed the investigation, a stance that advocates criticized as prioritizing economic relations with Saudi Arabia over human rights accountability. However, the Paris Court of Appeal ruled that the possibility of crimes against humanity "cannot be ruled out," paving the way for a formal investigation.

A 2021 US intelligence assessment under the Biden administration concluded that Mohammed bin Salman exercised "absolute control" over Saudi security organizations and approved an operation to "capture or kill" Khashoggi. Saudi Arabia has consistently denied the crown prince's involvement, claiming the killing was carried out by rogue officials without authorization. An investigating judge specializing in crimes against humanity will now examine the complaint, marking a significant step toward potential accountability in the case that shocked the international community and highlighted the dangers faced by journalists worldwide.
