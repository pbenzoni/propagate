An exhaustive eight-year investigation by Spanish newspaper El País has documented a devastating pattern of sexual abuse within the Catholic Church in Spain, identifying more than 3,000 survivors and over 1,600 individuals accused of abuse. The findings, published Monday, represent a significant expansion from the 34 officially recognized cases when the investigation began in 2018.

The investigation, which drew on survivor testimonies, judicial records, and church admissions, traced incidents dating back to the 1940s. The accused represent approximately 1.46% of the 110,000 priests and laypeople who have served in Spain over the past eight decades. The latest report includes 58 new testimonies from Spain and an additional 21 from eight Latin American countries, underscoring the transnational scope of the crisis.

El País shared its findings with the Spanish Episcopal Conference, the Vatican, and Spain's human rights commissioner. However, the newspaper noted that church leadership has largely responded with what it characterizes as "opacity and denial," with the Vatican delegating responsibility to Spanish bishops. The investigation documented a troubling pattern of accused clergy being transferred between parishes or sent abroad—sometimes to Latin America—without facing accountability, allowing some to maintain access to children in new locations.

These revelations align with a 2023 survey by Spain's human rights commissioner estimating that approximately 440,000 Spanish adults—roughly 1.13% of the population—may have experienced sexual abuse in Catholic settings. Similar crises have emerged globally, including in the United States, where the Diocese of Brooklyn is currently seeking settlement for 1,100 cases after already paying over $100 million to survivors.
