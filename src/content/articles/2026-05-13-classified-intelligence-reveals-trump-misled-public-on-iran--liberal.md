A classified intelligence assessment obtained by *The New York Times* directly contradicts President Trump's repeated assertions that U.S.-Israeli airstrikes devastated Iran's military capabilities, revealing that Tehran has maintained operational control over the vast majority of its strategic missile installations.

According to the intelligence report from earlier this month, Iran has restored access to 30 of 33 missile sites along the critical Strait of Hormuz—a narrow waterway through which approximately 20% of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas flows. The assessment confirms that Iran retained roughly 70% of its mobile missile launchers and 70% of its pre-conflict missile stockpile, despite the administration's public claims of comprehensive military degradation following the bombing campaign that ran from late February through early April.

The discrepancy between Trump's public statements and classified intelligence findings raises serious questions about transparency and accountability as negotiations remain at an impasse. Iran closed the strait to what it termed "enemy ships" in response to the strikes and continues to assert control over maritime traffic in the waterway. The administration has rejected Iran's terms for reopening negotiations, which include ending Israel's military operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon, lifting sanctions, providing reparations, and recognizing Iranian authority over the strait.

Trump has reportedly been briefed on additional military strike options and has intensified threats to resume the bombing campaign. Meanwhile, the administration continues to demand that Iran dismantle its nuclear and ballistic missile programs—terms Tehran has categorically rejected while maintaining its uranium enrichment activities serve exclusively civilian purposes.
