Albanian Prime Minister Edi Rama has issued a stark warning that the European Union's approach to Russia may have undermined its ability to influence the outcome of the Ukraine conflict. Speaking at the Delphi Economic Forum, Rama argued that cutting off all diplomatic channels with Moscow was a "big strategic mistake" that now threatens to leave Europe without a meaningful voice in peace negotiations.

"Europe has to be always, always, always talking to everyone," Rama told Politico, emphasizing that maintaining diplomatic engagement—even with adversaries—is essential to preserving influence. He noted that Russia "is not going anywhere" regardless of how the conflict ends, making sustained isolation an unsustainable long-term strategy. Rama stressed he could speak candidly because Albania has no economic dependence on Russia.

The comments reflect growing concern among some European leaders that the continent is being marginalized in trilateral Russia-US-Ukraine negotiations. While the EU has provided hundreds of billions in financial and military support to Ukraine since 2022, several leaders including French President Emmanuel Macron and others have recently suggested reopening communication with Moscow. However, three rounds of talks have stalled, with both sides acknowledging negotiations are currently frozen.

Russian officials have rejected European participation in the talks, with Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov stating the EU has "completely discredited itself" as a potential mediator. The Kremlin has also dismissed proposals for European troop deployments as part of security guarantees for Ukraine. The situation highlights the complex challenge facing European policymakers: how to balance principled support for Ukraine with the practical reality of needing diplomatic channels to shape any eventual settlement.
