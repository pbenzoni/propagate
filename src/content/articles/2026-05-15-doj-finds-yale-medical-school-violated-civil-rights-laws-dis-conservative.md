The Department of Justice has concluded a year-long civil rights investigation finding that Yale School of Medicine engaged in illegal discrimination against white and Asian applicants in violation of federal law.

According to the DOJ's findings, Yale's admissions process "intentionally selected applicants based on their race" and administrators actively worked to "use racial proxies" to evade the Supreme Court's 2023 ruling that prohibited race-based affirmative action in college admissions. The investigation found that black and Hispanic students enjoyed substantially higher admission rates than white or Asian students with identical test scores and academic credentials.

The data proved particularly stark: a black applicant had up to 29 times greater odds of receiving an interview than an equally qualified Asian applicant with the same GPA and standardized medical exam scores across the 2023, 2024, and 2025 admissions cycles. The DOJ emphasized that such "illegal race politics" pose special concerns in medical education, where merit and excellence directly impact public safety and patient care.

The findings come as the Trump administration has ramped up enforcement of civil rights laws at universities receiving federal taxpayer dollars. Medical schools, the DOJ noted, depend heavily on federal financial assistance and must comply with anti-discrimination statutes. Similar violations were uncovered at UCLA's David Geffen School of Medicine, which the DOJ also accused of unlawfully favoring applicants based on race.

The investigation is part of broader scrutiny of DEI programs at elite institutions. Several Ivy League schools, including Harvard, Columbia, Princeton, and Cornell, have faced federal funding suspensions over refusal to dismantle discriminatory diversity programs and address campus anti-Semitism.
