A troubling new YouGov survey reveals deepening public discontent in Germany, where nearly 80% of voters now express dissatisfaction with Chancellor Friedrich Merz's governing coalition—a stark 24-point increase since the government took office last May. The poll, conducted April 10-13 among 1,763 respondents, underscores growing frustration with the coalition's failure to address economic stagnation and deliver on campaign commitments.

Most alarming is the rise of the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD), which would capture 27% of the vote if elections were held today—marking the first time since September 2025 that the extremist party has led in polling. The AfD has built its support on a platform of anti-immigration policies, opposition to sanctions against Russia, and resistance to supporting Ukraine's defense against Russian aggression.

The coalition partners are experiencing historic lows in public support. Merz's Christian Democratic bloc (CDU/CSU) has fallen to just 23%—its worst performance since December 2021—while the Social Democrats have dropped to 13%, their poorest showing since late 2019. Even among CDU/CSU voters, satisfaction has collapsed, with only 34% expressing approval of their own coalition's performance.

The AfD's expansion beyond its traditional stronghold in former East Germany is particularly concerning to democracy advocates. Recent gains in western states like Rhineland-Palatinate and Baden-Württemberg suggest the party's divisive messaging is resonating more broadly, raising urgent questions about Germany's political future and the health of its democratic institutions.
