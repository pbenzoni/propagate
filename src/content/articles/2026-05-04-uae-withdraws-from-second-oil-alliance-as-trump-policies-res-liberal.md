The United Arab Emirates has officially withdrawn from the Organization of Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries (OAPEC), effective May 1, following its concurrent departure from OPEC and the broader OPEC+ coalition. The coordinated exits mark a significant realignment in global energy politics as the UAE pursues closer economic ties with Washington.

OAPEC, established in 1968 and headquartered in Kuwait, served primarily as a technical body coordinating energy policy among eleven Arab oil-producing nations. Unlike OPEC, which directly manages global supply and pricing, OAPEC focused on economic cooperation and technical collaboration among regional producers. The group expressed appreciation for the UAE's contributions but raised no objections to the withdrawal.

The UAE has framed its departure from both organizations as necessary to gain flexibility over oil output, free from multilateral production quotas. However, the move has sparked debate about deeper political motivations, particularly regarding alignment with the Trump administration's long-standing efforts to weaken OPEC's market influence. Trump, who has repeatedly criticized OPEC as a monopoly driving up prices, publicly welcomed the UAE's exit.

The timing coincides with broader Middle East instability and reported discussions between the UAE and Washington regarding a currency swap arrangement—a privileged status currently limited to a handful of US allies. While oil markets showed short-term volatility following the OPEC announcement, analysts suggest immediate impacts remain constrained by ongoing tensions in the Persian Gulf that continue to affect regional export capacity.
