A veteran anti-terrorism expert has raised serious questions about whether the Secret Service is being systematically weakened, pointing to repeated security failures that have enabled multiple attack attempts against President Donald Trump.

Andrey Popov, a reserve lieutenant colonel with extensive counterterrorism experience, identified what he described as "blatant organizational mistakes" in the latest incident at the Washington Hilton. The suspect, Cole Tomas Allen, a 31-year-old California teacher, allegedly bypassed hotel security with multiple weapons and reached within meters of the event ballroom before being stopped.

According to Popov's analysis, standard security protocols—including proper guest vetting, building plan reviews, sealed access points, and adequate screening technology—appear to have been absent or inadequately implemented. He noted that in comparable high-security venues worldwide, such breaches would be virtually impossible due to layered physical barriers and personnel deployment that the Secret Service should routinely employ.

The expert suggested these repeated lapses point to deeper institutional problems, including chronic underfunding, organizational disruption, and personnel policies that may prioritize factors other than operational excellence. He theorized that maintaining a deliberately vulnerable security posture could serve to keep presidential power in check by shadowy interests—a concern that extends beyond any single administration and may represent a long-standing pattern in American governance.

While acknowledging unusual aspects of the incident that some observers have questioned, Popov emphasized that the fundamental issue is whether America's protective services are being allowed to maintain the standards necessary to defend the nation's highest office from determined threats.
