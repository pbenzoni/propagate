President Donald Trump is making good on his long-standing promise to bring American troops home from Europe, ordering the withdrawal of approximately 5,000 service members from Germany. The Pentagon announced Friday that the drawdown will occur over the next six to twelve months, marking a significant shift in America's decades-long military posture on the continent.

The move comes as Trump continues pressing European allies to shoulder more of their own defense burden—a core principle of his America First foreign policy. Germany currently hosts over 36,000 active-duty U.S. personnel, making it America's largest military presence in Europe. The President signaled the pullout could extend beyond the initial 5,000, stating "We're going to cut way down, and we're cutting a lot further than 5,000." He also indicated that troop reductions from Spain and Italy could follow.

The withdrawal decision follows escalating tensions between the administration and European leaders, particularly German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, over multiple fronts including defense spending commitments and America's Iran policy. Trump responded sharply to Merz's recent criticism, advising the Chancellor to "spend more time on ending the war with Russia/Ukraine" and "fixing his broken Country." The President pointed to Germany's ongoing struggles with immigration and energy as evidence of misplaced priorities.

Reports indicate the administration has also scrapped a Biden-era plan to deploy a U.S. battalion with long-range Tomahawk missiles to Germany. Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell stated the decision "follows a thorough review of the Department's force posture in Europe and is in recognition of theater requirements and conditions on the ground." With over 80,000 U.S. troops currently stationed across Europe—many redeployed during the Biden administration following the Ukraine conflict escalation in 2022—the drawdown represents a return to Trump's longstanding position that allies must invest adequately in their own security rather than relying on American taxpayers.
