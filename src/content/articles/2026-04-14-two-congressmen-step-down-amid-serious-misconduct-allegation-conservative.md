Democrat Eric Swalwell of California and Republican Tony Gonzalez of Texas have announced their resignations from the U.S. House of Representatives following separate allegations of sexual misconduct. The departures mark a rare moment of bipartisan accountability in Congress, though questions remain about the handling of such allegations.

Swalwell, who has represented California since 2013, faced accusations from four women, including serious allegations from a former staffer. While denying wrongdoing and claiming the allegations were politically motivated to derail his gubernatorial campaign, Swalwell acknowledged "mistakes in judgment" and said he didn't want constituents "distracted" by the controversy. Notably, Swalwell was previously removed from the House Intelligence Committee in 2023 after reports surfaced about his contacts with a suspected Chinese intelligence operative—a security concern that raised red flags about his judgment and vetting.

Gonzalez, a Texas Republican first elected in 2021, suspended his re-election campaign last month after revelations of an affair with an aide who later died by suicide in 2025. Gonzalez admitted to the relationship, calling it "a lapse in judgment," and announced he would formally submit his resignation when Congress returns to session.

The resignations underscore ongoing concerns about personal conduct and professional standards among elected officials in Washington. Both cases involve serious allegations that demanded accountability, regardless of party affiliation. The departures will trigger special elections in their respective districts.
