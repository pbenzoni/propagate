A French soldier serving with UN peacekeepers in Lebanon has died from wounds sustained in an attack last week, President Emmanuel Macron announced Wednesday. Chief Corporal Anicet Girardin had been evacuated to France for treatment following the incident in the village of Ghandourieh in southern Lebanon, which left another French peacekeeper dead and two others injured when their patrol came under small-arms fire.

The attack highlights the precarious position of international peacekeeping forces operating in a region engulfed by escalating violence. More than 2,300 people have been killed and over 1.2 million displaced due to ongoing fighting and airstrikes in Lebanon, according to authorities in Beirut. The violence follows an Israeli invasion of southern Lebanon that began in early March, shortly after a U.S.-Israeli operation targeting Iran.

While Macron attributed responsibility to Hezbollah, the Lebanese movement denied involvement and called for caution pending a Lebanese military investigation. The incident reflects the broader humanitarian crisis unfolding across the region, where civilian populations and peacekeeping personnel alike face mounting dangers. A temporary ten-day truce brokered earlier this month is set to expire Sunday, raising concerns about further escalation.

France maintains approximately 700 troops in Lebanon as part of a 7,505-strong UN peacekeeping mission drawn from 47 nations. The country's decades-long commitment to stability in Lebanon—where it has deployed forces since 1978—has cost more than 160 French lives. The latest casualties underscore the urgent need for diplomatic solutions to protect both local communities and international personnel working to prevent further violence.
