During President Donald Trump's historic visit to Beijing—the first by an American president to China in nearly a decade—Chinese leader Xi Jinping delivered an unmistakable message: mishandling Taiwan could lead the world's two largest powers into direct confrontation.

"The Taiwan question is the most important issue in China-US relations," Xi told Trump at the Great Hall of the People on Thursday. "If it is handled properly, the bilateral relationship will enjoy overall stability. Otherwise, the two countries may clash or even come into conflict, pushing the entire China-US relationship into a very dangerous situation." Xi emphasized that Taiwan's push for independence and cross-strait peace are fundamentally incompatible, describing them as "irreconcilable as fire and water."

The meeting comes amid complex diplomatic dynamics. While the United States officially acknowledges the One China policy, Washington has maintained robust security ties with Taiwan, approving tens of billions in arms sales over recent decades. The Trump administration approved an $11 billion weapons package to Taiwan in December, though reports indicate a larger $13 billion package was temporarily shelved ahead of this summit.

Xi's warning reflects Beijing's firm stance that Taiwan remains Chinese sovereign territory, while Taiwan's government countered that "China's military threat is the sole source of insecurity" in the region. The summit agenda extends beyond Taiwan to include discussions on the Iran conflict, trade disputes, and artificial intelligence competition—all critical issues as Trump seeks to navigate America's relationship with its largest geopolitical rival while protecting American interests and long-standing commitments to regional allies.
