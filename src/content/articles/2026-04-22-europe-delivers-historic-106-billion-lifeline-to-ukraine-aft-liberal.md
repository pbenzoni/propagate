In a significant victory for international solidarity, European Union ambassadors have approved a €90 billion ($106 billion) loan package for Ukraine, marking one of the largest coordinated financial commitments to support the embattled nation. The measure is expected to receive formal approval from all 27 member states on Thursday.

The loan had faced procedural delays for several months due to objections from Hungary, which cited concerns over energy security after Ukraine suspended certain Russian oil transit arrangements. Diplomatic efforts eventually resolved the impasse, allowing the critical funding to move forward.

The massive financial package represents a continuation of the EU's commitment to supporting Ukraine's economic stability and reconstruction efforts. According to media reports, the approval came Wednesday and was confirmed by a spokesman for Cyprus, which currently holds the rotating EU presidency.

This development underscores the importance of multilateral cooperation in addressing complex geopolitical challenges, even when member states have differing priorities and concerns about energy policy and regional security.
