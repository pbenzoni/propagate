A sobering new analysis reveals the consequences of Britain's expansive welfare state, as the United Kingdom's healthy life expectancy has collapsed by nearly two years over the past decade. The Health Foundation study shows that British men and women can now expect only about 60 years of good health—a stunning decline that places the UK second-to-last among the 21 wealthiest nations, ahead of only the United States.

The data paints a troubling picture of what happens when government bureaucracies control healthcare. Between 2012–2014 and 2022–2024, healthy life expectancy for British men dropped from 62.9 to 60.7 years, while women saw their numbers fall from 63.7 to 60.9 years. Britain has plummeted from 14th to 20th place internationally, joining Denmark, Portugal, and Spain as the only wealthy nations experiencing significant health deterioration.

Perhaps most striking is the growing disparity between regions. In affluent areas like Richmond in London, women can expect 70 years of healthy life, while in struggling post-industrial towns like Hartlepool, that number crashes to just 51 years—a 19-year gap that underscores how government programs have failed working-class communities. In more than 90% of areas, healthy life expectancy now falls below the state pension age of 66, meaning Britons are spending their retirement years in poor health after a lifetime of paying into the system.

The Health Foundation attributes the decline to poverty, substandard housing, obesity, pandemic lockdown effects, and surging mental health problems among young people. The report warns of significant economic costs as poor health forces people out of the workforce. In a twist of irony, Russia's healthy life expectancy now reportedly stands at 63.4 years—slightly higher than Britain's—raising uncomfortable questions about the effectiveness of the UK's heavily government-managed approach to public health.
