Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps announced it conducted missile and drone strikes against U.S. military positions in Bahrain, Jordan, and Kuwait, calling the operation retaliation for American strikes carried out earlier this week.

The escalation follows an incident in which a U.S. Apache helicopter was lost near the Strait of Hormuz—a loss Washington attributed to Iranian action. U.S. Central Command responded with what it termed "defensive" and "proportional" strikes inside Iran on Tuesday, which Tehran says damaged a telecommunications tower and destroyed water infrastructure.

According to the IRGC, its response targeted the headquarters of the U.S. Fifth Fleet in Bahrain and multiple sites at Jordan's al-Azraq air base, including hangars reportedly housing F-35 fighter jets. Jordan's military said it intercepted five ballistic missiles, while Kuwaiti officials also reported intercepting aerial threats. Air raid sirens were heard in Bahrain, according to regional media.

The confrontation comes amid broader regional tensions involving Iran and Israel. Tehran recently struck targets in Israel, citing retaliation for Israel's military operations in southern Lebanon. Israel responded with its own strikes despite calls from President Trump to avoid further escalation. Trump has maintained that limited military actions do not necessarily violate the ceasefire framework announced in April, though Iran views Israel's Lebanon operations as a breach of that agreement.
