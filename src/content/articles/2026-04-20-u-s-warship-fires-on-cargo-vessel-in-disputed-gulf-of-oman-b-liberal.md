The U.S. Central Command released footage showing the guided-missile destroyer USS Spruance firing on the Iranian-flagged cargo vessel M/V Touska in the Gulf of Oman. The incident occurred as the ship attempted to reach the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas through the Strait of Hormuz, a route blocked by what CENTCOM described as a U.S.-imposed naval blockade.

According to military officials, the Touska's crew ignored repeated warnings over a six-hour period before the destroyer disabled the vessel's engine by firing rounds from its 5-inch gun into the engine room. Marines from the 31st Marine Expeditionary Unit subsequently boarded the ship.

Iranian officials have condemned the blockade as a violation of international law, arguing it contravenes the terms of a two-week ceasefire agreement set to expire Wednesday. The Iranian military has indicated it will respond to what it characterizes as the illegal seizure of a civilian vessel. Iran's Tasnim news agency later reported that Iranian forces launched a drone toward U.S. ships, though the Pentagon has not confirmed any attack on American vessels.

The escalation in the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz—through which roughly one-fifth of global oil supplies pass—underscores mounting tensions over maritime law, blockade authority, and the fragile ceasefire's uncertain future.
