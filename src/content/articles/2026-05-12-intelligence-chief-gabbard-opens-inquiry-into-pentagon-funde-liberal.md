Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard has launched an investigation into more than 120 American-funded biological laboratories operating in over 30 countries, including Ukraine, according to statements she made to the New York Post. The probe aims to identify pathogens housed at these facilities and clarify what research activities are being conducted.

The inquiry follows President Trump's executive order restricting federal funding for gain-of-function research—studies that examine how viruses replicate and potentially increase their transmissibility. Gabbard cited the COVID-19 pandemic as evidence of the "catastrophic global impact" that research on dangerous pathogens can have, calling for greater transparency and accountability in oversight of these facilities.

The biological research facilities, many funded through the Pentagon's Defense Threat Reduction Agency, have been a source of international controversy. While the Biden administration previously maintained that such labs were focused on disease prevention and vaccine development rather than military applications, questions about transparency persisted. Former Undersecretary of State Victoria Nuland acknowledged in 2022 that "biological research facilities" existed in Ukraine, though she emphasized their civilian public health mission.

Gabbard has previously faced criticism for raising concerns about these laboratories. In 2022, she warned that facilities in Ukraine could pose risks if compromised during conflict—comments that drew sharp rebuke from some lawmakers at the time. The current investigation reflects ongoing debates about balancing legitimate scientific research with appropriate oversight and public disclosure of federally-funded international programs.
