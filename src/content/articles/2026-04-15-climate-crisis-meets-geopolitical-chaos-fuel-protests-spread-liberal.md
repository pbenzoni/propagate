Traffic disruptions have spread across Northern Ireland as protesters voice frustration over skyrocketing fuel prices linked to ongoing conflict in the Middle East. Police intervened in several slow-moving vehicle demonstrations along major routes, issuing fines to participants who blocked traffic near Belfast City Airport and other key corridors.

The protests follow similar demonstrations that began in the Republic of Ireland last week, where gasoline prices have jumped 15% and diesel costs have surged nearly 30% since mid-February. The price spike has been driven by disruptions to oil shipping through the Strait of Hormuz—a critical waterway that previously carried roughly a quarter of the world's seaborne oil trade—amid escalating U.S.-Israeli military operations against Iran.

The crisis has hit farmers, small businesses, and working families particularly hard, exposing the vulnerability of communities dependent on fossil fuels in an era of geopolitical instability. In Ireland, taxes and environmental levies comprise nearly 60% of fuel costs, leading protesters to demand government intervention to ease the burden on struggling households and businesses.

The situation has also highlighted tensions around climate policy implementation. While green energy levies are intended to fund the transition away from fossil fuels, critics argue that the current structure places disproportionate burdens on rural communities and essential workers during periods of external supply shocks.
