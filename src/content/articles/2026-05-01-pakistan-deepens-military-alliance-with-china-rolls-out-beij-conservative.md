Pakistan has commissioned the first of eight Chinese-manufactured Hangor-class submarines in a ceremony that underscores the deepening military partnership between Beijing and Islamabad. Pakistani President Asif Ali Zardari attended the launch in the southern Chinese port city of Sanya, calling it a "historic milestone" for his nation's navy.

The submarine deal, signed in 2015 and valued at approximately $5 billion, represents China's expanding footprint in South Asia. Four submarines are being built directly in China and delivered to Pakistan, while the remaining four will be constructed on Pakistani soil using Chinese technology. According to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute, China now accounts for roughly 81 percent of Pakistan's arms imports—a striking indicator of Beijing's growing influence over a key regional player.

The timing raises serious strategic concerns. Pakistan has fought three wars with India since 1947, and Islamabad continues to rely heavily on its submarine fleet as a cornerstone of its military posture against New Delhi. During a 2025 conflict over Kashmir, Pakistan claimed its Chinese-made J-10C fighter jets shot down Indian aircraft, including French-made Rafale fighters—a claim India has not confirmed. The new submarines will further expand Pakistan's undersea capabilities in already-tense waters.

India has responded by accelerating its own naval expansion programs, particularly its nuclear and conventional submarine initiatives, while closely monitoring China's submarine deliveries to its neighbor. Earlier this week, Indian Defense Minister Rajnath Singh accused Pakistan of supporting terrorism and pledged that New Delhi stands ready to respond to any threat. As China extends its naval presence throughout the strategically vital Indian Ocean, the balance of power in the region continues to shift in ways that demand American attention.
