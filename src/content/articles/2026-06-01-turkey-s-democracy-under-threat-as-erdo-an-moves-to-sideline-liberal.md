Turkey's fragile democracy faces a pivotal moment as President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's government employs increasingly authoritarian tactics to neutralize political rivals. The recent arrest of Istanbul Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu on corruption charges—widely viewed as politically motivated—and a controversial court decision removing opposition party leader Özgür Özel have raised alarm bells among democracy advocates about the erosion of democratic norms in the NATO ally.

İmamoğlu, a rising star in the center-left Republican People's Party (CHP) and considered the opposition's best hope to challenge Erdoğan, was detained just as his party prepared to select its presidential candidate. His success governing Istanbul, Turkey's economic and cultural heart, had made him a potent symbol of democratic renewal and good governance—precisely what made him a threat to the incumbent regime.

The judicial removal of Özel, who had revitalized the CHP after its 2023 presidential defeat and led the party to significant gains in 2024 municipal elections, represents another blow to opposition momentum. Courts ordered leadership returned to Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu, the previous leader associated with electoral losses, effectively undermining the party's reform efforts through legal maneuvering rather than democratic processes.

These moves appear designed to preserve the presidential system Erdoğan has built over two decades—one that critics say has concentrated power, weakened institutional checks and balances, and moved Turkey away from its democratic foundations. With the opposition destabilized and key challengers sidelined through legal rather than electoral means, upcoming elections may determine whether Turkey's democratic backsliding can be reversed or whether authoritarian consolidation will continue unchecked.
