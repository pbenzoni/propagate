Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi dismissed President Trump's peace proposals this week, claiming the United States seeks Iran's "surrender" rather than genuine negotiation. The statement comes as indirect talks continue following last month's fragile ceasefire after weeks of military confrontation.

Trump has repeatedly characterized Iran's counterproposals as unacceptable, maintaining that Tehran suffered military losses and must accept American conditions to resolve the crisis. Iran's demands include compensation for damages from U.S. and Israeli strikes in late February, guarantees against future attacks, and recognition of what it calls its "sovereign rights" under international law.

The United States has called for Iran to completely halt uranium enrichment and restore unrestricted commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz as preconditions for any settlement. Tehran, however, insists on maintaining control over the strategically vital waterway and preserving maritime toll systems it introduced during the conflict. Iran also claims its nuclear energy program falls within rights granted under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.

As diplomatic efforts mediated by Pakistan have stalled, the Trump administration launched a naval blockade of Iranian ports, further disrupting global energy markets. Trump warned last week that Americans would know the ceasefire had collapsed when they see "one big glow coming out of Iran." The president is currently visiting China, temporarily reducing the likelihood of immediate military escalation, though Washington has imposed sanctions on Chinese entities accused of assisting Tehran with satellite imagery, weapons components, and oil purchases.
