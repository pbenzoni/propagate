The Pentagon continues to refuse acknowledgment of U.S. responsibility for a February 28 strike on an Iranian elementary school that killed more than 170 people—most of them children—despite mounting pressure from congressional Democrats and international condemnation.

At a House Armed Services Committee hearing Tuesday, Representative Adam Smith, the ranking Democrat on the committee, confronted Admiral Brad Cooper, head of U.S. Central Command, over what he characterized as "endless stalling" in the 80 days since the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school was bombed. "It's really pretty clear what happened there," Smith said, pressing Cooper to acknowledge U.S. culpability. Cooper deflected, claiming the investigation remains ongoing and asserting the school was located "on an active IRGC cruise missile base"—a claim contradicted by archived records and satellite imagery showing the school was adjacent to, not inside, a military facility and separated by a visible fence.

Media investigations and an internal military probe reportedly concluded that U.S. forces likely used "outdated targeting data" that misidentified the civilian school. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called Cooper's claims "an appalling lie" and "a clear attempt to obscure the severe reality," describing the strike as a war crime and grave violation of international humanitarian law.

The tragedy has drawn international condemnation, with leaders from Russia, Italy, and Spain expressing solidarity with victims of what some called a "massacre." Smith noted the Pentagon has historically moved far more quickly to acknowledge similar mistakes, raising questions about why this investigation remains incomplete months later. Meanwhile, prospects for a broader peace deal remain stalled, with threats of resumed military action complicating diplomatic efforts.
