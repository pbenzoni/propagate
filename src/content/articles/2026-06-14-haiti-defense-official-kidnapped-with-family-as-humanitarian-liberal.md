James Boyard, chief of staff of Haiti's Defense Ministry and inspector general of the national police, was reportedly kidnapped Thursday along with his wife and six-year-old daughter—a U.S. citizen—in what marks one of the most brazen attacks on state authority in years.

The abduction occurred in Bourdon, a Port-au-Prince neighborhood previously considered among the capital's safer areas, highlighting how armed groups have expanded their territorial control even as international interventions struggle to restore stability. Police sources indicate a ransom was demanded, with investigators reportedly suspecting involvement by a major gang leader.

Haiti has been engulfed in a humanitarian catastrophe since the 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moïse created a power vacuum that enabled armed groups to seize control of highways, ports, and communities. The resulting instability has prevented scheduled elections and devastated basic governance. According to UN figures, nearly 1.5 million people have been displaced by violence since 2022, with more than 16,000 killed—though local human rights organizations estimate the death toll closer to 20,000.

A UN-backed security mission deployed in 2024 achieved limited gains, including securing the presidential palace and reopening some roads, but has faced significant challenges in stemming the broader crisis. At least 647 kidnappings were recorded last year alone, with women and children among the victims. The targeting of Boyard, a respected security expert serving under Haiti's newly appointed defense minister, represents an alarming escalation that underscores the fragility of state institutions and the urgent need for comprehensive international support beyond military intervention.
