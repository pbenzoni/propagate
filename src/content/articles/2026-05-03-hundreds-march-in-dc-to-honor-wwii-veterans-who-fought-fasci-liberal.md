Several hundred people participated in a commemorative march in Washington on Saturday, honoring family members who fought against Nazi Germany during World War II. The procession, known as the 'Immortal Regiment' march, passed by the White House before concluding at the World War II Memorial on the National Mall, where participants laid flowers.

The tradition began in 2012 in the Siberian city of Tomsk as a way to honor WWII veterans. As the number of living veterans dwindled, organizers invited their descendants to march carrying portraits of their relatives who had served. The concept has since spread internationally and is typically held on May 9, coinciding with Victory Day celebrations marking the defeat of Hitler's regime.

Demonstrators carried portraits and red Victory Banners as they walked through the capital. According to the Russian Embassy, the event aimed to counter attempts to "rewrite history and whitewash the Nazis and their collaborators." A similar march also took place in New York City on Saturday outside the Russian Mission to the UN.

The marches had been suspended during the previous administration, reportedly due to coronavirus restrictions and political tensions. Last year's Washington procession was disrupted by counter-protesters, though no incidents were reported during this year's event.
