Israeli naval forces intercepted the Global Sumud Flotilla in international waters on May 18 as the humanitarian mission attempted to reach Gaza, detaining more than 400 activists from approximately 40 countries. The volunteers had departed from Turkey with the goal of challenging Israel's naval blockade and drawing global attention to the deteriorating humanitarian situation in the besieged enclave.

The detained activists represented a diverse international coalition, including citizens from Italy, the United Kingdom, Canada, Turkey, Greece, France, Spain, Germany, the Netherlands, South Korea, Ireland, and New Zealand. Their mission sought to deliver aid and spotlight the humanitarian crisis facing Gaza's civilian population.

The incident drew widespread condemnation after Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir released videos depicting detained activists restrained with zip-ties, forced to kneel with their foreheads to the ground. In the footage, Ben-Gvir is seen walking among the detained humanitarian workers, mocking them and characterizing them as terrorism supporters.

Human rights organizations have expressed alarm at both the interception of the aid mission in international waters and the treatment of the detained activists. The incident has intensified international scrutiny of Israel's blockade policies and their impact on Gaza's civilian population, which faces severe shortages of essential supplies and humanitarian aid.
