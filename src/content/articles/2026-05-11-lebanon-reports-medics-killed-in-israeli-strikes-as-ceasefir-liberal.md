The Lebanese Health Ministry reported Sunday that two healthcare workers were among 51 people killed in the latest Israeli airstrikes on the country, raising concerns about compliance with international humanitarian law protecting medical personnel.

The strikes come despite a U.S.-brokered ceasefire that took effect in mid-April. According to ministry data, more than 550 deaths have occurred in Lebanon since the truce was implemented, part of a total exceeding 2,800 casualties since military operations intensified in early March. The United Nations has documented at least 103 Lebanese medical workers killed and 230 injured in more than 130 strikes during the current escalation.

The pattern echoes concerns raised during the Gaza conflict, where healthcare facilities have also come under fire. A war surgeon who has worked in both Gaza and Lebanon told Al Jazeera that the targeting of medical workers appears "consistent" across both theaters. International humanitarian law, including the Geneva Conventions, provides special protections for medical personnel and facilities during armed conflict.

Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam stated that 86 villages in southern Lebanon remain under occupation, with some areas experiencing destruction he compared to Gaza. Israeli forces have said they are targeting military facilities, though the Lebanese government maintains that a sustainable peace agreement requires the withdrawal of troops and full implementation of ceasefire terms.
