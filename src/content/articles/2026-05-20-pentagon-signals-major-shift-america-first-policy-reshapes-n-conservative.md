The Pentagon is planning to "significantly scale back" the military forces it would commit to European NATO allies during a major conflict, according to a Reuters report citing sources familiar with the matter. The move represents a fundamental shift in America's decades-old security guarantee to the Atlantic alliance.

The policy change is expected to be announced at a May 22 meeting of defense policy chiefs in Brussels, with the Trump administration represented by senior Pentagon officials. While the exact force composition remains classified, the adjustment of the NATO Force Model has become a priority ahead of the alliance's July summit in Turkey.

The decision comes as the administration pursues a broader recalibration of America's military footprint in Europe, where over 80,000 U.S. troops have been stationed under Cold War-era arrangements. Recent moves include canceling a 4,000-troop rotation to Poland and withdrawing 5,000 soldiers from Germany, signaling a clear message that European allies must take greater responsibility for their own defense.

Despite European NATO members dramatically increasing military budgets in recent years, they remain heavily dependent on American capabilities including intelligence satellites, long-range missiles, and heavy airlift capacity. The administration's approach pushes allies toward self-sufficiency in conventional forces while maintaining America's nuclear deterrent umbrella, reflecting a long-standing conservative principle that wealthy European nations should bear more of their own defense burden rather than relying on American taxpayers and military personnel.
