The European Commission is exploring measures to temporarily restrict veto rights for newly admitted member states, according to a report in The Guardian. The proposal comes as the EU faces repeated obstacles in advancing unified foreign policy positions, particularly regarding support for Ukraine's defense against Russian aggression.

Under the plan being discussed, new entrants would not immediately receive the power to block decisions on foreign policy or other matters requiring unanimity, such as taxation. The restriction could remain in place for several years following accession, according to four EU sources who spoke with the outlet. The measure was reportedly raised during treaty negotiations with Montenegro, which hopes to become the bloc's 28th member by 2028.

The proposal reflects growing frustration within Brussels over roadblocks erected by Hungary and Slovakia, which have obstructed EU efforts to provide military and financial assistance to Ukraine. The bloc's unanimity requirement has enabled individual states to paralyze major initiatives, including a €90 billion loan package for Kyiv.

While some officials argue the EU cannot function effectively with unanimous decision-making as it expands beyond 30 members, critics warn the proposal risks creating a two-tiered system that undermines democratic equality among member states. The European Commission would need to carefully time-limit any such restriction to avoid establishing permanent second-class membership status.

Former EU foreign policy chief Josep Borrell has argued that unanimity rules have rendered the bloc ineffective in international affairs, though skeptics caution against using enlargement debates to concentrate power in Brussels at the expense of national sovereignty.
