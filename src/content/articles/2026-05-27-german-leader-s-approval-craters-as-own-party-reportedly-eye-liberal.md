Senior figures within Germany's Christian Democratic Union (CDU) are reportedly discussing whether to replace Chancellor Friedrich Merz as their leader, according to multiple German media outlets. The conversations come as Merz's approval ratings have plummeted to historic lows, with recent polling showing a 76% disapproval rating.

According to reports in Bild and other German publications, the discussions involve prominent regional leaders including Hendrik Wüst of North Rhine-Westphalia, who has emerged as a potential alternative. Wüst leads Germany's most populous state and ranks as one of the country's most popular politicians, in stark contrast to Merz's struggling numbers. One political analyst noted that even CDU members who don't openly oppose Merz now "consider the chancellor weak" and express "a palpable feeling of unease."

Merz's unpopularity appears linked to his policy agenda since taking office in May 2025. He has called on Germans to work longer hours while characterizing the country's social welfare system as "outdated and unsustainable"—positions that have drawn criticism from workers and social advocates. He has also significantly increased military spending and escalated Germany's involvement in supporting Ukraine, framing these moves as responses to security concerns.

While German outlets describe the replacement discussions as "rumors" with no concrete plans yet materialized, the reports underscore growing unease within the conservative party about Merz's political viability. As of mid-April, only 19% of Germans expressed satisfaction with his performance as chancellor.
