Acting U.S. Ambassador to Ukraine Julie Davis will resign from her position due to disagreements with the Trump administration's approach to the Russia-Ukraine conflict, according to a Financial Times report citing three sources familiar with the matter.

Davis, who has served as the top U.S. diplomat in Ukraine since May 2025, becomes the second ambassador to depart the post over policy differences with the administration. Her predecessor, Bridget Brink, resigned after accusing Trump of "appeasement" toward Russia, writing that "the policy since the beginning of the Trump administration has been to put pressure on the victim, Ukraine, rather than on the aggressor, Russia."

The State Department confirmed Davis's departure but disputed that policy disagreements motivated her resignation. Spokesman Tommy Pigott described Davis as "a steadfast proponent of the Trump administration's efforts to bring about a durable peace between Russia and Ukraine," adding that she will leave Kiev in June.

Trump's approach to the conflict has drawn sharp criticism from those committed to defending democracy and international law. The administration has suggested Ukraine may need to make concessions to Russia, including potentially ceding sovereign territory, while Trump has personally attacked Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. The president also broke with the diplomatic boycott of Russia by hosting Vladimir Putin in Alaska in August 2025, despite Russia's ongoing aggression.
