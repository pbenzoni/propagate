The Trump administration has determined that U.S. military operations against Iran have been "terminated" for purposes of the 1973 War Powers Act, according to the Associated Press, meaning no congressional authorization is required to continue military presence in the region.

With the 60-day deadline mandated by the War Powers Act rapidly approaching, a senior White House official stated that "the hostilities that began on Saturday, February 28, have terminated." This assessment follows the ceasefire reached between Washington and Tehran on April 7, which has been extended indefinitely. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth reinforced this position during Senate hearings Thursday, arguing the conflict has been "paused" and that the 60-day clock is on hold.

Democratic Senator Tim Kaine challenged Hegseth's interpretation as "a very novel argument that I've never heard before" with "no legal support." Multiple legal scholars have argued the 60-day period cannot be "terminated" or "paused" under the statute. However, some national security experts have backed the administration's reading of the law, suggesting it provides flexibility for future defensive operations if needed.

The administration has continued deploying military assets to the Middle East since the ceasefire, including three aircraft carrier strike groups and marine expeditionary units. Some forces are enforcing the naval blockade of Iran, while others remain on standby in the region. The White House maintains that any future operations would be framed as defensive measures focused on freedom of navigation and national security interests.
