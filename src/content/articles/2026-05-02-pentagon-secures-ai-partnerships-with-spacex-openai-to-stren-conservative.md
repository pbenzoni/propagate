The Department of Defense has successfully negotiated agreements with America's leading technology companies to integrate artificial intelligence into classified military networks, marking a significant advancement in national defense capabilities. The partnerships include SpaceX, OpenAI, Google, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services, and Oracle—representing the best of American innovation serving those who protect our freedoms.

These AI systems will be deployed for lawful operational use across the Pentagon's most secure networks, designed to streamline data analysis, enhance situational awareness, and support warfighter decision-making in increasingly complex operational environments. The Defense Department reports that over 1.3 million military personnel have already utilized its AI platform in recent months, accelerating mission-critical tasks from months down to mere days—a testament to American technological superiority.

Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth has been forthright about the necessity of these partnerships for maintaining military readiness. During Senate testimony, he emphasized that defense contractors cannot pick and choose how their technology serves national security interests, comparing such selectivity to "Boeing giving us airplanes and telling us who we can shoot at."

Notably absent from these agreements is Anthropic, an AI startup that refused to modify its technology safeguards for Pentagon use. The company raised concerns about potential domestic surveillance applications—objections the Defense Department found unacceptable given the urgent needs of national security. The Pentagon subsequently designated Anthropic a "supply-chain risk," a classification usually reserved for entities with foreign adversary connections. Anthropic is currently challenging this designation in court.
