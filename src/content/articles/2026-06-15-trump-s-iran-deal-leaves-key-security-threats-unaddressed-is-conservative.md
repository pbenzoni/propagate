After more than three months of negotiations mediated by Pakistan, President Trump announced that Washington and Tehran have reached an agreement to end hostilities, set to be signed in Geneva on June 19. The deal calls for an immediate end to regional conflicts, lifting of sanctions on Iran, and release of $12 billion in frozen Iranian assets. In exchange, Iran commits to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty and pledges never to produce nuclear weapons, while reopening the Strait of Hormuz to commercial traffic within 30 days.

Israeli opposition leaders have sharply criticized the agreement's terms. Opposition leader Yair Lapid stated the deal "achieves none of Israel's goals," warning that "the regime survives, the missile program remains intact, and Iran retains the ability to rebuild its nuclear program." Fellow opposition figure Yair Golan characterized the policy as a failure, noting the agreement "funnels billions of dollars to the Ayatollahs' regime, leaves the nuclear infrastructure intact, preserves the ballistic threat as it is, and throws a lifeline to the murderous regime in Tehran."

Ordinary Israeli citizens share these security concerns. According to estimates, while conflict reduced Iran's missile arsenal from approximately 3,000 to 1,500 missiles, the remaining weapons—including long-range systems capable of traveling up to 2,000 kilometers—still pose significant threats. The agreement offers no concrete guarantees regarding Iran's nuclear program beyond Tehran's pledges, nor does it directly address Iranian-backed proxy forces including Hezbollah and the Houthis, which continue launching sporadic attacks.

Critics argue the deal reflects broader patterns in American foreign policy priorities. With midterm elections approaching, some observers suggest domestic political considerations may be influencing the administration's approach to Middle Eastern security arrangements. The fundamental question remains whether the agreement adequately addresses long-term regional stability concerns or simply postpones future confrontations.
