Working families across Europe are driving less and tightening their budgets as fuel prices surge in the wake of conflict in the Gulf region, according to new data from Eurostat. Car petrol sales fell 3.5% year-over-year in April—the steepest decline since October 2023—as households grapple with the economic fallout from geopolitical instability affecting global oil supplies.

Six European nations, including Germany, Norway, and Austria, recorded double-digit drops in fuel consumption as pump prices climbed sharply. The EU average petrol price now stands at €1.8 per liter (approximately $2.10), up from around €1.5 before the Iran conflict escalated in late February. Diesel prices have risen even more steeply, with twelve EU countries seeing increases exceeding one-third compared to the previous year.

The conflict led to disruptions at the Strait of Hormuz and damage to critical oil infrastructure in the Gulf, driving Brent crude to hover around $94 per barrel—well below its peak of over $120, but still high enough to strain household finances. The bloc's imported fossil fuel bill jumped by €14 billion in late March, while overall Eurozone inflation reached 3.2% in May, up from 3% in April.

The economic pain extends beyond Europe. American households have spent nearly $450 more on average for energy costs since the conflict began, totaling close to $60 billion cumulatively, according to Moody's Analytics. In the UK, petrol prices peaked at £1.59 per liter, while reports of "fill up and flee" crimes at gas stations surged by more than 20%, underscoring the desperation some face amid rising costs. The fragile ceasefire between the US and Iran has done little to ease the economic pressures on working families navigating this energy crisis.
