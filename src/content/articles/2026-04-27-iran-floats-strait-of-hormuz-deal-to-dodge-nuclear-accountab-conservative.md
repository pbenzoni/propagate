Iran has presented Washington with a proposal that would postpone nuclear negotiations while reopening the Strait of Hormuz, according to Axios sources. The maneuver comes as the Islamic Republic faces mounting pressure to dismantle its nuclear program and surrender its enriched uranium stockpiles.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi reportedly shared the framework with Pakistani, Egyptian, Turkish, and Qatari intermediaries, acknowledging that Tehran's leadership lacks internal consensus on meeting American nuclear demands. The proposal would lift the U.S. blockade of the Strait of Hormuz—a chokepoint handling 20% of global seaborne oil trade—before any nuclear discussions begin.

According to Al-Mayadeen, Iran's three-stage plan seeks binding guarantees against military action, a new legal framework for managing the strait with Oman, and only then would address nuclear concerns. The regime is demanding lifting of all sanctions, control over the strategic waterway, and recognition of its "right" to nuclear enrichment—all while dismissing longstanding concerns from Washington and Jerusalem about its weapons ambitions.

The proposal surfaces after talks in Islamabad stalled, prompting President Trump to cancel a planned trip by envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. "You're not going to be making any more 18-hour flights to sit around talking about nothing," Trump said Saturday. The Obama administration spent over two years negotiating the 2015 nuclear deal, which President Trump withdrew from in 2018 after determining it failed to adequately constrain Iranian nuclear development.
