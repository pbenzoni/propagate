World Health Organization Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus personally traveled to Spain's Tenerife island to oversee the containment of a hantavirus outbreak aboard the MV Hondius cruise ship, attempting to reassure local residents that the deadly pathogen poses little risk of widespread contagion.

The Dutch-flagged vessel, carrying 147 passengers and crew from 23 countries, experienced an outbreak of the rare Andes strain of hantavirus after departing Argentina on April 1. Three people have died from the illness: a 70-year-old Dutch man, his 69-year-old wife, and a German woman. The WHO has reported eight total cases linked to the ship. The virus is typically spread through contact with infected rodent droppings, urine, or saliva, though the Andes strain can transmit between humans through close contact. There is no known cure.

"The risk to you, living your daily life in Tenerife, is low," Ghebreyesus wrote in a letter Saturday, emphasizing that hantavirus "is not another COVID." He stated no symptomatic passengers currently remain aboard and assured that those on the ship would be evacuated through strict containment protocols at an industrial port "far from residential areas" using sealed vehicles through cordoned-off corridors before being repatriated to their home countries.

Authorities face the additional challenge of tracing more than two dozen people who disembarked before the outbreak was confirmed. The WHO has stationed an expert aboard the vessel to monitor the situation as the containment operation proceeds under direct oversight from the organization's top official.
