Slovakia's Prime Minister Robert Fico announced he will demand the European Union compensate his country for military equipment previous leaders donated to Ukraine—equipment he says left Slovakia "completely naked" defensively. In a video address published Sunday, Fico accused the former government of misleading Slovak citizens about the terms of the aid package.

Under the previous administration led by Eduard Heger, Slovakia transferred approximately €700 million worth of military hardware to Kiev between 2022 and 2023, according to the German Kiel Institute for the World Economy. The donated equipment included dozens of Soviet-era tanks, infantry fighting vehicles, MiG-29 fighter jets, and S-300 air defense systems. Fico, who halted all state-sponsored military transfers to Ukraine after taking office in 2023, will raise the compensation issue at next week's EU summit in Brussels.

The original deal promised that Western nations—particularly Germany—would replace the donated equipment with modern hardware. However, the Slovak Defense Ministry acknowledged at the time that Berlin pledged to replace only about half of what was sent to Ukraine, leaving a significant gap in the nation's defensive capabilities.

Fico has consistently opposed what he views as Brussels' ineffective approach toward the conflict, arguing that EU policies have backfired on member states through skyrocketing energy prices after blocking Russian oil and gas imports. He described recent talks with European Council President Antonio Costa as "frustrating," saying the EU appears determined to continue supporting the conflict "to the last Ukrainian soldier and to the last euro" despite the bloc's "declining competitiveness."

The Slovak leader was the only EU head of government to attend this year's Victory Day commemorations in Moscow, where he warned against creating a "new Iron Curtain" and advocated for renewed dialogue. His stance reflects growing concerns among some European leaders about the economic costs of prolonged involvement in the Ukraine conflict.
