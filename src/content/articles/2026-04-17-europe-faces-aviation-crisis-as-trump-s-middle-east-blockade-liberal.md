Europe could run out of jet fuel within six weeks as disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz choke off critical oil supplies, according to International Energy Agency Executive Director Fatih Birol. The crisis stems from U.S.-Israeli military operations that prompted Iran to restrict the strategic waterway, through which roughly 20% of global oil flows.

In an interview with the Associated Press, Birol described the situation as "the largest energy crisis we have ever faced," warning that flight cancellations and soaring energy costs could soon follow. President Trump's declaration of a blockade on the strait after failed diplomatic talks with Tehran has caused oil tankers to turn back, pushing prices toward $100 per barrel and leaving European airports scrambling for alternative fuel sources.

The crisis is already impacting air travel across the continent. London's Heathrow Airport has seen flight cancellations tied to surging fuel costs, while Scandinavian carrier SAS has scrapped approximately 1,000 flights. Smaller British regional airlines have also cut services as fuel prices have spiked roughly 120% year-over-year, and Air France has raised fares on long-haul routes. Some EU countries maintain jet fuel reserves covering only eight to ten days.

Western Europe faces particular vulnerability due to its heavy reliance on Middle Eastern oil supplies—a dependency that deepened after European nations imposed sanctions on Russian energy imports. Birol warned that prolonged disruptions would mean "higher petrol prices, higher gas prices, high electricity prices" with significant impacts on economic growth and inflation worldwide. The crisis underscores the complex geopolitical consequences of military interventions in energy-critical regions and the challenges facing nations that have sought to diversify away from certain energy suppliers.
