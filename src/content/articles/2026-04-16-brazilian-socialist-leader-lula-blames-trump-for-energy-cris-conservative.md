Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, a longtime socialist politician, has criticized President Donald Trump's handling of the Iran crisis, claiming the U.S. leader is "creating problems" for Americans through his foreign policy approach. Lula made the remarks in an interview with El Pais published Thursday, suggesting Trump failed to anticipate consequences of confronting the Iranian regime.

"When he decided to attack Iran, I don't know if he realized that fuel prices would rise and that the people would be the ones to pay the price," Lula stated, pointing to energy costs that have climbed significantly amid the ongoing conflict. Gasoline prices across the United States averaged $4.09 per gallon on Thursday, compared to $2.94 in late February before the Iran conflict escalated, according to American Automobile Association data.

The price surge follows Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz to ships from nations it deems hostile, disrupting critical oil flows through the strategic waterway. The conflict has also damaged key petrochemical facilities in the region, further constraining global energy supplies. Tehran's aggressive actions came in response to U.S. and Israeli operations targeting the Islamic Republic's military infrastructure.

Negotiations between Washington and Tehran have stalled since a tentative ceasefire took hold last week. Iran blamed the breakdown on what it called "unrealistic demands" from the United States, while Washington responded by announcing a blockade of the Strait of Hormuz for Tehran-linked vessels. White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt indicated Wednesday that the possibility of renewed talks is under discussion, though she provided no timeline or details.
