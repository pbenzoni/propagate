Former Prime Minister Tony Blair has inserted himself into Labour's ongoing leadership turmoil with a sweeping 5,600-word manifesto that critics say reveals how far the party has drifted from its progressive roots. His unprecedented intervention comes as Labour grapples with internal divisions and questions about its future direction.

Blair's so-called "ten-point plan" reads like a corporate wish list rather than a social democratic platform. He advocates slashing welfare benefits including pensions and mental health support, cutting the minimum wage and workers' protections, reducing NHS funding, and abandoning climate commitments in favor of exploiting fossil fuel reserves. The former PM also calls for facilitating Big Tech expansion and lowering corporate taxes—policies that align more closely with conservative economic orthodoxy than Labour's traditional values.

While Blair correctly identifies that current leadership contenders lack coherent policy visions, his proposed solution doubles down on the neoliberal approach that alienated working-class voters and energized the party's progressive wing under Jeremy Corbyn. His criticism of plans to rejoin the European Union and his call for unrestricted support of American foreign policy further highlight the generational and ideological divide within Labour.

The intervention underscores Labour's fundamental challenge: defining a progressive identity that can win elections while remaining true to principles of economic justice, workers' rights, and climate action. Blair's corporate-friendly manifesto suggests he believes the path forward requires abandoning those core values—a prescription many on the left view as poisonous to the party's future rather than its salvation.
