Elon Musk testified Wednesday that he was misled into funding OpenAI with $38 million between 2015 and 2017, believing he was supporting a nonprofit organization dedicated to advancing artificial intelligence for the public good. The tech billionaire called himself a "fool" for trusting assurances from CEO Sam Altman that the company would maintain its charitable structure.

Musk's lawsuit, filed in 2024, alleges that OpenAI—now valued at approximately $85 billion—betrayed its founding mission by restructuring into a for-profit entity. The company maintains a nonprofit parent organization that holds a stake in its commercial arm, which produces ChatGPT. Musk argues this arrangement allows leadership to "have it both ways"—enjoying tax benefits and public goodwill associated with nonprofit status while personally enriching themselves.

The case raises broader questions about accountability in the tech sector, particularly when companies founded on public-interest principles pivot toward profit-maximization. Musk described his evolving view of OpenAI's leadership as moving from "enthusiastic support" to a belief "they're looting a nonprofit." His legal challenge seeks to reverse the for-profit transition, remove current leadership, and secure damages for the nonprofit entity.

OpenAI has contested the allegations, maintaining that no binding commitment existed to remain a nonprofit indefinitely. The company suggests Musk's lawsuit is motivated by competitive interests—he left OpenAI in 2018 over strategic disagreements and subsequently launched rival AI company xAI in 2023. The trial in Oakland federal court began Monday and is expected to continue for approximately four weeks.
