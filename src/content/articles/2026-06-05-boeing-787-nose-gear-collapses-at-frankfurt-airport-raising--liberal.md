Several Lufthansa crew members and ground staff were injured Thursday when the front landing gear of a Boeing 787 Dreamliner unexpectedly collapsed while the aircraft sat parked at a gate in Frankfurt Airport, according to the airline. The plane was scheduled to fly to Los Angeles, but passengers had not yet boarded when the incident occurred.

Dramatic security footage shared on social media shows the aircraft's nose suddenly dropping to the tarmac, with a panel appearing to dislodge as the plane settled. The Boeing 787 involved was built last year and had only entered service with Lufthansa in February, according to flight tracking data from Flightradar24.

The incident adds to growing alarm over Boeing's manufacturing standards and corporate accountability. Whistleblowers and former employees have alleged systemic production shortcuts, ignored defects, and inadequate quality control across the company's aircraft programs—concerns that have drawn scrutiny from regulators and lawmakers alike.

Boeing's recent history includes multiple serious safety events. In January 2024, a door plug blew out of an Alaska Airlines 737 MAX 9 mid-flight, forcing an emergency landing. The company continues to face consequences from two fatal 737 MAX crashes in 2018 and 2019 that killed 346 people and led to a worldwide grounding. Other Boeing 787 incidents in recent years have resulted in injuries and, in one case in India last year, a catastrophic crash that killed hundreds.

Lufthansa stated that authorities are investigating the circumstances of Thursday's collapse. The incident underscores ongoing questions about corporate oversight, worker safety protections, and the need for stronger regulatory enforcement in the aviation industry.
