The Daily Telegraph's new owner, German media conglomerate Axel Springer, has established editorial principles that include explicit support for Israel's right to exist, according to reports from British left-wing journalist Owen Jones. The UK government recently approved Axel Springer's £575 million ($778 million) acquisition of the Telegraph, with the deal expected to finalize by the end of June.

CEO Mathias Döpfner reportedly circulated a letter to employees outlining the company's binding "Essentials." The principles prioritize "freedom, freedom of expression, the rule of law, and democracy" as the first point, followed by support for "the right of Israel to exist and oppose all forms of antisemitism." The document also includes opposition to "political and religious extremism, as well as all forms of discrimination." Axel Springer already owns prominent outlets including Germany's Bild and Die Welt, as well as Politico.

Jones criticized the editorial standards as "Orwellian," questioning why Israel is the only country specifically mentioned among nearly 200 nations worldwide. He also claimed that an anonymous Telegraph insider expressed concern about the policy. However, Döpfner—who has described himself as a "non-Jewish Zionist"—has been transparent about his views, writing in October 2023 that calls for Palestinian control "from the River to the Sea" amount to genocidal rhetoric against Israelis.

The editorial principles have sparked debate about the role of corporate values in newsroom operations. Supporters of clear editorial standards argue that defending fundamental rights—including a nation's right to exist and opposing antisemitism—represents principled journalism. Critics, particularly those aligned with Jones's political perspective, contend the policy could influence coverage of Middle Eastern conflicts. The Telegraph, sometimes called the 'Torygraph' for its conservative lean, has long maintained editorial positions aligned with center-right principles.
