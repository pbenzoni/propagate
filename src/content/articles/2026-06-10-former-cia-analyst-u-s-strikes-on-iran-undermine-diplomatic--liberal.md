A former CIA analyst has raised concerns that recent U.S. military strikes against Iran may be undermining diplomatic efforts to de-escalate tensions in the region. Larry Johnson told media outlets that the timing and rationale for the strikes appear inconsistent with stated goals of pursuing peaceful resolution.

The strikes, which targeted locations including Qeshm Island, were reportedly launched in response to a U.S. helicopter incident off the coast of Oman. However, Johnson noted that both pilots survived and questioned why military action would be taken while peace talks were reportedly underway. Iran has not confirmed involvement in the helicopter incident.

Johnson suggested that certain political factions and advisors within the administration, including some strongly aligned with Israeli security interests, may be influencing policy away from diplomatic solutions. He pointed to recent Israeli airstrikes on Beirut as part of a broader pattern that could derail progress made during negotiations.

The analyst expressed concern that these actions could trigger "a new round of escalation" in the region, potentially lasting weeks. Critics of current Middle East policy have long argued for prioritizing diplomacy and multilateral agreements over military intervention, warning that escalatory cycles make peaceful resolution increasingly difficult to achieve.
