The U.S. Army is preparing to carry out its first military executions in more than six decades, according to internal planning documents obtained by ABC News. The preparations come after President Trump reversed a moratorium on federal executions that had been established by President Biden in 2021.

An internal plan known as "Operation Resolute Justice" reportedly requires the military to be ready to execute four death-row service members within 150 days of presidential approval. The preparations include reviewing execution procedures and coordinating with the Federal Bureau of Prisons to transfer inmates from Fort Leavenworth, Kansas to the federal execution facility in Terre Haute, Indiana.

The move represents a stark reversal from the Biden administration's approach to criminal justice reform and capital punishment. During Trump's first term, his administration oversaw 13 federal executions—more than any other modern presidency—after a 17-year pause in federal death sentences. Trump has long advocated for expanded use of capital punishment, framing it as a deterrent to violent crime despite research showing limited evidence for such deterrent effects.

The four service members on military death row were convicted of serious crimes including mass shootings and multiple murders. Among them is former Army Major Nidal Hasan, who was sentenced to death for the 2009 Fort Hood shooting that killed 13 people and wounded 32 others. While an Army spokesperson characterized the preparations as "standard planning," the timing coincides with the administration's broader rollback of criminal justice reforms implemented under the previous administration.
