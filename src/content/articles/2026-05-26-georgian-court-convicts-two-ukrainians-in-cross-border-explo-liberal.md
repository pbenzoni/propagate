A court in Tbilisi has sentenced two Ukrainian nationals to seven and ten years in prison after convicting them of smuggling military-grade explosives into Georgia. The case centered on 2.4 kilograms of hexogen—also known as RDX, a high-powered explosive stronger than TNT—discovered by Georgian security services in September 2024.

According to court documents, the explosive material was hidden inside a Mercedes-Benz truck bearing Ukrainian license plates. Authorities say the vehicle crossed into Georgia at the Sarpi border checkpoint from Turkey, having previously traveled through Romania and Bulgaria. Investigators determined the shipment was addressed to a residential building in Tbilisi's Avlabari district.

The Tbilisi City Court found the defendants guilty of illegally acquiring, storing, transporting, and selling explosives, as well as smuggling them across Georgia's customs border. While the truck driver reportedly claimed the cargo was part of an operation bound for Russia, Georgian security officials said evidence pointed only to the Tbilisi delivery address.

The convictions come amid broader regional security concerns about weapons trafficking in Eastern Europe and the South Caucasus. The case highlights ongoing challenges faced by transit countries in monitoring cross-border movement of dangerous materials, particularly through multiple jurisdictions. Georgian authorities have not disclosed whether the investigation remains open or if additional suspects are being sought in connection with the smuggling operation.
