Iran has flatly rejected U.S. military claims that American forces destroyed six Iranian naval boats in the Strait of Hormuz, calling the assertion a "lie" as tensions continue to mount in the strategically vital waterway.

Admiral Brad Cooper, head of U.S. Central Command, told reporters Monday that U.S. forces had "eliminated" what he described as Iranian vessels allegedly threatening commercial shipping. The reported military action came as part of "Project Freedom," an operation announced by President Donald Trump to escort tankers through the strait following military strikes on Iran in late February. Cooper boasted of deploying "an enormous amount of capability and firepower" including Apache and Seahawk helicopters to the region.

The Strait of Hormuz, which carries approximately 20% of the world's seaborne oil trade, has become a flashpoint in escalating U.S.-Iran tensions. Iran has reportedly proposed a new mechanism to govern the waterway as part of broader peace negotiations, but the U.S. rejected the offer. Trump has ordered a naval blockade of Iranian ports in an effort to pressure Tehran into accepting Washington's terms for a settlement.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi condemned the blockade as an "act of war" that violates a ceasefire the two nations reached in early April. "Project Freedom is Project Deadlock," Araghchi warned on social media, cautioning Washington against "being dragged back into quagmire by ill-wishers." Both sides have accused each other of attacks on naval vessels in the contested waters, with each denying the other's claims.
