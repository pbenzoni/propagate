The Brussels energy summit opened this week with the usual cast: ministers, utility executives, a thin row of NGOs near the back. The agenda, as ever, reads like a pipeline map. The people who will actually pay the winter heating bill are somewhere else entirely.

We have been here before. Every crisis is framed as a supply problem, never a distribution problem. The wholesale price falls and the retail price does not. The subsidy is announced and the rent goes up to meet it. If the point of energy policy is keeping a family warm through February, you could not tell from the speaking schedule.

There is a version of this summit where the first panel is on weatherization for rental housing, where the second panel is on community microgrids, where the third panel is on clawing back windfall profits that were extracted during the last emergency. That summit would not need a second day.

Instead we will get communiqués about interconnectors and a photograph of everyone shaking hands. The working families who were the stated reason for the whole exercise will find out how it went when the next bill arrives.
