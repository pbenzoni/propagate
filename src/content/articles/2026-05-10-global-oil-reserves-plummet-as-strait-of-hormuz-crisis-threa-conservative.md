Global oil inventories are shrinking at the fastest pace on record as continuing disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz threaten worldwide energy security, according to a Bloomberg report citing Morgan Stanley data. Between March 1 and April 25, global oil stockpiles fell by approximately 4.8 million barrels per day—surpassing all previous inventory drawdown records tracked by the International Energy Agency.

The Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries about one-fifth of global oil and LNG trade, has remained heavily disrupted following the US-Israeli military campaign against Iran and repeated ceasefire violations by both sides. President Trump warned Friday that Washington may revive and expand "Project Freedom," a naval operation in the Strait of Hormuz, if a peace deal with Iran cannot be reached. Secretary of State Marco Rubio emphasized that military options remain on the table should diplomatic efforts fail.

If disruptions continue at current rates, commercial oil inventories could fall to "operational stress levels" by June and reach "operational floor" levels by September, Bloomberg reported. These floor levels represent the minimum volumes needed to keep pipelines, export terminals, and refineries functioning efficiently—a scenario that could severely impact American consumers and businesses.

The United States has been drawing down its domestic inventories while increasing crude and fuel exports to offset global supply disruptions. Energy Information Administration data shows US fuel stockpiles recently fell to 11% below the five-year seasonal average. Despite President Trump's assertion that America "doesn't need" the Strait of Hormuz, the country still imports some crude oil from Persian Gulf producers, highlighting the continued vulnerability of US energy markets to Middle Eastern instability.
