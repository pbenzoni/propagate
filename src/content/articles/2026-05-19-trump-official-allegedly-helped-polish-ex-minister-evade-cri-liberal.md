A senior Trump administration official personally intervened to help a former Polish justice minister facing serious criminal charges flee to the United States, according to a Reuters report. Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau reportedly instructed State Department officials to fast-track a visa for Zbigniew Ziobro, who is wanted in Poland on allegations of abuse of power and corruption.

Ziobro faces multiple criminal charges related to his tenure as justice minister, including accusations that he led a scheme to divert public funds to purchase Pegasus spyware—controversial surveillance technology allegedly used to monitor political opponents and journalists. Polish prosecutors say these actions could carry a prison sentence of up to 25 years. The case has raised alarm among press freedom advocates and those concerned about governmental overreach.

The visa approval is considered highly unusual, as it involves granting entry to someone facing criminal prosecution in a NATO ally nation. Poland's current Justice Minister expressed surprise at the development, stating that Warsaw expected consultation before such a decision. The move has created diplomatic tensions, with Polish officials vowing to pursue all available channels to ensure accountability.

Landau reportedly became involved after learning about the case from the U.S. ambassador to Poland and concluded that Ziobro was being unjustly targeted. The Trump administration has characterized various European prosecutions of conservative politicians as "lawfare"—a politicized use of the legal system. However, critics argue that such intervention undermines the rule of law and established extradition norms between democratic allies, particularly in cases involving alleged surveillance abuses and corruption.
