A specialized European law enforcement task force has arrested 280 individuals in its first year of operation targeting a disturbing trend: criminal networks recruiting teenagers through social media platforms to carry out acts of violence for hire, according to Europol.

The unit, called GRIMM, was established last April to combat what experts describe as the "gig-ification" of violence—a system in which criminals use encrypted messaging apps and gaming platforms to hire young people, some as young as 14, to commit crimes ranging from assault to murder. The investigation has identified more than 1,400 individuals connected to these exploitative networks across Europe.

"Violence is no longer confined to local dynamics. It is increasingly offered as a service: accessible, scalable and driven by online ecosystems that enable recruitment, coordination, and execution across borders," Europol stated. Recruitment primarily occurs on platforms including Snapchat, Telegram, and TikTok, as well as within online gaming environments where vulnerable youth congregate.

The findings underscore growing concerns about child safety on social media and the need for stronger platform accountability. Recent cases include teenagers in Denmark and Sweden recruited online to carry out shootings, highlighting how unregulated digital spaces can facilitate the exploitation of minors. The investigation involves cooperation between EU member states, the UK, Norway, and Iceland.

The revelations come as European governments increasingly debate stricter age verification requirements and safety standards for social media platforms. France has already passed legislation requiring age verification for users under 15, while Germany and the UK are considering similar measures to protect young people from online harms and predatory recruitment.
