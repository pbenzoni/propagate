International nuclear safety experts are sounding alarms about the growing dangers at the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant, Europe's largest nuclear facility, which has been under Russian military occupation since March 2022. The head of Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom claimed that recent damage to one of the plant's units could pose radiation risks extending beyond Ukraine's borders.

The facility has become a flashpoint in the ongoing conflict, with Russia maintaining control despite international calls for demilitarization of the area. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has stated that the only path to ensuring safety at the plant is its return to Ukrainian control. The competing claims highlight the unprecedented challenge of protecting critical nuclear infrastructure in an active war zone.

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), which has inspectors stationed at the plant, has documented multiple incidents affecting the facility but has not attributed responsibility for attacks. IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi has repeatedly warned that the situation represents a serious nuclear safety concern. The agency continues to call for establishment of a nuclear safety and security protection zone around the plant.

Nuclear safety advocates have emphasized that any major incident at Zaporizhzhia could have catastrophic consequences for Ukraine, neighboring countries, and the broader European region, making the protection of the facility a matter of urgent international concern regardless of territorial disputes.
