A senior Israeli diplomat has responded dismissively to France's call for an emergency UN Security Council meeting over Israel's expanding military operations in Lebanon, despite a ceasefire agreement reached more than six weeks ago.

France requested the extraordinary session after Israeli forces intensified bombing campaigns and advanced further into Lebanese territory, including the seizure of the historic Beaufort Castle. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu publicly stated he had ordered the military to "expand its ground maneuver in Lebanon," raising concerns about the scope of the incursion.

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot emphasized that "nothing can justify the continuation of Israeli military operations in Lebanon and its ever-deepening occupation of Lebanese territory." In response, Waleed Gadban, Israel's political advisor to the UN permanent representative, posted social media content from recent celebrations in Paris following a football championship victory, suggesting France should prioritize domestic matters.

The dismissive response comes amid growing international concern over the violation of the ceasefire agreement and the humanitarian implications of continued military operations in Lebanon. The United Nations Security Council meeting represents an effort by France and other member states to address what they view as an escalating crisis in the region.
