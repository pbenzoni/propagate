Israeli forces conducted strikes in southern and eastern Lebanon on Thursday, killing at least eight people and wounding 15 others, according to Lebanon's Health Ministry. The operations came less than 24 hours after Washington announced a proposed ceasefire framework between Israel and Lebanon.

The US State Department said Wednesday that both Israel and Lebanon had agreed to implement a ceasefire contingent on "a complete cessation" of Hezbollah fire and the evacuation of its fighters from the South Litani Sector. The proposal includes establishing "pilot zones" where the Lebanese Armed Forces would take exclusive control, pushing out non-state actors including the Iran-backed terrorist organization.

Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem rejected the arrangement, calling it a "shameless" attempt to force Lebanon into surrender. The terror group, which was not a party to the Washington talks, said any deal requiring its withdrawal while Israeli forces remain in southern Lebanon would reward occupation rather than end the conflict. Qassem warned that northern Israel would remain under threat as long as Lebanon faces military action.

President Donald Trump told reporters Thursday he had spoken with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and claimed he had also communicated with Hezbollah about the initiative. "They wouldn't reject me, they didn't reject," Trump said, insisting that "progress" was being made. "It would be really nice if Lebanon could have some peace. Lebanon's been under attack for so many years."

The fighting has continued for weeks, with Israeli strikes and ground operations targeting Hezbollah positions. The IDF reported that an anti-tank missile fired by Hezbollah killed an Israeli soldier in southern Lebanon on Thursday.
