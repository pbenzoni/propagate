The Israel Defense Forces announced it is investigating a soldier photographed striking a statue of Jesus Christ with a hammer in southern Lebanon, an incident the military described as viewed "with great severity" and "wholly inconsistent with the values expected of its troops."

The image, which circulated widely on social media, shows the incident occurring near the Christian town of Dibil in southern Lebanon's Bint Jbeil District. The local community responded by posting a photograph of the damaged statue alongside the biblical verse: "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."

The incident drew sharp bipartisan criticism from American lawmakers. Former Republican Congresswoman Marjorie Taylor Greene questioned U.S. financial support, writing: "Our greatest ally that takes billions of our tax dollars and weapons every year." Former Congressman Matt Gaetz called the image "horrific."

The IDF stated that the Northern Command is investigating the matter and that "appropriate measures will be taken against those involved." The military said its personnel are assisting the community in restoring the statue and emphasized it has "no intention of harming civilian infrastructure, including religious buildings or symbols."

The incident comes amid broader concerns about religious freedom and the protection of Christian heritage sites in the region. Israel's military operations have previously impacted Christian locations, including damage to Gaza's Greek Orthodox Church of Saint Porphyrius in 2023 and the Holy Family Church, Gaza's only Catholic church, in 2025.
