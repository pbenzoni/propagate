Elon Musk's SpaceX has pushed back against Pentagon expectations for satellite connectivity pricing, insisting the military pay appropriate rates for the service tier it's actually using to operate unmanned combat drones during the Iran conflict, according to a Reuters report.

The disagreement centered on the Low-Cost Uncrewed Combat Attack System (LUCAS), where SpaceX argued the Pentagon had been paying approximately $5,000 per terminal while utilizing a premium service tier legitimately priced at $25,000 monthly. In March, Musk clarified on social media that using commercial Starlink terminals for weapons systems violates the company's terms of service, emphasizing that military operators should instead use Starshield—a separate government-controlled network designed specifically for defense applications.

While the Pentagon denies any breach of its agreement, the military ultimately agreed to nearly double the original $30,000 cost per LUCAS drone. Defense officials now say they're exploring alternative satellite providers, though industry experts acknowledge no competitor currently matches SpaceX's scale or capabilities. Rivals like OneWeb and Amazon's Project Kuiper remain substantially smaller or are still in deployment phases.

The pricing dispute comes as the Iran war has imposed massive financial strain on the Pentagon, with analysts estimating the conflict costs hundreds of millions of dollars daily. The first six days alone reportedly consumed roughly $12.7 billion, driving military planners toward cheaper, mass-produced weapons from newer defense contractors. Despite concerns about dependence on a single provider, the Pentagon is reportedly considering purchasing more than 3,500 additional Starshield subscriptions—a deal potentially worth hundreds of millions annually for SpaceX ahead of its planned IPO next month.
