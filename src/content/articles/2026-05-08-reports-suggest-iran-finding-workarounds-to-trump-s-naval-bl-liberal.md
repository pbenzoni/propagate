Three Iranian oil tankers have reportedly navigated around U.S. naval forces in the Strait of Hormuz, reaching Iranian ports through Pakistani waters, according to Tehran's state news agency IRNA citing maritime tracking data. The development raises questions about the effectiveness of the Trump administration's naval blockade strategy imposed last month.

The blockade, which Trump has characterized as "a wall of steel," was designed to pressure Iran into accepting a nuclear agreement by cutting off its oil trade. However, Iran operates ports outside the Strait of Hormuz chokepoint—including Chabahar and Jask near the Pakistani border—allowing vessels to reach Iranian territory via the Gulf of Oman without transiting the heavily-patrolled narrow corridor.

A confidential U.S. intelligence assessment obtained by the Washington Post suggests Iran can withstand the blockade for at least three to four months before experiencing severe economic hardship. While crude exports have been significantly disrupted, Tehran has reportedly adapted by using tankers as floating storage and reducing output to preserve infrastructure. Analysts estimate Iran currently has access to approximately 65-75 million barrels of floating storage capacity.

The intelligence finding that Iran's economic situation is "nowhere near as dire as some have claimed" contrasts sharply with Trump's public assertions that Iran's economy is "crashing." Pakistan has also opened overland transit routes connecting its ports and border crossings to Iran, providing additional channels for continued trade flows despite the naval pressure campaign.
