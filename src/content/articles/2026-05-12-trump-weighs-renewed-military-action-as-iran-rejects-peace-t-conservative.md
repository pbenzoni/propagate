President Donald Trump is considering resuming military operations against Iran after peace negotiations reached an impasse, with Iranian officials rejecting American terms and demanding concessions that White House sources describe as unacceptable.

According to reports, Trump met with his national security team Monday to discuss restarting airstrikes against remaining military targets in Iran. The administration had suspended operations approximately one month ago to pursue diplomatic solutions, but Iranian negotiators have since demanded the lifting of sanctions, reparations payments, and recognition of Tehran's "sovereign control" over the Strait of Hormuz—a critical shipping lane through which much of the world's oil passes.

The stalemate comes as intelligence assessments indicate Iran retained significant military capability despite earlier strikes. A CIA report suggests Tehran maintains roughly three-quarters of its mobile missile launchers and could withstand economic pressure for several months. Iranian officials have publicly stated the United States has "no alternative" but to accept their terms, with one negotiator warning that delays would cost American taxpayers more.

The administration previously attempted to launch Project Freedom, an operation to escort commercial ships through the Strait of Hormuz, but suspended it after regional partners including Saudi Arabia declined to provide base access and airspace. Pakistan has served as an intermediary in the conflict, though Tehran's insistence on controlling the strategic waterway and receiving financial compensation has proven a major obstacle to any agreement. Iran's demand that America shut down enforcement of nuclear site inspections remains what officials call "a non-starter."
