A powerful 7.8 magnitude earthquake struck off the southern Philippines Monday morning, sending residents fleeing into the streets and triggering tsunami warnings across the Pacific region. The quake hit near Mindanao shortly before 7:40 AM local time at a depth of approximately 35 kilometers, followed by multiple strong aftershocks including one measuring 6.1 magnitude.

Dramatic footage from the scene shows buildings violently swaying, structural damage to homes and businesses, and citizens rushing to safety as debris fell into streets. At least one person was confirmed killed and four others injured as local authorities and emergency responders worked to assess damage across the affected region. "Many buildings were affected, but I cannot enumerate them now because we are busy with ongoing rescues," Master Sergeant Robert Dagon of the General Santos City police told reporters.

The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center issued alerts for potential waves up to 3 meters along portions of the Philippine coast, with smaller tsunami threats extending to Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, Taiwan, and other western Pacific areas. Authorities ordered coastal evacuations and urged citizens to move inland to higher ground. Waves between 0.09 and 0.19 meters were subsequently detected at three Indonesian coastal locations.

The Philippines sits on the Pacific "Ring of Fire," one of Earth's most seismically active zones where tectonic plate movements regularly generate earthquakes and volcanic activity. Officials warned residents to expect continued aftershocks and advised staying in open areas until buildings could be properly inspected for structural integrity. The earthquake was also felt in parts of eastern Indonesia, demonstrating the widespread impact of this significant natural disaster.
