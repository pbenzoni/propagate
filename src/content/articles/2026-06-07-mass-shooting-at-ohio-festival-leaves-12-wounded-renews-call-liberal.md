At least 12 people were wounded when gunfire erupted near the Old West End Festival in Toledo, Ohio, on Saturday afternoon, transforming what should have been a safe community gathering into yet another scene of gun violence. The victims ranged in age from 14 to 61, with two people reported in critical condition.

Police responded to reports of shots fired around 5:37 p.m. local time and found multiple gunshot victims at the scene. Toledo Deputy Police Chief Joe Heffernan indicated that at least two individuals appeared to be firing weapons, "probably shooting at each other," suggesting the violence may have stemmed from a dispute that endangered innocent bystanders at the family-friendly event.

Witnesses described scenes of terror as festivalgoers—including children and families—fled for cover. Kevin Berry, a Navy veteran with medical training who was present during the shooting, recounted seeing at least five wounded people scattered across the area. Video footage captured the chaos as crowds ran in panic while gunshots echoed through what was meant to be a celebration of community.

Ohio Governor Mike DeWine expressed concern over the incident, stating that "summer festivals should be safe spaces for families to spend time together without fear of violence." The shooting occurred at the 53rd annual Old West End Festival, a two-day event featuring live music, food vendors, and family activities in Toledo's historic district. As of Sunday, the suspects remained at large while authorities continued their investigation.
