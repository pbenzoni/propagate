A former Slovak interior minister has alleged that European intelligence services conducted surveillance on Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán and his foreign minister for years, according to claims published this week. The allegations come after Orbán's Fidesz party suffered a decisive electoral defeat to Peter Magyar's opposition Tisza party.

Vladimir Palko, who served in Slovakia's intelligence apparatus in the 1990s and as interior minister from 2002 to 2006, claims the surveillance operation monitored communications between Hungarian officials and their Russian counterparts. The alleged intelligence-sharing involved providing journalists with information about contacts between Hungary's foreign minister and Russian officials during a period when Orbán's government had become increasingly isolated within the EU over its opposition to Ukraine aid and migration policies.

The claims raise complex questions about the balance between protecting democratic institutions and respecting national sovereignty. Orbán's government has faced years of criticism from EU officials and human rights organizations over concerns about judicial independence, press freedom, and democratic backsliding. The European Parliament has formally declared that Hungary can no longer be considered a full democracy, citing systematic erosion of democratic norms.

While the surveillance allegations remain unverified, they emerge against a backdrop of documented EU actions, including the activation of fact-checking mechanisms aimed at combating disinformation during the Hungarian election. Critics of Orbán's government have long pointed to his close relationship with Russia and efforts to undermine EU unity as justifications for heightened scrutiny, even as debates continue over appropriate methods of accountability.
