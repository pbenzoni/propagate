A European arts festival has buckled to cancel culture pressure, pulling a scheduled discussion featuring PayPal and Palantir co-founder Peter Thiel after left-wing activists and government bureaucrats orchestrated a coordinated campaign against the conservative tech entrepreneur.

The Vienna Festival had invited Thiel to participate in a panel titled 'Armageddon and the Antichrist? From Theology to Realpolitik' as part of this year's programming. But rather than allowing open debate and the free exchange of ideas, critics demanded the Silicon Valley investor be deplatformed entirely. Vienna's City Councillor for Culture even publicly attacked the invitation, claiming it caused "great discontent among the public"—a familiar refrain from government officials seeking to suppress viewpoints that challenge progressive orthodoxy.

Facing threats of mass withdrawal from other participants and mounting pressure from the festival's government sponsors, organizers ultimately caved. Artistic Director Milo Rau admitted cancelling the event, though he acknowledged finding it "extremely compelling" within the festival's own stated mission of creating a "space of radical criticism."

Thiel, known for his outspoken conservative and entrepreneurial views, has faced similar attempts at silencing before. The incident underscores a troubling pattern across Western institutions: the stated commitment to "radical criticism" and "new beginnings" apparently doesn't extend to ideas that challenge left-wing conventional wisdom.
