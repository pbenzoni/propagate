America could face serious oil shortages within weeks as the ongoing Middle East conflict and closure of the Strait of Hormuz threaten to choke off global energy supplies, Chevron CEO Mike Wirth warned at a major industry conference.

The Strait of Hormuz—a critical chokepoint carrying roughly one-fifth of the world's seaborne oil and natural gas—has been effectively blocked since late February following US and Israeli strikes on Iran. Multiple tankers remain stranded, and tensions reignited Monday when American and Iranian forces exchanged fire as the US military attempted to escort vessels through the waterway. Washington and Tehran have failed to reach agreement on reopening the strait, with the US rejecting Iran's governance proposal.

"We will start to see physical shortages," Wirth told the Milken Institute Global Conference in Los Angeles. "Demand needs to move to meet supply. Economies are going to have to slow." The Chevron chief warned the impact could rival the devastating oil crises of the 1970s, which triggered widespread fuel shortages and economic disruption across America. He noted that strategic reserves and stockpiles are already being depleted to delay the inevitable shortages.

While the US—now a net crude exporter—will be somewhat insulated initially, Wirth cautioned that Americans will eventually feel the pain through higher prices at the pump and throughout the economy. Asia and Europe, far more dependent on Middle Eastern oil, will suffer first. European airlines are already restricting jet fuel use and cutting flights, while several Asian nations have introduced emergency demand-reduction measures. Budget carrier Spirit Airlines announced over the weekend it was shutting down, blaming skyrocketing fuel costs.

The International Energy Agency called the Hormuz disruption "the biggest energy security threat in history," with some 13 million barrels per day lost. The World Bank projects energy prices will jump 24% this year. Even once the strait reopens, Wirth warned it would take months to stabilize supply chains and restore normal operations.
