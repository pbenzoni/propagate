The Department of Energy has chosen five private nuclear firms to participate in an innovative program converting weapons-grade plutonium from dismantled Cold War-era warheads into fuel for next-generation small reactors. The initiative aims to put America's 50-metric-ton stockpile of secured fissile material to productive use while strengthening domestic energy independence.

Among the selected companies is Oklo Inc., a California-based advanced reactor developer, alongside Exodys Energy, SHINE Technologies, Standard Nuclear, and Flibe Energy. The program allocates 20 tons of surplus plutonium for transfer to private partners, who say the material will serve as "bridge fuel" to accelerate deployment of cutting-edge reactor technology. Energy officials note that access to plutonium supplies will help these American companies attract investment and compete globally.

The approach mirrors successful Cold War demilitarization efforts. A previous U.S.-Russian program converted weapons-grade uranium—enough for roughly 20,000 warheads—into reactor fuel before expiring in 2013, demonstrating that repurposing military materials for civilian energy production can work at scale.

Some Democratic lawmakers have raised concerns about the security protocols involved. The administration maintains that strict handling and oversight requirements will govern all plutonium transfers, emphasizing that proper safeguards and regulatory frameworks are built into the program. The Energy Department has invested $2.7 billion over ten years in domestic enrichment capacity after decades of reliance on foreign suppliers, including Russia, left America's nuclear fuel sector weakened. This plutonium utilization program represents another step toward rebuilding American energy self-sufficiency and leveraging private-sector innovation to solve national challenges.
