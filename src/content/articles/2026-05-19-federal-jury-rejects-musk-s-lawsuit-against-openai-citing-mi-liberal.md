A federal jury has rejected Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI and its CEO Sam Altman, dismissing the billionaire's allegations that executives unlawfully enriched themselves by transforming the artificial intelligence company from a nonprofit into a for-profit venture.

Musk had claimed he invested $38 million in OpenAI and sought $150 billion in damages, arguing that Altman and other executives betrayed the company's founding mission as a nonprofit research lab. The nine-member advisory jury found that Musk had waited too long to file his claims, missing the statutory deadline for such complaints. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers subsequently adopted the jury's verdict and dismissed the case.

According to testimony cited in court documents, OpenAI defendants argued that Musk's true motivation was gaining control of the company. Altman testified that Musk had once demanded a 90% stake in OpenAI and proposed merging it with Tesla, which Musk said would provide necessary funding. OpenAI's chairman noted that the company received a formal takeover offer from a consortium led by Musk's rival firm xAI in February 2025—six months after Musk initially filed his lawsuit.

Musk vowed to appeal the ruling, claiming on social media that the decision sets a dangerous precedent. The case highlights ongoing tensions in the AI industry over corporate governance, the balance between profit and public interest, and questions about accountability in rapidly evolving technology sectors.
