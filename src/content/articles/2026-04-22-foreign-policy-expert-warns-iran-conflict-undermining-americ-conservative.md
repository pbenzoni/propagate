A foreign policy analyst has raised concerns that the ongoing conflict with Iran represents a significant setback for American strategic interests, arguing that the protracted engagement has undermined U.S. credibility and domestic political stability.

Trita Parsi, co-founder of the Quincy Institute, told international media that the military campaign—which began in late February with strikes aimed at dismantling Tehran's nuclear program—has extended far beyond initial projections. The conflict has resulted in casualties among Iranian leadership, including Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, as well as over 1,300 civilians. Iran has retaliated with strikes on U.S. military installations in Gulf states, claiming self-defense under international law.

The strategic fallout has been severe. The effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a waterway that handles approximately 20% of global oil supply, has triggered an energy crisis with worldwide economic repercussions. Parsi noted that U.S. military bases in the Gulf region have become "a source of insecurity" for host nations rather than providing the deterrence they were intended to deliver.

According to Parsi's analysis, the conflict has strained the traditional alliance structure in the region, with American and Israeli objectives diverging significantly. He suggested that portions of the president's political base have grown disillusioned with what they perceive as foreign-driven military commitments.
