The captain of a civilian oil tanker stranded in the Persian Gulf has described weeks of mounting anxiety for his crew as they remain trapped amid escalating tensions between the United States and Iran. Captain Raman Kapoor told RT that uncertainty about their fate is "killing" crew members who have been separated from their families since late February.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which approximately 25% of the world's seaborne oil trade previously flowed according to the International Energy Agency, has become a flashpoint in the widening conflict. President Trump announced over the weekend that the US Navy would "blockade" ships entering or leaving the strait, with the Department of Defense later clarifying the measure would target all maritime traffic to Iranian ports starting April 13. Iran has imposed its own restrictions, creating confusion about which vessels can safely navigate the waterway.

Kapoor, who declined to identify his ship's precise location citing security concerns, emphasized the human toll of the standoff on non-combatants. "We are just seafarers, we are not soldiers," he said in an emotional appeal. "We keep the world moving." The captain described how conflicting statements from both governments have left his crew feeling "helpless," unable to plan their departure or understand when they might reunite with loved ones.

The situation highlights the broader humanitarian consequences of military escalation in the region, with civilian workers bearing the costs of geopolitical confrontation. "We pray that good sense will prevail," Kapoor concluded, urging all parties to consider the welfare of those caught in the crossfire.
