The United States may be preparing to release frozen Iranian assets held in Qatar and other countries, according to a Reuters report citing senior Iranian sources. The potential move comes as high-level delegations from both nations have arrived in Pakistan for diplomatic talks aimed at establishing a proposed peace framework.

According to the report, unfreezing the assets is "directly linked to ensuring safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz," a critical shipping corridor for global oil supplies. One Iranian official indicated the U.S. was ready to release $6 billion held in Qatar, though a senior U.S. official told CBS News that the White House has not yet agreed to authorize the release. The exact value of Iran's frozen assets worldwide remains unclear, with estimates exceeding $100 billion across various accounts and jurisdictions.

The $6 billion in question was originally transferred to Qatar in September 2023 as part of a prisoner exchange mediated by Doha, which secured the release of five Americans detained in Iran and five Iranians held in the U.S. At the time, Washington stated the funds would be restricted to humanitarian purposes only, with payments subject to U.S. Treasury oversight and limited to approved vendors.

However, following the October 7, 2023 attacks on Israel by Hamas, the Biden administration re-froze access to the money, asserting that Iran would not be able to use the funds for the foreseeable future. The assets originally stem from Iranian oil sales to South Korea and were frozen in South Korean banks in 2018 after the Trump administration withdrew from the Iran nuclear deal and reimposed sanctions during his first term in office.
