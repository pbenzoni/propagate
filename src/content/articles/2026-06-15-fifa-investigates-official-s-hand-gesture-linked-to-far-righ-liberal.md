Evans, serving as a Video Assistant Referee (VAR) supervisor, displayed an upside-down 'OK' sign while looking directly into the camera as the video review team was shown before Sunday's match.

Fare, a long-standing FIFA partner focused on anti-discrimination efforts in football, immediately called for Evans to be removed from the tournament. The organization noted that the gesture "clearly resembles" a symbol that has been appropriated by far-right extremists as a white-power sign. "Why is a VAR supervisor using this symbol at a global football event at the very moment he knows the cameras are on him?" the group asked, describing the gesture as having "neo-Nazi" connotations.

While the symbol originated as an internet hoax in 2017, it has since been genuinely adopted by extremist movements, leading organizations like the Anti-Defamation League to recognize it as a hate symbol in certain contexts. The gesture's dual meaning has created ongoing challenges for determining intent in individual cases.

FIFA confirmed it is seeking an explanation from Evans about the incident.
