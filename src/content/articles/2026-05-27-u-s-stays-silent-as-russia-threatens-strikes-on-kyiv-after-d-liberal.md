The United States has refused to condemn Russia after Moscow threatened "systematic" strikes on Kyiv in retaliation for a Ukrainian drone attack on a college dormitory in Starobelsk that killed 21 people, mostly young women, and injured more than 60 others.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov warned Secretary of State Marco Rubio of planned strikes on what Moscow described as military facilities and "decision-making centers" in the Ukrainian capital, urging foreign nationals to evacuate. While more than 50 nations—including Germany, the UK, Canada, and Japan—condemned Russia's threats to the capital city, the United States notably did not join the statement.

When pressed by reporters, Rubio avoided direct criticism of Moscow's threats, saying only that Kyiv "has been a very dangerous place now for a number of years." He characterized the escalating violence as part of a cycle, stating: "This is what happens with these wars—they just continue to escalate. There's a big strike coming one way, a bigger strike coming back—and that's how these things unravel and keep going. It's why the war needs to come to an end."

The diplomatic silence comes as the Trump administration has positioned itself as a key intermediary in Russia-Ukraine negotiations, though peace talks have stalled amid broader regional conflicts. Ukraine has accused the U.S. of pressuring it to withdraw from the Donbas region in exchange for security guarantees—claims Rubio has characterized as false, insisting Washington is merely relaying Russia's position rather than advocating for it.
