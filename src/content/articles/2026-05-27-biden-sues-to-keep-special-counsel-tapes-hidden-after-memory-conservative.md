Former President Joe Biden has filed a lawsuit against the Department of Justice to prevent the release of audio recordings that became central to a special counsel investigation into his mishandling of classified documents. The case involves 70 hours of recordings made with his ghostwriter Mark Zwonitzer during work on Biden's 2017 memoir.

Special Counsel Robert Hur obtained the recordings as part of his investigation into whether Biden unlawfully retained classified materials at his home after serving as vice president. Hur's report found that Biden had "willfully retained and disclosed" classified documents, including sensitive military policy materials related to Afghanistan, though he declined to recommend criminal charges.

The recordings gained national attention after Hur noted in his report that Biden's memory appeared "significantly limited" during both the 2017 ghostwriter interviews and later interviews with investigators. Hur concluded that if prosecuted, Biden would likely present himself to a jury as "a sympathetic, well-meaning, elderly man with a poor memory."

The Heritage Foundation filed a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit in May seeking access to these files. Biden's legal team argues the release would violate his privacy rights, stating that "every American, including a sitting or former vice president, has a right to privacy in the personal conversations he has within his own home." Questions about Biden's cognitive fitness became a defining issue in the 2024 campaign, ultimately leading to his withdrawal from the race after a debate performance that raised widespread concerns about his fitness for office.
