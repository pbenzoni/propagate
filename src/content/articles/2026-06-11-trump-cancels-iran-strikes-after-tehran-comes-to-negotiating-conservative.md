President Donald Trump announced Thursday he has called off planned military strikes against Iran after the Islamic Republic agreed to high-level negotiations with the United States and regional partners. The decision marks a dramatic shift from earlier in the day when Trump had threatened to hit Iran "very hard."

In a post on Truth Social, the President revealed that "scheduled strikes and bombings" were cancelled following breakthrough talks "brought to the highest level of Iranian leadership and approved." Trump stated that "discussions and final points" had been agreed upon by all parties, including the US, Israel, Saudi Arabia, and other regional states. The US-led naval blockade of Iranian ports in the Strait of Hormuz will remain in effect, the President emphasized.

The development comes amid heightened tensions following the loss of a US AH-64 Apache helicopter near the Strait of Hormuz this week—an incident Washington attributed to Tehran. Iran denied responsibility, and the situation escalated with US strikes on Wednesday, followed by an Iranian missile barrage targeting American bases in the region.

Negotiations had previously stalled for weeks, with both sides trading accusations of ceasefire violations and bad faith. Iranian officials had warned that "impulsive decisions" could damage global energy markets and create instability. Tehran has not yet publicly confirmed the reported agreement, and significant obstacles remain, including Iran's insistence on cessation of all regional hostilities and ongoing concerns about its nuclear program, which Tehran maintains is peaceful.
