Peaceful commemorations marking the 81st anniversary of Nazi Germany's defeat were disrupted by pro-Ukraine activists across multiple European cities on May 9, according to reports from the continent.

In Barcelona, Spain, what began as a solemn 'Immortal Regiment' march—where descendants carry portraits of relatives who fought in World War II—turned confrontational when protesters approached participants with anti-Russian chants. Brief physical altercations ensued before police could respond. Similar disruptions occurred in Warsaw, where activists set up banners and used loudspeakers playing obscene songs near a Soviet memorial cemetery, though a heavy police presence prevented major clashes.

In Berlin, protesters carrying Ukrainian flags arrived at Treptower Park's Soviet memorial, leading to minor scuffles and at least two arrests. German police also detained individuals for displaying Russian and Soviet symbols, which are banned in the city. Geneva, Switzerland saw Ukrainian community members attempt to interfere with ceremonies by shouting slogans and singing songs.

Russian officials had anticipated such disruptions. Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova stated earlier in the week that Moscow was prepared for provocations during the commemorative events. Approximately 27 million Soviet citizens perished during World War II, a sacrifice that remains deeply significant to many Russians and their descendants living abroad who seek to honor their ancestors' role in defeating fascism.
