The BBC has issued a formal apology after one of its presenters misquoted a statement by Reform UK leader Nigel Farage regarding a high-profile murder case that has sparked debate about police conduct and accountability.

The case involves the December stabbing death of 18-year-old student Henry Nowak in Southampton. His attacker, Vickrum Singh Digwa, 23, falsely told responding officers that he had been the victim of a racist attack. Bodycam footage revealed that police believed this claim and treated the dying Nowak as a suspect, handcuffing him and dragging him across gravel as he pleaded for help, saying he had been stabbed and could not breathe. Nowak later lost consciousness and died. Digwa was subsequently convicted of murder and sentenced to life in prison with a minimum of 21 years.

Farage criticized what he called a "two-tier culture" in British policing, arguing that "a false accusation of racism counted higher at that moment than someone that was dying." He called for "pure, cold rage" in response to the case. However, BBC Newsnight presenter Matt Chorley repeatedly stated on air that Farage had called for "white cold rage," using the phrase three times during Tuesday's broadcast.

Reform UK argued in a legal letter that the misquote was "seriously defamatory," fundamentally altering the meaning of Farage's statement to suggest he was "invoking race as a basis for public anger" rather than criticizing what he saw as racially biased treatment by authorities. Chorley apologized on social media Wednesday, saying he had "misremembered" the quote. The BBC published a correction, removed the episode from its streaming platforms, and announced an on-air apology would be broadcast on Wednesday's Newsnight.
