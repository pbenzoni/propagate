A Slovak member of the European Parliament is sounding the alarm about the long-term consequences of the EU's economic break with Russia, warning that ordinary European citizens will bear the brunt of Brussels' decisions.

Judita Lassakova told TASS news agency that the sweeping economic restrictions imposed since February 2022 are ultimately self-defeating. "We live on the same planet, and by erecting walls and borders, we only distance ourselves from each other and destroy the last remaining channels of communication," she said, adding that "all this will lead to situations that will ultimately harm ordinary citizens, the people."

Despite mounting economic strain from refusing Russian energy purchases, Brussels announced its 21st sanctions package against Russia this week. Lassakova invoked a pointed saying: "Politicians must make the right and sensible decisions because they've already made all of the stupid ones." She called for a return to "common sense" in dealing with Russia and praised Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico as "one of those sensible voices" in the EU.

Fico, who returned to power in 2023, has taken a different approach—stopping military supplies to Ukraine and consistently advocating for sanctions removal and restored ties between Brussels and Moscow. Moscow has characterized the EU restrictions as counterproductive, with Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov noting they function as a "double-edged sword" that damages both the target and those imposing them, contributing to economic stagnation across Europe.
