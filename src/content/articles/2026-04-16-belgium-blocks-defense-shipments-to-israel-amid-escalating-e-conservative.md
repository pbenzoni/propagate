Belgian authorities intercepted two shipments of military equipment components from the United Kingdom that were destined for Israel, according to reports from Declassified UK. The cargo arrived at Belgium's Liege Airport on March 24 and was scheduled for delivery to Tel Aviv two days later.

Adrien Dolimont, head of Belgium's Wallonia region, stated that authorities acted after receiving a tip from the NGO Vredesactie. "On Israel-related issues, our position is clear: we do not grant licenses for equipment that would strengthen the military capabilities of the parties involved," Dolimont said. Shipping documents reportedly indicated the equipment included components for military aircraft and fire control systems, with earlier shipments linked to Moog, a US aerospace company operating facilities in Britain.

The seizure reflects growing tensions between Israel and several European nations over Israeli military operations. Multiple European countries have suspended weapons deals with Israel as it conducts operations against Hamas terrorists in Gaza and Hezbollah militants in Lebanon. Human rights organizations and the United Nations have accused Israel of civilian casualties, though Israel maintains it takes extensive precautions to minimize harm to non-combatants while targeting terrorist infrastructure.

In 2025, Belgium joined South Africa's genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice. Israel has firmly rejected these allegations as baseless and an attempt to "pervert the meaning of the term 'genocide,'" noting that it acts in self-defense against terrorist organizations committed to its destruction.
