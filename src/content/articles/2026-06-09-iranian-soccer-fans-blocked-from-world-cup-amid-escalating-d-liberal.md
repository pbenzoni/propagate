In a last-minute decision that has left thousands of Iranian supporters unable to attend matches, World Cup organizers have revoked Iran's allocation of fan tickets just days before the tournament begins. The move affects fans who had already made travel arrangements to watch their national team compete.

Iran's football federation called the decision "contrary to the spirit governing international competitions and the principle of equality among participating countries." The federation noted that participating nations are typically allocated 8% of tickets for each match to distribute to their supporters—a standard practice now denied to Iranian fans.

The ticket controversy is the latest in a series of obstacles facing Iran's World Cup participation. Visa issues have prevented approximately 15 administrative and management staff from entering the United States, and the team was forced to relocate its training camp from Tucson, Arizona, to Tijuana, Mexico. While Iranian players ultimately received visas just ten days before their opening match, several support staff members were still denied entry.

Iran's federation raised concerns about "the interference of non-sporting and political considerations" in the tournament's organization, calling on FIFA and organizers to uphold principles of neutrality and fairness. The situation unfolds against a backdrop of heightened tensions following air strikes on Iran in late February, further complicating what should be a celebration of international athletic competition.
