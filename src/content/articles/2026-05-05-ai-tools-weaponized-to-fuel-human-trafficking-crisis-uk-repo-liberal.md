Artificial intelligence is enabling a disturbing expansion of human trafficking and modern slavery, according to a troubling new report commissioned by the UK's Independent Anti-Slavery Commissioner. The study reveals that criminal networks are weaponizing AI-driven tools—including deepfakes, synthetic identities, and algorithmic targeting—to identify vulnerable individuals, recruit victims, and maintain control at a scale previously impossible.

Reported cases of modern slavery in the UK reached 23,411 in 2025, a 22% increase from the previous year and the highest number on record. The report emphasizes that AI is making exploitation both more widespread and significantly harder to detect, as traffickers leverage technology to operate with greater efficiency and anonymity. Victims face coercion into forced labor, sexual exploitation, and other forms of trafficking, with digital tools expanding the reach of these abusive networks.

Economic factors are compounding the crisis. Rising costs of living are creating what Commissioner Eleanor Lyons describes as a "pipeline" of people at heightened risk of exploitation. She warned that without urgent intervention—including increased resources for law enforcement, stronger public awareness campaigns, and proactive government prioritization—modern slavery will become "more complex, more hidden, and harder to combat."

The UK findings echo broader concerns about AI-enabled crime. Recent research has documented how chatbot systems can facilitate planning for violent attacks and provide actionable guidance in dangerous scenarios.
