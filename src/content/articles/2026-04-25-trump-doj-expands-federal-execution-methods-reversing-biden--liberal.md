The Justice Department announced Friday it will permit the use of firing squads, electrocution, and gas asphyxiation for federal executions, marking a sharp reversal from the previous administration's approach to capital punishment.

The policy change expands beyond the lethal injection protocol used during Trump's first term and streamlines internal processes to "expedite death penalty cases," according to the department's statement. This comes after President Trump directed the DOJ to "vigorously pursue" the death penalty, framing it as protection against violent crime.

The move represents a dramatic shift from the Biden administration, which had paused federal executions. In one of his final acts in office, Biden commuted the sentences of 37 people on federal death row to life without parole, leaving only three federal inmates still facing execution.

The policy shift comes as American public opinion on capital punishment reaches historic lows. According to Gallup, only 52% of Americans support the death penalty as of late 2025—down from 80% in 1994—while a record-low share believe it is applied fairly. The United States remains the only country in the Americas that actively carries out executions, even as more than two-thirds of the world's nations have abolished the practice in law or practice.
