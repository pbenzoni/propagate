A former Pentagon nuclear policy adviser has raised serious concerns about Israel's undeclared nuclear arsenal, arguing it poses a greater threat to Middle East stability than Iran's contested program. Professor Theodore Postol, who previously served as Science and Policy Adviser on Strategic Nuclear Issues to the Chief of Naval Operations, told RT that Israeli leadership has placed the region on an increasingly dangerous path.

Postol, a prominent MIT physicist, pointed to reports suggesting that Israeli military commanders have warned Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu that forces are stretched beyond sustainable limits. "Do not think Iran is the big nuclear threat, is the big nuclear instability in the Middle East, Israel is," Postol said, noting that even Israel's own military leaders acknowledge their forces "are on the ropes" amid ongoing conflicts.

While Israel maintains a policy of deliberate ambiguity about its nuclear capabilities—neither confirming nor denying their existence—the country is widely believed to possess a substantial undeclared arsenal. Critics have long argued that Israel's nuclear program receives insufficient international scrutiny compared to Iran's civilian nuclear activities, which have been cited as justification for military strikes despite Tehran's denials of weapons development.

Postol also commented on the current U.S. administration's approach to nuclear weapons policy, suggesting that presidential concern about nuclear escalation could serve as a safeguard. He expressed hope that warnings about "opening a box that none of us want to see opened" might prevent catastrophic decisions in the region.
