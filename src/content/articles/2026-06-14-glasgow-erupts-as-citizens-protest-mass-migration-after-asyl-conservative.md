Frustrated citizens took to the streets of Glasgow, Scotland on Saturday to voice concerns about immigration policy following a brutal stabbing attack allegedly committed by a Sudanese asylum seeker. Around 70 protesters arrived to express opposition to mass migration, many wearing face coverings amid what they described as an increasingly hostile environment for expressing such views.

The demonstration came in response to Monday's stabbing incident in which a Sudanese asylum seeker was charged with attacking a man in the street, allegedly blinding him in one eye. The attack has reignited long-standing debates about immigration policy and public safety, with many questioning whether current asylum procedures adequately protect citizens.

Police Scotland worked to separate the immigration-concerned demonstrators from counter-protesters organized by the NGO Stand Up to Racism, which held its own rally calling to "reclaim the streets." Scuffles broke out as both groups pushed against police barriers. One man was arrested for threatening an officer, while another officer was struck by a thrown egg during the unrest. Police said they would investigate "a number of reported hate crimes."

The Glasgow incident follows another stabbing on Friday in Brierfield, northwest England, where a 17-year-old girl was attacked in the street, sustaining non-life-threatening injuries. Police identified the suspect as a British man of Pakistani descent. These incidents have fueled ongoing concerns about what critics call "two-tier" policing—the allegation that law enforcement applies different standards based on the backgrounds of offenders, failing to adequately address crimes committed by certain groups.
