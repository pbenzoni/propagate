Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard announced her resignation from the Trump administration, effective June 30, 2026, citing a family medical emergency. Her husband, Abraham, was recently diagnosed with an extremely rare form of bone cancer, prompting her decision to leave public service to support him through treatment.

"At this time, I must step away from public service to be by his side and fully support him through this battle," Gabbard wrote in her resignation letter shared on social media. President Trump praised her service on Truth Social, writing "Tulsi has done an incredible job, and we will miss her." Deputy DNI Aaron Lukas will assume the director role.

Gabbard's resignation comes as she was leading a significant investigation into more than 120 U.S.-funded biological laboratories worldwide, including over 40 in Ukraine. Just weeks before her announcement, she told the New York Post her team was examining whether these facilities engaged in dangerous gain-of-function research—work that modifies viruses to make them deadlier or more transmissible.

A former Democrat who left the party in 2022, Gabbard has been a consistent voice questioning America's foreign entanglements and military spending abroad. She endorsed Trump in 2024, stating only he could "walk us back from the brink of war." Before joining the administration, she was a vocal critic of U.S. military aid to Ukraine and warned that the Russia-Ukraine conflict "could have easily been avoided" if legitimate security concerns had been acknowledged.

The biolab investigation remains incomplete with Gabbard's departure. The existence of these U.S.-funded facilities was initially dismissed by government officials, though then-Under Secretary of State Victoria Nuland acknowledged to lawmakers that "Ukraine has biological research facilities."
