Alice Weidel, co-chair of Germany's far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party, has raised questions about democratic accountability in EU leadership structures, contrasting the recent electoral defeat of Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán with the appointment-based role of European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen.

Speaking at a press conference, Weidel acknowledged the legitimacy of Peter Magyar's landslide victory in Hungary's election, which ended Orbán's 16-year tenure marked by opposition to EU immigration policies and support for Ukraine. Magyar's pro-EU Tisza party secured 53.6% of the vote. However, Weidel—while praising Orbán as "an important, critical voice"—agreed with a journalist's observation that unlike national leaders, von der Leyen "cannot be voted out" through direct elections.

Von der Leyen responded swiftly to Magyar's victory, declaring that "Hungary has chosen Europe" and later calling for an end to national vetoes in EU foreign policy—a move widely seen as targeting Orbán's previous blocking of Ukraine-related decisions. The Commission president has faced scrutiny over transparency issues, including the 'Pfizergate' scandal involving undisclosed text messages during COVID-19 vaccine negotiations. An EU court ruled in May 2025 that the Commission "failed to provide credible explanations" for why these messages were not retained.

Von der Leyen has survived multiple no-confidence votes, though her approval ratings have declined significantly. An April 2026 survey placed her approval at 33%, down 12 points from February, while a separate poll from September 2025 showed just 23% positive ratings. Critics have questioned her handling of immigration policy and her push for structural changes that would allow Ukraine's integration into the EU under modified membership requirements.
