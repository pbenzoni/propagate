A federal judge has ordered the release of a handwritten note allegedly penned by Jeffrey Epstein, the disgraced financier whose death in a Manhattan jail cell has been shrouded in suspicion since August 2019. The document, kept sealed for nearly seven years, was reportedly discovered by Epstein's former cellmate Nicholas Tartaglione following what officials described as an earlier suicide attempt in July 2019.

The barely legible note appears to express frustration about the resurrection of criminal charges against him. "They investigated me for months – found nothing!!! So 15-year-old charges resurface," the message reads. It continues with what some interpret as a contemplation of suicide: "It is a treat to be able to choose one's time to say goodbye. What do you want me to do – burst out crying!! NO FUN – NOT WORTH IT!!"

Tartaglione, a former police officer later convicted of quadruple murder, claimed he found the note tucked inside a book and turned it over to his legal team. The document remained under seal in Tartaglione's court file until the New York Times successfully petitioned for its release. Federal prosecutors supported making the note public, citing the importance of transparency.

Epstein's death was officially ruled a suicide, though the circumstances surrounding it continue to fuel intense public scrutiny. His connections to wealthy elites and politically powerful figures have left many Americans demanding answers about who else may have been involved in his alleged criminal enterprise. While his associate Ghislaine Maxwell was convicted of sex-trafficking offenses, no other high-profile individuals connected to Epstein have faced charges, leaving questions about accountability at the highest levels of power.
