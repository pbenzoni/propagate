As Hungary heads to the polls Sunday, Prime Minister Viktor Orban's government is accusing Facebook of election interference—a charge the tech giant flatly denies. Government spokesman Zoltan Kovacs told Politico that Facebook's algorithm is "working against the government parties," claiming Orban's official page faces stricter limits while opposition leader Peter Magyar's profile enjoys greater reach.

Meta has rejected the allegations entirely, stating "there are no restrictions on the prime minister's accounts, nor have any posts been removed." Magyar's team attributes their social media success to understanding how platforms work and staying responsive to breaking news—a stark contrast to the government's more rigid messaging approach.

The dispute comes amid broader concerns about democratic backsliding in Hungary under Orban's increasingly authoritarian rule. The prime minister has clashed repeatedly with the European Union over rule-of-law issues, press freedom, and LGBTQ+ rights. Foreign Minister Peter Szijjarto has even accused EU intelligence services of wiretapping his phone, while Orban has blocked aid to Ukraine and maintained close ties to Russia—positions that have isolated Hungary within the Western alliance.

In a controversial move, U.S. Vice President J.D. Vance visited Budapest Tuesday to express support for Orban, accusing EU officials of "foreign election interference" against the Hungarian leader. The visit underscored deepening concerns among democracy advocates about the current U.S. administration's embrace of authoritarian-leaning governments, even as they claim to defend national sovereignty against outside influence.
