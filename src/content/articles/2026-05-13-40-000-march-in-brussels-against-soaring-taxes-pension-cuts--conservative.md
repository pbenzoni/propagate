Over 40,000 Belgians flooded the streets of Brussels this week to protest what they're calling anti-worker fiscal reforms, an ongoing energy crisis, and skyrocketing military spending that's draining resources from schools and healthcare. The massive demonstration highlights growing frustration with government priorities that seem to put international commitments ahead of citizens' basic needs.

The protests target recent reforms that include stricter pension rules, reduced retirement benefits, and changes to employment protections—measures that demonstrators say unfairly burden working families. Meanwhile, Belgium has been ramping up defense spending to meet NATO's 2% of GDP target, a move driven by pressure from the US-led military alliance. Protesters carried signs reading "€3.6 billion for aircraft, €86.7 million less for schools in 2026—this is not the kind of society we want" and "There goes our budget, healthcare flying away."

Demonstrators threw shoes at cardboard cutouts of government officials and held banners declaring "They are also stealing your pensions" and "Public education is in danger."
