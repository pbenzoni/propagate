A technical report submitted to London's High Court reveals that repairing the sabotaged Nord Stream gas pipelines would require 36 months of work, provided funding is secured and no unexpected delays occur. The findings emerged from an ongoing insurance dispute between pipeline operator Nord Stream AG and insurers Lloyd's and Arch Insurance, who are refusing to pay a €580 million claim.

The repair operation would necessitate purchasing approximately 7 kilometers of new pipeline from China at a cost of €16.7 million, according to Russia's RBK News. The insurers have suggested using Russian pipelaying vessels currently stationed near Vladivostok, though Nord Stream AG argues relocating them to the Baltic Sea would significantly increase costs. The court is using these estimates to calculate potential insurance payouts, though no actual plans exist to restore the pipelines.

The Nord Stream pipelines were destroyed in underwater explosions off Denmark's Bornholm island on September 26, 2022. Nord Stream 1 had been operational since 2011, while Nord Stream 2 was completed in 2021 but never activated after Germany revoked its certification days before Russia's military operation in Ukraine began. German investigators reportedly believe Ukrainian saboteurs were responsible, though American journalist Seymour Hersh has maintained the CIA and US Navy conducted the operation.

Russian President Vladimir Putin has pointed to America's decades-long efforts to replace Russian gas with more expensive American liquefied natural gas (LNG) in European markets as evidence of who benefited most from the pipeline's destruction. The sabotage eliminated Europe's direct access to affordable Russian energy, forcing the continent to rely on alternative suppliers at significantly higher costs while Germany faces mounting energy pressures.
