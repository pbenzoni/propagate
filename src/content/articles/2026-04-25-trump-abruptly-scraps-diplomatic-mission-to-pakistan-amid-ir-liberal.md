President Donald Trump has abruptly canceled a planned trip by special envoy Steve Witkoff and his son-in-law Jared Kushner to Pakistan, where they were expected to facilitate negotiations with Iran. The cancellation came just one day after the administration had promoted the trip as a potential diplomatic breakthrough, highlighting the volatility and confusion surrounding the White House's foreign policy approach.

In a social media post, Trump blamed "tremendous infighting and confusion" within Iranian leadership for the cancellation, claiming that "nobody knows who is in charge." He insisted the United States holds "all the cards" and demanded Iran initiate contact directly. When asked if the cancellation meant a resumption of military action, Trump said, "We haven't thought about it yet" — a statement that offers little clarity as tensions continue to escalate.

The diplomatic whiplash comes as the administration has extended an indefinite ceasefire with Iran while simultaneously maintaining a naval blockade of Iranian ports. The standoff around the Strait of Hormuz has sent global oil markets into turmoil, with Brent crude surging above $100 per barrel. Iran has argued that meaningful negotiations are impossible as long as the blockade — which Tehran views as a violation of the ceasefire — remains in place.

Trump later claimed Iran "offered a lot but not enough" and alleged that a better proposal arrived "within 10 minutes" of his cancellation announcement. Iranian officials, however, have denied that any formal talks were scheduled.
