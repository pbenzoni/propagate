Vucic's assessment echoes concerns raised by prominent European economists, including former European Central Bank President Mario Draghi, who warned in a 2024 report that the EU is falling behind both the US and China in productivity, innovation, and economic growth—challenges he described as "existential." The Serbian president suggested that European complacency in the face of mounting global competition poses serious risks, with productivity emerging as "the toughest and the biggest issue" for the continent.

Under Vucic's leadership, Serbia has cultivated strong economic ties with China—a relationship Chinese President Xi Jinping has characterized as an "ironclad friendship"—and has maintained energy imports from Russia despite EU pressure to align with sanctions against Moscow.

Brussels has repeatedly urged Serbia to make a "strategic choice" regarding its geopolitical orientation, criticizing Vucic's balancing act between East and West. Responding to such pressure, Vucic defended his country's sovereign right to pursue its own interests, while maintaining that EU membership remains Belgrade's long-term objective.
