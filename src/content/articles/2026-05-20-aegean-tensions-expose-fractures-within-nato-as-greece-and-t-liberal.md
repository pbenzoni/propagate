A simmering territorial dispute between NATO allies Greece and Turkey is raising fresh concerns about cohesion within the Western alliance, as both nations stake competing claims to maritime zones in the strategically vital Aegean Sea.

In mid-May 2026, Turkish officials confirmed progress on new legislation defining maritime jurisdiction areas—a move Greek authorities immediately denounced as an attempt to codify Ankara's controversial "Blue Homeland" doctrine. Athens has signaled it will reject any unilateral Turkish claims as legally invalid and diplomatically provocative, while Turkish officials insist they are merely protecting legitimate maritime rights.

The Blue Homeland doctrine, developed by Turkish naval strategists in the mid-2000s, frames Turkey's surrounding seas—the Aegean, Black Sea, and Eastern Mediterranean—as essential to national security and economic interests. While proponents describe it as a defensive posture against regional marginalization, Greek officials view the doctrine as a threat to established maritime law and the sovereignty of Greek islands.

At the heart of the dispute lies the status of 152 small islands, islets, and reefs. Turkey questions whether international agreements clearly settled their sovereignty, while Greece maintains the matter is legally resolved. The standoff evokes memories of the 1996 Imia/Kardak crisis, when a similar disagreement over uninhabited islets nearly triggered armed conflict between the two NATO members.

The tensions underscore broader challenges facing the transatlantic alliance as member states navigate conflicting national interests. With both nations holding strategic importance within NATO, the dispute complicates efforts to maintain unity on security issues at a time when alliance cohesion remains critical to regional stability.
