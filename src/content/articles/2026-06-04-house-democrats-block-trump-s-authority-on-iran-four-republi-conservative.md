The House of Representatives passed a resolution Wednesday that would force President Donald Trump to seek congressional approval before taking further military action against Iran, despite warnings from Republican leadership that the measure could sabotage delicate peace negotiations.

The resolution passed 215-208, with all Democrats voting in favor alongside four Republicans: Thomas Massie of Kentucky, Brian Fitzpatrick of Pennsylvania, Tom Barrett of Michigan, and Warren Davidson of Ohio. The measure invokes the War Powers Resolution of 1973, which requires presidents to withdraw forces after 60 days without congressional authorization.

House Speaker Mike Johnson strongly opposed the resolution, warning colleagues it could have a "very negative" impact on talks with Iran. "It weakens us, our position, and our leverage in negotiation on the peace in that situation," Johnson told CNN, noting that Operation Epic Fury has already concluded. The Trump administration launched the operation alongside Israel in late February without consulting Congress, though Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth argued that an April ceasefire announcement reset the 60-day clock.

Representative Tom Barrett defended his decision to vote with Democrats, citing constituent concerns about economic impacts. "The American people are tired of this war… they're tired of $5 gallon gas and $6 gallon diesel, and fertilizer we can't afford to put on our fields," Barrett said. The resolution still faces an uphill battle in the Republican-controlled Senate, where passage appears unlikely.

Tensions with Iran remain high, with Washington and Tehran exchanging fire this week even as negotiations continue. The U.S. Central Command announced "self-defense strikes" on Iran's Qeshm Island Wednesday, while Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps reported targeting U.S.-linked assets in the Gulf in response.
