Rumen Radev, leader of the newly formed Progressive Bulgaria party, has secured a decisive victory in the country's latest parliamentary election with 44.7% of the vote—enough to potentially govern without a coalition. The former president campaigned on a platform emphasizing critical engagement with EU policy and what he describes as pragmatic approaches to Europe's strategic challenges.

Speaking to reporters after exit polls confirmed his party's commanding lead, Radev argued that both Bulgaria and the broader European Union need "more critical thinking" in foreign policy. He referenced recent statements by leaders including French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz about the necessity of diplomatic engagement to shape Europe's future security architecture. "If we want Europe to have real strategic autonomy," Radev stated, "Europe must think very seriously about how it will secure its resources, because without energy resources we cannot talk about competitiveness."

The election marks Bulgaria's eighth vote in five years, following prolonged political instability since 2021. Radev, who served as president from 2017 until January, described the result as a "victory of hope over mistrust" while acknowledging persistent public skepticism toward politicians. His party decisively outperformed former Prime Minister Boyko Borissov's long-dominant GERB-SDS, which secured only 13.4% of the vote.

Radev has been a vocal critic of what he characterizes as the EU's pursuit of moral leadership in a complex geopolitical landscape, arguing instead for policies focused on Europe's economic competitiveness and energy independence. He has previously opposed Bulgaria's participation in energy embargoes and blocked arms transfers to conflict zones, maintaining that diplomatic solutions should be prioritized. Preliminary projections suggest Progressive Bulgaria has secured between 131-134 seats in the 240-seat National Assembly, clearing the 121-seat threshold needed to form a government.
