In a striking departure from traditional conservative orthodoxy, Tucker Carlson told Israeli Channel 13 that the United States should immediately halt all aid and special agreements with Israel. The former Fox News host criticized what he described as Israel's treatment of Palestinians and argued that America's association with Israeli policies has severely damaged U.S. credibility worldwide.

"I think we should stop all aid to Israel, all special deals for Israel. Tomorrow," Carlson said in the interview aired Tuesday. He added that the U.S. has no obligation to "lend its moral authority" to Israel and expressed concern that the relationship "is hurting the US very badly."

Carlson specifically faulted President Trump for what he characterized as capitulating to pressure from Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, leading to U.S. involvement in a war with Iran. The commentator, once a staunch Trump ally during the re-election campaign, has increasingly voiced foreign policy critiques of the administration.

The remarks represent a notable shift in the conservative media landscape, where questioning the U.S.-Israel relationship has historically been taboo. Carlson joins a growing faction of right-wing figures, including former Congresswoman Marjorie Taylor Greene and commentator Candace Owens, who have expressed reservations about the administration's Middle East policies and their impact on American interests and values.
