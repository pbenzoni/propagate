Iran may be considering backing down from its closure of the Strait of Hormuz, according to a Wednesday Reuters report citing sources briefed on Tehran's position. The regime has reportedly floated a proposal that would allow ships to pass freely through the Omani side of the vital waterway—but only if the United States offers concessions in return.

The potential shift comes after President Trump declared a U.S. blockade of the strait following failed peace negotiations. The move represents a firm response to Iran's initial closure of the route to "enemy ships" after U.S. and Israeli military operations against Iranian targets on February 28. U.S. Central Command reported that ten vessels had been turned around since the American blockade began Monday, demonstrating the effectiveness of the administration's pressure campaign.

The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly 20 percent of global oil and liquefied natural gas flows, making it one of the world's most strategically important chokepoints. Iran's closure has already caused energy price spikes and prompted warnings from the International Monetary Fund about rising global inflation. The regime's apparent willingness to negotiate, even partially, suggests Trump's maximum pressure approach may be yielding results.

Reuters noted that the source did not clarify whether Iran would permit all vessels—including those connected to Israel—to pass through the Omani side. A Western source confirmed the proposal has been under consideration but provided no details on any U.S. response. The situation remains fluid as both sides weigh their options in this high-stakes standoff over one of the world's most critical shipping lanes.
