Congressional Democrats are attempting to strip President Trump of military authority over Iran operations, even as a ceasefire that began in early April continues to hold. The push comes after the 60-day deadline under the 1973 War Powers Resolution expired on May 1, triggering a legal dispute over presidential war-making powers.

In a letter to congressional leaders Friday, President Trump argued that hostilities with Iran have been "terminated," noting that "since April 7, 2026 not a single exchange of fire has been recorded between US forces and Iran." Secretary of War Pete Hegseth defended the administration's position during Senate testimony, arguing that the ceasefire effectively pauses the 60-day clock mandated by the War Powers Resolution. House Speaker Mike Johnson backed this interpretation, stating "we are not at war" and describing current operations as focused on securing freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz.

Democratic Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer dismissed Trump's legal argument as "nonsense," calling it an "illegal war" and accusing Republicans of complicity. Democratic-led resolutions to curb presidential war powers have repeatedly failed in Congress, though some Republican lawmakers including Senator Todd Young have begun questioning the administration's legal reasoning. The debate centers on whether the executive branch can continue military operations indefinitely by declaring temporary pauses in hostilities.

The constitutional dispute unfolds against a backdrop of declining public support for the conflict, with recent polling showing 61% of Americans believe entering the Iran war was a mistake. Iran proposed a 14-point peace plan last week seeking permanent resolution rather than temporary ceasefires, which Trump received skeptically. Meanwhile, naval operations continue with both US enforcement of a blockade on Iranian ports and Iranian restrictions on traffic through the strategic Strait of Hormuz, which remains effectively closed to normal commerce.
