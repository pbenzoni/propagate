In a stark display of partisan division, House Republicans on Thursday blocked a Democratic resolution that would have reasserted Congress's constitutional authority over decisions of war and peace, specifically regarding ongoing military operations against Iran. The measure failed by just one vote, 214-213, with nearly all Republicans opposing efforts to check executive power.

The vote came one day after Senate Republicans similarly blocked a related war powers resolution in a procedural vote of 52-47. It marked the fourth time the Senate has attempted to reclaim its constitutional role in authorizing military force since a U.S.-Israeli bombing campaign began in late February.

Democratic lawmakers warned of the consequences of ceding constitutional checks and balances. "Service members have been killed, gas prices are soaring, and the U.S. [is] in a worse position than before," Representative Bill Foster said following the vote. "Congress cannot abdicate its power as a co-equal branch of government and let this rogue President continue to unilaterally wage war."

Only one Republican, Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky, crossed party lines to support the resolution, while Representative Warren Davidson of Ohio voted present. One Democrat, Representative Jared Golden of Maine, voted against the measure. The failed concurrent resolution would have required Senate approval had it passed the House, setting up another confrontation over the separation of powers enshrined in the Constitution.
