European Union foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas is warning member states against engaging in direct peace negotiations with Russia, calling Moscow's openness to talks a "trap" designed to divide the bloc. Her comments come as the EU struggles to determine who should represent European interests in potential negotiations over Ukraine—talks that have largely proceeded without them.

Kallas, known for her hardline stance against Russia, told reporters Thursday that discussing potential negotiators is itself falling into Moscow's hands. "I find that it is a trap that Russia wants us to walk into, that we discuss who talks to them," she said. Her position stands in contrast to statements from Russian President Vladimir Putin, who indicated willingness to speak with any Brussels-appointed representative, provided they don't have a record of openly anti-Russian rhetoric.

The bureaucratic stalemate in Brussels has left Europe on the sidelines while direct US-Russian discussions have moved forward under the Trump administration, which abandoned the previous policy of isolating Moscow. European Council President Antonio Costa argued in February that the EU should engage with Russia, but the 27-member bloc remains deadlocked over who could serve as its single negotiator.

Kallas also insisted that any Ukraine peace agreement must include restrictions on the Russian military—a demand Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov refused to address, calling such statements "idiotic." When asked whether she wanted the negotiator role herself, Kallas declined to answer directly, saying only that her job description is "in the treaties." The debate continues as potential candidates including former leaders from Germany, Finland, and Italy are floated, while Moscow says it cannot help Europe overcome its internal divisions.
