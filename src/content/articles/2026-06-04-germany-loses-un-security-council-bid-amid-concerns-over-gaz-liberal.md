For the first time in its modern history, Germany failed to win a temporary seat on the UN Security Council, losing to Portugal and Austria in Wednesday's vote. The outcome represents a stunning reversal for a nation that had won every Security Council election it entered since 1977, typically with strong support from European and NATO allies.

Germany secured only 104 votes compared to Portugal's 134 and Austria's 131, prompting immediate criticism of Berlin's foreign policy approach. Human rights advocates and political observers suggested the defeat reflected global unease with Germany's divergent stances on international conflicts—particularly its support for Israeli military actions in Gaza while simultaneously championing strict adherence to international law regarding Ukraine.

"When it comes to Russia, international law counts, but when it comes to the USA and Israel, it doesn't," left-wing politician Sahra Wagenknecht told the Berliner Zeitung. "This double standard was voted out by the international community yesterday." Human rights lawyer Craig Mokhiber similarly noted that "Germany's scandalous support for genocide in Palestine and aggression against Iran" were factors in the vote.

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul rejected the criticism, attributing the loss to Russia's opposition to Germany's support for Ukraine. He maintained that Germany "must always assume a special responsibility for Israel," defending Berlin's policy positions despite the diplomatic setback. The defeat raises questions about Germany's long-standing ambition to secure a permanent seat on the Security Council.
