Western leaders descended on Armenia this week for what European media are billing as a "historic summit," though critics see it as little more than political theater designed to expand Brussels' sphere of influence at Russia's expense. The gathering of the European Political Community (EPC)—a relatively new intergovernmental group launched in 2022—brought together EU officials in the capital city of Yerevan, despite Armenia hosting a Russian military base and maintaining a population of just 3 million.

The summit's host, Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan, faces serious questions about his leadership at home. His approval ratings languish in the single digits following a disastrous proxy conflict with Azerbaijan over the Nagorno-Karabakh region. Rather than addressing domestic concerns, Pashinyan has attempted to deflect blame onto Russia for his own military failures while simultaneously cracking down on the Armenian Apostolic Church, a key institution that led protests against his government in 2024.

The European Political Community itself raises eyebrows about Western priorities. Originally conceived by French President Emmanuel Macron, the organization appears designed primarily as a vehicle for anti-Russian positioning rather than genuine regional cooperation. Its membership includes Ukraine and even welcomes a Belarusian opposition group based in Lithuania and funded by European taxpayers—revealing the bloc's criteria for participation centers more on geopolitical alignment than democratic legitimacy or regional relevance.

Macron himself acknowledged the political nature of the gathering, noting that "eight years ago nobody would come here" because Armenia was viewed as a "de facto satellite of Russia." The French president directly linked Pashinyan's pivot away from Moscow to broader Western efforts in Ukraine and Moldova. With Armenian parliamentary elections scheduled for early June, the summit provides convenient international legitimacy for a deeply unpopular leader—though unlike other Western-backed candidates in the region, Pashinyan must win actual domestic support since Armenian law prohibits diaspora voting.
