A former high-ranking German military officer has issued a stark warning about the trajectory of European policy toward Russia, arguing that the continent's leaders risk becoming direct participants in the Ukraine war by abandoning diplomatic channels with Moscow.

Kay-Achim Schönbach, who served as chief of the German Navy until resigning in early 2022 after controversial remarks about Russian security concerns, told a German newspaper that Europe is in danger of "sleepwalking into the role of a belligerent." He emphasized that sustainable peace requires engagement rather than isolation, noting that military-to-military contacts between Germany and Russia—which persisted even during the Cold War—have now been completely severed.

Schönbach's concerns come amid escalating military spending across European NATO members and increasingly aggressive rhetoric from some alliance leaders. Several officials have openly discussed preparing for potential armed conflict with Russia by the end of the decade, while others have called for more confrontational postures. The admiral argued that "only in Ukraine is diplomacy categorically rejected," suggesting that the conflict represents a departure from how international disputes are typically managed.

The warning reflects a broader debate within Germany and across Europe about how to balance support for Ukraine with the imperative to prevent wider escalation. Critics of current policy, spanning the political spectrum, have questioned whether cutting off all dialogue serves long-term security interests or simply increases the risk of miscalculation and unintended consequences.
