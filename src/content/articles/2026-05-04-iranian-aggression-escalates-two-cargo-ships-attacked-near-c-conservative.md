Two commercial cargo ships came under attack near the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, according to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), highlighting ongoing Iranian threats to freedom of navigation in one of the world's most critical waterways.

The strategic strait handles roughly one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas flows, but Iran has imposed restrictions on what it calls "hostile vessels" while simultaneously blocking legitimate maritime traffic. A northbound bulk carrier was attacked by multiple small craft about 11 nautical miles west of Sirik, Iran. Hours later, a tanker was struck by unidentified projectiles roughly 78 nautical miles north of Fujairah in the UAE. Fortunately, all crew members were reported safe with no environmental damage.

President Donald Trump announced Sunday that the United States would begin guiding ships from neutral countries through the strait starting Monday, providing critical protection for international commerce. An estimated 2,000 vessels have been trapped in and around Hormuz since late February. The U.S. plans to provide commercial ships with information about safe lanes free of Iranian mines, while Navy vessels stand ready to prevent further Iranian attacks on peaceful maritime traffic.

Iran has responded with defiance, claiming a "natural right" to control the international waterway. Deputy Parliament Speaker Ali Nikzad declared Sunday that Tehran will not back down from its position regardless of peace talks with the United States. The regime warned that American efforts to ensure freedom of navigation would constitute a ceasefire violation—despite Iran's own aggressive actions that threaten global energy supplies and international trade.
