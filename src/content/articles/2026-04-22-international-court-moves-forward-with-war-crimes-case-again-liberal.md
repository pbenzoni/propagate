The International Criminal Court has rejected an appeal by former Philippines President Rodrigo Duterte to secure his release, ruling that the tribunal retains jurisdiction over alleged crimes against humanity committed during his administration's controversial anti-drug campaign. The 81-year-old remains in custody at The Hague.

Duterte was taken into custody at Manila airport in March 2025 and transferred to the ICC's detention facility in the Netherlands. He faces charges related to a brutal crackdown on drug-related activity that resulted in thousands of deaths. While official police records document approximately 7,000 fatalities, independent human rights organizations estimate the actual death toll is significantly higher.

Duterte's legal team argued that the court lost authority over the case when the Philippines formally withdrew from the Rome Statute in 2018. However, the ICC's appeals chamber determined that because the alleged crimes occurred while the country was still a member state, and because a preliminary examination had already commenced before the withdrawal became effective, the court maintains legitimate jurisdiction. The ruling paves the way for a potential full trial.

The case represents a significant moment for international accountability. If charges are confirmed by the pre-trial chamber—a decision expected by the end of April—Duterte would become the first former Asian head of state to stand trial at The Hague. The proceedings underscore ongoing debates about the reach of international human rights law and the responsibility of national leaders for systematic violence against civilian populations.
