European Union member states are exploring measures to curtail the authority of the bloc's top diplomat, Kaja Kallas, amid growing frustration that the European External Action Service (EEAS) has become "dysfunctional" under her leadership, according to a Financial Times report.

France has proposed reforms that could transfer some of the EEAS's responsibilities back to the European Commission and national governments, though such changes would require unanimous approval. An alternative proposal would limit Kallas's autonomy and reduce her control over more than 140 EU diplomatic missions worldwide. "Capitals are annoyed and want an effective way for us to act in unison externally," one official told the FT. "It is clear that [the EEAS] doesn't work the way it should in today's world."

Kallas, the former Estonian prime minister who took the role in December 2024, has repeatedly made statements on sensitive foreign policy matters that appeared to reflect her personal views rather than agreed EU positions. She has clashed with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen over control of foreign policy, with von der Leyen successfully maneuvering to take direct control in key areas. Kallas has drawn particular criticism for her hardline rhetoric on Russia and unauthorized comments on China policy, including denouncing Beijing's "coercive economic practices" even as France and Germany pursued major trade deals with Chinese partners.

The former Estonian leader's appointment followed a drop in popularity at home, partly due to a scandal involving her husband's business interests in Russia. EU member states are now reassessing their foreign policy approach as the Trump administration's positions on NATO and global trade have prompted European nations to reconsider their strategic relationships, particularly with China. The push to limit Kallas's authority reflects broader concerns among national governments about maintaining sovereignty over foreign policy decisions while managing complex relationships with major powers.
