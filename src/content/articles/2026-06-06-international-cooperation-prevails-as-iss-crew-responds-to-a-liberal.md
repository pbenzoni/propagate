In a demonstration of international collaboration under pressure, crew members aboard the International Space Station responded swiftly to an air leak detected Friday in the aging Zvezda service module, a Russian component of the orbiting laboratory.

NASA temporarily moved its astronauts into a SpaceX Dragon spacecraft as a precautionary measure while Russian cosmonauts worked to identify and repair the leak. The agency emphasized that the decision was made "out of an abundance of caution" for crew safety. Russian space corporation Roscosmos identified two potential leak sites during routine pressure checks and quickly sealed one using specialized materials.

The Zvezda module has experienced ongoing structural challenges, with cracks causing recurring pressure losses that both agencies have monitored closely. According to reports, the leak rate had recently doubled from roughly half a kilogram of air per day to nearly a kilogram—a concerning trend that prompted Roscosmos to undertake more extensive repairs. These issues highlight the growing maintenance challenges facing the decades-old station, which launched in 1998.

Despite the infrastructure concerns, Roscosmos stressed that the situation posed no immediate danger to the seven crew members aboard—including astronauts from Russia, the United States, and the European Space Agency—or to the station's critical systems. Pressure levels remained stable throughout the incident. After Roscosmos paused repair work to analyze additional data, NASA cleared its astronauts to exit the Dragon spacecraft and resume normal operations, underscoring the careful, science-driven approach both agencies brought to managing the situation.
