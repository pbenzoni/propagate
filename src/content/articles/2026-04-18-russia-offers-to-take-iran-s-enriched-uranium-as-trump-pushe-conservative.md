Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom has reiterated its willingness to help remove Iran's enriched uranium stockpile as negotiations between Washington and Tehran remain at an impasse. Rosatom CEO Aleksey Likhachev told the company's newspaper that Russia has proven experience with Iran and stands ready to handle the sensitive transfer.

President Trump has taken a firm stance, demanding that Iran dismantle its nuclear infrastructure entirely, hand over its uranium stockpile, accept a 20-year enrichment ban, and shut down key facilities including Natanz and Fordow. Iran currently holds over 400 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60% purity—approaching weapons-grade levels—according to the International Atomic Energy Agency.

Tehran has categorically rejected Trump's terms as excessive. Iranian officials have called their enriched uranium "as sacred as Iranian soil" and ruled out shipping any material to the United States. Instead, Iran has offered to downblend its uranium under international oversight and accept only a limited five-year freeze, while demanding sanctions relief and guarantees that Washington won't withdraw from any future agreement.

Russia has positioned itself as a potential intermediary, noting it successfully removed enriched uranium from Iran in 2015 and operates Iran's Bushehr nuclear power plant. Moscow argues it has the technical capability and trust necessary to handle the material. However, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov indicated earlier this week that the U.S. has rejected Russia's offer to host the stockpile, leaving the negotiations deadlocked over Tehran's nuclear program.
