Swiss voters on Sunday firmly rejected a far-right proposal to cap the country's population at 10 million people, with 55% opposing the measure that critics warned would have damaged the economy and isolated Switzerland from its European neighbors.

The referendum, pushed by the right-wing Swiss People's Party (SVP), would have forced the government to guarantee that the population—currently about 9.1 million—never exceed the threshold. Approaching that limit would have required scrapping Switzerland's free-movement agreement with the European Union, a pact that has been in place since 2002 and allows Swiss businesses crucial access to skilled workers.

Before the vote, major employers including Nestlé, Roche, and UBS warned that imposing a fixed population cap—an unprecedented move for a modern economy—would restrict access to foreign labor and harm investment. The government and a majority of lawmakers also opposed the measure, arguing it would undermine Switzerland's economic competitiveness.

The SVP framed its campaign around fears of "mass immigration," blaming newcomers for housing shortages and crowded infrastructure. However, the proposal's defeat suggests Swiss voters prioritized economic stability and international cooperation over nationalist rhetoric. Foreigners currently make up about 27% of Switzerland's population, many filling essential roles in the country's highly skilled workforce.
