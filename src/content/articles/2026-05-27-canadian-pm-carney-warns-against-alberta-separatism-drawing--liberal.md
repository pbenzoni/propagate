Prime Minister Mark Carney has issued a stern warning against growing separatist sentiment in Alberta, comparing the western province's push for independence to the United Kingdom's disastrous Brexit vote. The oil-rich province plans to hold a public vote in October on whether to pursue formal separation from Canada, following a petition drive that gathered over 300,000 signatures.

Carney, who witnessed Brexit's aftermath firsthand as governor of the Bank of England, called the separatist push a "very dangerous bluff." Speaking to reporters Monday, he emphasized that Canada is "absolutely stronger together" and cautioned Albertans against repeating Britain's mistake. "I saw first-hand what happened in the United Kingdom when the view was, vote for this, it'll be soft, and then we'll negotiate," Carney said. "They're still 10 years later trying to undo what people didn't think they were voting for but what they ended up having."

The separatist movement in Alberta, driven by the pro-independence group Stay Free Alberta, stems from longstanding tensions over federal environmental regulations, taxation, and energy policy. While Alberta Premier Danielle Smith claims to support a "united Canada," she has defended the vote as giving Albertans a voice on their province's future. However, polling indicates that full independence remains a minority position.

Carney has attempted to ease tensions by backing new energy infrastructure projects, including a recent agreement with Smith's government for a potential Pacific coast pipeline. Yet this move has drawn criticism from British Columbia officials, Indigenous groups, and environmental advocates who accuse the Prime Minister of compromising climate commitments. The Alberta debate unfolds against a backdrop of trade tensions with the United States, where Donald Trump has imposed sweeping tariffs on Canadian goods while repeatedly referring to Canada as "the 51st state."
