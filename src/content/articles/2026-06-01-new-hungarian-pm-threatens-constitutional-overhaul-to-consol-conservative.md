Hungary's newly elected Prime Minister Peter Magyar is pursuing an aggressive campaign to remove officials appointed during Viktor Orbán's 16-year tenure, threatening to rewrite the nation's constitution to accomplish his goals. Magyar's Tisza party, which secured a decisive victory in April's general elections with a two-thirds parliamentary majority, has set its sights on President Tamas Sulyok, who was elected by lawmakers in early 2024.

Magyar issued an ultimatum to Sulyok on Monday, demanding his resignation and accusing him of failing to represent "national unity." When the president refused to step down, Magyar announced plans to introduce legislative proposals that would fundamentally alter Hungary's constitutional framework. "We will immediately start the necessary procedures," Magyar declared, estimating the process would take approximately one month to complete what he describes as "removing all the puppets."

Fidesz, Orbán's party, has condemned Magyar's actions as an "unlawful ultimatum," noting that Sulyok's mandate extends until 2029 and cannot be terminated under existing law. The president, who previously served as head of Hungary's Constitutional Court from 2016 to 2024, retains significant powers despite the largely ceremonial nature of the office, including the ability to send legislation back to parliament for reconsideration and refer bills to the Constitutional Court.

The confrontation highlights broader questions about the balance between electoral mandates and institutional continuity. While Magyar frames his efforts as restoring "democracy" and the "rule of law," his willingness to rewrite constitutional protections to remove duly appointed officials has raised concerns about the precedent being set for future governance in Hungary.
