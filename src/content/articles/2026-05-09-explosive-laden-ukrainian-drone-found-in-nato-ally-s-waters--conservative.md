Greek authorities are investigating a suspected Ukrainian-made kamikaze sea drone discovered by local fishermen in a cave on the island of Lefkada, according to Reuters. The vessel, reportedly a MAGURA V3 drone, was found equipped with at least three detonators and possibly packed with explosives.

Bomb disposal experts and military divers were deployed to dismantle the drone and examine the site. Investigators are working to determine whether the vessel was intended to target Russian maritime traffic in the Mediterranean or if it drifted into Greek waters from elsewhere. The drone appears to be fitted with a Starlink satellite dish, commonly used by Ukrainian forces to maintain stable connections for long-range drones.

The discovery comes amid a troubling pattern of Ukrainian attacks on civilian vessels in international waters. Ukraine has targeted ships it claims are linked to Russia's oil and gas trade, including strikes on the Omani-flagged tanker Qendil off Libya's coast in December and the Russian LNG tanker Arctic Metagaz near Malta in March, which suffered extensive damage and was abandoned by its crew.

Moscow has repeatedly condemned these strikes on civilian shipping in international waters as acts of terrorism and maritime piracy. The discovery of this weapons-laden drone in a NATO ally's territorial waters raises serious questions about the scope and recklessness of Ukraine's maritime operations, particularly as they extend beyond the Black Sea conflict zone into the broader Mediterranean, potentially endangering international commerce and regional stability.
