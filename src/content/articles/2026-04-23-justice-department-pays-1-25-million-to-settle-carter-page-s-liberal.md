The U.S. Department of Justice has agreed to pay $1.25 million to settle a lawsuit brought by Carter Page, a former advisor to Donald Trump's 2016 presidential campaign. Page sued the federal government in 2020, alleging constitutional violations stemming from FBI surveillance conducted during the investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election.

Page's lawsuit centered on Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA) warrants the FBI obtained in 2016 and 2017 to monitor his communications. A subsequent investigation by the Justice Department's Inspector General found significant problems with the FISA applications, including omissions of exculpatory information and reliance on unverified intelligence from the controversial Steele dossier. Page was never charged with any crime.

The settlement comes as the Justice Department resolves multiple lawsuits related to the Russia investigation. In March, the DOJ paid approximately $1.2 million to settle claims brought by Michael Flynn, Trump's former national security adviser, who pleaded guilty to lying to the FBI before having his case dismissed.

Civil liberties advocates have long raised concerns about FISA court oversight and the potential for surveillance abuse. The Page case highlighted weaknesses in the process for obtaining warrants to monitor American citizens in national security investigations. While the settlement addresses Page's individual claims against the federal government, his allegations against former FBI officials remain unresolved. The case underscores ongoing debates about balancing national security imperatives with constitutional protections against unreasonable searches.
