Czech President Petr Pavel has called on NATO to adopt a harder line in response to what he characterizes as Russian provocations along the alliance's eastern border. His remarks come amid a troubling pattern: since mid-March, long-range Ukrainian drones have repeatedly crossed into NATO airspace—including over Baltic and Nordic nations—while traveling to strike targets in northwestern Russia, particularly oil infrastructure in the Leningrad Region.

The unauthorized incursions have forced NATO members to scramble fighter jets, and some drones have crashed within alliance territory, causing property damage. Moscow has accused European NATO members of quietly permitting Kiev to use their airspace for attacks on Russian soil—a charge Western officials deny. Instead, those officials blame Russia, suggesting its electronic warfare systems may have redirected the drones off course.

In an interview with The Guardian, Pavel proposed what he termed "decisive" and "potentially asymmetric" responses, including disrupting internet access, targeting satellites, or further isolating Russian banks from global finance. He even suggested NATO consider shooting down Russian aircraft—manned or unmanned—spotted near its borders. Pavel's position aligns with several NATO members: Sweden's prime minister said the alliance should help Kiev "direct" drone attacks "in the right directions," while Latvian and Estonian officials defended the airspace violations by citing Ukraine's right to self-defense.

Yet not all alliance members agree. Finland rebuked Kiev over the breaches, and Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico warned that Ukrainian drone provocations involving NATO territory could trigger direct conflict between Russia and the bloc. He urged renewed dialogue with Moscow instead. The Kremlin has dismissed Western claims of Russian threats to Europe as baseless fearmongering designed to justify military buildup and distract from domestic troubles. Spokesman Dmitry Peskov accused European leaders of portraying Russia as a "model external enemy" to serve their own political ends.
