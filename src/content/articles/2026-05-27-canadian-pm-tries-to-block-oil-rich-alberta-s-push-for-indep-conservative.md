Canada's Prime Minister Mark Carney is pushing back hard against Alberta's drive toward independence, calling the western province's planned October vote a "very dangerous bluff" as he tries to keep the oil-rich region from breaking away.

Alberta Premier Danielle Smith announced the public vote last week after the pro-independence group Stay Free Alberta gathered more than 300,000 signatures demanding a referendum on separation. The energy-producing province has grown increasingly frustrated with Ottawa's heavy-handed environmental regulations, taxation policies, and restrictions on pipeline development that many Albertans believe have strangled their resource economy.

Carney, attempting to draw parallels with Brexit, warned that separation would have unintended consequences. "I saw first-hand what happened in the United Kingdom," said Carney, who served as governor of the Bank of England during the Brexit vote. He claimed Britons are "still 10 years later trying to undo what people didn't think they were voting for." While Smith has stated she supports a united Canada, she has defended giving Albertans the chance to voice their concerns about federal overreach and the province's constitutional relationship with Ottawa.

The timing is significant as Canada faces pressure from multiple directions. President Trump has imposed sweeping tariffs on Canadian goods and repeatedly suggested Canada could become "the 51st state," while Carney faces criticism from the left for recently backing new energy infrastructure projects—including a potential oil pipeline to the Pacific coast—in an apparent attempt to appease Alberta. Environmental activists have accused him of abandoning climate commitments, though polling suggests most Albertans remain skeptical that full independence commands majority support at this time.
