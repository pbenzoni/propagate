Diplomatic teams from Washington and Tehran are expected to arrive in Islamabad Tuesday evening for a critical second round of negotiations, as a fragile two-week ceasefire approaches its Wednesday expiration. Vice President J.D. Vance will lead the U.S. delegation, while Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf heads Tehran's team.

The talks come amid heightened tensions after President Trump threatened "overwhelming military force" and warned that "lots of bombs [will] start going off" if no agreement is reached. Trump has also imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports—a move Iranian officials call a violation of the ceasefire terms—and has indicated he will not extend the truce.

Iranian leaders have rejected what they describe as negotiations "under the shadow of threats," with President Masoud Pezeshkian calling U.S. conduct "non-constructive and contradictory." Despite the harsh rhetoric from both sides, mediators from Pakistan, Egypt, and Turkey have urged continued dialogue.

Back in Washington, dozens of military veterans and their families were arrested Monday after staging a sit-in at the Cannon House Office Building to protest U.S. military action against Iran. The demonstrators, organized by groups including Veterans For Peace and About Face, held red tulips to honor Iranian civilians killed in strikes and performed a flag-folding ceremony for the 13 U.S. service members who have died in the conflict. Iraq War veteran Mike Prysner, among those detained, urged current military personnel to refuse participation in what he called "a crisis for the Trump administration."
