The Justice Department unsealed an indictment Wednesday against former Cuban President Raúl Castro, now 96, charging him in connection with the 1996 downing of two aircraft operated by an anti-communist exile group. The move represents the latest escalation in the Trump administration's pressure campaign against the island nation, raising concerns among observers about potential military intervention.

The charges stem from a decades-old incident in which Cuban forces shot down planes operated by Brothers to the Rescue after what Cuba described as repeated airspace violations. A former pilot with the group, who defected to Cuba days before the incident, alleged the organization was involved in weapons smuggling to support anti-government activities on the island. Cuba's embassy noted the group had committed over 25 airspace violations despite written warnings, leaving Havana with "no choice but the direct defense of its borders."

Cuban President Miguel Díaz-Canel condemned the indictment as "a political maneuver, devoid of any legal foundation," warning it appears designed to justify "military aggression against Cuba." Legal experts note Castro is extremely unlikely to ever face trial, as Cuba has no extradition treaty with the United States. The timing has drawn scrutiny given the administration's recent actions against Venezuela, where President Nicolás Maduro was indicted and detained in a controversial operation.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, a longtime advocate of overthrowing Cuba's government, told NBC News that officials in Havana "should be concerned." The administration has since imposed energy restrictions on the island. Human rights advocates and Latin America scholars have expressed alarm that the indictment follows a familiar pattern of using legal pretexts to justify destabilizing interventions in the region.
