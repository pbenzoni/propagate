President Donald Trump announced Thursday that the United States will deploy an additional 5,000 troops to Poland, reinforcing America's commitment to allies who share conservative values and meet their defense obligations.

The deployment follows Poland's recent election of conservative President Karol Nawrocki, whom Trump endorsed. "Based on the successful Election of the now President of Poland, Karol Nawrocki, who I was proud to Endorse, and our relationship with him, I am pleased to announce that the United States will be sending an additional 5,000 Troops to Poland," Trump wrote on Truth Social.

The move comes after confusion last week when the Pentagon paused a scheduled rotation of 4,000 troops into Poland. Vice President J.D. Vance clarified the situation was a delay rather than a reduction, criticizing media overreaction to the temporary pause.

Trump's decision also follows his withdrawal of 5,000 troops from Germany amid growing tensions with Berlin. The President has long criticized European NATO members for failing to meet their defense spending commitments while relying on American protection. Poland, which currently hosts about 10,000 US troops and has been called a "model ally" by Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth, stands in stark contrast to Germany's approach.

The White House has not yet clarified whether these "additional troops" represent the delayed rotation, forces being pulled from Germany, or entirely new deployments. The United States currently maintains approximately 80,000 troops across Europe, with more than 38,000 stationed in Germany alone.
