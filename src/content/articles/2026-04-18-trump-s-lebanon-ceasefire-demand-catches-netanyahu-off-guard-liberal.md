Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his advisers were reportedly caught off guard when President Donald Trump announced he would not permit Israel to continue airstrikes in Lebanon, according to Axios, citing sources familiar with the matter.

The statement came as a ten-day ceasefire brokered by the Trump administration took effect between Israel and the Lebanese government. Netanyahu was reportedly alarmed by Trump's declaration that the U.S. had "prohibited" further strikes, prompting Israeli officials to urgently seek clarification from Washington about the terms of the agreement.

The ceasefire agreement allows Israel to halt offensive operations while reserving the right to respond to "planned, imminent, or ongoing attacks." However, Hezbollah, which boycotted the negotiations, has demanded Israel withdraw all troops from southern Lebanon to positions held before hostilities escalated in early March.

The conflict has exacted a devastating humanitarian toll on Lebanon. According to Lebanese officials, nearly 2,200 people have been killed and 1.2 million displaced since early March, when Israel expanded its airstrikes on Beirut and other cities. The strikes followed Hezbollah rocket attacks launched in support of Iran. Tehran has since made an end to Israeli operations in Lebanon a precondition for any potential peace agreement with the U.S. and Israel.
