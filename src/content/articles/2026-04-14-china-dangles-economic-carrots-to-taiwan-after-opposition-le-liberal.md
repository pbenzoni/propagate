Beijing announced a package of economic incentives aimed at Taiwan on Sunday, following a controversial visit by an opposition leader that has drawn sharp criticism from the island's democratically elected government.

The ten-point proposal includes resuming direct flights across the Taiwan Strait, easing market access for Taiwanese food products, and sharing energy and water resources with Taiwan-controlled islands near the mainland. The announcement came after Kuomintang (KMT) party leader Cheng Li-wun met with Chinese President Xi Jinping during a six-day visit that Taiwan's ruling Democratic Progressive Party repeatedly condemned.

Taiwan's government has expressed deep skepticism about Beijing's overtures, warning that they lack institutional protections and represent a pattern of unreliable commitments. "Any exchanges should not be subject to political preconditions, nor should they be used as tools for political maneuvering or deals by specific parties," said Presidential Office spokesperson Karen Kuo. The island's Mainland Affairs Council noted that China has repeatedly introduced and then suspended similar measures in the past, calling the new proposals "highly risky" for Taiwan's industries and public.

The timing underscores Beijing's strategy of engaging with opposition forces while bypassing Taiwan's elected leadership. Taiwan has functioned as a self-governing democracy since 1949, though China claims the island as its territory under the "One China" policy. The international community remains divided on Taiwan's status, with most nations formally recognizing Beijing's position while maintaining unofficial ties with Taipei.
