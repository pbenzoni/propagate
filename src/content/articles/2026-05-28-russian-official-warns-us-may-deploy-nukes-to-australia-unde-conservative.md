Russian Security Council Secretary Sergey Shoigu has issued a warning that the United States could deploy nuclear weapons to Australia as part of the AUKUS security partnership. Speaking at a Russia-ASEAN meeting Wednesday, Shoigu also cited Japan and South Korea as potential deployment locations for American nuclear assets in the region.

The warning comes as the U.S. military prepares to deploy Typhon missile systems—capable of firing nuclear-armed Tomahawk missiles—to southwestern Japan as early as next month for joint military exercises. While the Japanese embassy in Moscow stated the systems would be "stored" at a U.S. base after the drills rather than "permanently deployed," Shoigu suggested this distinction may be more semantic than substantive.

Australia joined the AUKUS security pact with the United States and United Kingdom in 2021. Under that agreement, Washington committed to providing Canberra with nuclear submarine technology, specifically enabling Australia to acquire and operate Virginia-class submarines. The Australian government maintains this move complies with international Non-Proliferation Treaty obligations.

Shoigu characterized these developments as part of a broader American military buildup strategy across the Asia-Pacific region. He warned that Washington is employing "tried and true methods for extracting money from its vassals for American adventures" and noted ongoing U.S.-Japan discussions about forming an Indo-Pacific Treaty Organization modeled after NATO. A bill introduced in Congress last month would require President Trump to establish a task force exploring the feasibility of such a NATO-like structure in the region.

Both Russia and China have previously voiced opposition to potential NATO expansion into Asia. In October, Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov accused the alliance of seeking to transform the Eurasian continent into a sphere of influence designed to contain China, isolate Russia, and confront North Korea.
