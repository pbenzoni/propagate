The United Kingdom announced a £210 million ($280 million) agreement to supply enriched uranium to Ukraine's state nuclear energy company, Energoatom, in a move aimed at bolstering the war-torn nation's beleaguered power grid. The two-year deal, backed by UK Export Finance, will enable British-based supplier Urenco to provide critical nuclear fuel to help keep Ukraine's lights on.

Prime Minister Keir Starmer and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky finalized the arrangement during their meeting at Downing Street last week. The agreement comes as Ukraine's energy infrastructure continues to face systematic targeting, threatening civilians' access to heat and electricity. Starmer reaffirmed Britain's commitment to supporting Ukraine "for as long as it takes."

The deal takes on added urgency given ongoing concerns about nuclear safety in the conflict zone. The Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant, Europe's largest nuclear facility, has been under Russian occupation since March 2022. Earlier this month, a drone strike hit the plant's sixth power unit, with the International Atomic Energy Agency confirming damage to the facility. Moscow has made unsubstantiated allegations about Ukraine potentially developing nuclear weapons, claims that both Kyiv and Western governments have rejected.

While corruption challenges exist across Ukraine's institutions—a legacy of decades under Russian influence that the country has been working to address through Western-backed anti-corruption reforms—the immediate priority remains ensuring Ukrainian civilians have access to essential services as winter approaches and Russian attacks on civilian infrastructure continue.
