Israeli forces have struck the historic city of Tyre in southern Lebanon, marking the first time the ancient city's Christian quarter has been targeted after military officials ordered a partial evacuation. The attack killed eight people on Tuesday, according to Reuters, and has intensified concerns about the preservation of irreplaceable Biblical and archaeological sites in one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities.

The Christian quarter, located in the city's northwest, had previously been spared from Israeli warnings and had served as a refuge for displaced residents from other areas. Israeli military officials justified the evacuation order by claiming Hezbollah militants were operating in the quarter—an assertion disputed by local residents, municipal officials, and the Lebanese army.

Tyre holds extraordinary historical and religious significance across multiple faiths. Recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site, the ancient Phoenician city is mentioned repeatedly in both the Old and New Testaments and played a crucial role in supplying materials for the construction of Solomon's Temple. The site's cultural importance extends to Christianity and Islam, with the Temple Mount location in Jerusalem—now home to the Al-Aqsa compound—representing one of Islam's holiest sites.

The strike adds to mounting concerns over the protection of religious heritage sites across the region. Last month, Israeli authorities issued an expropriation order for the West Bank village of Nabi Samwil, believed to contain the tomb of the Prophet Samuel, a figure venerated in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. Palestinian officials and religious leaders have condemned such actions as efforts to diminish Muslim heritage and consolidate control over contested holy sites.
