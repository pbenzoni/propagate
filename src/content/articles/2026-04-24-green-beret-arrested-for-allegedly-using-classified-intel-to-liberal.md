A U.S. Army Green Beret has been taken into custody on federal charges alleging he illegally profited from classified military information about an operation targeting Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro. Master Sgt. Gannon Ken Van Dyke allegedly wagered approximately $32,000 on the prediction platform Polymarket and walked away with more than $400,000 by betting on Maduro's removal from power—using insider knowledge from his direct involvement in planning the January raid.

According to the Justice Department, Van Dyke faces multiple charges including wire fraud, commodities fraud, and unlawful use of government information. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has filed a parallel civil complaint. Polymarket representatives stated the company identified the suspicious trading activity internally and cooperated fully with federal investigators.

The arrest has intensified broader concerns about the ethical and legal implications of prediction markets that effectively turn military operations and geopolitical crises into financial instruments. During recent Middle East conflicts, traders placed over $1 billion in suspiciously well-timed wagers linked to airstrikes, ceasefires, and diplomatic developments, according to reporting by *The Guardian*. One particularly striking example involved an $850,000 bet placed just before U.S. strikes, while oil futures markets saw roughly $950 million in trades hours before a presidential ceasefire announcement.

Oversight questions have also emerged regarding potential conflicts of interest involving political figures with ties to the prediction market industry. *The New York Times* reported that Donald Trump Jr. held advisory positions with both Polymarket and rival platform Kalshi, and maintained an investment in Polymarket through his venture capital firm. The White House issued guidance to staff against using insider information for trading purposes in late March, underscoring growing alarm about the intersection of classified government knowledge and speculative financial markets.
