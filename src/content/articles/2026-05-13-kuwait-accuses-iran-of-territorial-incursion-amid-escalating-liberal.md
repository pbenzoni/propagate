Kuwait has accused Iran of attempting to infiltrate strategically important Bubiyan Island after detaining four men allegedly linked to Iran's Revolutionary Guards. The incident, which Kuwaiti authorities say occurred on May 1, involved individuals attempting to enter the country by sea, resulting in a clash that wounded a Kuwaiti soldier.

Bubiyan Island, Kuwait's largest, sits near the Iraqi border at a critical juncture for Gulf shipping routes and northern oilfields. The island also hosts a U.S. military base, underscoring the geopolitical stakes of any territorial breach in the region.

Iran has categorically rejected Kuwait's accusations, characterizing them as baseless and politically motivated. According to Iranian officials, the four individuals were sailors aboard a vessel that accidentally entered Kuwaiti waters due to navigational equipment failure during a routine patrol. Tehran has demanded consular access and the sailors' release, accusing Kuwait of exploiting the incident for political gain.

The alleged incursion comes at a moment of heightened regional volatility. Tensions across the Gulf have intensified following U.S. and Israeli military strikes on Iran in February, which prompted retaliatory actions by Tehran against Israel and U.S. allies hosting American military installations.
