In a historic setback, Germany failed to secure a temporary seat on the UN Security Council for the first time, losing to Austria and Portugal in a General Assembly vote on Wednesday. Portugal garnered 134 votes and Austria 131, while Berlin received only 104 votes in the secret ballot—a result German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul called "a real disappointment."

The outcome comes amid intensifying calls to reform the Security Council to better represent the Global South. African leaders have demanded at least two permanent seats for their continent, while UN Secretary-General António Guterres has described Africa's exclusion from permanent membership as "indefensible." India has also advocated for permanent status, citing its democratic credentials and role in UN peacekeeping.

Germany itself has championed Security Council expansion, arguing the body must evolve to reflect contemporary political and economic realities. Wadephul previously stated the council should include additional seats, "especially for underrepresented regions in Africa, Asia, and Latin America." Berlin has positioned its own bid for permanent membership as aligned with Global South demands for reforming institutions still dominated by Western powers.

The incoming elected members—Portugal, Austria, Zimbabwe, Trinidad and Tobago, and Kyrgyzstan—will begin their two-year terms on January 1, 2027. Notably, the vote was presided over by former German Foreign Minister Annalena Baerbock, currently serving as president of the UN General Assembly. The Security Council maintains five permanent veto-holding members—the US, UK, France, Russia, and China—alongside ten rotating elected seats.
