The Pentagon has prohibited reporters from entering its press office after reclassifying the space as a facility for handling classified information, marking the latest erosion of press access under the current administration. The restriction, first reported by the Washington Post, represents a significant departure from decades of established practice that allowed journalists routine contact with Defense Department public affairs officials.

Acting Pentagon Press Secretary Joel Valdez defended the move, stating that speechwriters for Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth regularly handle sensitive materials in the office. While Valdez claimed the department maintains unprecedented transparency, the policy prevents journalists from the informal interactions and spontaneous briefings that have long been standard practice for Pentagon reporting.

The restriction is part of a broader pattern of limiting media access that has intensified over the past year. The Defense Department previously introduced requirements mandating that journalists be escorted within the building and pledge not to seek unauthorized information—even unclassified material. Major news organizations including Fox News, CNN, the Associated Press, and The New York Times refused to sign such agreements, with hundreds of reporters surrendering their credentials in protest.

These policies have sparked legal challenges raising First Amendment concerns. A federal judge struck down portions of the restrictions in March following a lawsuit by The New York Times, which argues the measures constitute an unconstitutional attempt to control independent reporting on military affairs. The Pentagon has appealed that ruling, and litigation continues as press freedom advocates warn of a troubling precedent for government accountability and the public's right to information about defense policy.
