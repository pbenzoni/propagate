Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson has called for NATO countries to assist Ukraine in better coordinating its drone operations, framing recent airspace incidents as unintended consequences of Russia's ongoing aggression and alleged electronic interference.

Speaking alongside NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, Kristersson emphasized the importance of rejecting what he characterized as Moscow's misleading narratives. "We should really not be open for the Russian narrative on this, but instead, of course, help the Ukrainians as much as we can to direct their attacks in the right directions," he stated. The Swedish leader attributed wayward drones to technical disruptions including jamming and other interference, insisting that Ukraine has no intention of violating allied airspace.

Several NATO member states have recently reported Ukrainian drones entering their territory while apparently en route to targets in northwestern Russia. Estonia, Lithuania, Finland, and Romania have all documented such incidents since mid-March. The issue gained particular prominence in Latvia, where a failure to intercept drones that struck an oil facility contributed to a government collapse.

Rutte echoed Kristersson's position, attributing the airspace incidents to the broader context of Russia's full-scale invasion forcing Ukraine to defend itself. Both leaders pushed back against Moscow's suggestions that NATO countries are deliberately facilitating strikes, instead portraying the incidents as technical mishaps occurring amid Ukraine's legitimate right to self-defense against ongoing Russian attacks.
