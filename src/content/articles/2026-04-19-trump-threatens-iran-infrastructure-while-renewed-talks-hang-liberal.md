President Donald Trump announced Sunday that U.S. negotiators will travel to Pakistan on Monday for another round of talks with Iran, even as he threatened to destroy Iranian infrastructure if Tehran does not accept what he called a "fair and reasonable deal."

In a Truth Social post, Trump accused Iran of violating a temporary ceasefire—set to expire Wednesday—by allegedly firing on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. "Iran decided to fire bullets yesterday in the Strait of Hormuz – a total violation of our ceasefire agreement," Trump wrote, warning there would be "No more MR. Nice Guy" and threatening to "knock out every single power plant, and every single bridge, in Iran."

The talks have stalled over Iran's nuclear program, with Trump demanding Tehran dismantle nuclear infrastructure and surrender enriched uranium—proposals Iranian officials have rejected. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian stated Sunday that Trump has no justification to "deprive Iran of its nuclear rights," while Iranian news agency Tasnim reported the country will not return to negotiations unless Washington withdraws its "excessive demands."

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman characterized the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports as "not only a violation" of the ceasefire but also "unlawful and criminal" under the UN Charter, calling it a potential "war crime" and "collective punishment." Iranian military officials have vowed to defend the country's sovereignty, while maritime authorities have again closed the Strait of Hormuz to shipping, raising concerns about global energy markets and regional stability.
