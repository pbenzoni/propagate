A top White House economic advisor openly celebrated the prospect of mass suffering in Iran during a weekend television appearance, raising alarm among human rights advocates about the administration's approach to international conflict.

Kevin Hassett, director of the National Economic Council, described Iran as facing "extreme calamity" and hunger during an interview with CBS News on Sunday, portraying the deteriorating humanitarian situation as a policy success. The comments came as the Trump administration maintains a naval blockade of Iranian ports—traditionally considered an act of war—while intensifying economic sanctions following a bombing campaign that failed to topple the government in Tehran.

When pressed on whether the blockade meant the US was effectively at war with Iran despite the president's statements to Congress, Hassett avoided giving a direct answer, saying he was uncertain "what the definition of war is when we're not shooting and we're negotiating." Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent separately described the administration's approach as "economic fury," part of what the Pentagon has designated Operation Epic Fury.

Critics have long argued that unilateral sanctions function as a form of economic warfare that disproportionately harms civilian populations. A 2025 study in *The Lancet Global Health* estimated that US and EU sanctions between 2010 and 2021 contributed to approximately 564,000 excess deaths annually. Meanwhile, the administration faces mounting challenges: Iran has not accepted US demands, critical munitions stockpiles have been depleted, relations with traditional allies have been strained, and experts warn the crisis could trigger a severe global economic downturn.
