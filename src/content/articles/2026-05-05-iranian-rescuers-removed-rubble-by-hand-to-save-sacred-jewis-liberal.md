Iranian rescue workers manually cleared debris brick by brick to recover sacred Torah scrolls from a Tehran synagogue damaged in an Israeli airstrike last month, according to Marjan Yeshayayi, a member of Iran's Jewish community.

In an interview, Yeshayayi described the painstaking effort to preserve the religious texts after the Rafi Niya synagogue near central Tehran was damaged when Israeli forces struck a nearby residential building. "When I made the request, I did not believe they would accept it, but instead they said, 'OK, we will remove it manually,' and they really did. Each brick was removed by hand, and the scrolls came out safely," she recalled.

The Israeli Defense Forces later acknowledged the strike, stating they had targeted a senior Iranian commander and expressing regret over what they termed "collateral damage" to the synagogue. Yeshayayi said three of her friends died in the attack.

According to Yeshayayi, Iran's constitution protects religious minorities' rights to practice their faith freely. Approximately 8,000 Jews live in Iran and worship in dozens of synagogues across the country. The account highlights the human cost of military operations and the often-overlooked religious diversity within Iran.
