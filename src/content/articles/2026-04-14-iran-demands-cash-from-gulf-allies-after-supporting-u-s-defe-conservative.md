Iran has formally demanded that five Arab nations—Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, and Jordan—pay reparations for allowing U.S. military operations from bases on their soil. The demand comes after American and Israeli forces conducted strikes aimed at dismantling Iran's nuclear weapons and ballistic missile programs.

In a letter to UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres, Iranian envoy Amir Saeid Iravani claimed the Gulf states were complicit in what he called "unlawful armed attacks." Iran is seeking compensation for what it describes as material and moral damages, while simultaneously rejecting earlier demands from Gulf states that Iran be held liable for regional war damage.

The February 28 strikes by U.S. and Israeli forces targeted Iran's military infrastructure and nuclear facilities, resulting in significant casualties including senior Iranian officials and over 1,300 civilians according to Iranian claims. Tehran responded by launching strikes against U.S. bases in the region and attacking civilian infrastructure in Gulf states, including critical energy facilities and transportation hubs.

The Iranian demand highlights the complex alliances in the Middle East, where Gulf Arab states have increasingly coordinated with the United States on regional security matters. These nations view Iran's nuclear ambitions and regional influence operations as direct threats to their sovereignty and stability.
