A comprehensive security report released Friday by Sweden's Defense Committee has identified Russia as a persistent threat to the NATO alliance, warning that Moscow could exploit political divisions to test the collective defense commitments that underpin European security.

The assessment, which received backing from Sweden's government and all parliamentary parties, comes as the newly admitted NATO member grapples with an evolving transatlantic relationship. While acknowledging that the United States continues to play a vital role in European security, Swedish policymakers emphasized the need for deeper cooperation within the European Union as Washington's engagement with the continent remains in flux.

Sweden's commitment to supporting Ukraine remains central to its security strategy, according to the document. Since joining NATO in 2024 following Russia's escalation of the conflict, Stockholm has emerged as one of Kyiv's most significant supporters, recently announcing plans to provide dozens of advanced fighter jets to help defend Ukrainian sovereignty.

The report reflects a broader pattern across Europe, where democratic nations have substantially increased defense investments in response to authoritarian threats. Sweden raised its defense budget by 18% for 2026, bringing military spending to 2.8% of GDP—exceeding NATO's target threshold. The country has also strengthened defenses on the strategic Baltic island of Gotland, located less than 200 miles from Russian territory, as part of efforts to ensure regional stability and deter potential aggression against alliance members.
