Iranian parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf has accused President Donald Trump of making false claims following Tehran's brief announcement that it would temporarily reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The vital waterway handles roughly 25% of global crude oil shipments, making control over its access a critical leverage point in ongoing tensions between Washington and Tehran.

On Friday, Iran initially declared the strait "completely open" for commercial vessels during a ten-day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon, causing oil prices to drop approximately 10%. However, Tehran reversed course within 24 hours, returning the waterway to what officials called "strict management and control" by Iranian military forces. Iranian officials blamed the reversal on Washington's refusal to lift a naval blockade imposed after initial negotiations in Pakistan failed to produce results.

President Trump responded to Iran's initial reopening announcement on Truth Social, stating that the U.S. naval blockade would "remain in full force and effect" until a comprehensive peace agreement is reached. He also indicated that Iran had agreed never to close the strait again and that most points of a final deal had already been negotiated.

Ghalibaf disputed these characterizations, stating that "control of the Strait of Hormuz will be determined on the ground field, not on social media." He added that Iran remains unmoved by what he termed American "media warfare" tactics. According to Iranian news sources, Tehran has not committed to another round of negotiations, citing continued U.S. naval operations and what officials describe as excessive American demands during talks. The standoff highlights the complex challenge facing the Trump administration as it seeks to reassert American strength in the region while navigating Iran's strategic control over critical energy infrastructure.
