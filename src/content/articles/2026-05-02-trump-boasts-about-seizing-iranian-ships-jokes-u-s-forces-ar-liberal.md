President Donald Trump's approval rating has fallen to a historic low of 34% in Reuters/Ipsos polling, even as he continues to escalate rhetoric around military operations in the Persian Gulf. Speaking at a private dinner in Florida on Friday, Trump described U.S. forces seizing Iranian vessels as behaving "like pirates," a characterization that has drawn swift condemnation from critics who view the comment as trivializing serious questions about international law.

The president was referring to an April 19 incident in which a U.S. destroyer fired on the Iranian-flagged cargo vessel *Touska* near the Strait of Hormuz before U.S. Marines boarded the ship. "We took over the ship. We took over the cargo, took over the oil. It's a very profitable business," Trump told the audience, prompting laughter. "Who would have thought we were doing that? We're like pirates. We're sort of like pirates."

The seizure has drawn international criticism, with Iran condemning the action as illegal and promising an "unprecedented" response to what Iranian officials have termed U.S. "piracy and banditry." The incident comes amid a broader military standoff: Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz—a chokepoint for roughly one-fifth of global oil trade—to "enemy ships" following a U.S.-Israeli bombing campaign that began on February 28. The United States subsequently declared a naval blockade of Iran last month.

The escalating tensions have had immediate economic consequences, with oil prices surging past $120 per barrel this week for the first time since 2022.
