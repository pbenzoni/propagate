A $14 million Australian government public awareness campaign encouraging citizens to conserve fuel through basic vehicle maintenance and alternative transportation has sparked debate over fiscal priorities during a national fuel crisis.

The "Every Little Bit Helps" initiative, which launched Monday across multiple media platforms, advises motorists to maintain proper tire pressure, remove roof racks when not in use, and consider walking or cycling for short trips. The campaign comes as Australia confronts severe fuel supply disruptions stemming from the closure of the Strait of Hormuz—a critical shipping route for global oil—amid escalating Middle East tensions. Australia imports approximately 90% of its fuel, with over 80% coming from Asian refineries that depend on crude oil from the affected region.

Employment Minister Tanya Plibersek defended the expenditure during a television interview, arguing the campaign was necessary to counter misinformation and provide credible information about fuel security. She noted that a previous government had spent $340 million on industrial relations advertising, suggesting the current investment was comparatively modest. The initiative is part of Australia's National Fuel Security Plan, currently at Level 2, described as "Keep Australia moving."

Critics, including opposition politicians, have questioned whether the same messaging could have been delivered more cost-effectively through existing government communication channels. The debate highlights tensions over public spending priorities as Australia navigates supply chain vulnerabilities, with six fuel tankers scheduled for delivery canceled in late March. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has stated the campaign aims to prevent panic buying and maintain public confidence, asserting that fuel supplies are secured through late May.
