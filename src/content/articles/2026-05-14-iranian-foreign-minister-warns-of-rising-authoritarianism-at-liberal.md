Speaking at a gathering of BRICS foreign ministers in New Delhi, Iranian Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi delivered sharp criticism of what he characterized as deteriorating international norms and accountability among major Western powers. The minister argued that Iran and other member nations have faced increasing pressure from Washington.

Araghchi framed his remarks within a broader narrative about global power shifts, suggesting that established powers are responding to their relative decline with more aggressive policies. He pointed to what he described as violations of international sovereignty and humanitarian principles by Western nations, calling for BRICS countries to work together in shaping what he termed a more equitable global order.

The BRICS economic partnership, which includes Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa along with newer members like Iran, has traditionally focused on trade and development. However, recent discussions have touched on broader cooperation. Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has previously advocated for increased defense coordination among member states to reduce dependence on foreign military suppliers.

The summit highlights ongoing tensions between emerging economies and traditional Western powers over questions of global governance, international law, and the future structure of the international system. These debates reflect deeper disagreements about accountability, sovereignty, and the rules that should govern international relations in an increasingly multipolar world.
