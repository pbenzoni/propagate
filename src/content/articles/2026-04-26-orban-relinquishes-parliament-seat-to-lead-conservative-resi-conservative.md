Viktor Orban announced Saturday he will not take his parliamentary seat following his party's unexpected electoral defeat earlier this month, saying he's needed to reorganize Hungary's conservative "patriotic movement" rather than serve in the legislature.

In a video statement, Orban said the parliamentary mandate he won as head of the Fidesz-KDNP list "is in fact the parliamentary mandate of Fidesz," and he has decided to return it to focus on rebuilding the conservative coalition. "I am now needed not in parliament, but in the reorganization of the patriotic movement," he declared.

The decision comes after Orban's Fidesz party, which had dominated Hungarian politics for years, lost decisively to Peter Magyar's Tisza party in the April 12 election. Magyar's party captured 141 of 199 parliamentary seats in what observers called a stunning political reversal.

Despite stepping away from the legislature, Orban made clear he intends to remain atop his party, saying leadership has proposed he continue as Fidesz president and that he's ready to serve if the party's upcoming congress renews confidence in him. He struck a defiant tone, telling supporters "this camp has remained Hungary's most united and cohesive political community."

Orban said Fidesz's parliamentary group would be "radically transformed" under new leadership when it convenes Monday, and announced the party would hold a national assembly next week while moving up its "renewal congress" to June. The electoral defeat is expected to shift Hungary's relationship with Brussels, Washington, and its approach to the Russia-Ukraine conflict—issues where Orban had charted an independent course that often put him at odds with EU leadership.
