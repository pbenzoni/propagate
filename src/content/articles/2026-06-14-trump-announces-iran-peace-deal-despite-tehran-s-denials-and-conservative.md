President Donald Trump announced Saturday that a peace agreement with Iran will be signed Sunday, June 14, despite Tehran's denial that a deal would be finalized by that date. The announcement comes after more than two months of Pakistan-mediated negotiations following an April truce.

The President made clear on Truth Social that this deal protects American interests without capitulating to Iranian demands. "No money will exchange hands," Trump wrote, signaling that unlike previous administrations, he will not unfreeze billions in Iranian assets as part of any agreement. The deal would prevent Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons "through purchase, development, or any other form of procurement," and the US would take possession of Iran's enriched uranium stockpile to "downblend and destroy it."

Trump also announced the immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz—a critical waterway for global energy markets that Iran closed during recent conflicts. Iranian actions in the strait, which normally handles a quarter of global seaborne oil and LNG trade, had threatened American economic security and energy independence.

However, Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei claimed the signing "will not happen tomorrow," though he acknowledged a deal could materialize "in the coming days." Baghaei accused Israel of attempting to sabotage the negotiations, stating that parties are "not fully committed to their obligations" and "take advantage of every opportunity to break their promises."

Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif expressed optimism that finalization was "likely expected in the next 24 hours." The memorandum under discussion would give both sides 60 days to negotiate Iran's nuclear program and would include an end to Israeli operations in Lebanon, according to Iranian officials.
