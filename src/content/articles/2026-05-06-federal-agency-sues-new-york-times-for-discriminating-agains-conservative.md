The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission has filed a federal lawsuit against the New York Times, alleging the newspaper discriminated against a qualified white male employee in favor of advancing its diversity agenda. The case, filed in Manhattan federal court Tuesday, centers on a 2025 promotion decision for a deputy real estate editor position.

According to the EEOC complaint, a senior editor who had worked at the Times since 2014 was passed over for the role despite being more qualified than other candidates. The agency alleges he was excluded from final interviews because of his race and sex, with the decision driven by the newspaper's publicly stated diversity goals. "Every candidate who advanced through to the final interview process was not a white male," the EEOC stated in its filing.

EEOC Chair Andrea Lucas emphasized that federal anti-discrimination law applies to everyone equally. "Federal law does not allow race or sex-based employment decisions," Lucas said, adding that there is "no diversity exception" to these protections. The lawsuit argues the Times violated Title VII of the Civil Rights Act by prioritizing demographic targets over individual merit and qualifications.

The New York Times has denied the allegations, claiming its hiring practices are merit-based and calling the lawsuit "politically motivated." Company spokesperson Danielle Rhoades Ha insisted that "neither race nor gender played a role" in the promotion decision. The case represents the latest flashpoint in the national debate over corporate diversity programs, with critics arguing these initiatives have morphed from promoting equal opportunity into systems that discriminate against qualified employees based on immutable characteristics.
