Iran's ambassador to Moscow told Russian state media that Tehran remains committed to diplomatic engagement despite what he characterized as repeated American sabotage of peace efforts, particularly under the Trump administration.

In an interview with RT, Ambassador Kazem Jalali said Iran has "never betrayed or left the negotiating table," contrasting this with the United States' withdrawal from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) nuclear deal during Trump's first presidency. "If the other side is willing to negotiate, we will negotiate," Jalali said, while emphasizing that Iran would defend itself if attacked.

The statements come amid escalating tensions in the Strait of Hormuz, where the Trump administration has imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports and threatened military action. On Thursday, Trump ordered the U.S. Navy to "shoot and kill" any Iranian vessels allegedly laying mines in the strategically vital waterway—a directive that human rights advocates have warned could constitute a dangerous escalation.

Iranian officials have rejected Trump's characterization of their leadership as fractured, instead presenting a unified front. President Masoud Pezeshkian and parliamentary leaders issued coordinated statements emphasizing national unity, while Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran's "battlefield and diplomacy are fully coordinated fronts." Tehran has argued that meaningful negotiations cannot occur while an American blockade—which it considers a violation of the ceasefire—remains in place.
