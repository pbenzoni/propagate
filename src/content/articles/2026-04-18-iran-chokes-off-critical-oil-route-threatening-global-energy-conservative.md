Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baqaei defended the move in an interview with RT, claiming international law permits Tehran to control the strategic chokepoint as a coastal state responding to threats.

The renewed restrictions follow the breakdown of US-Iran negotiations in Islamabad last weekend and President Donald Trump's decision to maintain a naval blockade on Iranian ports. "We cannot allow enemy vessels, especially military ones or those linked to countries involved in aggression, to pass through the strait normally, as they pose a direct threat," Baqaei stated. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard Navy Command has reasserted "strict control" over the waterway, vowing to maintain restrictions as long as US measures remain in place.

The disruption has sent shockwaves through global energy markets, with crude oil prices surging back toward $100 per barrel after briefly easing when Iran temporarily opened the strait during the Israel-Lebanon ceasefire. Tehran reversed that decision after Trump confirmed the US blockade would continue until a comprehensive peace agreement is reached.
