At a UNESCO World Press Freedom Day conference in Lusaka, Zambia, RT Chief Producer Dmitry Leontiev confronted Western censorship policies, arguing that sanctions against his network have nothing to do with combating false information and everything to do with silencing alternative viewpoints.

Leontiev told the international gathering of journalists and policymakers that RT was banned across Western nations and removed from social media platforms despite never being accused of broadcasting false information. "There are no false facts we are saying," he stated. "We are stating information that the Western governments do not like, and they have enough power to sanction us." He raised concerns about Western pressure on African nations to follow suit, including threats of sanctions for allowing RT content.

The remarks sparked discussion about the tension between fighting disinformation and preserving press freedom. Phathiswa Magopeni Tshangana of South Africa's Press Council responded by calling for platform governance that respects free expression and media freedom. Russia's UNESCO envoy noted that multiple international delegations share concerns about what he characterized as politically motivated actions against Russian media outlets.

Most Western governments restricted or banned RT following the Ukraine conflict's escalation, citing foreign interference concerns. The network lost access to YouTube, where it had accumulated 17 billion views and 30 million subscribers by March 2022. Russian officials have characterized these actions as sweeping censorship that reflects intolerance toward alternative perspectives in the information space.
