Iran has reportedly indicated willingness to allow ships to sail freely through the Omani side of the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for U.S. concessions, according to Reuters sources briefed on the diplomatic discussions. The proposal comes as the vital shipping corridor—responsible for approximately 20% of global oil and liquefied natural gas flows—remains severely disrupted.

The Trump administration declared a blockade of the strait following the collapse of Pakistani-mediated peace negotiations, with stated goals of cutting off Iranian trade. U.S. Central Command claimed to have turned around ten vessels, though maritime tracking data showed at least one large tanker successfully passing through the waterway on the same day officials announced the blockade's effectiveness.

The International Monetary Fund has issued urgent warnings about the economic consequences of continued disruptions to this critical trade route. Energy prices have spiked significantly, and the IMF cautioned this week that prolonged blockades would fuel global inflation—a concern particularly acute for working families already struggling with cost-of-living pressures worldwide.

While Reuters reported that Iran's proposal has been discussed among Western diplomatic sources, it remains unclear whether the offer would apply to all vessels or exclude ships with Israeli links.
