Two former Israeli Prime Ministers are joining forces in an unprecedented bid to end Benjamin Netanyahu's divisive leadership. Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid announced Sunday they are combining their political parties ahead of elections that must be held by October, creating a unified opposition front called 'Together, Led by Bennett.'

The alliance comes as Netanyahu faces mounting legal and political crises. The International Criminal Court issued arrest warrants against him in 2024 for alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity related to Israel's military campaign in Gaza, which has killed more than 72,000 Palestinians according to health authorities. Domestically, Netanyahu is on trial for bribery, fraud, and breach of trust—charges he has dismissed as politically motivated.

Bennett and Lapid previously collaborated to end Netanyahu's 12-year tenure in 2021, forming a historic "government of change" coalition that included parties across the political spectrum, even bringing an Arab party into government for the first time. That coalition proved short-lived, and Netanyahu returned to power after the 2022 elections.

Recent polling suggests the unified opposition could pose a serious challenge to Netanyahu's Likud party, with both camps tied at 24 projected Knesset seats. The partnership has pledged to pursue universal conscription reform and impose term limits on the prime minister's office—measures seen as directly targeting Netanyahu's decades-long dominance of Israeli politics. However, the new alliance has stated it will not include Arab parties in any future coalition government.
