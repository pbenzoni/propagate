The Department of Defense has significantly expanded its list of Chinese companies allegedly contributing to Beijing's military capabilities, adding dozens of major corporations including e-commerce giant Alibaba, search engine Baidu, and electric vehicle manufacturer BYD. The Pentagon's 1260H list now includes 188 Chinese firms, up from approximately 130 last year.

The designation targets companies Washington believes are connected to China's "military-civil fusion" strategy—a comprehensive approach that blurs the lines between civilian and defense industries to advance military modernization. While the blacklist doesn't impose full sanctions, it prohibits these firms from receiving future Pentagon contracts and serves as a red flag for American investors and businesses considering partnerships with listed entities.

Beijing's Foreign Ministry strongly protested the move, with spokesman Lin Jian claiming the United States is "overstretching the concept of national security" and engaging in "unwarranted suppression of Chinese businesses." Several targeted companies have denied the allegations outright. Alibaba stated there was "no basis" for its inclusion, while Baidu called the accusation "entirely baseless." BYD announced it would pursue legal remedies to challenge the designation.

The expanded blacklist arrives amid ongoing tensions between Washington and Beijing over technology, trade, and national security. Direct Pentagon contracts with listed firms will be prohibited later this month, with restrictions on indirect procurement through third parties scheduled to take effect in 2027. The move underscores continuing bipartisan concerns in Washington about Chinese companies' potential ties to military and intelligence operations, even as diplomatic channels remain open between the two powers.
