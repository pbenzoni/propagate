The Trump administration is reportedly preparing to drastically reduce the military support the United States would provide to European NATO allies in the event of a major conflict, according to a Reuters report citing anonymous sources familiar with Pentagon planning.

The planned "significant scale down" of force commitments under the NATO Force Model—the classified framework detailing what military capabilities each member contributes—represents a potential weakening of the collective defense principle that has underpinned transatlantic security for decades. The announcement is expected at a May 22 meeting of defense policy officials in Brussels, where the administration will be represented by an aide to Undersecretary Elbridge Colby.

This shift comes as the U.S. has already begun withdrawing forces from Europe, canceling a planned 4,000-troop rotation to Poland and pulling 5,000 soldiers from Germany. Reports suggest the administration has created a rewards-and-punishments framework for NATO allies based on their support for recent U.S. military actions in the Middle East, potentially redirecting military cooperation away from countries deemed insufficiently supportive.

The timing raises concerns among national security experts, as European nations remain heavily dependent on American intelligence satellites, long-range strike capabilities, airlift capacity, and advanced naval assets—even after years of increased defense spending in response to regional security challenges. While administration officials have pushed European allies to expand their conventional forces, they have simultaneously opposed Europe developing independent nuclear deterrence to compensate for any reduction in the American security umbrella.
