Iran's foreign minister has declared that the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz will not return to its previous status following the recent U.S.-Israeli military operations against Iranian targets. Abbas Araghchi stated that Iran and Oman will jointly administer the waterway and collect transit fees from passing vessels.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical chokepoint through which approximately one quarter of the world's seaborne oil and liquefied natural gas flows under normal conditions. The waterway has remained largely closed throughout the conflict, which began on February 28 with joint U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iranian territory that resulted in the death of senior Iranian officials, including longtime Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei.

According to Araghchi, the U.S. and Iran are finalizing an agreement to end hostilities. Under the proposed memorandum of understanding, the United States would commit to respecting Iran's sovereignty and non-interference in its domestic affairs—a stance Tehran claims would mark the first such commitment in 47 years. President Trump has expressed optimism about reaching a deal, saying he called off additional strikes to advance peace negotiations.

The Iranian diplomat emphasized that "the Strait of Hormuz lies under the sovereignty of Iran and Oman" and that the two nations will soon issue a joint statement establishing a new administrative framework for the waterway. Iran most recently declared the strait closed to all ships in response to U.S. military strikes earlier this week, though Trump has indicated that more than 200 ships have been guided through with American military assistance during the conflict.
