Five U.S. servicemembers and contractors sustained minor injuries when an Iranian ballistic missile strike hit Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait, according to Bloomberg sources. The attack destroyed at least one MQ-9 Reaper drone and seriously damaged another, marking another costly blow to America's military capabilities in the region.

The incident occurred during Thursday's exchange of fire between U.S. and Iranian forces. While the U.S. military reported destroying five Iranian kamikaze drones threatening the Strait of Hormuz and striking an Iranian military site near Bandar Abbas, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps retaliated by targeting the American air base. Kuwaiti air defenses intercepted the Iranian Fateh-110 missile, but falling debris struck the base where U.S. forces were stationed.

The losses highlight mounting concerns about America's drone capabilities. Bloomberg reports that the U.S. military has lost approximately 30 MQ-9 Reaper drones worth nearly $1 billion, with the majority destroyed by Iranian fire. This represents roughly one-fifth of the entire fleet. The current inventory has dropped to around 135 drones, well below the Air Force's minimum requirement of 189.

Replacing these critical reconnaissance and strike assets won't be straightforward. General Atomics, the manufacturer of the MQ-9 Reaper, halted production of the flagship model last year and now only produces variants designated for foreign customers. The ongoing depletion of America's drone fleet raises serious questions about military readiness and strategic capabilities in an increasingly volatile Middle East.
