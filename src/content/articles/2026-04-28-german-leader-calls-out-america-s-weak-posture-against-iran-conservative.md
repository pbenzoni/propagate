German Chancellor Friedrich Merz delivered a sobering assessment of America's current standoff with Iran, warning that the United States appears to lack a coherent strategy while Tehran continues to block vital shipping lanes and drive up energy costs for families worldwide.

Speaking candidly during a school visit in North Rhine-Westphalia, Merz pointed to the ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz—a critical waterway for global oil supplies—as evidence that Iran has proven more resilient than anticipated. "The Iranians are clearly stronger than expected, and the Americans clearly have no truly convincing strategy in the negotiations either," the chancellor stated, according to Deutsche Welle.

Merz drew parallels to America's prolonged entanglements in Afghanistan and Iraq, emphasizing the importance of having an exit strategy before military engagement. "The problem with conflicts like this is always that you don't just have to get in—you also have to get out again," he warned. The chancellor observed that Iran's Revolutionary Guards are "negotiating very skillfully—or very skillfully not negotiating," leaving the United States without clear options.

The comments come as President Trump rejected Iran's latest proposal to reopen the strait, with the administration maintaining that it holds "all the cards" in negotiations. Despite ongoing military operations aimed at dismantling Iran's nuclear and missile capabilities, Tehran has continued launching strikes against Israel and U.S. bases in the region while keeping the strategic waterway closed to what it terms "hostile" shipping.
