Europe faces a critical window to strengthen its defense capabilities as the transatlantic security framework grows increasingly uncertain, according to Belgium's Chief of the General Staff Frederik Vansina. Speaking to Le Soir, the senior military official outlined a sobering timeline for European strategic independence.

Vansina emphasized that the continent is navigating "the most unstable period since the end of the Cold War," with a global arms race accelerating across multiple regions. While stressing that no immediate threat exists, he argued that Europe must achieve military self-sufficiency by 2030—a timeline driven partly by the Trump administration's demands that European nations shoulder primary responsibility for their own defense rather than relying on American security guarantees.

The Belgian defense chief's comments reflect growing concerns among European leaders about the reliability of U.S. commitments to collective security. Recent fractures within NATO have deepened as the Trump administration pushes allies to dramatically increase military spending while simultaneously pursuing unilateral policies that diverge from European interests. Vansina suggested that true "strategic autonomy" for Europe remains achievable by 2035, but only with sustained increases in defense budgets beyond current NATO targets.

The warning comes as European policymakers grapple with the dual challenge of maintaining democratic values while addressing legitimate security concerns in an increasingly multipolar world. Russia has dismissed suggestions it poses a threat to NATO as pretextual justifications for militarization, while European officials argue that prudent defense planning requires preparation for multiple contingencies in an era of great power competition.
