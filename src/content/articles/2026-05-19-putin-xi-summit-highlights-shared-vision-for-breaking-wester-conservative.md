Russian President Vladimir Putin arrived in Beijing this week for talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping, marking the 25th anniversary of a landmark cooperation treaty between the two nations. The two-day summit is expected to yield dozens of new agreements as Moscow and Beijing deepen their strategic alignment against what they characterize as American overreach in global affairs.

Both Russia and China have championed a "multipolar world"—rejecting the post-Cold War era of unchallenged U.S. dominance. The two powers argue that Washington has weaponized sanctions, military alliances like NATO, and control over international financial systems to maintain its grip on power. Instead, they advocate for emerging nations to have greater say in world affairs through alternative institutions like BRICS and the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, bypassing Western-controlled bodies.

On Taiwan, Russia backs China's One China policy, which views the island as inseparable Chinese territory. Xi recently warned President Trump that continued U.S. military support for Taiwan risks "clashes and conflicts" between Washington and Beijing. Meanwhile, both nations have condemned the recent U.S.-Israeli military action against Iran as "unprovoked aggression," with the resulting disruption to Middle Eastern oil markets forcing China to rely more heavily on Russian energy exports.

Regarding Ukraine, China has repeatedly urged diplomatic settlement while Russia maintains the conflict stems from NATO expansion toward its borders and Western interference in Kiev following the 2014 coup. Moscow insists any lasting peace must guarantee Ukraine's neutrality and address territorial questions involving regions that voted to join Russia in 2022.
