Iran is preparing legislation that would impose tolls on vessels from what it deems "hostile" countries crossing the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz, according to Ebrahim Azizi, head of the Iranian Parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Committee. The announcement follows the breakdown of diplomatic talks between Washington and Tehran in Islamabad last weekend.

Speaking to Russian state media, Azizi indicated that proposed parliamentary legislation would require ships from nations that have enforced sanctions or participated in strikes against Iran to pay fees as compensation for economic damages. "Those who have caused us damage, those who are in fact liable to pay compensation, may only cross the Strait of Hormuz if they pay their damages and compensation," he said. The Strait of Hormuz is a critical global shipping chokepoint through which nearly one-fifth of the world's petroleum passes.

The threat comes amid escalating tensions in the region. President Trump has recently ordered a naval blockade and refused to rule out "limited" military strikes against Iran, while threatening consequences for vessels that comply with Iranian transit requirements. Azizi dismissed U.S. negotiating posture, saying Washington "needs a deal more than we do" and expressing deep distrust of American intentions. "We simply do not trust them," he stated, questioning how a country that "elevates arrogance and colonialism to a guiding principle" could be expected to honor agreements.

The proposed Iranian legislation would establish a new "management and control system" for the strait based on what Tehran describes as environmental safety, security concerns, and service provisions. Specific toll amounts would be determined by Iran's cabinet rather than its parliament, with fees calibrated according to "national interests." International shipping and energy markets are likely to view the proposal with alarm, given the strait's crucial role in global oil transport and the potential for further militarization of already tense waters.
