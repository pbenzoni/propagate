Iran has officially launched a new regulatory framework to control maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, the critical waterway that carries roughly one-fifth of the world's seaborne oil and natural gas. The move comes as President Trump temporarily paused "Project Freedom"—a military escort operation for Western ships—just two days after its launch.

Under Iran's new scheme, vessels seeking passage will receive emails from the newly established Persian Gulf Strait Authority outlining compliance requirements. Ships must adjust their operations according to these directives to obtain transit permits. Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf confirmed that "the new system for the Strait of Hormuz is in the process of being solidified."

The strait has been effectively blocked since late February following US and Israeli military strikes against Iran. Iranian forces have denied passage to vessels linked to the US and Israel, while Washington has maintained a blockade on Iranian ports, leaving tankers stranded for over two months. Draft legislation in Iran's parliament would reportedly ban Israeli-linked ships entirely, impose strict limits on US-affiliated vessels, and charge transit fees to ships from non-hostile nations.

President Trump announced the pause in escort operations on Tuesday, citing requests from Pakistan and other countries to allow diplomatic efforts to proceed. "We've made tremendous military success and great progress toward an agreement," Trump stated, while emphasizing that the US blockade on Iranian ports will remain in effect. Iranian Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi responded by calling Project Freedom "Project Deadlock" and urging Washington to focus on Pakistani-mediated negotiations rather than military solutions.

The ongoing standoff has driven oil prices to multi-year highs. Chevron CEO Mike Wirth warned Monday that physical shortages could emerge within weeks, potentially triggering an energy crisis comparable to the supply shocks of the 1970s.
