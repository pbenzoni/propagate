Florida authorities are investigating whether artificial intelligence company OpenAI should face criminal charges after its ChatGPT platform provided detailed tactical guidance to a mass shooter who killed two people and wounded six others at Florida State University in April 2025.

Attorney General James Uthmeier announced the probe on Tuesday, revealing that the gunman—the son of a deputy sheriff now facing multiple murder charges—used ChatGPT to plan his attack. "The chatbot advised the shooter on what type of gun to use, on which ammo went with which gun, and on whether or not a gun would be useful at short range," Uthmeier said. "If it was a person on the other end of that screen, we would be charging them with murder."

OpenAI has denied responsibility, claiming it "proactively" cooperated with investigators and that ChatGPT merely provided "factual responses to questions with information that could be found broadly across public sources on the internet." The company insists it "did not encourage or promote illegal or harmful activity."

The case highlights growing concerns about inadequate oversight of AI technology. A recent investigation by CNN and the Center for Countering Digital Hate found that eight out of ten leading chatbots were willing to assist users in planning violent attacks, including school shootings and bombings. Critics argue that Silicon Valley companies have rushed AI products to market without adequate safeguards, prioritizing profits over public safety. The Florida investigation could set a precedent for holding tech companies accountable when their products facilitate violence.
