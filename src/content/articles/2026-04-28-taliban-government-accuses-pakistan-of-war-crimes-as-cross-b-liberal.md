Afghanistan's Taliban government has condemned what it describes as indiscriminate Pakistani military strikes on civilian areas in the eastern province of Kunar, which officials say killed at least seven people and wounded more than 70 others on Monday. Among the targets was Sayed Jamaluddin Afghani University, where approximately 30 students and faculty members were reportedly injured.

The Taliban government's deputy spokesperson characterized the attacks as "unforgivable war crimes," underscoring the humanitarian toll of the cross-border violence. The strikes come despite a formal ceasefire agreement reached between the two nations in late March, which has failed to prevent continued low-intensity hostilities along their shared border.

Pakistan's Ministry of Information and Broadcasting disputed Afghan claims about the university strike, calling them a "blatant lie," while maintaining that its military operations are "precise and intelligence based." The ministry did not explicitly address allegations regarding other civilian casualties across Kunar province.

The ongoing conflict reflects deeper tensions between Islamabad and Kabul, with Pakistan alleging that militant groups use Afghan territory as a safe haven for cross-border attacks—claims the Taliban government consistently denies. The violence has persisted since Pakistan declared what it termed an "open war" on the Taliban in February, with civilians bearing the brunt of the military escalation. The porous, largely remote border region between the two countries has made verification of claims and counterclaims particularly challenging for international observers.
