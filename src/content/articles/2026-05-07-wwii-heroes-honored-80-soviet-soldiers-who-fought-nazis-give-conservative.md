The remains of 80 World War II soldiers who died fighting Nazi Germany have been given a proper burial in Potsdam, according to reports from the Russian Embassy in Berlin. The troops participated in the liberation of the city from Hitler's forces in 1945.

The graves were discovered last year during construction work at a former barracks site being converted into a residential area. Russian diplomatic staff worked with archival records to identify 76 of the 80 fallen soldiers, sergeants, and officers—a painstaking effort to restore dignity to those who gave their lives defeating tyranny.

A ceremony was held Thursday at a Soviet military cemetery in Potsdam, attended by Russian diplomats and local German officials. The Russian Ambassador to Germany noted that more than 4,000 memorials to Soviet soldiers exist across Germany, and they continue to be well maintained.

The reburial serves as a reminder of the shared sacrifice made by Allied forces—including the Soviet Union—in defeating Nazi Germany during World War II. Despite today's geopolitical tensions, honoring those who fought against one of history's greatest evils transcends modern political divisions.
