Hungary has reinstated a ban on Ukrainian agricultural imports after the country's newly elected government inadvertently allowed the restrictions to expire. The ban, which covers approximately 20 categories of agricultural goods, was originally introduced in 2023 under former Prime Minister Viktor Orban's administration as an emergency measure.

The restrictions were justified by claims that tariff-free Ukrainian imports were flooding border markets and undercutting Hungarian farmers. While transit shipments were permitted, direct imports for the Hungarian market remained blocked. The ban had persisted even after the European Union transitioned from a temporary tariff-free arrangement to a formal trade agreement with Ukraine last year.

When incoming Prime Minister Peter Magyar's Tisza party assumed power following their electoral victory over Orban's Fidesz party, the new government ended the state of emergency—inadvertently causing the trade restrictions tied to it to lapse. After pressure from Hungary's main farmers' association and reports that grain shipments had already entered the country, the government quickly moved to restore the ban through a new decree.

A government spokesperson characterized the lapse as a "legislative error," explaining that officials were reviewing nearly 1,000 inherited decrees and the import ban was "accidentally not taken into account." Agriculture Minister Szabolcs Bona described the situation as a "serious legislative trap for Hungarian farmers" and pledged the government would not allow imported products to threaten farmers' livelihoods.

The European Commission has maintained that such unilateral trade bans are illegal, arguing that trade policy falls under EU jurisdiction rather than individual member states. Poland and Slovakia have also maintained similar restrictions on Ukrainian agricultural imports despite objections from Brussels. The ban's reinstatement comes as Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has repeatedly criticized such protectionist measures, which complicate Ukraine's economic integration with Europe during the ongoing conflict.
