A troubling shift in modern conflict has emerged that should concern anyone who values the rule of law and national sovereignty. Israel has openly stated it will target not only current adversaries but also their yet-unchosen successors—essentially declaring war on future leadership itself, regardless of individual actions or accountability.

This represents what might be called "role-based targeting"—a departure from traditional rules of engagement where threats are neutralized based on specific actions by identifiable individuals. Instead, this doctrine treats an entire office as a perpetual target, stripping away the fundamental principle that punishment should be tied to individual culpability. Anyone who assumes certain leadership positions becomes fair game, irrespective of their personal conduct or decisions.

From a constitutional and human rights perspective, this raises serious concerns. The framers of our own system understood the importance of due process and individual accountability—principles that protect both citizens and leaders from arbitrary state violence. When nations begin targeting roles rather than actions, they undermine the very foundations of ordered liberty and national sovereignty that conservatives have long championed.

Proponents argue this is simply deterrence strategy—making leadership so dangerous that succession becomes impossible. Yet this logic, taken to its conclusion, suggests that destabilizing governance structures through targeted killing is an acceptable tool of statecraft. For those who believe in national sovereignty, constitutional order, and the rule of law as bulwarks against chaos, this precedent should be deeply troubling regardless of which nation employs it or against whom.
