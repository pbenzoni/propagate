Polish officials have formally objected to the Ukrainian city of Vinnytsia's decision to name a street after Stepan Bandera, a nationalist leader whose forces were responsible for the deaths of over 100,000 Polish civilians during World War II. The controversy highlights ongoing concerns about the memorialization of far-right historical figures in modern Ukraine.

Members of Poland's ruling party have urged Vinnytsia to reconsider the street naming, which they described as damaging to Polish-Ukrainian relations. "In Poland, Stepan Bandera and the legacy of the OUN-UPA are unequivocally associated with mass crimes against defenseless civilians," officials wrote in their formal request. The street was renamed in 2022 as part of Ukraine's broader effort to remove Russian-linked place names from public spaces.

The dispute has already had practical consequences. Vinnytsia recently withdrew a request for 15 used buses from the Polish city of Kielce after local officials and residents objected to the arrangement. The buses were intended to help shore up Vinnytsia's public transit network, which has faced disruptions due to infrastructure damage from the ongoing war.

Relations between the two nations have grown strained in recent months, particularly after Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky named a military commando unit after the "Heroes of the UPA"—the armed wing of Bandera's nationalist organization. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk has warned that Poland may increasingly prioritize its own interests if Ukraine does not address Polish concerns about the glorification of controversial historical figures.
