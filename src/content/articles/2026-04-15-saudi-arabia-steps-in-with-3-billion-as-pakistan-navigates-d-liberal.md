Saudi Arabia has pledged $3 billion in financial assistance to Pakistan, offering a lifeline as the South Asian nation grapples with a complex debt crisis exacerbated by climate change and geopolitical pressures. Pakistani Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb announced the commitment on Tuesday while attending the World Bank-IMF Spring Meetings in Washington, stating that disbursement is expected within days.

The Saudi support arrives at a critical moment for Pakistan, which must repay $3.5 billion to the United Arab Emirates this month. That UAE loan, originally extended in 2019 to help Pakistan weather a balance of payments crisis, has been rolled over multiple times with increasingly shorter extensions—signaling growing unease from Abu Dhabi about the arrangement. According to reports, Qatar is expected to replace the UAE as a financial partner going forward.

Pakistan's economic challenges stem from a confluence of crises: the aftermath of COVID-19 lockdowns, supply chain disruptions linked to the war in Ukraine, and devastating floods in 2022 that submerged a third of the country. The International Monetary Fund intervened in 2024 with a three-year, $7 billion package, but the agreement requires Pakistan to secure approximately $12.5 billion in loan rollovers from China, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE to meet external financing requirements.

The shifting dynamics reflect broader realignments in Gulf state relationships. While Pakistan has signed a mutual defense pact with Riyadh, the UAE has moved toward closer security cooperation with India and recently imposed visa restrictions affecting Pakistani nationals. These developments underscore how vulnerable nations navigate between great power interests while confronting the mounting costs of climate catastrophe and global economic instability.
