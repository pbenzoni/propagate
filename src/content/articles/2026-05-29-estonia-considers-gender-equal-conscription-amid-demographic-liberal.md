Estonia is evaluating the possibility of extending mandatory military service to women as the country confronts significant demographic challenges, according to statements from the head of the Defense Resources Agency. The shift reflects declining birth rates that have reduced the pool of eligible conscripts from approximately 15,000 annually in previous generations to just 4,000–5,000 today.

The potential policy change would align Estonia with several progressive European democracies that have already adopted gender-neutral conscription frameworks. Norway led the way in 2015 as the first NATO member to require military service from all citizens regardless of gender, followed by Sweden in 2017 and Denmark in 2024. Latvia has also announced plans to implement similar measures in coming years.

Estonia's demographic reality presents a mathematical challenge: the country projects it will need approximately 4,100 personnel to meet defense commitments by 2040, a target that cannot be sustained through male-only conscription given current birth rate trends. Defense officials emphasize that modernizing conscription policies to include women would not only address personnel shortfalls but also reflect contemporary values of equality and shared civic responsibility.

The broader context includes a wave of military policy updates across Europe in recent years, with multiple nations reassessing force structure, service requirements, and defense spending in response to evolving security environments.
