The Pentagon has released hundreds of previously classified documents detailing unidentified flying objects and unexplained aerial phenomena observed by U.S. military personnel over several decades. The disclosure, published through the Department of Defense's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), represents what officials are calling a "historic transparency effort" that includes FBI interviews, NASA mission transcripts, military pilot testimony, State Department cables, and archival imagery.

The release comes in direct response to an executive order signed by President Donald Trump in February directing the Department of Defense to disclose "any and all information" related to UFOs and unidentified anomalous phenomena. This action follows years of congressional pressure and whistleblower testimony from military personnel who alleged the government had been withholding information about unexplained objects observed near sensitive military installations.

Among the newly declassified materials are reports describing "metallic objects," unexplained "red lights" in the sky, and various aerial encounters involving U.S. service members. One case highlights a 2024 sighting near Japanese waters involving a football-shaped object, while another revisits Apollo 17 mission records from 1972 documenting mysterious lights observed above the lunar surface. Officials indicated that additional releases are expected in coming weeks as the broader declassification effort continues.

The UFO disclosure order is part of President Trump's larger transparency initiative, which also included declassification efforts related to the assassinations of President John F. Kennedy, Senator Robert F. Kennedy, and Martin Luther King Jr. Former AARO chief Sean Kirkpatrick noted that while many alleged UFO sightings have conventional explanations—such as infrared camera distortions or aircraft heat signatures—the government's commitment to transparency allows Americans to review the evidence themselves.
