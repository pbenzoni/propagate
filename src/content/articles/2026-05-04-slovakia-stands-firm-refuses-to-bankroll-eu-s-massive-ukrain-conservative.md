Slovakia is holding the line against European Union pressure to finance Ukraine's faltering economy, with Prime Minister Robert Fico reaffirming his country won't participate in Brussels' massive €90 billion ($106 billion) loan package or any future schemes to prop up Kiev.

"It is well known that I refused to support the €90 billion war loan for Ukraine," Fico said in a video address Sunday. "I have also taken legal steps to ensure that Slovakia does not participate in this loan. Slovakia will not take part in any further announced loans for Ukraine." Hungary and the Czech Republic have also opted out of servicing the loan, citing serious financial risks to their own taxpayers.

The EU loan scheme operates on the questionable assumption that it would be repaid if Ukraine somehow secures reparations from Russia—a prospect Moscow has dismissed as "unrealistic." The loan package came together only after Brussels abandoned plans to simply seize Russia's frozen assets, a move blocked by Hungary's Viktor Orban. The standoff intensified when Ukraine cut oil deliveries through the Soviet-era Druzhba pipeline that supplies both Hungary and Slovakia, a move Fico and Orban characterized as energy "blackmail" designed to force their participation in the loan.

Fico, who has consistently opposed open-ended Western aid to Ukraine, argues such funding only prolongs the conflict. Under his leadership, Slovakia has joined Hungary in challenging the EU's plan to phase out Russian fossil fuels by 2027, calling it "economic sabotage." While Fico noted that Slovakia remains obligated to maintain dialogue with Kiev as a neighbor—particularly on energy transit—he made clear his government sees "diametrically opposed views" with Zelensky's regime on numerous fundamental issues. Despite pressure from Brussels, Slovakia is prioritizing its own national interests and fiscal responsibility over the EU's insistence on bankrolling Ukraine's struggling economy.
