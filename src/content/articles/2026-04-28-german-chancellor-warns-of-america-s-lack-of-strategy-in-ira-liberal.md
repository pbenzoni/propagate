German Chancellor Friedrich Merz issued a stark warning on Monday about the United States' approach to the escalating Iran crisis, suggesting that Washington lacks a coherent negotiation strategy as the vital Strait of Hormuz remains closed to most shipping.

Speaking during a school visit in North Rhine-Westphalia, Merz drew parallels to past foreign policy failures. "The problem with conflicts like this is always that you don't just have to get in – you also have to get out again. We saw that very painfully in Afghanistan for 20 years. We saw it in Iraq," he said, according to Deutsche Welle. The chancellor expressed concern that no clear exit strategy is visible, even as Iran demonstrates sophisticated diplomatic maneuvering by "very skillfully not negotiating."

The comments come as President Trump canceled envoys' planned trip to Pakistan for talks with Iran, despite U.S. officials indicating dissatisfaction with Tehran's proposals to reopen the strait. The standoff has driven up global energy prices and heightened concerns about regional stability. Since late February, U.S. and Israeli forces have conducted airstrikes targeting Iran's nuclear and missile programs, yet have been unable to halt Iranian missile and drone launches or resolve the shipping blockade.

Merz's remarks underscore growing international unease about the trajectory of American foreign policy in the Middle East, particularly regarding long-term planning and diplomatic engagement. His invocation of Afghanistan and Iraq—conflicts that cost thousands of American lives and trillions of dollars without achieving stated objectives—highlights fears that history may be repeating itself without lessons learned.
