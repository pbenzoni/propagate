A fragile ceasefire in the Middle East and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz face serious threats from those opposed to diplomatic progress, according to Professor Seyed Mohammad Marandi of the University of Tehran. Speaking to media outlets this week, Marandi expressed concern that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu may work to undermine recent diplomatic efforts.

Marandi argued that the Trump administration has been compelled to engage with Iran's negotiating framework, but warned that the continuation of military operations in Lebanon suggests that commitments made during ceasefire negotiations are not being honored. "This means the commitments given by the United States are not being fulfilled. And that means that we could have a continuation of the crisis," he said.

The professor emphasized Iran's preference for normalized energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz, stating that Iran had been conducting routine business before recent escalations. However, he expressed skepticism about whether the ceasefire would hold, citing ongoing violations and the influence of hardline elements opposed to peace. Marandi also noted Iran's willingness to conduct trade in currencies other than the U.S. dollar, given existing sanctions, and mentioned the possibility of using Indian, Russian, or Chinese currencies in international commerce.
