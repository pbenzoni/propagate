President Donald Trump announced Sunday that U.S. negotiators will head to Pakistan on Monday for another round of talks with Iran, even as he warned Tehran that America's patience is wearing thin. The president made clear that if the regime refuses to accept what he called a "fair and reasonable deal," the United States is prepared to take decisive military action.

In a Truth Social post, Trump accused Iran of violating the temporary ceasefire—set to expire Wednesday—by firing on ships in the Strait of Hormuz. "Iran decided to fire bullets yesterday in the Strait of Hormuz – a total violation of our ceasefire agreement! That wasn't nice, was it?" the president wrote, noting that French and British vessels were reportedly targeted. He warned there would be "No more MR. Nice Guy" if Tehran continues to reject negotiations, threatening to "knock out every single power plant, and every single bridge, in Iran."

The first round of talks in Pakistan failed to produce results, with Iran's nuclear program remaining the central obstacle. Trump is demanding that Tehran dismantle its nuclear infrastructure and hand over enriched uranium—proposals the regime has flatly rejected. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian claimed Sunday that Trump has no justification to "deprive Iran of its nuclear rights," while Iranian media reported the regime will not return to talks unless the U.S. drops what it calls "excessive demands."

Meanwhile, Iran has ramped up its own aggressive posturing. The regime announced it was tightening control over the Strait of Hormuz and warned mariners the waterway was closed again. Iran's foreign ministry accused the United States of committing "war crimes" through its naval blockade of Iranian ports, while the country's army chief vowed to defend Iran's "independence" and "territorial integrity" if American strikes resume. Pakistani security sources suggest talks are likely before Friday, despite Tehran's public reluctance.
