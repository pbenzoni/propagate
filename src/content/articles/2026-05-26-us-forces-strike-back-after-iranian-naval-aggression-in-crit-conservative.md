US fighter jets conducted defensive strikes against Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) speedboats in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, as American forces worked to protect vital shipping lanes and counter Iranian attempts to mine international waters.

The engagement occurred in the narrowest section of the strait near Larak Island, a chokepoint through which nearly one-fifth of the world's oil supply passes. US Central Command confirmed that American forces "conducted self-defense strikes in southern Iran today to protect our troops from threats posed by Iranian forces." The military statement indicated targets included missile sites and Iranian vessels attempting to place mines in the waterway.

The clash comes as the strait remains under a dual blockade, with Iran having closed the shipping lane to vessels from what it deems "hostile countries" following US and Israeli airstrikes in late February. The Iranian regime had previously announced the closure in response to American enforcement of a blockade on Iranian ports—part of ongoing efforts to counter Tehran's destabilizing activities in the region.

Explosions were also reported near Bandar Abbas, home to an Iranian naval base. According to Iranian media, at least three Iranian sailors were killed in the engagement. The incident occurred even as US and Iranian negotiators arrived in Qatar hoping to advance stalled diplomatic talks, underscoring the fragility of the temporary truce reached in early April despite President Trump's warnings about resuming military pressure unless Iran meets American demands.
