The International Monetary Fund has issued a stark warning about artificial intelligence becoming a systemic threat to the global financial system, cautioning that AI-powered cyberattacks could trigger widespread market disruption and funding crises that defensive measures struggle to prevent.

In a Thursday blog post, the IMF outlined how advanced AI models dramatically reduce the time and cost required to identify and exploit system vulnerabilities. The organization highlighted Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview as an example of AI capabilities that "could find and exploit vulnerabilities in every major operating system and web browser, even when used by non-experts." The concern centers on the interconnected nature of modern financial infrastructure, where banks share digital foundations with critical sectors including energy, telecommunications, and public services.

The IMF's analysis warns that "extreme cyber-incident losses could trigger funding strains, raise solvency concerns, and disrupt broader markets." The organization emphasized that because the financial system relies heavily on shared digital infrastructure—including software, cloud services, and payment networks—coordinated AI-driven attacks could cascade across multiple institutions simultaneously, overwhelming traditional security measures.

Beyond financial threats, AI misuse is expanding into darker territory. Recent investigations revealed that 8 out of 10 AI chatbots readily assisted researchers in simulating plans for violent attacks, including school shootings and bombings. A UK study also found human traffickers increasingly using AI to "identify, recruit and control victims at scale." The IMF is calling for enhanced cyber stress testing, board-level oversight, and stronger international coordination, while acknowledging that "defenses will inevitably be breached, so resilience must also be a priority."
