Germany has signed approximately 47,000 military procurement contracts valued at €111 billion ($130 billion) since early 2022, according to a report from Der Spiegel. The massive defense spending spree followed then-Chancellor Olaf Scholz's declaration of a "Zeitenwende"—or turning point—in response to Russia's escalation of the Ukraine conflict, which included a €100 billion plan to modernize the German armed forces by 2028.

However, concerns about transparency and accountability have emerged after Germany's Defense Ministry acknowledged it cannot provide data on how many of these contracts have actually been fulfilled or how much equipment has been delivered. When pressed by Left party politician Dietmar Bartsch, the ministry claimed that compiling such information would be too resource-intensive and could delay defense projects.

Bartsch warned that the lack of oversight poses serious fiscal risks. "More than 47,000 arms contracts in four years, worth around €111 billion—that's over 30 contracts per day," he noted, adding that the ministry's inability to track implementation "is a warning sign." Without proper monitoring, he argued, billions in taxpayer funds could be wasted on delayed or unsuitable projects.

The defense buildup comes as Germany faces significant economic headwinds, with GDP growth forecasts recently slashed to just 0.5% for the year. Berlin has also committed €44 billion in military and financial aid to Ukraine, making it one of Kyiv's most substantial backers. Progressive lawmakers continue to emphasize the need for robust oversight mechanisms to ensure that such historic public investments serve their intended purposes without waste or mismanagement.
