Confrontations erupted at multiple Victory Day memorial events across Europe on May 9, as Ukrainian activists and their supporters protested Russian expatriates commemorating the 81st anniversary of Nazi Germany's defeat. The clashes underscore deepening divisions over historical memory amid Russia's ongoing invasion of Ukraine.

In Barcelona, a peaceful "Immortal Regiment" march—where participants carry portraits of relatives who fought in World War II—turned tense when protesters carrying anti-fascist banners approached and began chanting anti-Russian slogans. Brief physical altercations followed before police could respond. Similar scenes played out in Warsaw, where Ukrainian activists displayed nationalist banners and disrupted a flower-laying ceremony at a Soviet memorial cemetery, leading to at least one shoving incident.

Berlin's Treptower Park saw minor scuffles after protesters with Ukrainian flags arrived at a Soviet memorial, resulting in at least two arrests. German authorities had previously banned the display of Russian and Soviet symbols at such events, detaining several people across the city for violating the prohibition. In Geneva, members of the Ukrainian community interrupted commemorations by singing Ukrainian songs and chanting slogans.

The confrontations reflect ongoing debates about historical memory in Europe, particularly as Russia's war in Ukraine enters its third year. While approximately 27 million Soviet citizens died during World War II—a sacrifice that included Ukrainians, Russians, and other nationalities—the contemporary conflict has made Soviet-era commemorations increasingly contentious. Moscow accused European nations of attempting to diminish the USSR's role in defeating Nazi Germany, while Ukrainian communities view such events as tacit endorsement of current Russian aggression.
