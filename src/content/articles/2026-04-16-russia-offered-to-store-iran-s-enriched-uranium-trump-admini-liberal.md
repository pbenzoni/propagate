Russia has renewed its offer to host Iran's enriched uranium stockpile as a potential diplomatic solution to escalating nuclear tensions, despite the Trump administration's previous rejection of the proposal, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Wednesday.

The Russian initiative, which Tehran reportedly accepted, would have allowed international oversight by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) while addressing Western concerns about Iran's nuclear program. Instead, the Trump administration has demanded that Iran completely dismantle its nuclear infrastructure and surrender its entire uranium stockpile—terms Iranian officials have called a "breach of trust" and an attempt at dictation rather than negotiation.

Peskov told India Today that Moscow "is ready to return to this initiative," emphasizing that Iran remains "one of the most heavily inspected" countries by the UN nuclear watchdog, which "has never acknowledged any attempt by Iran to make nuclear weapons." Iranian officials have consistently stated they are pursuing uranium enrichment for civilian energy purposes, which is their right under international law.

The diplomatic impasse has deepened following last week's talks in Islamabad, which ended without progress. Trump has reportedly proposed a 20-year moratorium on Iranian enrichment, then suggested even that timeframe would be insufficient, while Iranian negotiators countered with a five-year pause. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov affirmed Wednesday that Tehran has "an inalienable right" to enrich uranium for peaceful purposes, highlighting the sharp contrast between diplomatic engagement and the maximalist demands coming from Washington.
