Southern Lebanon continues to face deadly airstrikes even after an official ceasefire was declared, raising urgent questions about the protection of civilian populations in conflict zones. Local officials report that 14 people were killed in a strike on the town of Burj al-Shamali on Thursday, including six women and six children who were sheltering in a residential area.

Witnesses described harrowing scenes of destruction, with entire families trapped beneath collapsed buildings as neighbors desperately searched through rubble for survivors. "I only heard the sound of the impact. I screamed," one survivor recalled. In another village, rescuers pulled a young girl alive from the debris of her family home, though she remained in shock from the trauma. Local officials characterized the attacks as a "massacre" and part of a pattern of military operations affecting the region.

The strikes came as Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced plans to expand military operations and enlarge a so-called "security zone" along the border, stating that forces were "seizing dominant terrain" aimed at pushing Hezbollah farther from northern Israel. While Israeli officials maintain that operations target Hezbollah positions, humanitarian groups warn of a mounting civilian toll. According to the Lebanese Health Ministry, more than 3,269 people have been killed and over 9,800 injured since the conflict escalated in early March.

The ongoing violence despite ceasefire arrangements underscores the fragility of diplomatic efforts and the devastating human cost borne by communities caught in the crossfire. Residential neighborhoods have sustained heavy damage, with families displaced and critical infrastructure destroyed, creating an expanding humanitarian crisis that demands international attention.
