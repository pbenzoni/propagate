Iran's sole operational nuclear power plant continues to function safely despite recent military exchanges between the United States and Iran, according to Aleksey Likhachev, CEO of Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom.

Russian personnel at the Bushehr facility have not observed attacks near the plant in recent days, and the situation remains "generally calm," Likhachev told the corporate newspaper Strana Rosatom on Friday. The plant's single operational reactor continues running at full capacity, he said. Located roughly 155 miles from the Strait of Hormuz, the facility holds substantial nuclear fuel stockpiles that experts warn could pose catastrophic regional risks if struck.

The update comes after the U.S. military launched strikes on Iranian targets near the strategic waterway earlier this week. Tehran accused Washington of violating a ceasefire agreement reached last month and responded with retaliatory action against American warships in the area. The escalation prompted Rosatom to drastically reduce its on-site workforce from approximately 700 specialists to just 20 people following strikes and explosions reported near the plant in March and April.

Construction work at the site is gradually resuming, with around 2,200 Iranian contractors returning to focus on two additional reactors that have been under development since 2016. The Bushehr plant, originally started by a West German company in the 1970s before being suspended after Iran's 1979 revolution, was revived by Rosatom in the 1990s and produces electricity for Iran's national power grid.
