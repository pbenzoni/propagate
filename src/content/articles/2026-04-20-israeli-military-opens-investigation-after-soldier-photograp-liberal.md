The Israel Defense Forces has launched an investigation after a soldier was photographed striking a statue of Jesus Christ with a hammer in southern Lebanon, an incident the military described as viewing "with great severity."

The image, which circulated widely on social media, shows the soldier damaging the statue located on the outskirts of Dibil, a Christian municipality near the Israeli border. In response, the IDF stated the soldier's conduct was "wholly inconsistent with the values expected of its troops" and said personnel are assisting the local community in restoring the statue.

The incident has intensified ongoing concerns about the protection of religious and civilian infrastructure during military operations. Israel has struck multiple Christian sites during recent campaigns, including a 2023 airstrike on a building adjacent to Gaza's oldest Christian shrine, the Greek Orthodox Church of Saint Porphyrius, which killed 18 people. Earlier this year, shrapnel from an Israeli tank shell damaged Gaza's only Catholic church, the Holy Family Church, killing three people.

The IDF emphasized it had "no intention of harming civilian infrastructure, including religious buildings or symbols," and said the Northern Command is conducting the investigation with appropriate measures to be taken based on findings. The official Dibil community page shared a photograph of the damaged statue alongside a biblical quote: "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."
