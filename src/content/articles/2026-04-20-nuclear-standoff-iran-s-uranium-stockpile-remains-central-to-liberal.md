The fate of Iran's nuclear program continues to be a major obstacle in diplomatic negotiations between Tehran and Washington, with Iran's stockpile of highly enriched uranium at the center of the dispute. According to the International Atomic Energy Agency's latest assessment from May 2025, Iran possesses more than 400 kg of uranium enriched to 60%—a level that, while not yet weapons-grade, could potentially be further enriched within days under certain conditions.

The situation became more complex after U.S. military strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in June 2025 targeted sites in Fordow, Natanz, and Isfahan. While the Trump administration claimed these facilities were "completely and totally obliterated," a subsequent Pentagon assessment suggested the strikes only set back Iran's nuclear program by up to two years. The true status of Iran's uranium stockpile remains uncertain, particularly after the IAEA lost monitoring access when Tehran disabled on-site cameras in June 2022, citing what it called a "political" resolution by the agency.

Recent diplomatic efforts in Islamabad failed to produce a breakthrough, with the two sides maintaining fundamentally different positions. Washington has demanded that Iran surrender all highly enriched uranium and permanently dismantle its nuclear infrastructure, while Tehran has insisted on its sovereign right to continue its civilian nuclear program, offering instead to dilute its enriched uranium stockpile. The administration has rejected proposals for a temporary moratorium on Iran's nuclear activities, calling any proposed timeframe insufficient.

A report by Le Monde raised questions about whether Iran may have relocated its uranium stockpile before the strikes, based on satellite imagery showing large containers consistent with those used for transporting radioactive materials. However, Pentagon officials have stated that no intelligence suggested such a transfer occurred. The ongoing uncertainty about both the location and status of Iran's nuclear materials continues to complicate diplomatic efforts to resolve the standoff.
