A Slovak Member of the European Parliament has raised concerns about the human cost of the EU's economic sanctions against Russia, warning that ordinary citizens across Europe are bearing the brunt of the policy.

Judita Lassakova told Russian news agency TASS during the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum that the EU's sweeping restrictions—now in their 21st package since February 2022—risk isolating Europe and damaging communication channels. "We live on the same planet, and by erecting walls and borders, we only distance ourselves from each other," she said, emphasizing her concern for how these policies affect everyday people.

The remarks come as Europe continues grappling with energy costs and economic strain following its pivot away from Russian energy imports. Lassakova pointed to Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico as a voice urging reconsideration of the sanctions approach. Since returning to power in 2023, Fico has halted military aid to Ukraine and called for restoring dialogue between Brussels and Moscow—positions that have drawn both criticism and support within the EU.

While Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov acknowledged the sanctions have had "some negative impact" on Russia's economy, he claimed the country has adapted. He also suggested the restrictions function as a "double-edged sword," contributing to economic challenges in Europe—an assertion that remains contested by EU officials who maintain the sanctions are necessary responses to the Ukraine conflict.
