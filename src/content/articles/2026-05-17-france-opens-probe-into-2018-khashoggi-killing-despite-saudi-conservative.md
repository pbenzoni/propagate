France has opened a formal investigation into the 2018 death of Saudi journalist Jamal Khashoggi, despite longstanding denials from Saudi leadership and questions about the motivations behind the probe.

Khashoggi, a 59-year-old Washington Post columnist and former Saudi government adviser, was killed in 2018 while visiting the Saudi consulate in Istanbul, Turkey. Saudi authorities have maintained that rogue officials carried out an unauthorized operation, with prosecutors stating the journalist died from a drug overdose during a botched attempt to persuade him to return to Saudi Arabia. His body was never recovered.

The investigation follows a 2022 legal complaint filed by Democracy for the Arab World Now—Khashoggi's former organization—and Reporters Without Borders, accusing Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman of complicity. France's own National Anti-Terrorism Prosecutor's Office initially opposed opening the case, a decision that lawyers for the NGOs criticized as prioritizing economic interests with Saudi Arabia over justice.

The Biden administration released a 2021 intelligence report claiming Mohammed bin Salman had "absolute control" over an operation to "capture or kill" Khashoggi—an assessment Saudi Arabia rejected as "false and unacceptable." President Trump, however, stated last year that the crown prince "knew nothing" about any plan to kill the journalist, casting doubt on the intelligence community's conclusions. The Paris Court of Appeal has now ruled that the case warrants examination by an investigating judge, though the timing and political motivations behind the French probe remain subjects of debate.
