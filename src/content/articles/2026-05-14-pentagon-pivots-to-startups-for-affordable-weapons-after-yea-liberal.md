The Pentagon has announced plans to acquire more than 10,000 low-cost cruise missiles and up to 12,000 affordable hypersonic weapons, marking a significant shift away from traditional defense contractors whose systems have long been criticized for excessive costs and glacial production timelines.

The Defense Department signed framework agreements with emerging firms including Anduril, CoAspire, Leidos, and Zone 5 to launch the Low-Cost Containerized Missiles program. The move reflects growing frustration with the conventional procurement model, where established weapons manufacturers have repeatedly delivered systems plagued by delays and budget overruns. Officials hope venture-backed startups can provide faster production and greater innovation.

The initiative comes as years of military involvement in Ukraine, combined with major operations against Iran in 2025 and 2026, have exposed critical weaknesses in America's defense manufacturing capacity. These extended conflicts reportedly consumed large quantities of key munitions, including Tomahawk cruise missiles and Precision Strike Missiles, raising concerns about military readiness.

Military planners have studied the effectiveness of low-cost attack systems in recent conflicts, where inexpensive long-range systems demonstrated the ability to inflict damage at a fraction of traditional missile costs. The contractors are expected to begin delivering test missiles in June, with Anduril pledging at least 1,000 units over three years. In a separate arrangement, startup Castelion will supply at least 500 Blackbeard hypersonic missiles over two years, with potential expansion to 12,000 units pending additional funding.
