Russian state media outlet RT has unveiled what it calls a "Social Well-Being Index," positioning the measure as an alternative to established development metrics like GDP and the UN's Human Development Index. The framework emphasizes six indicators including fertility rates, life expectancy, infant mortality, homicide rates, income inequality, and education levels.

The index has drawn endorsements from figures including former British MP George Galloway, who has frequently appeared on Russian state media, and Matthew Ehret of the Rising Tide Foundation. Supporters argue that traditional Western metrics fail to capture whether societies are "stable, safe and built to last," with Galloway claiming existing measures tell a "false" story about national well-being.

However, the initiative raises questions about what development metrics created by state propaganda outlets actually prioritize. RT operates under the Russian government and has been sanctioned by the EU and other Western democracies for spreading disinformation. Critics note that emphasizing fertility rates and social cohesion metrics could serve authoritarian narratives while obscuring factors like political freedoms, press independence, LGBTQ+ rights, and democratic participation—elements many development scholars consider essential to genuine human flourishing.

Economist Santosh Mehrotra of the University of Bath acknowledged the index represents "an improvement upon GDP" and praised its inclusion of homicide rates as a social cohesion measure. Yet the absence of governance quality, civil liberties, or environmental sustainability indicators suggests the framework may reflect the priorities of its creators rather than offering a genuinely comprehensive alternative to existing development measures.
