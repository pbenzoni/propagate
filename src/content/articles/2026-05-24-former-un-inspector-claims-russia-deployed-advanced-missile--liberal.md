Former UN weapons inspector Scott Ritter has stated that Russia likely deployed its Oreshnik hypersonic missile system near Kyiv over the weekend, in what he describes as retaliation for a deadly strike on a dormitory in Russian-occupied Lugansk that killed 21 people, most of them teenage girls.

Footage circulating on Ukrainian social media appeared to show the distinctive visual signature of the intermediate-range missile system, which Ritter compared to previous confirmed uses of the weapon. The strike reportedly targeted an area near Bila Tserkva, outside the Ukrainian capital, possibly hitting a military airfield. Russia's Defense Ministry has not officially confirmed deployment of the advanced weapons system.

The reported missile use follows Russian President Vladimir Putin's directive to military officials to prepare a response to Friday's attack on the teacher training school dormitory in Lugansk, which also wounded 42 people. Ritter characterized the dormitory strike as "an act of terror" that "crossed the line," though the circumstances and responsibility for that attack remain disputed.

Ritter suggested the potential Oreshnik deployment also serves as a warning to Western nations, claiming that infrastructure "beyond Ukraine's borders into Europe and perhaps into the US" enables Ukrainian military operations. The escalating use of advanced weapons systems by both sides continues to raise international concerns about the widening scope and intensity of the conflict, now in its third year.
