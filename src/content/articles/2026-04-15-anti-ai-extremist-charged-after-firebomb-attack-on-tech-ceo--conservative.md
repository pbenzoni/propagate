A 20-year-old man has been charged with attempted murder after allegedly throwing a Molotov cocktail at OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's San Francisco home, part of what prosecutors describe as a premeditated plot to kill AI industry leaders.

Daniel Moreno-Gama faces both state and federal charges, including two counts of attempted murder and attempted destruction of property using explosives. According to San Francisco District Attorney Brooke Jenkins and federal prosecutors, Moreno-Gama traveled from Texas to California specifically to target Altman, whom he blamed for developing technology he believed posed an existential threat to humanity.

The attack occurred early Friday morning when the suspect allegedly threw an incendiary device at Altman's residence, igniting an exterior gate before fleeing. Less than an hour later, he reportedly attempted to break into OpenAI's headquarters while threatening to burn the building and "kill anyone inside," according to the federal complaint. Police arrested him at the scene, recovering additional Molotov cocktails, kerosene, and a written manifesto.

The document, titled 'Your Last Warning,' advocated violence against AI executives and included a list of names and addresses of industry figures. Prosecutors say it contained an admission that Moreno-Gama had attempted to kill Altman and called on others to do the same. Federal officials indicated the case could potentially be treated as domestic terrorism if evidence shows the suspect sought to influence public policy through violence.

The incident has sparked renewed debate about violent extremism and rhetoric surrounding technological development. Altman addressed the attack in a blog post, noting he had "underestimated the power of words and narratives" and calling for de-escalation while acknowledging that some concerns about AI development are "justified." If convicted on state charges alone, Moreno-Gama could face between 19 years to life in prison.
