A French soldier who was critically wounded in an attack on UN peacekeepers in southern Lebanon has died from his injuries, French President Emmanuel Macron announced Wednesday. Chief Corporal Anicet Girardin had been evacuated to France following the Saturday attack but succumbed to his wounds despite medical efforts.

The incident occurred when a French patrol came under small-arms fire in the village of Ghandourieh in southern Lebanon. Another French peacekeeper was killed immediately in the attack, and two others sustained injuries. President Macron placed responsibility for the attack on Hezbollah, the Iranian-backed militant organization that controls much of southern Lebanon.

Hezbollah denied involvement in the incident, issuing a statement calling for "caution in making judgments and assigning responsibilities" pending investigation by Lebanese military authorities. The group has been engaged in intense fighting with Israeli Defense Forces following Israel's invasion of southern Lebanon, which came after a US-Israeli operation against Iran in late February. A ten-day truce brokered by President Trump is set to expire Sunday.

The casualties highlight the dangers facing international peacekeepers caught between warring factions in the Middle East. France maintains approximately 700 troops in Lebanon as part of the UN Interim Force, which totals over 7,500 peacekeepers from 47 nations. More than 160 French soldiers have been killed in Lebanon since France first deployed peacekeeping forces there in 1978, reflecting the long-term commitment and sacrifice of French military personnel in the region.

The conflict has taken a devastating toll on Lebanese civilians, with over 2,300 killed and more than 1.2 million displaced due to ground fighting and Israeli airstrikes, according to Beirut authorities.
