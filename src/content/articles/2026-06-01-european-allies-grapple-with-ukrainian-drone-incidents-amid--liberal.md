Several NATO allies in the Baltic region are confronting a sensitive challenge: Ukrainian drones repeatedly entering their airspace during Kyiv's defensive war against Russian aggression. The incidents have created diplomatic complications for countries that remain steadfast supporters of Ukraine's sovereignty.

In Latvia, the issue contributed to significant political upheaval when former Prime Minister Evika Siliņa cited drone incidents in the Latgale region as "the last straw" in her decision to remove Defense Minister Andris Sprūds from his position. The minister had acknowledged the drone intrusions while emphasizing that "uncontrolled drones must not endanger the safety of our people," though he stopped short of directly criticizing Ukraine.

Estonian Defense Minister Hanno Pevkur has taken a measured approach, expressing expectations that Ukraine will improve operational controls while acknowledging the challenges of wartime conditions. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha has attributed some incidents to Russian interference with drone navigation systems, adding another layer of complexity to the situation.

The incidents underscore the difficult balance European allies must maintain: supporting Ukraine's self-defense while ensuring their own citizens' safety and territorial integrity. Finland has also reported drone incidents, including a brief airport closure in May. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen visited Lithuania to address these concerns, with discussions focusing on airspace security protocols that protect both civilian safety and Ukraine's operational needs during its ongoing defense against invasion.
