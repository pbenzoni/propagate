Hundreds of Bolivians flooded the streets of La Paz this week to protest worsening economic conditions under President Rodrigo Paz, a conservative who took office just six months ago. The demonstrations—driven by farmers, miners, teachers, laborers, and Indigenous activists—have escalated as residents grapple with fuel shortages, rising living costs, and stagnant wages following the administration's elimination of longstanding fuel subsidies.

Paz, a member of the centrist-right Christian Democratic Party, scrapped the decades-old subsidies that had provided crucial support to working families, citing drained foreign reserves. However, his administration has struggled to stabilize fuel supplies, intensifying public anger. Protesters have also raised alarms about potential privatization of state-owned enterprises, which many view as a threat to Bolivia's economic sovereignty.

Supporters of former President Evo Morales, the country's first Indigenous leader who was ousted in 2019 under disputed circumstances, joined the demonstrations. Feliciano Vegamonte, leader of the Confederation of Peasant Workers, voiced concerns about foreign influence in Bolivian affairs, stating that "the US gives Rodrigo Paz instructions" and criticizing the government's response to Indigenous communities.

Video from the protests showed clashes between demonstrators and riot police, who deployed tear gas as protesters threw projectiles near government buildings. Local media reported more than 100 arrests during Monday's unrest. The turmoil reflects Bolivia's ongoing political instability since the 2019 crisis that removed Morales from power, raising questions about democratic governance and economic justice in the South American nation.
