SpaceX's massive Starship rocket completed a successful test flight Friday evening, achieving critical objectives for future space exploration before ending in a dramatic but anticipated explosion after splashdown in the Indian Ocean.

The spacecraft lifted off from the company's Texas facility around 6:30pm ET and flew for approximately 66 minutes, successfully deploying test satellites and evaluating new heat-shield technology designed to protect the vehicle during atmospheric reentry. The upper-stage Starship reached its planned splashdown zone before tipping over and bursting into flames—an outcome engineers had expected, as the vehicle was not designed for ocean recovery.

Despite at least one engine malfunction during flight, the spacecraft met its primary mission goals, demonstrating progress toward SpaceX's role in NASA's Artemis program to return humans to the Moon. The company's iterative testing approach treats explosions and failures as learning opportunities in its rapid development cycle.

The latest test used an upgraded Starship version that SpaceX envisions as a reusable transport system for satellites, cargo, and eventually human crews traveling to lunar and Martian destinations. The Super Heavy booster, which separated earlier in the flight, performed a controlled splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico, though only a portion of its engines successfully reignited for the return burn.
