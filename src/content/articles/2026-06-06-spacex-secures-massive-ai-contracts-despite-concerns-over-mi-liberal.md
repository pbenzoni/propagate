SpaceX has entered into major agreements with AI companies Google and Anthropic, securing approximately $75 billion in combined contracts for AI computing infrastructure through 2029. According to SEC filings, Google will pay $920 million monthly through June 2029—nearly $30 billion total—for access to GPU capacity, while Anthropic committed to a similar arrangement worth roughly $45 billion over three years.

The deals allow SpaceX to monetize unused computing capacity from its data centers while maintaining flexibility for internal operations. Google characterized the partnership as supporting its Gemini Enterprise AI platform, describing SpaceX as a longtime cloud partner. These agreements have become central to SpaceX's upcoming $1.77 trillion public offering, particularly as the merged entity—which now includes Elon Musk's X and xAI—reported significant losses of $4.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

However, the arrangements emerge amid growing concerns about AI technology's military applications. Reports indicate that Anthropic's Claude AI was deployed during a controversial operation involving Venezuela's leadership, despite the company's previous public resistance to defense contracts. Additionally, Palantir software utilizing Anthropic's AI workflows was reportedly involved in targeting decisions that mistakenly identified a girls' elementary school in Iran as a valid military target.

The Pentagon has formalized partnerships with major tech companies—including SpaceX, Google, and Anthropic—to integrate AI systems into classified military networks. These systems are intended to support "warfighter decision-making," raising questions about oversight, accountability, and the ethical implications of AI in military contexts. The developments underscore ongoing tensions between Silicon Valley's commercial ambitions and the responsible deployment of powerful AI technologies.
