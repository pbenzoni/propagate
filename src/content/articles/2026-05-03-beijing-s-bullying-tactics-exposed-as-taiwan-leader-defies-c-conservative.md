In a stark display of communist China's growing aggression, Beijing launched a verbal assault on Taiwan's President Lai Ching-te after he successfully completed a diplomatic visit to Eswatini, calling him a "rat crossing the street." The inflammatory rhetoric from China's Taiwan Affairs Office came after the democratic island nation's leader refused to be intimidated by Beijing's pressure campaign.

Lai's visit to Eswatini—the only African nation that still recognizes Taiwan's sovereignty—nearly didn't happen. Three countries (Seychelles, Mauritius, and Madagascar) revoked overflight permits for the Taiwanese leader's aircraft at the last minute, a move Taipei attributed to Chinese Communist Party interference. Rather than surrender to Beijing's strong-arm tactics, Lai boarded an Eswatini government plane to complete the journey to the landlocked nation of 1.3 million people, Taiwan's sole remaining African ally.

China's Taiwan Affairs Office didn't stop at name-calling. The communist regime accused Lai of being a "troublemaker" and claimed he abandoned Taiwan's residents following a significant earthquake to make the trip. Lai pushed back against the authoritarian regime's intimidation, writing that Taiwan "will never be deterred by external pressures" and would "continue to engage with the world—no matter the challenges faced."

The incident highlights Beijing's increasingly aggressive posture toward the democratic island. While China considers Taiwan part of its territory and claims to seek "peaceful reunification," the communist government signaled in 2022 that it "would not renounce the use of force" to absorb the self-governing democracy. Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council dismissed Beijing's threats as "fishwife's gutter talk," underscoring the democratic island's determination to resist communist intimidation and maintain its international relationships.
