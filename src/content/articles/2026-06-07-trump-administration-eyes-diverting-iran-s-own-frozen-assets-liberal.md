The Trump administration is considering redirecting frozen Iranian assets to repair infrastructure in Gulf Arab states that has been damaged during recent regional military escalations, according to reports from Bloomberg and CBS News. The Treasury Department plans to use "all available authorities" to make Iranian assets accessible for rebuilding efforts, particularly for damage attributed to retaliatory strikes.

The initiative has emerged as indirect talks between Washington and Tehran have reached an impasse. Iran is demanding access to billions in its own assets—money frozen under U.S. sanctions—as a precondition for any lasting agreement. Iranian officials have called for at least $12 billion to be released immediately upon signing any memorandum of understanding, with the remainder following within one to two months. "This is our own money, not America's money," said Mohsen Rezaei, a military adviser to Iran's Supreme Leader, framing the demand as a test of trust.

The regional conflict has intensified since late February, when the U.S. and Israel launched attacks on Iran. In mid-March, Israel struck the world's largest natural gas field at South Pars, disrupting 12% of Iran's gas production. Tehran subsequently declared that energy facilities in Gulf states hosting U.S. forces had become "direct and legitimate targets," leading to damage across Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, and Bahrain before a Pakistan-brokered ceasefire took effect in April.

The proposal to use Iranian assets for Gulf reconstruction adds another layer of complexity to already fraught negotiations. While Washington reportedly offered to unblock some funds to bring Iran to talks in Islamabad, it has publicly rejected Tehran's demands as unacceptable—even as Iran insists that access to its own frozen money is a baseline requirement for any diplomatic progress.
