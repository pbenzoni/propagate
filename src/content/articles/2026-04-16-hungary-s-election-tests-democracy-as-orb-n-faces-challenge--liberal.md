Hungarian voters will decide the future direction of their country on April 12, in an election that has drawn international attention and raised concerns about democratic integrity. Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, who has held power since 2010, faces his most serious electoral challenge in years from Péter Magyar, a former Fidesz party insider turned opposition leader.

Orbán's tenure has been marked by growing tensions with the European Union over his government's curtailing of LGBTQ rights, restrictions on asylum seekers, and opposition to supporting Ukraine against Russian aggression. His administration has repeatedly blocked EU sanctions on Russia and currently opposes a major aid package for Kyiv, prioritizing Hungary's continued access to Russian energy. Critics characterize his economic program, dubbed "Orbánomics," as a nationalist approach that has enriched loyalists while isolating Hungary from Western democratic norms.

Magyar, running under the Tisza party banner, has positioned himself as a reformist who would repair Hungary's fractured relationship with Brussels—a reconciliation his ambitious public spending plans depend upon, as nearly €20 billion in EU funds remain frozen due to rule-of-law concerns. However, Magyar's campaign has been shadowed by domestic abuse allegations from his ex-wife, former Justice Minister Judit Varga, raising questions about his personal conduct even as he testified about alleged corruption within Orbán's government.

Polling has been contentious and contradictory. While EU-funded surveys show Tisza leading by significant margins, pro-government pollsters paint a different picture. Orbán allies claim this disparity is intentional, designed to delegitimize any Fidesz victory by framing it as fraudulent. The competing narratives reflect deeper concerns about foreign influence and disinformation in a contest that will determine whether Hungary moves toward greater alignment with European democratic values or continues its current authoritarian trajectory.
