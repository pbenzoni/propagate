The Lebanese Health Ministry has accused Israeli Defense Forces of deliberately targeting medical personnel during ongoing military operations in the country, raising questions about compliance with a US-brokered ceasefire that took effect in mid-April.

According to Lebanon's health ministry, 51 people were killed in the latest wave of Israeli strikes, including two medics. The ministry stated that Israeli forces "directly targeted two points of the Health Authority in Qalawiya and Tibnin" in separate raids. Since Israel launched its military operation against Hezbollah on March 2—shortly after joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran—Lebanese authorities report 2,846 total fatalities, with more than 550 occurring after the ceasefire was implemented.

The IDF maintains it targeted more than 20 Hezbollah facilities in southern Lebanon, including weapons depots, command centers, and "buildings used for military purposes." Israel has consistently stated that civilian infrastructure, including medical facilities, becomes legitimate military targets when used by militant groups like Hezbollah and Hamas—a position it has defended throughout the Gaza conflict as well.

Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam reported that 86 villages in southern Lebanon remain under Israeli occupation and described the city of Bint Jbeil as resembling Gaza due to extensive destruction. Salam indicated Lebanon remains open to a peace agreement once key terms, including the withdrawal of Israeli troops, are fulfilled. The situation highlights ongoing tensions in the region despite international mediation efforts to establish a lasting ceasefire.
