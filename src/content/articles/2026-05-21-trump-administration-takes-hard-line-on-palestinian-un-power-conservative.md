The Trump administration is standing firm against Palestinian efforts to expand their diplomatic footprint at the United Nations, with the State Department reportedly warning Palestinian UN envoy Riyad Mansour that his bid for a senior General Assembly position could jeopardize ongoing peace efforts in Gaza.

According to reports citing an internal State Department cable, U.S. diplomats have been instructed to convey to Mansour that his candidacy for a vice-presidential seat in the General Assembly "fuels tensions" and risks undermining President Trump's comprehensive Gaza peace plan. The administration has made clear there could be consequences—including potential visa revocations for Palestinian diplomatic delegations—if the bid moves forward.

The dispute highlights the Palestinian Authority's ongoing campaign to elevate its status at the UN despite holding only non-member observer status without voting rights in the 193-member General Assembly. While the Assembly previously backed Palestinian full membership in 2024, any upgrade requires Security Council approval, where the United States has consistently exercised its veto power to block such measures.

The Trump administration has also reminded Palestinian officials that progress on recovering tax and customs revenues currently withheld by Israel—funds that comprise much of the Palestinian Authority's budget—depends on their willingness to "engage in good faith" with diplomatic efforts. These revenues have been largely frozen since the October 2023 Hamas terrorist attack on southern Israel that triggered the current conflict.

Mansour had already withdrawn an earlier bid to become president of the General Assembly following U.S. pressure, according to reports. The General Assembly is scheduled to elect its next president and 16 vice-presidents on June 2, setting up a potential diplomatic showdown if the Palestinian envoy refuses to step aside.
