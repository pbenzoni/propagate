President Donald Trump offered a characteristically candid tribute to outgoing Apple CEO Tim Cook, recalling how the tech executive would reach out directly to resolve major business challenges during Trump's first term. Cook announced Monday he will step down as CEO in September to become executive chairman, with hardware chief John Ternus taking the helm.

Trump praised Cook's no-nonsense approach to problem-solving, noting the CEO would call the White House directly rather than relying on costly Washington consultants. "He makes these calls to me, I help him out (but not always, because he will, on occasion, be too aggressive in his ask!), and he gets the job done, QUICKLY, without a dime being given to those very expensive (millions of dollars!) consultants around town who sometimes get it done, and sometimes don't," Trump wrote on social media Tuesday.

The relationship between the two leaders wasn't always smooth. Trump has consistently pressed Apple to bring manufacturing jobs back to American soil and publicly criticized the company's diversity initiatives, calling on Cook to abandon DEI programs he described as "a hoax that has been very bad" for the country. The president also expressed concerns about Apple's plans to expand production in India rather than investing more heavily in domestic manufacturing.

According to reports, Cook's direct lobbying efforts proved effective during the US-China trade conflict in early 2025, when Apple successfully secured tariff exemptions that other major firms couldn't obtain. Despite their occasional disagreements, Trump called Cook "an incredible guy" who understood how to work with the administration to achieve results for his company.
