The Senate voted 50-47 on Tuesday to advance a largely symbolic resolution that would restrict President Trump's authority to conduct military operations against Iran without additional congressional approval. The measure, which has failed seven previous times, remains unlikely to become law.

Forty-three Republican senators stood firm against the resolution, which critics say would hamstring the President's constitutional authority as Commander-in-Chief during ongoing national security operations. Only four Republicans—Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, Rand Paul of Kentucky, Susan Collins of Maine, and Lisa Murkowski of Alaska—joined Senate Democrats in supporting the measure. Notably, Senator John Fetterman of Pennsylvania was the sole Democrat to vote against it.

Even if the resolution passes the Senate, it faces steep odds in the Republican-controlled House and would almost certainly be vetoed by President Trump. Democrats have acknowledged the measure is largely symbolic but argue it serves to put Republicans on record regarding the Iran conflict.

The resolution invokes the 1973 War Powers Resolution, which requires presidents to obtain congressional authorization within 60 days of introducing forces into hostilities. President Trump has maintained that the "little excursion" against Iran has effectively ended, pointing to the fragile ceasefire in place since early April. The administration argues the ceasefire may pause the 60-day clock, though some lawmakers have questioned that interpretation.

Critics of the resolution argue that Congress is attempting to second-guess military and diplomatic strategy during delicate negotiations, potentially undermining American credibility and the President's ability to respond to threats in real time.
