Republican Senator Lindsey Graham is sounding the alarm on a U.S.-brokered ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon, warning that the agreement could inadvertently strengthen the Iran-backed terrorist organization Hezbollah rather than weaken it.

The 10-day truce, which took effect Friday, aims to halt Israel's military operations in Lebanon that began after Hezbollah fired rockets at Israeli territory in support of Iran. Graham emphasized that while diplomatic efforts toward peace are valuable, they cannot come at the expense of Israel's national security or its ability to defend itself against terrorist threats.

"The last thing we need to do in the name of peace is throw Hezbollah a lifeline by restricting Israel's ability to attack this terrorist organization," Graham stated on X. The senator expressed particular concern about Lebanon's military capacity, noting the Lebanese army has been "woefully inadequate in credibly disarming Hezbollah."

According to the State Department, Israel has agreed to halt offensive operations against Lebanese targets while maintaining its inherent right to self-defense. The deal also requires the Lebanese government to prevent Hezbollah from attacking Israeli targets. However, Hezbollah—which was not involved in the negotiations—is demanding all Israeli Defense Forces activities in Lebanon cease entirely.

Graham made clear his position: "I will not support any agreement regarding peace in Lebanon that fails to convince me that it will result in the disarmament and demise of Hezbollah as a terrorist organization." His concerns reflect broader questions about whether diplomatic agreements adequately address the threat posed by Iranian proxies in the region.
