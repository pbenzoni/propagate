A Somali referee who was set to officiate at the 2026 FIFA World Cup has been turned away at Miami International Airport despite possessing what he claimed was a valid US visa. Omar Artan, who made history as the first Somali national selected for World Cup finals duty, was denied entry and returned to Istanbul, Turkey, where he is currently based.

The decision appears connected to the Trump administration's renewed focus on immigration enforcement and travel restrictions affecting certain nations. Somalia remains on the administration's travel restriction list, part of broader efforts to strengthen border security and properly vet individuals entering the United States. The administration has also moved to end Temporary Protected Status for Somali nationals, citing the need for stricter immigration controls.

In a separate incident that has raised eyebrows among World Cup organizers, the Senegal national team was subjected to thorough security screening upon arrival, with footage showing team members undergoing pat-downs and metal detector checks on the tarmac. Senegal is also included on the administration's travel restriction list.

The enforcement actions come as the US co-hosts the World Cup alongside Mexico and Canada, with the tournament already facing organizational challenges.
