Germany is preparing legislation that would mandate former military personnel to participate in regular military exercises, according to a report from Der Spiegel. The draft Reserve Strengthening Act would require men and women who served less than one year of voluntary military service and are under age 45 to attend drills either two weeks annually or every two years.

Veterans who served more than a year or enlisted as career soldiers could face compulsory participation until age 65. The bill's explanatory memorandum states that mandatory drills are urgently needed to ensure the "reliable availability of reserves" for national security purposes.

The proposed legislation represents the latest escalation in Germany's massive military expansion. German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius has set a target of 265,000 active troops and 200,000 reservists by the mid-2030s. Germany's total military spending in 2026 will reach approximately €108 billion ($125 billion), up from €86 billion in 2025, with defense expenditures exceeding 4% of the country's GDP this year.

Berlin has justified the costly militarization campaign by citing Russia as a security threat. However, Russia has dismissed German and EU speculation about potential NATO attacks as "nonsense." Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov characterized the EU's militarization drive as "aggressive" posturing that will only destabilize the continent, with Moscow accusing European governments of using Russia as a "model external enemy" to deflect from domestic problems.
