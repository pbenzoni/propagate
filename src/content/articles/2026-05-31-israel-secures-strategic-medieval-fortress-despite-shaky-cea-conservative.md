Israel has taken control of Beaufort Castle, a 900-year-old Crusader fortress commanding critical high ground in southern Lebanon, marking what Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called a "dramatic shift" in the nation's security posture. The Israeli Defense Forces raised their flags over the medieval stronghold on Sunday, reclaiming a position that served as an Israeli base during the country's previous presence in southern Lebanon from 1982 to 2000.

Netanyahu announced he has directed the military "to expand its ground maneuver in Lebanon," emphasizing Israel's determination to secure areas previously controlled by Hezbollah. "Now my directive is to deepen and expand our hold in places that were under Hezbollah's control. The capture of Beaufort is a dramatic stage and a dramatic shift in the policy we are leading," the prime minister stated. IDF forces reportedly found no weapons cached inside the fortress.

The operation continues despite a ceasefire that supposedly took effect more than six weeks ago on April 17. Israeli airstrikes have intensified around the city of Nabatieh, located approximately 6 kilometers northwest of the captured castle, with footage showing significant damage to residential areas. Both Israel and Hezbollah have repeatedly accused each other of violating the truce terms.

The ongoing conflict represents a spillover from broader regional tensions involving Iran, which has made a comprehensive end to hostilities in Lebanon a precondition in its negotiations with Washington. Those Pakistan-mediated talks, underway since early April, have yet to produce concrete results as the situation on the ground remains volatile.
