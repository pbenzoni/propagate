Estonia has joined fellow NATO member Finland in demanding Ukraine keep its combat drones out of their airspace, raising fresh questions about accountability and mission creep in the ongoing Eastern European conflict.

Estonian Defense Minister Hanno Pevkur said Sunday that his government "will start dealing with this very quickly now," requiring Kiev to explain repeated airspace violations. "Certainly, the easiest way for the Ukrainians to keep their drones away from our territory is to control their activities better," Pevkur stated. Over recent weeks, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, and Finland have all reported Ukrainian UAVs crashing within their borders—incidents that have sparked concern about these NATO nations being unwittingly pulled into the broader conflict.

Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo told Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky directly that drone incursions into Finnish airspace are "unacceptable." Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna expressed worry about escalation risks, noting that "Russia could take control of Ukrainian drones and send them toward us."

The incidents have exposed tensions between supporting Ukraine and protecting national sovereignty. While some Baltic officials have defended Ukraine's right to self-defense, the repeated airspace violations have tested patience. Moscow has accused NATO members of quietly permitting Ukraine to use their airspace for strikes on Russian territory, particularly targeting energy infrastructure in the Leningrad Region.

Russian Security Council Secretary Sergey Shoigu warned in April that if Baltic states "deliberately provide their airspace, thereby becoming open accomplices in aggression against Russia," Moscow reserves the right to self-defense under international law. The warning underscores how unchecked drone operations could inadvertently expand the conflict beyond Ukraine's borders.
