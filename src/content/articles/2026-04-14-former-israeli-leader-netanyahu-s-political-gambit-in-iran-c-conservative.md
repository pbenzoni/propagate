A fundamental disconnect between American and Israeli interests during the recent Iran conflict has come to light, according to former acting Israeli President Avraham Burg. In an interview, Burg outlined how President Trump's focus on wrapping up the military campaign before U.S. midterm elections clashed with Prime Minister Netanyahu's domestic political calculus.

Burg, a former speaker of the Knesset, explained that Netanyahu sought to prolong the conflict to boost his standing ahead of Israel's October elections, banking on the traditional rally-around-the-flag effect during wartime. "Israelis, when there is a state of war, usually rally around the flag of the government," Burg noted. However, he argued this strategy ultimately failed: "As for now, Netanyahu did not profit a thing politically from this campaign – he may have lost a lot."

The former Israeli leader predicted voters would reject what he characterized as Netanyahu's political maneuvering, suggesting "the next elections will show that Israelis are fed up with his manipulation, his tricks, his shticks, and his endurance." Burg also disputed the premise that Iran posed an existential threat to Israel, arguing the situation "could have been dealt with by diplomacy, by agreements, by different equilibrium and balances."

Regarding the ceasefire, Burg described Trump's unilateral decision as effectively divorcing American policy from Israeli preferences. He assessed that Iran emerged as the conflict's winner, having "got hit very strong but survived, and therefore it won" – a conclusion that underscores the complex strategic landscape facing both American and Israeli policymakers.
