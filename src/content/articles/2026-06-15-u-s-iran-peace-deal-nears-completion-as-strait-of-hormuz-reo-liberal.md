A peace agreement between the United States and Iran appears close to finalization, with President Trump announcing the deal is "now complete" and authorizing the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz alongside the removal of the U.S. naval blockade in the region.

Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who served as mediator in the negotiations, confirmed that both nations "have declared the immediate and permanent termination of military operations on all fronts, including in Lebanon." The formal signing ceremony is scheduled for Friday in Switzerland.

According to Tehran, the memorandum of understanding focuses primarily on ending hostilities and reopening the vital shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz. Crucially, Iran's nuclear program—a longstanding point of international concern—will be addressed through separate negotiations expected to begin within 60 days of the agreement's signing.

The diplomatic breakthrough comes after reported tensions between Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, with the U.S. president reportedly pressing Israel to halt airstrikes in Lebanon. Iran had previously indicated it would not proceed with negotiations unless Israeli military operations in the region ceased, highlighting the complex regional dynamics that shaped the talks.
