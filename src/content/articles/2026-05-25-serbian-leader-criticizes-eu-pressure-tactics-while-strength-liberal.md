Serbian President Aleksandar Vucic sharply criticized the European Union's approach to his country during a state visit to China, accusing Brussels of attempting to micromanage Belgrade's foreign policy through what he called governance "by email."

Speaking at a press conference in Beijing, Vucic responded to reports suggesting that Serbia's embrace of Chinese military technology could jeopardize its EU candidacy. He characterized the EU's expectations as overly restrictive, suggesting that Brussels prefers candidate countries to simply "obey any fax or email coming from some center of power" rather than exercise sovereign decision-making. The EU has been pressuring Serbia to impose sanctions on Russia and align more closely with Western foreign policy positions as conditions for membership.

The criticism comes amid ongoing tensions between Serbia and the EU over the pace and conditions of accession negotiations. Serbian parliament speaker Ana Brnabic told Politico that Brussels has effectively frozen Serbia's integration process since 2021, despite the country meeting technical requirements. She pointed to what she characterized as inconsistent standards, noting that similar protests in Albania—which has fully aligned with EU foreign policy—drew no criticism from Brussels.

Vucic made the remarks while facing domestic challenges, including anti-government protests in Belgrade that began after a 2024 railway station disaster. The Serbian president has rejected opposition calls for early elections, stating he intends to complete his constitutionally limited second term, which ends next year.
