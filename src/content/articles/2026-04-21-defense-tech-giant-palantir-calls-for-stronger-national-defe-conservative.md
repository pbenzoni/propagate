Defense technology company Palantir released a bold 22-point manifesto over the weekend, calling for a "new era" of AI-enabled American military supremacy and sparking intense debate across the political spectrum. The document, posted on X Saturday, goes beyond typical corporate mission statements to outline positions on technology's role in preserving national security.

The manifesto declares that "Silicon Valley has an affirmative obligation to participate in the defense of the nation" and argues that "hard power in this century will be built on software." It also calls for universal national service and suggests reversing the "postwar neutering of Germany and Japan." Critics on the left immediately labeled the document a blueprint for what they call "technofascism," though supporters see it as a much-needed wake-up call about America's strategic position.

Palantir, valued at approximately $352 billion, specializes in data analytics and artificial intelligence systems primarily serving defense and intelligence agencies. The company was founded in 2003 by PayPal co-founder Peter Thiel and others, with early investment from the CIA's venture capital arm, In-Q-Tel. Former CIA Director George Tenet reportedly told Forbes in 2013: "I wish I had Palantir when I was director. I wish we had the tool of its power."

The company's flagship product, an operating system called 'Gotham,' integrates existing intelligence data from multiple agencies to support military operations and law enforcement. Palantir maintains that human decision-makers retain final authority over military strikes, with AI systems serving only to identify and analyze potential targets. The manifesto reflects growing concerns among some technology leaders that America must maintain its competitive edge against adversaries who face fewer restrictions on deploying advanced military technologies.
