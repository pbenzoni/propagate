Armed robbers took approximately 25 people hostage during a bank robbery in Naples, Italy on Thursday, according to local media reports. The incident unfolded at a Credit Agricole branch in the Vomero district, where attackers seized customers and staff shortly before noon.

Police quickly surrounded the building and coordinated with firefighters to mount a rescue operation. After a tense two-hour standoff in one of the city's busiest neighborhoods, security forces breached the bank by smashing through a reinforced window. All hostages were successfully freed, with at least six people treated for shock but no serious injuries reported.

The incident highlights ongoing public safety challenges in Naples, where bank robberies involving hostage-taking have become disturbingly routine. Attackers typically strike during business hours, holding staff and customers inside while waiting for time-locked safes to open. In a similar incident in May 2024, an armed robber held hostages for approximately 40 minutes before escaping with over €12,000.

Naples chief prosecutor Nicola Gratteri arrived at the scene as investigators worked to establish the sequence of events. Reports suggest at least two suspects were involved, though it remains unclear whether any arrests were made or if the attackers managed to escape.
