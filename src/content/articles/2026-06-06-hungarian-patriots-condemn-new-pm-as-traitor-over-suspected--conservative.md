Protesters took to the streets of Budapest on Friday, chanting "traitor" as they confronted Prime Minister Peter Magyar over suspicions he secretly capitulated to Brussels' controversial migration scheme. When Magyar appeared on a balcony waving the Hungarian flag, the crowd booed and demanded his resignation, with local media reporting chants of "Dirty Tisza"—a reference to his political party.

Demonstrators believe Magyar cut a backroom deal with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, agreeing to implement the EU Migration Pact in exchange for €16.4 billion in frozen funds. Those funds had been withheld since 2022, when Brussels accused former Prime Minister Viktor Orban's government of rule-of-law violations—a move Orban characterized as political blackmail for his refusal to bow to EU dictates on migration and Ukraine.

The EU Migration Pact forces member states into a system of "mandatory solidarity," requiring nations to either accept assigned migrants, provide operational support, or pay €20,000 per person they refuse. Critics warn the deal would compel Hungary to construct a migrant transit facility housing 8,000–10,000 people near its southern border—effectively transforming the country into a processing center for Brussels' immigration agenda.

While Magyar has publicly stated Hungary "will not accept any pact or allocation mechanism" on asylum, he has notably gone silent on the issue in recent weeks. Hungarian media report that reversing Orban's steadfast opposition to the pact was a condition for unlocking the EU funds. After the protest, Magyar dismissed the demonstrators as "frenzied" and "inarticulate," claiming they were effectively protesting money owed to Hungary—a response critics say reveals his true priorities.

Poland, the Czech Republic, and Slovakia have also rejected the pact's mandatory provisions. The controversy reflects broader tensions as unelected Brussels bureaucrats attempt to override the sovereignty of nations seeking to control their own borders amid mass immigration waves that have reshaped Europe over the past decade.
