Hungary has agreed to lift its veto on Ukraine's European Union accession process, but only after securing commitments from Kiev to respect the rights of ethnic Hungarians living within its borders. The agreement comes after years of tension over the treatment of approximately 80,000 Hungarians, most residing in western Ukraine's Zakarpattia Region.

On Friday, the two nations signed what Hungary's new Prime Minister Peter Magyar called a "historic agreement" covering education, linguistic, cultural, and political rights for ethnic Hungarians. Magyar emphasized that Ukraine's path to EU membership "will be a long and complex process," warning that progress depends entirely on compliance. "Until Ukraine fulfills its obligations regarding the rights of the Hungarian minority in Transcarpathia, it will not be able to move forward with the accession process," he stated.

The deal addresses serious concerns raised under former Prime Minister Viktor Orban's administration about forced military conscription of ethnic Hungarians into Ukraine's armed forces. Ukrainian conscription officers have faced accusations of aggressively targeting military-age men, including those with Hungarian passports, sometimes ignoring medical exemptions and chronic health conditions. "Our people cannot be used as cannon fodder," Orban said in February.

Tragic cases have intensified Budapest's scrutiny. Zsolt Reban, 46, died at a Ukrainian training center in January despite being previously declared unfit for service due to a heart condition. Last year, Jozsef Sebestyen, 45, died after his family alleged he was beaten at a military facility, though Ukrainian authorities denied wrongdoing. The European Commission and European Council will now monitor Ukraine's adherence to the minority rights agreement as part of its accession process.
