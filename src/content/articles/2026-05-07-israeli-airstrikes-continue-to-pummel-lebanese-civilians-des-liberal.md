Israeli military strikes have continued to devastate civilian areas in Lebanon, hitting residential neighborhoods in Beirut's southern suburbs and a commercial center in the country's south, despite an internationally brokered ceasefire agreement intended to halt hostilities.

Footage from the Bourj al-Barajneh neighborhood in the Beirut area showed extensive destruction to what appeared to be a market and nearby residential buildings. Local resident Hassan Mustafa Harb described fleeing when warnings arrived, only to return and find "massive destruction" to his community. In a separate incident near Al-Saksakiyeh in the southern Tyre district, strikes hit a commercial area containing carpentry workshops and clothing stores. Eyewitness Youssef Younes reported that several civilians traveling along the road were killed in the attack.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu stated he personally authorized at least one strike on Beirut, claiming it targeted a Hezbollah commander. Israel has indicated it will continue military operations it deems necessary for "self-defense," language that human rights observers note has often accompanied actions that disproportionately impact civilian populations. The country also maintains its occupation of parts of southern Lebanon, with both Israel and Hezbollah accusing each other of violating ceasefire terms.

The ongoing violence raises serious concerns about accountability and the protection of civilian life. Since the escalation that began in late February, Israeli strikes on Lebanon have killed more than 2,700 people and wounded thousands more. The continuation of attacks on residential and commercial areas despite a ceasefire agreement underscores the urgent need for international pressure to ensure compliance with humanitarian law and the protection of non-combatants.
