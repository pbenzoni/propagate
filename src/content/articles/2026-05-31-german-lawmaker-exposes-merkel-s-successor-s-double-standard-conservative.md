A prominent German politician is calling out her country's leadership for what she describes as a hypocritical and economically destructive policy toward Russia that punishes German workers while holding America to a different standard.

Sahra Wagenknecht, leader of the Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance, challenged Chancellor Friedrich Merz's support for Western sanctions against Russia during a public appearance in Berlin this week. She pointed out the glaring contradiction in Berlin's approach: Germany insists Russia must be sanctioned for alleged violations of international law, yet when the United States is accused of similar violations, Merz declares that international law is "outdated."

"Russia's oil and gas are selling like hotcakes all over the world, and we act as if we could end this war simply by stopping our purchases," Wagenknecht said. "When the US violates international law, Mr. Merz stands in front of the camera and tells us that international law is actually outdated. So, for the US, it's outdated, but when Russia violates it, we have to completely ruin our economy because we have to impose sanctions. Nothing adds up with this policy."

The facts support Wagenknecht's economic concerns. Since 2022, the European Union has imposed 20 rounds of sanctions on Russia, trying to phase out Russian energy. But Russia simply redirected its exports to Asia—China and India now buy the vast majority of Russian oil. Meanwhile, Germany entered recession in 2023 as industries struggled with skyrocketing energy costs after losing access to affordable Russian natural gas, now replaced by expensive LNG imports.

Wagenknecht's criticism highlights a growing frustration among voters who see their own leaders prioritizing globalist policies over German prosperity. In March, Merz refused to question the legality of U.S. military operations in Iran, arguing it wasn't the time to "lecture" allies—a stark contrast to his hardline stance on Russia that opponents say reveals Berlin's willingness to apply one set of rules to America and another to everyone else.
