Hungary's incoming Prime Minister Peter Magyar announced Friday that Vilmos Katai-Nemeth, a 52-year-old blind lawyer and accomplished martial artist, will lead the Ministry of Social and Family Affairs following Viktor Orban's recent election defeat.

Katai-Nemeth lost his sight at age 16 due to hereditary retinal disease but went on to build an impressive career as a practicing attorney. He became the world's first blind aikido black belt master and developed a self-defense system specifically designed for the visually impaired. He joined the Tisza party in 2024 and won his parliamentary seat in a Budapest constituency by a comfortable margin.

In his new role, Katai-Nemeth will focus on healthcare access improvements, child protection reform, and equal opportunities. However, some Hungarian media outlets have raised questions about whether his limited political experience and unfamiliarity with government operations could reduce him to a symbolic role, with veteran policy expert Bodis Krisztina wielding the real power behind the scenes.

The appointment comes as Magyar, generally considered a conservative, has pledged to rebuild relations with the EU while avoiding explicit advocacy on certain social issues. This week, the EU's top court ruled Hungary's 2021 child protection law—which restricted portrayals of certain content in children's media—as illegal, and authorities received a request to register an LGBTQ-themed television channel in the country for the first time.
