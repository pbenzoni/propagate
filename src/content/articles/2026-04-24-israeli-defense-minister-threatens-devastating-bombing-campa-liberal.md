Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz has issued stark threats to launch a renewed military campaign against Iran, pending approval from the United States, in comments that have raised concerns among international observers about the potential for massive civilian casualties and regional destabilization.

Katz stated Thursday that Israel is "awaiting a green light from the United States" to target Iran's energy infrastructure and economic facilities in what he described as an effort to "return Iran to the Dark Age and the Stone Age." The inflammatory language echoes rhetoric previously used by President Trump, who suggested in April that U.S. forces could "bring them back to the Stone Ages" within weeks.

The threats come against a backdrop of fragile diplomacy. Just days before the initial hostilities in late February, Iranian and U.S. delegations had engaged in indirect talks in Oman, with Oman's foreign minister suggesting that "peace was within reach" through diplomatic channels. Iran's ambassador to Moscow told RT that Tehran "remains open to both diplomacy and continued fighting," while accusing Washington of undermining peace efforts—including its withdrawal from the 2015 nuclear agreement.

Critics have pointed to the disproportionate nature of the threatened campaign, which would target critical civilian infrastructure including energy facilities that millions of ordinary Iranians depend upon. Iran has maintained that its nuclear enrichment program serves civilian purposes, including energy production and medical applications. Human rights advocates have expressed alarm that such a bombing campaign could constitute collective punishment of Iran's population and potentially violate international humanitarian law governing armed conflict.
