Israeli Defense Forces are confronting a growing challenge from Hezbollah's drone warfare tactics following the seizure of the strategically significant Beaufort Castle in southern Lebanon. The 900-year-old fortress, which Israel controlled for two decades during its previous occupation ending in 2000, was recaptured over the weekend in operations West Jerusalem described as a major military achievement.

The Iran-backed militant group Hezbollah released video footage showing its first-person-view (FPV) kamikaze drones targeting Israeli soldiers near the castle. According to reports, the fortress was empty when Israeli forces arrived, with no militant presence or military equipment found at the historic site. The drone footage shows strikes on both a building housing IDF personnel and military vehicles positioned near the castle's commanding hilltop position.

The latest escalation, sparked by recent Iranian aggression and regional instability, has seen Hezbollah significantly increase its use of small, hard-to-detect FPV drones against Israeli forces. Multiple videos released by the terrorist organization show drone operators targeting Israeli soldiers, vehicles, and defensive systems including Iron Dome launchers—though some targets appear to have been decoys rather than operational equipment.

Israeli forces are adapting to counter this evolving threat, with some armored vehicles now equipped with protective netting and cages designed to defend against drone strikes. Israeli media reports indicate IDF command is reassessing vehicle deployment strategies in southern Lebanon and investigating the effectiveness of nighttime drone attacks as the military develops more sophisticated countermeasures to protect troops operating in hostile territory.
