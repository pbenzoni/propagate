German police have detained a 33-year-old Polish citizen living in Bavaria after Nazi-themed structures appeared outside a tax office in the town of Eggenfelden. The suspect allegedly erected a replica of the Auschwitz concentration camp gates complete with the infamous "Arbeit macht frei" slogan and swastikas outside the government building in late March.

A second installation appeared on April 13: a chimney-like structure resembling a crematorium oven, labeled "Zyklon B" and marked with SS runes, placed in a disabled parking space outside the same building. Investigators matched the suspect's fingerprints to the structures, and additional Nazi symbols were reportedly found in his apartment during the arrest.

Local Mayor Martin Biber condemned the incidents as "brazen and disgusting" and an "insult to society." He noted the town's sizable migrant population but stressed that residents are well integrated and that there is no "noticeable far-right scene" in Eggenfelden. The suspect faces charges of incitement to hatred and use of unconstitutional symbols under German law, offenses that carry lengthy prison terms and fines.

German authorities have not disclosed the suspect's motive or why he targeted the tax office specifically. The incidents come as Germany reports a sharp rise in crimes involving Nazi symbols—nearly 37,000 in 2025, almost double the annual average between 2015 and 2022. Most are classified as "propaganda offenses," though many also involve hate crimes targeting migrants.
