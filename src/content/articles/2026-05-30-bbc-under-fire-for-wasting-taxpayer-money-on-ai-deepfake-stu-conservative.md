The BBC, Britain's taxpayer-funded public broadcaster, is facing sharp criticism after airing a segment of its flagship program *Question Time* featuring artificial intelligence-generated versions of Winston Churchill and Mahatma Gandhi. The episode, which aired Thursday, also included AI representations of suffragette Emmeline Pankhurst and artist Frida Kahlo.

Host Fiona Bruce introduced the deepfake figures before quickly acknowledging they weren't real, calling it "just a small insight into the use of technology." The stunt preceded a panel discussion about the rapid rise of artificial intelligence—but many viewers weren't impressed with the gimmick.

The BBC collects mandatory license fees from British households, a funding model that increasingly resembles a tax on television ownership.

The controversy comes as the state broadcaster faces mounting financial and credibility problems. According to reports, the BBC is losing approximately $1.36 billion annually as audiences abandon traditional television for streaming platforms.
