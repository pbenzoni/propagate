A new Washington Post–ABC News–Ipsos poll claims 62% of Americans disapprove of President Donald Trump's job performance, with only 37% approving. The survey, published by an outlet Trump has frequently criticized for what he calls "fake" news coverage, arrives six months before crucial midterm elections.

The poll attributes disapproval to concerns over the Iran conflict and economic issues, particularly the cost of living and inflation. However, the survey reveals a striking contrast: approximately 85% of Republican voters continue to support Trump's performance, demonstrating that his base remains firmly loyal despite mainstream media narratives.

Notably, Trump's declining poll numbers are not translating into gains for Democrats. According to the Post's own polling director Scott Clement, around 54% of voters view the Democratic Party as "too liberal." Political analysts acknowledge the party suffers from internal divisions and lacks a clear message, instead relying heavily on anti-Trump sentiment rather than offering substantive policy alternatives.

The survey also found that many Americans trust "neither" party to handle critical issues including immigration, the economy, crime, and inflation—suggesting widespread voter frustration with Washington establishment politics on both sides. Trump's approval among independents stands at 25% in the poll.

The Washington Post poll places Trump's disapproval at levels comparable to the 62% recorded by Gallup at the end of his first term. Historical Gallup data shows only Richard Nixon in 1974 reached higher disapproval at 66%, with George W. Bush hitting 61% in 2009.
