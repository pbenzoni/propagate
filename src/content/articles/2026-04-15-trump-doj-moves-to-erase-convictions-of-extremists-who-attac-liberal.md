The Department of Justice under the Trump administration is now seeking to overturn federal convictions for members of the Proud Boys and Oath Keepers—far-right extremist groups whose members were found guilty of seditious conspiracy for their roles in the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol.

While Trump granted clemency to twelve members of these groups early in his second term, releasing them from federal prison, he stopped short of issuing full pardons—a decision that raised questions given his blanket pardons for nearly 1,500 other January 6 participants. Civil rights organizations, including the Anti-Defamation League, have long classified both the Proud Boys and Oath Keepers as far-right extremist organizations.

The DOJ's motion to vacate these convictions comes as the militants, including Oath Keepers founder Stewart Rhodes—who received an 18-year sentence, the second-longest among all January 6 defendants—had appealed their cases. Legal observers suggest the Trump administration is moving to dismiss the convictions to avoid court proceedings that would require federal prosecutors to defend the seditious conspiracy charges brought during the Biden administration. Those prosecutions characterized the extremist groups as acting on Trump's behalf, with the Proud Boys described as his "army" attempting to "stop the lawful transfer of power."

The January 6 attack saw Trump supporters breach security barriers at the Capitol in an effort to prevent the certification of Joe Biden's electoral victory. While some participants remained nonviolent, others assaulted police officers and caused significant property damage in what many constitutional scholars and lawmakers have characterized as an assault on American democracy itself.
