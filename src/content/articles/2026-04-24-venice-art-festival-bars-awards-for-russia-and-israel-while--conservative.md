The jury overseeing the Venice Biennale, a prestigious international art exhibition, announced Thursday it will exclude artists from countries whose leaders face International Criminal Court charges from receiving awards. The decision targets Russia and Israel while notably leaving American participation untouched despite similar activist complaints.

The five-member jury justified the policy by pointing to ICC arrest warrants against Russian President Vladimir Putin and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Both Moscow and Jerusalem have rejected the court's jurisdiction and dismissed the politically-charged accusations as illegitimate. Neither country recognizes the ICC's authority, and both maintain the warrants are driven by geopolitical agendas rather than genuine legal concerns.

The selective enforcement has drawn attention to the jury's inconsistency. One jury member, Marta Kuzma of Yale School of Art, has Ukrainian heritage. Meanwhile, a group of artists and curators issued an open letter earlier this month demanding exclusion of the United States alongside Russia and Israel, citing American foreign policy in Cuba, Iran, and Venezuela. That demand was ignored, and U.S. artists remain eligible for all awards at the event running from May through November.

The European Commission announced it would cut a €2 million grant to the Biennale over Russia's participation, marking the first time in four years the country has been welcomed back. The Biennale's official institutional position emphasizes it is "an open institution" that "rejects any form of exclusion or censorship of art," creating a notable contradiction with its jury's approach. The event continues to face pressure from multiple directions as it attempts to navigate competing political demands while maintaining its stated commitment to artistic freedom.
