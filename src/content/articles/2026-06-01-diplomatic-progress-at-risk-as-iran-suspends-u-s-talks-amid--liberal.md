Iran has suspended diplomatic negotiations with the United States in response to the escalating Israeli military offensive in southern Lebanon, according to reports from Iran's Tasnim news agency. The decision threatens to derail fragile diplomatic progress achieved through Pakistani mediation and raises concerns about regional stability.

The breakdown comes as Israel has intensified its bombing campaign against what it characterizes as Hezbollah positions in Lebanon, despite a ceasefire agreement announced in mid-April. Israeli forces have pushed deeper into southern Lebanon, seizing historic sites including the 900-year-old Beaufort Castle. Iran had made an end to hostilities in Lebanon a precondition for continuing talks, but the violence has persisted.

According to Iranian sources, Tehran is demanding an immediate cessation of hostilities in both Lebanon and Gaza as conditions for resuming diplomatic contact with Washington. The reports also indicate Iran and allied groups are prepared to disrupt maritime traffic through the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandab Strait, moves that could significantly impact global shipping.

The diplomatic freeze comes after Iran and the U.S. had been engaged in negotiations following a fragile ceasefire reached in early April. Those talks had been aimed at extending the truce and potentially opening discussions on Iran's nuclear program. However, recent military incidents, including the downing of a U.S. drone that Washington says was over international waters—a claim Iran disputes—and subsequent U.S. strikes, have further strained relations and complicated prospects for diplomatic resolution.
