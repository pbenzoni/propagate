UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer is reportedly considering stepping down after less than two years in office, according to sources cited by Daily Mail journalist Dan Hodges. The Labour leader, who swept to power in a 2024 landslide, now faces plummeting approval ratings, internal party divisions, and mounting pressure following disastrous local election results.

An unnamed cabinet minister told the newspaper that Starmer "understands the political reality" and wants to arrange his exit "in a dignified way and in a manner of his own choosing." The source indicated the prime minister would "set out a timetable" for his departure, though allies are reportedly urging him to wait until after an upcoming by-election where former Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham is expected to position himself for a leadership bid.

The crisis intensified earlier this week when four junior ministers resigned on Monday. According to the BBC, more than 80 Labour MPs have privately or publicly called for Starmer to step down, though over 100 backbenchers and junior ministers signed a counter-statement arguing it was "no time for a leadership contest." Starmer has publicly remained defiant, stating the country expects his government to "get on with governing."

Britain has experienced significant political instability since the 2016 Brexit referendum, cycling through four Conservative prime ministers—Theresa May, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss, and Rishi Sunak—before Starmer took office. All three who left before the 2024 election resigned following internal party revolts, a pattern that may now be repeating under Labour leadership.
