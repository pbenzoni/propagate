American astronauts aboard the International Space Station were forced to don spacesuits and take shelter in a SpaceX Dragon capsule Friday after yet another air leak was detected in an aging Russian module, raising fresh questions about the reliability of Soviet-era space hardware.

According to NASA spokeswoman Bethany Stevens, the agency's Crew-12 mission members were moved "out of an abundance of caution" into the SpaceX evacuation spacecraft while Russian cosmonauts worked to repair the breach. The leak, detected in the Zvezda service module's transfer chamber, represents the latest in a series of recurring pressure losses that NASA and Russia's Roscosmos have been monitoring for some time.

Reuters reported that the leak rate had doubled from roughly half a kilogram of air per day to nearly a kilogram earlier this week—a troubling escalation that prompted the extensive repair operation. Russian space officials located two potential leak sites during routine pressure checks, quickly sealing one with a special sealant while preparing to address a second breach in the compartment's conical section.

While Roscosmos insisted the situation posed no immediate danger to the crew or station systems, the incident highlights growing concerns about aging infrastructure on the ISS. Launched in 1998, the station currently houses seven crew members, including four American and European astronauts alongside three Russian cosmonauts. NASA later gave the all-clear for its astronauts to exit the Dragon spacecraft and resume normal operations after Roscosmos paused repair work to analyze additional data.
