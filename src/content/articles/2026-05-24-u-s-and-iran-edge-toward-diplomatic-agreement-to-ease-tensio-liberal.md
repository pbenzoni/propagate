The United States and Iran are moving closer to a diplomatic agreement that could extend a fragile ceasefire and restore access to the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway through which roughly 25% of global oil trade flows, according to reporting by Axios citing American officials.

Under the proposed memorandum of understanding, navigation through the Strait of Hormuz would resume for all vessels without tolls, while Washington would lift its blockade on Iranian ports and allow unrestricted oil sales by Tehran. The agreement would extend the current ceasefire by 60 days, during which both sides would engage in negotiations on Iran's nuclear program and broader regional security issues.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei acknowledged that the two nations were achieving a "convergence of views," though he expressed caution about the possibility of Washington reversing course. The current discussions focus on ending hostilities and what Iran characterizes as unlawful maritime restrictions, Baghaei said in a television interview.

The potential agreement follows a month of intense hostilities that began in early April, initiated by U.S. and Israeli actions. According to U.S. officials cited by Axios, the proposed deal would also address the Israeli military campaign in Lebanon, though the lifting of broader economic sanctions and unfreezing of Iranian assets would only occur as part of a final, comprehensive agreement. Tehran continues to press for the removal of what it calls "illegal and inhumane" sanctions as a core demand in ongoing diplomatic contacts.
