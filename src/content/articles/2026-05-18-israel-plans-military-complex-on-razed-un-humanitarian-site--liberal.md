Israel has approved construction of a new military compound on the site of a demolished UN Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA) headquarters in East Jerusalem, a move that international observers say represents another challenge to established international norms. Defense Minister Israel Katz described the project as symbolizing "sovereignty, Zionism and security," stating there was "nothing more symbolic or just" than building defense facilities on the former UN site.

Israeli forces seized and partially demolished the UNRWA compound in 2024 following legislation that banned the agency's operations in Israeli-controlled areas. The new complex will span approximately nine acres and house military recruitment offices, an IDF museum, and facilities for the defense minister. UNRWA head Philippe Lazzarini condemned the destruction as "a new level of open and deliberate defiance of international law."

The demolition comes as UNRWA continues providing critical services to Palestinian refugees across the region. The agency operates schools, clinics, and aid programs for displaced populations in Gaza, the West Bank, Jordan, Lebanon, and Syria. More than 2.3 million people have been displaced by Israel's military campaign in Gaza since 2023, with over one million currently sheltering in UNRWA facilities, according to UN figures.

UN agencies and humanitarian organizations have issued repeated warnings about catastrophic conditions in Gaza, where more than 72,000 Palestinians have been killed since 2023, according to UN data. Humanitarian groups report widespread hunger and severely overcrowded refugee camps. The construction project in East Jerusalem raises concerns among international law experts about the treatment of humanitarian infrastructure in occupied territories and the protection of civilian populations during armed conflict.
