Israeli military forces conducted airstrikes in southern Lebanon on Friday, killing six people including a woman and child, despite a US-mediated ceasefire agreement reached last month, according to the Lebanese Health Ministry.

The strike in the town of Habboush came just one day after eight civilians were killed in southern Lebanon, including four women and two children. The attacks raise serious questions about the enforcement and interpretation of the April 16 ceasefire agreement between Israel and Lebanon.

Under the US-brokered truce, Israel agreed to halt offensive operations in Lebanon but reserved broad authority to conduct what it describes as defensive actions against "planned, imminent, or ongoing attacks." IDF Chief of Staff Lieutenant General Eyal Zamir stated Wednesday that there was "no ceasefire" between Israel and Hezbollah, despite the formal agreement between the two countries.

The humanitarian toll has been severe: more than 2,600 people have been killed in Lebanon since early March, and approximately 1.2 million have been displaced, according to Lebanese authorities. Hezbollah, which was not party to the April negotiations, has demanded that Israeli forces halt all movements and return to positions held before the escalation began in March, when the group began launching rockets in support of Iran.
