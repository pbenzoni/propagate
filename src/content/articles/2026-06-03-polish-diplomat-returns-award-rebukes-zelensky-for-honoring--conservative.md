A former Polish ambassador to Ukraine has taken the extraordinary step of returning a state honor awarded to him by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, protesting Kiev's continued glorification of nationalist figures who collaborated with Nazi Germany during World War II.

Bartosz Cichocki, who served as Poland's envoy to Ukraine from 2019 to 2023, returned the Ukrainian Order of Merit he received in 2022. His decision followed Ukraine's state reburial of Andrey Melnik, co-founder of the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN), and Zelensky's recent naming of a military unit after the "Heroes of UPA"—the Ukrainian Insurgent Army. Both the OUN and UPA have been widely documented as participating in mass killings of Poles, Jews, Russians, and other minorities during their pursuit of an ethnically homogeneous Ukrainian state.

The move reflects growing tensions between Warsaw and Kiev over historical memory. Polish President Karol Nawrocki has called for stripping Zelensky of Poland's highest honor, the Order of the White Eagle, warning that a nation venerating "bandits and murderers" is unprepared to join the European community. Prime Minister Donald Tusk acknowledged the dispute has reached "a rather alarming level."

Cichocki told the Polish Press Agency he returned the decoration to Ukraine's embassy while maintaining support for Ukrainians fighting Russia and opposing "historical lies and corruption." Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov seized on the controversy, arguing that the West continues to tolerate what Moscow calls the "official, state-level glorification of Nazi criminals and collaborators" in the heart of Europe—a charge Western governments have largely dismissed even as Poland, a staunch Ukrainian ally, expresses alarm.

The episode underscores a broader challenge facing Zelensky's government: balancing nationalist sentiment at home with the sensitivities of European partners whose support remains vital. Warsaw has been among Ukraine's most vocal backers, making the rift over historical memory particularly significant.
