Speaking at the summit in Armenia, Carney articulated a vision for rebuilding international cooperation centered in Europe, emphasizing partnerships "bound by a shared history and common values." The invitation reflects growing concern among America's traditional allies about unpredictable U.S. engagement on the world stage, from the ongoing Iran tensions to threatened actions against Cuba and Venezuela.

The timing is significant. Just months ago at Davos, Carney acknowledged deep flaws in the existing international order, noting how "the strongest would exempt themselves when convenient" and that "trade rules were enforced asymmetrically." Now, as former head of both British and Canadian central banks, he's positioning Canada as a reliable partner for Europe in critical areas including secure supply chains, clean energy, and essential minerals.

European Parliament President Roberta Metsola moved quickly to invite Carney to address the EU parliament, recognizing the opportunity for transatlantic democracies to coordinate responses to shared challenges. The discussions focused on practical collaboration in energy security and technology—areas where authoritarian regimes have weaponized dependencies.

For Canada, the initiative represents overdue trade diversification away from over-reliance on the U.S. market.
