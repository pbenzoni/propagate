The U.S. military conducted an emergency evacuation drill at its embassy in Caracas, Venezuela on Saturday, marking the first American military exercise in the country since the controversial detention of former President Nicolás Maduro.

The exercise, which Venezuelan authorities authorized, involved two Marine Corps MV-22B Osprey aircraft landing near the embassy compound, along with naval vessels operating in Caribbean waters. The U.S. Embassy characterized the operation as a "military response exercise" designed to maintain readiness for potential emergency evacuations.

The drill drew criticism from local activists, with several dozen protesters gathering to denounce what they called a "humiliation" to Venezuelan sovereignty. Demonstrators displayed a Venezuelan flag bearing the message "No to the Yankee drill."

Maduro, who was taken into U.S. custody along with his wife and transported to face charges including drug trafficking, remains detained in the United States. He has denied all allegations and described himself as a "prisoner of war." The operation that led to his detention has reshaped U.S.-Venezuela relations, with Venezuela's interim government cooperating closely with Washington. Human rights organizations have raised questions about the circumstances surrounding Maduro's removal from office and subsequent prosecution.
