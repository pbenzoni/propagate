President Donald Trump sparked outrage Wednesday by sharing a deeply xenophobic letter on Truth Social that describes immigrants from India and China as "gangsters with laptops" who have inflicted damage on America exceeding "all the mafia families put together."

The inflammatory post, originally written by former radio host Michael Savage, labels India, China, and other nations as "hellholes" while attacking the constitutional principle of birthright citizenship. The letter claims that children born in the U.S. to immigrant parents become "instant citizens," allowing families to immigrate—a process the author characterizes as enabling newcomers to rob Americans "blind" and treat them "like second-class citizens."

The screed also pushes racial grievance narratives, asserting without evidence that "white men need not apply" for jobs and alleging that hiring processes are "run by Indians and Chinese." Trump's decision to amplify this message comes as his administration faces multiple legal challenges over his January executive order attempting to end birthright citizenship for children whose parents lack citizenship or permanent residency—a directive that contradicts the Fourteenth Amendment's guarantee that all persons born in the U.S. are citizens.

The episode follows a pattern of inflammatory rhetoric from Trump, including his recent false claim that the United States is the only country offering birthright citizenship—a statement contradicted by the fact that Canada and most Latin American nations provide the same protection.
