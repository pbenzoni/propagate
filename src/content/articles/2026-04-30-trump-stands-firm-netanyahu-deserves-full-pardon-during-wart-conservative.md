President Donald Trump has doubled down on his call for Israeli President Isaac Herzog to grant a full pardon to Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who has been fighting corruption charges since 2019. The move underscores Trump's unwavering support for America's closest Middle Eastern ally during a time of unprecedented regional conflict.

According to Axios, Trump expressed frustration that Netanyahu—indicted on bribery, fraud, and breach of trust charges that he denies—must continue appearing in court while managing Israel's defense during wartime. "In the middle of a war? Give me a break!" the president reportedly said, emphasizing that the prime minister's focus should be on national security, not a drawn-out legal battle that has already stretched over five years.

Trump characterized the prosecution as politically motivated, calling it an "unjustified" case against a wartime leader. He told Axios that Herzog "will be a national hero" if he pardons Netanyahu, and suggested that the ongoing trial damages Israel's international standing. The president reportedly argued that Netanyahu "can't take" a settlement requiring an admission of guilt and needs a complete pardon instead.

The trial, which began in 2020 after Netanyahu pleaded not guilty, has faced numerous delays and could continue for years. Herzog recently invited attorneys and prosecutors to explore a potential settlement, though he has stated he will not consider pardoning Netanyahu until that option is fully exhausted. Trump has consistently supported the Israeli leader, previously sending an official letter calling the case a "WITCH HUNT" and a "political, unjustified prosecution."
