The European Union is threatening to withdraw millions in funding from the prestigious Venice Biennale after the historic art institution announced it would allow Russian artists to participate in its 2026 exhibition. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas declared Russia "should not be allowed to exhibit" its culture, demanding the 130-year-old exhibition exclude artists based on nationality.

Italian Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini pushed back forcefully against the EU's heavy-handed approach, calling the proposed €2 million funding cut "ignorant pettiness" given the billions member states contribute to Brussels. "No one in Brussels would dare threaten" the internationally respected institution, Salvini said, noting the Russian pavilion is Russian-owned property and its use falls within legal boundaries.

The controversy highlights growing concerns about cultural censorship emanating from EU bureaucrats. Since 2022, Russian artists have faced systematic event cancellations across EU member states, with performers from other nations also targeted if suspected of having sympathy for Russia. The Venice Biennale defended its decision to readmit Russian artists as "preserving a space for dialogue" and keeping art separate from politics.

Salvini announced he plans to attend the Biennale's opening "with all due respect to Latvia," mocking threats from Latvian Culture Minister Agnese Lace to boycott the event. The deputy prime minister questioned whether Italy should halt cultural events based on demands from smaller EU members, emphasizing the exhibition's autonomy and global standing. The standoff represents another flashpoint in debates over whether unelected EU officials should dictate cultural policy to sovereign member states.
