A member of Poland's far-right Confederation bloc displayed an Israeli flag altered to include a swastika in place of the Star of David during a parliamentary session on Tuesday, prompting widespread condemnation from Polish officials and international observers.

Konrad Berkowicz claimed the provocative display was meant to protest Israel's military operations in Gaza and Lebanon, including reported use of white phosphorus munitions. Parliamentary Speaker Wlodzimierz Czarzasty announced he would pursue fines against Berkowicz for violating parliamentary rules and notify legal authorities about potential crimes, including publicly insulting a foreign state's flag and promoting Nazi symbols.

The Polish Foreign Ministry issued a statement emphasizing that legitimate criticism of government policies "does not justify such a gesture, which is deeply offensive not only to Jews and Israelis but also to all those for whom the Holocaust and other Nazi crimes constitute an important element of memory and identity." The Israeli Embassy in Warsaw condemned what it called "antisemitic horror," noting the incident occurred on Israel's Holocaust Remembrance Day.

The episode underscores growing tensions around discourse on Middle East policy in Europe, where documented antisemitic incidents have increased since 2023. Civil rights organizations have repeatedly emphasized the critical distinction between political criticism of governmental actions and antisemitic rhetoric or imagery that invokes Nazi symbolism—a line Berkowicz's stunt clearly crossed, according to officials across the political spectrum.
