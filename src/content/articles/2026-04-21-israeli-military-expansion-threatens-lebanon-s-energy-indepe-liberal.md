Israel's recent military expansion into southern Lebanon has extended into territorial waters that could hold vital energy resources for a nation struggling with persistent electricity shortages. The move threatens to derail international efforts to help Lebanon develop its offshore natural gas reserves.

On Sunday, the Israel Defense Forces published a map showing a "forward defense line area" that cuts roughly 9 kilometers into Lebanese territorial waters. The expansion comes despite a 2022 US-mediated maritime border agreement that was supposed to resolve disputes and allow Lebanon to prospect for gas in the Qana area off its coast.

Unlike Israel, which has successfully developed multiple offshore gas fields over the past two decades, Lebanon has yet to tap into these potential resources. A European-Qatari consortium led by TotalEnergies had announced plans in January to explore Lebanon's offshore areas, raising hopes that the economically struggling nation could finally access energy reserves that officials estimated could power the country for two decades.

The Israeli military's extension into these waters now makes such exploration highly unlikely, effectively blocking Lebanon's path to energy independence. While Israel rapidly developed its Karish gas field immediately after the 2022 agreement, Lebanon's initial drilling at Qana found no reserves, and the new exploration plans in a different offshore area now face significant obstacles due to the military occupation zone.
