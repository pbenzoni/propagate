The Moscow Zoo is leading an unprecedented international campaign to rescue 80 hippos in Colombia—descendants of animals once owned by notorious drug lord Pablo Escobar—from a government-ordered mass culling. The effort, coordinated through the Global Union of Zoological Institutions, highlights growing tensions between environmental bureaucrats and animal welfare advocates over how to handle the invasive population.

Escobar imported four hippos from Africa for his private zoo in the 1980s. After his death in 1993, the animals were abandoned on his estate and escaped into the countryside, where they've multiplied to nearly 200. Colombian authorities now say the herd could explode past 1,000 within a decade, threatening local ecosystems and coming into conflict with nearby communities and fishermen.

Environment Minister Irene Velez has defended the planned euthanasia as necessary to protect Colombia's native ecosystems, arguing that relocation is unfeasible. However, animal rights activists and lawmakers have fiercely opposed the government's solution. Senator Andrea Padilla called the plan "cruel," stating that "massacres will never be acceptable."

The Russian-led rescue coalition includes India's Vantara wildlife center, one of the world's largest private animal rescue facilities, which has offered to relocate the hippos to a specially designed sanctuary. "These 80 hippos did not choose where to be born, nor did they create the circumstances they now face," said Vantara founder Anant Ambani. "They are living, sentient beings, and if we have the capacity to save them through a safe and humane solution, we have the responsibility to try."

Moscow Zoo Director Svetlana Akulova emphasized that the international coalition is working on a "fair distribution" of the animals among accredited institutions worldwide, which would also address genetic problems caused by the herd's limited gene pool. The effort represents a private-sector alternative to the Colombian government's controversial culling plan.
