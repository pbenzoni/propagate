A coordinated multi-agency effort resulted in the arrest of 341 suspects and the rescue of 40 minors during a two-week operation targeting internet-facilitated child exploitation across Southern California. Operation Firewall, which ran from April 19 through May 3, was led by the Internet Crimes Against Children (ICAC) Task Force and involved hundreds of officers from 30 different law enforcement agencies.

The operation spanned Los Angeles, Orange, San Bernardino, Santa Barbara, and Ventura counties, focusing on identifying individuals who use the internet to facilitate the sexual exploitation and abuse of children. Investigators conducted undercover operations across multiple social media platforms and executed numerous search and arrest warrants. Those arrested face charges including production, possession, and distribution of child sexual abuse material, sexual assault of minors, and human trafficking.

Law enforcement officials revealed that investigators posed as teenage girls as young as 13 in chat rooms and online gaming platforms to identify predators who attempted to groom and arrange meetings with minors. At a Thursday press conference, First Assistant U.S. Attorney for the Central District of California Bill Essayli emphasized that "the cases we're announcing today are just a small drop in the bucket," warning that many more offenses remain undetected.

Officials described the scale of internet-facilitated child exploitation as an "epidemic" in Southern California. Eddie Wang, special agent in charge for Homeland Security Investigations in Los Angeles, and other law enforcement leaders urged increased vigilance from parents and caregivers regarding children's online activities, emphasizing the need for greater awareness of digital threats facing young people.
