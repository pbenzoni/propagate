A visit by Israeli Knesset Education Committee Chairman Zvi Sukkot to a predominantly Bedouin Arab town in northern Israel last week has ignited controversy after local officials locked him out of a school he sought to inspect.

Sukkot, a member of the Religious Zionism party, traveled to Tuba-Zangariyye under police protection to investigate what he described as "crime and violence" in the local education system. However, town leaders rejected his oversight visit, calling it a "racist" stunt and physically barring entry by locking school gates. Video footage showed Sukkot with an electric saw during the confrontation outside the facility.

Town council head Muayyad Haib told media the visit was "extremely provocative," though Sukkot maintained that local officials had ignored his role as the elected chairman of Israel's Education Committee. The lawmaker defended his actions and indicated he would pursue cuts to state funding for the schools, arguing that Israel should not invest taxpayer resources in educational institutions that refuse government oversight.

The incident has drawn attention to ongoing tensions over educational standards and government accountability in Arab communities within Israel. Critics have accused Sukkot of political grandstanding, while supporters argue that elected officials have a duty to investigate concerns about violence and ensure proper use of public funds in all state-funded schools regardless of community demographics.
