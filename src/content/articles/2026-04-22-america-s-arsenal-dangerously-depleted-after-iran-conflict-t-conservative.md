A sobering new analysis from the Center for Strategic and International Studies reveals that America's military readiness has been severely compromised following the seven-week conflict with Iran. The report warns that the United States has burned through critical missile inventories at an alarming rate, potentially leaving our nation vulnerable should we need to confront a major adversary like China.

According to the CSIS findings, which reportedly align with classified Pentagon assessments, the drawdown is staggering: at least 45% of our Precision Strike Missiles are gone, nearly half of our Patriot air defense interceptors have been expended, and over half of our THAAD missile inventory has been depleted. The damage extends beyond air defense, with approximately 30% of our Tomahawk cruise missiles, more than 20% of our JASSM long-range strike weapons, and roughly 20% of our SM-3 and SM-6 interceptors consumed in the operations.

The report's authors caution that restoring these critical arsenals will require years of effort and substantial taxpayer investment—between one and four years just to replenish current stocks, with several additional years needed to expand inventories to adequate levels. President Trump has requested a record-breaking $1.5 trillion military budget for fiscal year 2027, with significant portions dedicated to rebuilding our munitions stockpiles.

While Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell maintains that our military "has everything it needs to execute at the time and place of the president's choosing," the CSIS analysis raises serious questions about our preparedness for a potential conflict with a near-peer competitor. The situation has grown urgent enough that the Pentagon has reportedly approached major American automakers GM and Ford about converting civilian production facilities to manufacture military equipment—a move reminiscent of World War II-era industrial mobilization.
