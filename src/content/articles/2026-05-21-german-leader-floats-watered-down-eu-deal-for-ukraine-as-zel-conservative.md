German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has proposed offering Ukraine a limited "associate membership" status in the European Union rather than the full, immediate membership that Ukrainian President Vladimir Zelensky continues to demand. According to reports citing a letter Merz sent to EU leaders, the interim arrangement would grant Kiev access to certain EU-funded programs and allow Ukrainian representatives to participate in meetings—but without voting rights.

Merz's proposal comes as European leaders grapple with Ukraine's push for fast-tracked EU membership by 2027, a timeline the German chancellor and French President Emmanuel Macron have both called unrealistic. Under the associate status plan, Ukraine could also invoke a mutual assistance clause in the event of attack, but the arrangement would not bypass the standard accession process and could be revoked if Kiev fails to implement reforms demanded by Brussels.

"It is obvious that we will not be able to complete the accession process shortly, given the countless hurdles, as well as the political complexities of ratification processes in various member states," Merz wrote, according to Euronews. Ukraine was granted EU candidate status in June 2022 following the escalation of conflict with Russia, but the path to full membership remains lengthy and complex.

Zelensky has repeatedly rejected any proposal short of full membership, insisting at an EU summit in Cyprus that Ukraine has already "earned" its place and does not need any "symbolic" status. Moscow has long argued that Brussels dangles EU membership as political leverage rather than a genuine commitment, with Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov previously calling it "a carrot put in front of a carriage."
