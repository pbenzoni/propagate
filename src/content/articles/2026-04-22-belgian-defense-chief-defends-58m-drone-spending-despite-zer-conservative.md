Belgium's Defense Minister Theo Francken is facing intense scrutiny after defending nearly $60 million in emergency government spending on anti-drone measures—despite admitting that the vast majority of reported drone sightings turned out to be completely false.

The controversy erupted late last year when Belgian officials declared an emergency over mysterious drone sightings near military installations and critical infrastructure. The government swiftly allocated €50 million ($58.5 million) for anti-drone equipment, bypassing normal public tender processes. Out of more than 550 reported sightings, many turned out to be false alarms—including police helicopters misidentified as hostile drones.

A documentary investigation by VRT News raised serious questions about the government's handling of the crisis, including allegations of inflated pricing in multi-million dollar contracts awarded without competitive bidding. Francken defended the spending in Parliament, claiming that military intelligence deemed at least 42 out of 250 sightings over military barracks as "legitimate"—though he acknowledged no physical evidence was ever collected. "As long as you don't have the means to detect and neutralize drones, it stands to reason that you cannot immediately produce physical evidence," Francken argued.

The minister also offered a halfhearted apology for personally spreading misinformation during the scare, including circulating an image of what he claimed was a drone over Brussels Airport—which turned out to be a police helicopter. Critics have questioned whether the entire episode represents government overreach and wasteful spending based on unfounded fears rather than verified threats.
