The British Foreign Office has shut down a specialized unit that tracked alleged breaches of international law by Israel during its military operations in Gaza, according to a report from The Guardian. The closure comes despite UK Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper's recent declaration that support for international law represents a "core British value" under her leadership.

The termination of the International Humanitarian Law (IHL) cell means London will also end funding for the Conflict and Security Monitoring Project, operated by the Center for Information Resilience. British officials have been warned they will lose access to a database containing some 26,000 entries of supposed violations compiled since Israel launched its military response following the deadly Hamas assault in October 2023. The database had reportedly been consulted when British authorities made decisions about arms export licenses to Israel.

The Guardian reports the closure appears linked to the UK cabinet's decision to reduce overseas aid spending to 0.3% of GDP. Critics have charged the move is designed to obscure information about the conflict, though defenders of Israel's military campaign argue the nation has the right to defend itself against terrorist threats and that such databases often lack proper context about Hamas's use of civilian infrastructure and human shields.

The UK previously suspended approximately 30 of its 350 weapons export licenses to Israel, citing concerns about potential international law violations. Palestinian health authorities report over 72,000 people have been killed in Gaza during the conflict, though Israel maintains it takes extensive measures to minimize civilian casualties while targeting Hamas military infrastructure embedded in populated areas.
