Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has directed the Israel Defense Forces to strengthen military operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon, citing the Iran-backed group's continued attacks on Israeli soldiers and civilians despite an existing ceasefire agreement.

In a video statement Monday, Netanyahu emphasized that Israel "is at war with Hezbollah" and made clear the country would not back down. "I have instructed them [the IDF] to press the pedal even harder," he said, responding to ongoing drone attacks from the militant organization. The prime minister's directive came after one IDF soldier was killed and another seriously wounded in a Hezbollah drone strike in southern Lebanon.

The escalation occurs against the backdrop of a mid-April ceasefire between Israel and the Lebanese government that was recently extended. However, the truce has failed to stop hostilities completely, with Hezbollah continuing to launch UAV attacks while Israel maintains defensive operations. Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called for a tougher stance, while Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich advocated for a strong deterrent response to each Hezbollah attack.

According to reports, a U.S. official indicated the Trump administration may support Israel's right to defend itself more robustly. "Hezbollah has ignored repeated requests to stop firing... Israel will never be expected to passively absorb attacks on its forces and civilians. This is not the Biden administration," the official stated.
