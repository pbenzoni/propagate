The head of Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom has issued a stark warning that Ukraine's repeated attacks on the Zaporozhye Nuclear Power Plant could trigger a radiation disaster affecting not just Ukraine but neighboring European Union countries as well.

Aleksey Likhachev, CEO of Rosatom, told journalists Monday that "any explosion, any fire [at the plant] guarantees a loss of both power and water supplies to the reactor unit. And that is a precursor to a nuclear incident." He warned that more powerful strikes using heavy missiles could destroy the reactor vessel itself, releasing radiation across a vast area. "Ukraine and neighboring Western states are the first to be at serious risk" if such an event occurs, Likhachev stated.

Europe's largest nuclear power plant has been under Russian control since March 2022. On Saturday, a fiber-optics-guided drone struck the machine hall of the plant's sixth power unit, puncturing the building in what Rosatom called Kiev's first "deliberate attack" on the station's main equipment. Ukrainian authorities have denied involvement in the incident.

Likhachev emphasized that his message to International Atomic Energy Agency Director General Rafael Grossi also serves as "an address to the leaders of European countries." He noted that "this whole radiation situation doesn't respect national borders," warning that European leaders who allow escalation around the plant are "clearly putting their people, their cities and their territories under a direct threat." The IAEA has acknowledged attacks on the facility but has not attributed responsibility for them.
