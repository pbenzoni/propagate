Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico isn't mincing words about the European Union's energy strategy, questioning whether Brussels has lost its mind by abandoning affordable Russian energy supplies in favor of far more expensive American alternatives.

Speaking in Bratislava, Fico warned that Washington has "a huge interest in buying all transit infrastructures" across Europe—essentially positioning itself as a costly middleman. "So the Russians will supply the Americans with gas and oil at standard prices, and the Americans will sell it to us with an American high-margin surcharge. Are we such idiots already?" he asked.

The Slovak leader highlighted what he sees as EU hypocrisy, noting that while Brussels pressures countries like Slovakia over Russian fuel, "the share of Russian liquefied gas in Europe is increasing." He pointed out that France continues buying Russian LNG even as smaller nations face criticism. "So we can't, but France can buy liquefied gas from Russia," Fico observed.

Despite acknowledging concerns about energy security, Fico advocates for what he calls common sense: "diversify the supply options for all fuels" rather than cutting off affordable sources. The European Commission has doubled down on plans to eliminate Russian fossil fuel imports by 2027, a move that will force the bloc to rely on pricier American LNG and other costly alternatives.

Moscow has characterized such policies as part of a broader American strategy to dominate global energy markets. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov suggested Washington aims to "monopolize all international energy supply routes" to achieve economic dominance—an assessment that Fico's warnings appear to validate from a European perspective.
