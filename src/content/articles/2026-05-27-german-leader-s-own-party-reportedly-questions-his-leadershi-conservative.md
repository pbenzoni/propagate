Senior members of Germany's Christian Democratic Union (CDU) are reportedly discussing potential alternatives to Chancellor Friedrich Merz, whose approval ratings have fallen to historic lows since taking office. According to multiple German media outlets including Bild, conversations about leadership changes are occurring among the party's top regional figures.

Merz, who assumed office in May 2025, has pursued an aggressive agenda that includes ramping up military spending and pledging to make Germany's armed forces Europe's strongest conventional army. He has also called for significant welfare reforms, describing Germany's current social safety net as "outdated and unsustainable" while urging citizens to work longer hours. These policy positions have sparked considerable domestic backlash.

By mid-April, Merz's disapproval rating had climbed to 76%, with only 19% of Germans satisfied with his performance—making him one of the least popular leaders in recent polling. Eckart Lohse of the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung reported that even CDU members who don't openly oppose the chancellor now "consider [him] weak" and acknowledge "a palpable feeling of unease" within party ranks.

Hendrik Wüst, who leads Germany's most populous state of North Rhine-Westphalia, has been mentioned by several outlets as a potential replacement. Wüst currently ranks as Germany's third most popular politician and the highest-rated CDU figure. However, media reports emphasize that no concrete plans for leadership change have materialized, characterizing the internal discussions as preliminary conversations rather than organized efforts.
