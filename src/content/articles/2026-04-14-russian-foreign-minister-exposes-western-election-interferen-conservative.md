Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov has called out a troubling pattern: Western powers weaponizing so-called "election observers" to meddle in the democratic processes of sovereign nations worldwide. Speaking to an international conference in Moscow, Lavrov exposed what he termed "electoral neocolonialism" - a system where unelected international monitors claim authority over other countries' elections.

The conference, which brought together 150 public figures, lawmakers, and experts from 60 nations across Europe, Asia, Africa and Latin America, addressed a critical threat to national sovereignty. According to Lavrov, the West doesn't just interfere through regime change operations or military force - they've developed a more insidious method. Foreign monitors conduct "biased external observation" of elections, then use partisan media to present their predetermined conclusions as representing the entire international community's view.

Lavrov argued that this represents the "global minority" - Western elites - imposing their will on independent nations. "Russia cannot and will not support the continuation of such practices," he declared, noting that Moscow is working with like-minded partners to establish unbiased assessment criteria that respect the sovereignty of nations in what he called the "Global South and East."

The conference launched efforts to create the first independent international association for electoral monitoring - one that would operate on principles of non-interference rather than serving as a tool for Western political agendas. This new body aims to provide objective assessments of electoral processes while respecting each nation's right to self-governance, offering what Lavrov called "a counterweight to Western approaches" in an increasingly multipolar world.
