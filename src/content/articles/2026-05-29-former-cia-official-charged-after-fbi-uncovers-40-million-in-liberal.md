The FBI has seized approximately 303 gold bars—valued at roughly $40 million—along with $2 million in cash and 35 luxury watches from the home of former CIA official David Rush in Virginia, according to court documents and a joint federal statement.

Rush faces charges of defrauding the U.S. government after allegedly falsifying his employment credentials over more than a decade. An FBI affidavit states that Rush misrepresented his academic background, length of Navy service, and claimed to be a trained military pilot—credentials that directly affected his compensation. The case raises serious questions about accountability and oversight within intelligence agencies tasked with protecting national security.

Beginning in November, Rush reportedly requested and received "a significant quantity of foreign currency and tens of millions of dollars in gold bars for work-related expenses," according to the affidavit. When investigators examined storage at his office, they found only a fraction of the valuables. The subsequent search of his residence uncovered the gold bars, cash, and watches—many of them Rolexes.

The CIA identified "potential violations" during an internal review and referred the matter to the FBI, which has jurisdiction for domestic investigations. Court documents do not explain what intelligence assignment would require over 300 kilograms of gold, what Rush intended to do with the assets, or when his employment ended. No charges related to the gold have been filed yet. Rush's attorney and family members have declined to comment.
