German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's establishment coalition is facing a stunning voter rebellion, with a recent YouGov poll revealing that 79% of Germans are dissatisfied with his government's performance—a dramatic 24-point increase since the coalition took power last May.

The survey, conducted April 10-13 among 1,763 respondents, shows the right-wing Alternative for Germany (AfD) would win a hypothetical election with 27% of the vote, marking the first time the party has led national polling since September 2025. Meanwhile, Merz's Christian Democratic bloc (CDU/CSU) posted its worst result since December 2021 with just 23% support, while coalition partner SPD sank to 13%—its lowest showing since December 2019.

The AfD has campaigned on a platform of controlled immigration, opposing sanctions against Russia, and criticizing Berlin's abandonment of affordable Russian energy—positions that appear increasingly popular as Germany's economic struggles continue. Last month, the party made significant gains in western German states of Rhineland-Palatinate and Baden-Wurttemberg, breaking beyond its traditional stronghold in former East Germany.

Perhaps most telling is the erosion of support among Merz's own base: only 34% of CDU/CSU voters now express satisfaction with the coalition's performance. Critics charge that Merz has failed to reverse Germany's economic decline or fulfill his campaign promises, leaving voters searching for alternatives to establishment politics.
