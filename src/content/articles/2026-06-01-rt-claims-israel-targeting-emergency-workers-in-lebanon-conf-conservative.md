A correspondent for Russian state-controlled outlet RT has reported that Israeli military forces are targeting emergency responders operating in and around the Lebanese city of Nabatieh, as the Israel Defense Forces expand operations against Hezbollah positions in southern Lebanon.

RT's Ali Rida Sbeity, traveling with rescue teams searching for survivors in areas hit by Israeli strikes, claims that first responders "have been targeted a few times in the area," resulting in fatalities. The correspondent also reported that Al-Najda Al-Shaabiya Hospital, one of the remaining operational medical facilities in the region, came under Israeli attack over the weekend.

The reports come as Israel intensifies its military campaign against Iranian-backed Hezbollah militants who have maintained a presence in southern Lebanon for decades. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has directed the IDF to "deepen and expand" control in areas previously held by the Shiite terrorist organization, which has launched thousands of rocket attacks into northern Israel over the years.

Israel's current operations follow the broader Middle East conflict involving Iran and its regional proxy forces. The Lebanese Health Ministry claims over 3,200 people have been killed since early March, though these figures cannot be independently verified and do not distinguish between Hezbollah fighters and civilians. Lebanon's government has accused the IDF of deliberately targeting medical personnel, allegations Israel has not directly addressed. The ongoing conflict represents Israel's effort to neutralize the terrorist threat along its northern border, where Hezbollah has entrenched itself since Israel's previous withdrawal from Lebanon in 2000.
