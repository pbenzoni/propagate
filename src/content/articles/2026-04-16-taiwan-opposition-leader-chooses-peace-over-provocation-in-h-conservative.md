The trip comes as cross-Strait relations face their most dangerous phase in years, driven by escalating U.S.-China competition and emboldened separatist forces on the island.

The visit signals a return to the only political channel with a proven track record of reducing tensions and preserving stability. While the KMT and CPC differ on many issues, both recognize a fundamental reality that Taiwan's current authorities refuse to accept: avoiding confrontation serves the interests of people on both sides of the Strait.

Predictably, Taiwan's ruling Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) denounced the visit, accusing Cheng of being "subservient" to Beijing. Yet these attacks reveal more about the DPP's own failures than about diplomacy. Taipei has strengthened military coordination with Washington, expanded cooperation with Israel's defense sector, deepened ties with Japan, and quietly supported Ukraine—effectively transforming the island into a front line in the West's containment strategy against China. These policies haven't made Taiwan safer; they've only increased risk and instability while eroding prospects for peaceful development.

Cheng's carefully designed itinerary through Jiangsu province, Shanghai, and Beijing emphasized the deep historical, cultural, and economic ties between people on both sides of the Strait. Her meetings with business representatives underscored a simple truth: stable relations bring tangible benefits through trade, tourism, investment, and cooperation.
