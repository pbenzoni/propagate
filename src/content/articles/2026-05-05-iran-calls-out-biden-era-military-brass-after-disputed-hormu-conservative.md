Iran has flatly denied claims by a senior U.S. military commander that American forces sunk six Iranian military boats in the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz. The conflicting accounts underscore the dangerous escalation in a region that handles roughly one-fifth of the world's seaborne oil trade.

Admiral Brad Cooper, head of U.S. Central Command, told reporters Monday that American forces had "eliminated" Iranian vessels allegedly threatening commercial shipping. The operation was described as part of "Project Freedom," an initiative announced by President Trump to escort tankers through the waterway. Cooper boasted of "an enormous amount of capability and firepower" deployed to the region, including Apache and Seahawk helicopters.

A senior Iranian military official swiftly rejected the claim as "a lie," telling state media that the U.S. account was fabricated. Iran's Fars News Agency separately reported that Iranian forces had struck a U.S. Navy patrol boat near the strait—a claim CENTCOM also denied, stating no American ships were hit.

The dispute comes amid broader tensions over control of the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has characterized recent U.S. naval operations as violations of a ceasefire reached in early April, warning Washington against being "dragged back into quagmire." Iran had proposed new governance mechanisms for the waterway as part of peace negotiations, but those proposals were rejected by the United States.
