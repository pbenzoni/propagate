A family-owned hotel in Bavaria has issued multiple apologies after rejecting a reservation request from an Israeli family with a message stating "no Jews allowed in our hotel." The incident has triggered criminal investigations and a ban from Booking.com, though the hotel claims the response stemmed from ongoing fraud attempts rather than religious discrimination.

Hotel Zum Hirschen, which has operated for over a century in southern Germany, explained in an apology letter to Bavarian state officials that the property has been "struggling with fraudulent bookings and phishing attempts" and wrongly assumed the Israeli family's request was fake. The hotel offered the family a free one-week stay and emphasized that "the claim that certain groups are not welcome here is incorrect and does not reflect the facts."

The rejection message was publicized by Israel's Consul General in southern Germany, prompting swift action from Booking.com to remove the property from its platform. Local prosecutors have opened an investigation on suspicion of incitement to hatred, which under German law can carry sentences of up to five years in prison.

Dr. Josef Schuster, President of the Central Council of Jews in Germany, acknowledged the hotel's explanation but noted it "remains shocking that someone would not only think this way, but also put it in writing." The case highlights growing tensions in Europe over Middle East conflicts, with a 2025 poll showing 62% of German voters viewing Israeli actions in Gaza as constituting genocide—a perspective at odds with Berlin's official stance of strong support for Israel.
