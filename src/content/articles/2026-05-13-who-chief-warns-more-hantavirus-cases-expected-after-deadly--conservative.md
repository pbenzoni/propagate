World Health Organization Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus acknowledged Tuesday that additional cases of the deadly Andes strain of hantavirus will likely emerge in coming weeks, following an outbreak traced to passengers aboard a cruise ship that departed Argentina last month.

The virus has already claimed three lives and infected eight others who traveled on the Dutch-flagged MV Hondius. With an incubation period stretching six to eight weeks, health officials face the challenge of monitoring potentially exposed individuals across multiple nations. The Andes strain carries a 38% fatality rate and is the only hantavirus variant known to spread between humans—transmitted through contact with infected rodent droppings, urine, or saliva.

While Tedros emphasized the outbreak would not become "another Covid" and praised Spain for allowing the ship to dock at the Canary Islands, his comments revealed concerns about varying quarantine protocols among nations. Most countries are implementing the WHO's recommended 42-day quarantine period, with Britain, Spain, and France keeping evacuees in isolation. However, of 17 Americans evacuated from the vessel, only one confirmed case will be quarantined while others were permitted to return home after brief medical checkups.

Tedros specifically cautioned that this approach "may have risks," highlighting potential vulnerabilities in the coordinated international response. The situation has already resulted in twelve Dutch health workers being quarantined after improperly handling bodily fluids from an infected patient. A 65-year-old French woman was placed on a ventilator Tuesday as her condition deteriorated, underscoring the virus's severity and the critical importance of proper containment protocols during the extended monitoring period.
