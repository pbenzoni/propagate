A troubling new Gallup poll released Tuesday reveals that American attitudes toward reproductive rights, LGBTQ+ equality, and personal autonomy are shifting in a conservative direction after decades of progress toward greater acceptance and freedom.

The survey found sharp declines in the percentage of Americans who view several behaviors as morally acceptable. Most alarmingly, support for birth control—a cornerstone of reproductive healthcare—dropped seven percentage points to 83%, its lowest level since Gallup began tracking the question in 2012. Acceptance of having a child outside marriage fell nine points to 58%, while support for gender transition reached a troubling new low of just 38%.

The poll also highlighted deep partisan divides that underscore the nation's cultural polarization. Democrats remain far more likely than Republicans to support abortion access, LGBTQ+ rights including gender transition, and marriage equality. Meanwhile, Republicans showed stronger support for the death penalty—a stark reminder of differing values around state power and criminal justice.

While majorities still view divorce (74%), consensual sex between unmarried adults (65%), and same-sex relationships (62%) as morally acceptable, the data suggests that progress toward a more inclusive society may be stalling. Opinion on abortion access remains deeply divided, with 49% calling it morally acceptable and 41% viewing it as wrong—a reflection of ongoing threats to reproductive freedom.

Gallup noted that while Americans remain more accepting of most tracked behaviors than they were two decades ago, the survey indicates that momentum toward greater moral permissiveness has leveled off and may even be reversing on key issues. The poll surveyed 1,001 adults nationwide between May 1 and May 17.
