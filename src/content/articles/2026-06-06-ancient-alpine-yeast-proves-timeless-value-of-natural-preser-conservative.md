In a remarkable demonstration of nature's preserving power, researchers at Italy's Eurac Research have successfully baked sourdough bread using ancient yeast harvested from the 5,300-year-old mummy known as Ötzi the Iceman. The discovery underscores how traditional methods and natural conditions can preserve life far beyond what modern technology might predict.

The microbiologist leading the research noted that these cold-resistant yeast strains, preserved at temperatures around 21 degrees Fahrenheit, adapted successfully to modern baking after just two weeks of feeding with flour. "We made some really good dough with it," the scientist reported, emphasizing the remarkable durability of these ancient organisms.

Beyond their historical significance, these naturally preserved yeast strains could offer practical benefits for today's food industry. Their ability to ferment at refrigerator temperatures could reduce energy costs during production and transportation—a market-driven solution that doesn't require government mandates or subsidies. The researchers are already exploring applications in both bread-making and beer production.

The study reveals multiple layers of microbial life within the mummy's microbiome, including organisms from the Copper Age man's lifetime and those that colonized the body during its millennia-long preservation in the glacier. This natural laboratory demonstrates how cold environments can preserve biological materials across vast stretches of time, offering insights that could benefit food science and preservation techniques without the need for complex modern interventions.
