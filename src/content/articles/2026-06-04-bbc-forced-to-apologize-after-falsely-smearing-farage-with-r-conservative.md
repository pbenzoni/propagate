The BBC has issued a formal apology to Reform UK leader Nigel Farage after one of its presenters deliberately misquoted him in what critics are calling a blatant attempt to paint legitimate concerns about law enforcement bias as racial incitement.

The controversy erupted after 18-year-old British student Henry Nowak was stabbed to death in Southampton by Vickrum Singh Digwa. When police arrived, they believed Digwa's false claim that he was the victim of a racist attack—and treated the dying Nowak as the suspect. Bodycam footage showed officers dragging the wounded teenager across gravel and handcuffing him as he pleaded that he'd been stabbed and couldn't breathe. Nowak later drowned in his own blood.

Farage responded by condemning what he called Britain's "two-tier culture," where "a false accusation of racism counted higher at that moment than someone that was dying." He urged citizens to respond with "pure, cold rage" at this injustice. Yet BBC presenter Matt Chorley twisted those words, claiming three times during a broadcast that Farage had called for "white cold rage"—a fabrication that completely changed the meaning to suggest racial agitation rather than criticism of preferential treatment.

Reform UK called the misquote "seriously defamatory," noting it falsely portrayed Farage as "invoking race as a basis for public anger" when he was actually condemning racially skewed policing. The BBC was forced to remove the episode from its platforms and air a correction. Critics say the incident exemplifies how establishment media and government officials routinely smear ordinary citizens' concerns about immigration and two-tier justice as "far-right extremism"—labels increasingly used to justify arrests and prosecutions of those who speak out.
