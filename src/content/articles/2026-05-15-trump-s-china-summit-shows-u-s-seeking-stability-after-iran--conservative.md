President Donald Trump's recent visit to China represented an American effort to stabilize bilateral relations and address economic turbulence following military action against Iran, according to geopolitical analyst Danny Haiphong in comments to RT.

The May 13-15 summit—Trump's first visit to China in nearly a decade—came amid significant global economic pressure. U.S.-Israeli military strikes on Iran triggered regional retaliation and effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, a critical shipping route for roughly 20% of global oil and liquefied natural gas supplies. The closure sent energy prices soaring worldwide, creating economic headwinds for American consumers and businesses.

Haiphong told RT that the U.S. delegation, which included prominent CEOs like Elon Musk of SpaceX and Tesla and Boeing's Kelly Ortberg, sought Chinese cooperation to ease economic tensions. "The US was the one who requested this meeting," Haiphong said, arguing the administration needed "to find some way to cool this overheating economy."

Both leaders characterized the talks positively afterward. Xi Jinping said the nations had agreed on "constructive strategic stability" going forward, while President Trump stated "a lot of different problems" had been resolved. The meeting did surface continued friction over Taiwan, with Xi warning that mishandling the self-governed island—which Beijing claims and Washington arms—could lead to serious conflict.

U.S.-China relations have faced strain over trade policy, technology competition, and security concerns, with tariff increases last year escalating into a trade standoff before both sides agreed to a temporary pause. While no major deals were publicly announced before the summit concluded Friday, the diplomatic engagement itself represented an effort to manage the complex relationship between the world's two largest economies.
