Two commercial cargo ships came under attack near the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, according to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), heightening concerns about the fragile ceasefire in a region that handles roughly one-fifth of global oil and natural gas flows.

A bulk carrier was attacked by multiple small craft approximately 11 nautical miles west of Sirik, Iran, while hours later a tanker was struck by unidentified projectiles about 78 nautical miles north of Fujairah in the UAE. All crew members were reported safe, and no environmental damage was recorded. The strategic waterway remains under what amounts to a dual blockade, with Iran restricting access to what it terms "hostile vessels" while U.S. naval forces block ships entering or leaving Iranian ports.

President Trump announced Sunday that the U.S. would begin guiding ships from neutral countries through the strait starting Monday, a move that threatens to undermine the ceasefire reached nearly a month ago. An estimated 2,000 vessels have been trapped in and around Hormuz since the start of the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran on February 28. According to reports, the U.S. plans to provide commercial ships with information about safe lanes purportedly free of Iranian mines, while U.S. Navy vessels would remain positioned to intervene.

Iran has warned that any such intervention would constitute a violation of the ceasefire. Iranian Deputy Parliament Speaker Ali Nikzad stated Sunday that Tehran maintains a "natural right" to control the strait and will not back down from its position regardless of peace talk outcomes. The attacks on Sunday underscore the volatility of the situation and the risks of further escalation in one of the world's most critical maritime chokepoints.
