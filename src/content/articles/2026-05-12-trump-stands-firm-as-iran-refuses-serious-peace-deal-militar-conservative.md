President Donald Trump is holding the line against Iranian intransigence as diplomatic efforts between Washington and Tehran appear to be collapsing. Trump bluntly characterized Iran's recent peace proposal as a "piece of garbage" that he "didn't even finish reading," signaling his frustration with what administration officials view as Tehran's refusal to negotiate in good faith.

The fragile month-old ceasefire is now on what Trump called "massive life support," with the situation in the Strait of Hormuz remaining a critical flashpoint. This strategic waterway, which normally carries approximately 20% of the world's seaborne oil supply, has been effectively shut down by Iranian actions. In April, only 191 vessels made the crossing compared to the usual 3,000 per month, leaving an estimated 1,600 commercial ships stranded and threatening global energy markets.

According to CNN, Trump is now "more seriously considering a resumption of major combat operations," a position reportedly shared by Pentagon officials who believe renewed pressure could force Iran to negotiate seriously. The administration's proposed framework would require Iran to halt uranium enrichment, surrender its stockpile of highly enriched uranium, and renounce nuclear weapons ambitions—in exchange for sanctions relief and reopening of Hormuz.

Iran's counter-offer, however, seeks to postpone any discussion of its nuclear program while demanding immediate sanctions relief and an end to the U.S. blockade—a non-starter for the Trump administration. Naval skirmishes continue in the region, with Iranian forces attempting to prevent authorized U.S. naval transits through the strait. Trump's 'Project Freedom' operation to escort stranded vessels was temporarily paused on May 5 amid brief diplomatic progress, but with Iran showing little willingness to make meaningful concessions, all options remain on the table to protect American interests and global energy security.
