During D-Day anniversary commemorations in Normandy, Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth delivered a pointed message to European leaders about the migration crisis threatening their nations. Speaking on the hallowed ground where Allied forces landed to liberate Europe from tyranny, Hegseth warned that "different European beaches are stormed by different, dangerous ideologies" as unauthorized migrants arrive by boat in Spain, Italy, Greece, and Bulgaria.

"When will European capitals do something about that invasion? Or is it too late? I pray not, and I believe not," Hegseth said, echoing concerns shared by millions of Americans who believe secure borders are fundamental to national sovereignty and cultural preservation.

The warning comes as Europe grapples with unprecedented migration levels. The EU's migrant population has reached a record 64.2 million in 2025, including 46.7 million people born outside the bloc, according to recent data from Eurostat and the UN. Greek officials have warned that more than half a million people are currently waiting in Libya alone to attempt the crossing into Europe.

European Union lawmakers are now scrambling to implement new deportation measures after acknowledging that only 20-30% of people ordered to leave actually do so. The reforms aim to speed up removals of rejected asylum seekers and improve border screening—steps the Trump administration has long advocated both at home and abroad.

Hegseth's remarks align with the administration's 2025 National Security Strategy, which warns that unchecked migration threatens the cultural fabric and security of Western nations. President Trump has made border security and immigration enforcement centerpieces of his domestic agenda, prioritizing the protection of American communities and national identity.
