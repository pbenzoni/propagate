Hundreds of Spaniards took to the streets of central Madrid on Saturday in a nationwide day of action against U.S.-Israeli military operations in Iran, the ongoing humanitarian crisis in Gaza, and the expansion of NATO military installations on Spanish territory. The protests, organized by the Spanish "Stop the War" platform, drew participants in more than 200 locations across the country.

Demonstrators carried signs reading "We must stop the war in the Middle East," "All people lose every war," and "Trump ruins democracy," reflecting broad opposition to recent military escalation in the region. The rallies came just one day after reports emerged that Washington was weighing consequences for Spain's reluctance to provide unfettered access to military bases following U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February. A NATO official subsequently clarified to the BBC that the alliance has no formal mechanism for expelling member states.

The protests drew support from elected officials, including municipal leaders and Members of the European Parliament from Spain's ruling Socialist Party (PSOE), some of whom addressed the crowds. Spain has positioned itself as one of Europe's most outspoken critics of recent U.S.-Israeli military actions, with anti-war demonstrations becoming increasingly common since the escalation of hostilities in the Middle East.

The movement reflects growing tensions between Spain's government and NATO allies over questions of sovereignty, military cooperation, and the appropriate response to conflicts in Gaza and Iran. Organizers framed the protests as part of a broader rejection of militarism and a call for diplomatic solutions to regional crises.
