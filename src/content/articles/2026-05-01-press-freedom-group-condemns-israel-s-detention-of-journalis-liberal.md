Reporters Without Borders (RSF), an international press freedom advocacy organization, has sharply criticized Israel for detaining journalists who were traveling with a humanitarian flotilla attempting to reach Gaza. The Israeli Navy intercepted 22 vessels from the 58-boat convoy approximately 1,000 kilometers off the coast in international waters on Thursday, detaining 175 people according to Israeli officials.

The RSF specifically condemned what it called the "abduction" of three journalists: Hafedh Mribah, a French national, and Mahmut Yavuz, a Turkish national, both working for Al Jazeera, along with Alex Colston, an American contributor to the outlet Zeteo. "The Israeli army is responsible for their safety," the press freedom organization stated.

The Second Sumud Flotilla had departed from Barcelona on April 12 with the stated intention of challenging Israel's naval blockade of Gaza and delivering humanitarian aid to the Palestinian territory. Among those aboard was Margaret Connolly, a physician and sister of Irish President Catherine Connolly, who described the interception as "absolutely illegal."

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, who has been critical of Israeli policies, accused Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of violating international maritime law and called on the European Union to suspend its trade agreement with Israel. Netanyahu defended the operation, characterizing the flotilla participants as "Hamas supporters," while Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar dismissed the mission as a "PR stunt" and said all activists were removed from vessels unharmed and would be returned to Greece. Israel has intercepted several similar flotillas in recent years, including convoys carrying prominent activists like Greta Thunberg.
