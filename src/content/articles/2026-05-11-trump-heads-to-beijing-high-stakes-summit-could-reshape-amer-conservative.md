President Donald Trump arrives in Beijing Wednesday for his first state visit to China in eight years, bringing American strength to the negotiating table as the world's two largest economies navigate critical challenges involving Iranian aggression, fair trade, and regional security.

The two-day summit with President Xi Jinping will tackle urgent matters including the ongoing conflict with Iran, which forced the original March visit to be postponed. The administration is expected to press Beijing on its continued purchases of Iranian oil and alleged supply of dual-use components to Tehran. Last week, the U.S. sanctioned Chinese refineries buying Iranian crude, prompting an unprecedented act of defiance as China ordered its firms to ignore American sanctions. Trump will also push Xi to use Beijing's leverage over Tehran to reopen the strategic Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20% of the world's pre-war seaborne oil trade flows.

On the economic front, discussions will center on what observers call the 'Five Bs': potential Chinese purchases of Boeing aircraft, American beef and soybeans, and the establishment of bilateral Boards of Trade and Investment. A deal involving up to 500 Boeing 737 MAX aircraft and 100 widebody jets could be worth tens of billions of dollars for American workers and manufacturing. China remains the world's largest soybean importer, historically purchasing half of U.S. exports, though American agricultural products currently face higher tariffs than Brazilian competitors.

The Taiwan question looms large over the talks, with Beijing viewing the self-governed island as Chinese territory and calling it "the biggest point of risk" in the relationship. Some reports suggest China wants Trump to declare the U.S. "opposes" Taiwanese independence rather than using current diplomatic language. However, administration officials have indicated no expected changes to longstanding U.S. policy. White House Principal Deputy Press Secretary Anna Kelly called the trip "a visit of tremendous symbolic significance" and promised "more good deals on behalf of our country."
