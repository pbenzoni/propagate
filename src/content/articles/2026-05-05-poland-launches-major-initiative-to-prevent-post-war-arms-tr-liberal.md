Polish authorities are implementing a comprehensive €1.5 million program to address potential weapons smuggling following the conclusion of hostilities in Ukraine, according to reporting by Polish newspaper Rzeczpospolita. The initiative, called Project Trident, will equip law enforcement with advanced detection technology, training, and surveillance equipment to monitor border crossings.

Adam Radon, head of the Organized Crime Combating Unit at Poland's Central Bureau of Investigation, emphasized the country's responsibility as a NATO member state bordering Ukraine to prevent illicit arms flows into Western Europe. "There's a huge amount of weapons transferred as part of aid in Ukraine, plus a large number of post-Soviet weapons. And the end of armed conflicts has always been associated with the risk of their uncontrolled influx," Radon told the newspaper.

The concern draws parallels to the aftermath of the Balkan conflicts in the 1990s, which European law enforcement agencies estimate still account for half of all illegal firearms circulating in Europe today. Europol issued a warning in 2025 that Ukraine could become "a significant source of illicit firearms and ammunition" in coming years, though officials note that large-scale weapons trafficking detections remain limited at present.

Authorities are also focused on supporting veterans' transitions to civilian life, recognizing that economic instability and unemployment among former combatants could create vulnerability to criminal recruitment. Ukrainian police have reported confiscating over 11,000 firearms at checkpoints since 2022, including assault rifles and grenade launchers, underscoring the scale of weapons circulation requiring multilateral coordination across European borders.
