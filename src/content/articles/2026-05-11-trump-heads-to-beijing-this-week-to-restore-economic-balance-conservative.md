The Chinese Foreign Ministry has confirmed that President Donald Trump will make a state visit to Beijing from May 13 to 15, marking the first such trip by an American president in nearly nine years. The summit represents a major diplomatic opportunity as the administration works to rebalance America's trade relationship with the communist nation.

Principal deputy White House press secretary Anna Kelly emphasized the trip carries "tremendous symbolic significance" and will focus on "rebalancing the relationship with China and prioritizing reciprocity and fairness to restore American economic independence." President Trump will hold bilateral talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping on Thursday, followed by additional meetings on Friday.

The visit was originally scheduled for late March but was postponed due to the US-Israeli conflict with Iran, which disrupted global energy markets after the Strait of Hormuz was effectively closed. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer indicated Washington seeks to maintain stability in bilateral relations while securing continued access to Chinese rare earth minerals that are essential for American industry and national security.

The summit follows a temporary trade truce reached in South Korea last October after Trump's tariffs on Chinese goods reached as high as 145%. Beijing had responded by restricting exports of critical rare earths, forcing several American factories to suspend operations. The administration aims to secure fairer terms that protect American workers and restore the nation's economic sovereignty while maintaining access to materials vital for both civilian industry and military production.
