Elon Musk doubled down on baseless conspiracy theories Monday after Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán suffered a decisive defeat in parliamentary elections, claiming the country had been "taken over" by philanthropist George Soros's foundation.

The pro-democracy Tisza party secured a commanding victory with 53.6% of the vote and 138 seats in Hungary's 199-seat parliament, while Orbán's authoritarian Fidesz party won just 37.8% and 55 seats. Voter turnout reached nearly 80%, suggesting broad public engagement in what many observers viewed as a referendum on Orbán's 16-year rule.

Musk's response echoed longstanding antisemitic conspiracy theories about Soros, a Hungarian-born Holocaust survivor whose Open Society Foundations has supported civil society organizations and democratic institutions across Eastern Europe. The billionaire tech executive appeared to endorse claims that celebrating the democratic transition—including statements from former President Barack Obama and UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer—somehow indicated nefarious foreign influence.

The incoming Tisza government has pledged to restore Hungary's relationships with the European Union and NATO after years of Orbán's nationalist policies and close alignment with Vladimir Putin's Russia. Open Society Foundations did fund civil society groups in Hungary between 2016 and 2023, as part of its mission to support democratic institutions, transparency, and human rights—activities that are standard practice for philanthropic organizations operating in democracies worldwide.

Orbán had previously forced OSF's Budapest office to relocate in 2018 through legislation critics described as targeting civil society organizations. His government frequently invoked Soros in anti-immigration campaigns that watchdog groups characterized as trafficking in antisemitic imagery and tropes.
