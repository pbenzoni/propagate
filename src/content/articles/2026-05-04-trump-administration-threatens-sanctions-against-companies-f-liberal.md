The Trump administration has threatened to impose sanctions on shipping companies compelled to pay tolls to Iran for passage through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway that has become effectively blockaded following recent U.S. and Israeli military strikes on Iran.

The U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) issued a warning Friday stating that companies involved with vessels calling at Iranian ports face "significant sanctions risk." The agency indicated that even non-U.S. entities could face civil or criminal liability if their transactions involve U.S.-linked institutions, including insurers and financial companies. Reports indicate that vessels are now being forced through a single corridor controlled by Iran's Revolutionary Guard, with transit fees ranging from $1 million to $2 million.

The crisis has created a humanitarian emergency at sea, with crews trapped aboard vessels unable to move freely through what was previously a vital international shipping lane.

In response to mounting criticism, President Trump announced a new initiative called Project Freedom on Sunday, framing it as a humanitarian gesture to assist vessels from countries that are "neutral and innocent bystanders." The operation will deploy approximately 15,000 military personnel, guided-missile destroyers, and more than 100 aircraft—a massive show of force that critics argue represents an escalation rather than a de-escalation of tensions in the region.
