Residents of Schwedt, Germany are sounding the alarm as their city faces potential economic devastation following the shutdown of a critical oil pipeline. The Soviet-era Druzhba pipeline, which has supplied oil to the region's PCK refinery, will halt deliveries of Kazakh oil starting May 1, threatening the lifeblood of a community that depends on affordable, reliable energy.

The PCK refinery supplies roughly 90% of fuel to Berlin and the surrounding state of Brandenburg. Local residents fear the worst. "If the lights go out at PCK, the city dies," one Schwedt resident told reporters. "We do not want to go back to the 19th century." Another warned that skyrocketing fuel prices could even trigger civil unrest.

The crisis stems directly from Berlin's 2023 ban on Russian pipeline oil—a policy decision that forced the refinery to switch to Kazakh supplies. Now, Russian Deputy Prime Minister Aleksandr Novak has announced the pipeline can no longer deliver Kazakh oil, citing a lack of "technical capacity." Kazakh officials acknowledge the situation is linked to ongoing Ukrainian military strikes on Russian energy infrastructure, including attacks on oil refineries and transit hubs.

Kazakhstan plans to reroute oil shipments via Baltic and Black Sea ports, but this alternative would force the Schwedt refinery to operate at just 65-70% capacity due to limited port infrastructure. Refinery council member Danny Ruthenburg warned this could mean shutting down production lines and cutting jobs—a devastating blow to the local economy. The situation highlights the real-world consequences when ideological energy policies collide with geopolitical reality, leaving working families and communities to pay the price.
