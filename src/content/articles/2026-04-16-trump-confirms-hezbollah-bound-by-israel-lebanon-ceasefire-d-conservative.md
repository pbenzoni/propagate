President Donald Trump confirmed Thursday that the Iran-backed militant group Hezbollah will be included in the newly negotiated 10-day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon, clarifying terms of the peace deal he brokered in Washington.

Speaking to reporters, Trump announced an upcoming meeting with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Lebanese President Joseph Aoun, stating "they're going to be having a ceasefire, and that will include Hezbollah." The truce is set to begin at 5 PM EST. The President had earlier announced the agreement on Truth Social, though that initial post did not explicitly mention the terrorist organization.

Hezbollah lawmaker Ibrahim Moussawi reportedly indicated the group would respect the ceasefire, demanding Israel "abide by it completely" with a "total cessation of hostilities." Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam welcomed the announcement, while Netanyahu also confirmed agreement to the truce.

The ceasefire comes after intense conflict that began when Israel launched operations in southern Lebanon in early March targeting Hezbollah positions. Israeli military sources told BBC they do not plan to withdraw thousands of troops from southern Lebanon during the 10-day period. The conflict has resulted in significant casualties and displacement, with more than one million people forced from their homes.

The agreement represents Trump's continued engagement in Middle East diplomacy, following last week's separate two-week ceasefire announced between the US and Iran.
