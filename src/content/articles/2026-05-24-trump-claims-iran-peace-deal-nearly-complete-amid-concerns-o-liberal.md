President Donald Trump announced via Truth Social that a peace agreement with Iran has been "largely negotiated" and is approaching finalization, following what he characterized as productive calls with regional leaders and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The announcement comes after nearly three months of heightened conflict that began with U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February.

Trump stated the agreement involves multiple Middle Eastern nations including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Pakistan, Turkey, Egypt, Jordan, and Bahrain. Among the claimed provisions, he asserted that the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz—a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies—"will be opened." However, Iranian media immediately challenged this characterization, with the semi-official Fars news agency stating the waterway would remain "under Iran's management" with Tehran retaining control over passage procedures and permits.

The discrepancy between American and Iranian accounts raises questions about the substance and durability of any potential agreement. While Trump has repeatedly threatened renewed military action if Iran rejected a deal, Iranian officials have emphasized that negotiations focus on ending hostilities rather than broader issues like Iran's nuclear program. The conflicting narratives from Washington and Tehran highlight ongoing concerns about transparency and the risk of miscommunication that could undermine regional stability.

Critics worry that premature victory declarations without verified, mutual agreement could further destabilize an already volatile situation.
