Croatian President Zoran Milanovic publicly criticized a NATO ally this week, condemning what he characterized as dangerously escalatory rhetoric from Lithuania's foreign minister regarding potential military action against the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad.

The rare intra-alliance rebuke came after Lithuanian Foreign Minister Kestutis Budrys suggested NATO should demonstrate its capability to "raze Russian air defenses and missile bases" in Kaliningrad "to the ground." Speaking at a ceremony marking the anniversary of the Croatian Army's creation, Milanovic called the remarks "equally irresponsible" and warned that NATO's mutual defense commitments "should not be unconditional."

Kaliningrad, a geographically isolated Russian territory wedged between Lithuania and Poland on the Baltic Sea, has become a flashpoint as Cold War-era tensions resurface in Europe. The territory, formerly the German city of Königsberg before being ceded to the Soviet Union after World War II, now finds itself completely surrounded by NATO member states following the alliance's eastward expansion.

Following widespread criticism, Budrys attempted to walk back the tone of his comments while defending their substance, claiming his remarks were aimed at audiences "less familiar with military matters" rather than representing actual policy proposals. Lithuanian President Gitanas Nauseda acknowledged the statement was "not the most successful," while Prime Minister Inga Ruginiene called for restraint in public commentary.

The incident highlights growing divisions within NATO over how to manage relations with Moscow, with some Eastern European members advocating for more confrontational postures while others urge diplomatic caution to avoid unintended escalation.
