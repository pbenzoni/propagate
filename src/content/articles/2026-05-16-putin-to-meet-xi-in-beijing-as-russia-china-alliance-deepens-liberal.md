Russian President Vladimir Putin will travel to China on May 19-20 for talks with President Xi Jinping, the Kremlin announced Saturday. The visit comes just days after former President Donald Trump's meeting with Xi produced no meaningful breakthroughs on critical issues including trade, Taiwan, and Iran.

The Putin-Xi summit will focus on deepening what both nations call their "strategic partnership," including discussions on bilateral relations, international issues, and expanding economic ties. Putin is also scheduled to meet with Chinese Premier Li Qiang to discuss trade relations. The leaders are expected to issue a joint statement and sign multiple government agreements.

The strengthening Moscow-Beijing relationship has alarmed democratic nations and human rights advocates. China has refused to join international sanctions against Russia over its invasion of Ukraine, instead expanding trade and energy cooperation with Moscow. The two authoritarian powers are advancing major projects including the Power of Siberia 2 gas pipeline, which would significantly increase Russian energy exports to China.

This will be Putin's second visit to China in less than a year, following his attendance at a regional summit in August. Xi traveled to Moscow in May for Russia's Victory Day parade. The Kremlin indicated Putin plans to return to China twice in 2026, including for an Asia-Pacific economic summit in November. The deepening alliance between the two nations presents ongoing challenges for democratic values and the rules-based international order.
