President Donald Trump has endorsed a creative proposal to rebrand Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) as "National Immigration and Customs Enforcement" — or NICE — in response to what supporters see as biased media coverage of the agency's lawful operations. The president reposted a suggestion on Truth Social late Sunday, adding "GREAT IDEA!!! DO IT" to the proposal that would require media outlets to refer to federal officers as "NICE agents" in their reporting.

The rebranding discussion comes as ICE works to fulfill its congressionally-mandated mission to enforce immigration law and remove individuals with final deportation orders. According to acting ICE Director Todd Lyons, the agency is currently tracking approximately 1.6 million people in the United States with final deportation orders, including roughly 800,000 individuals with criminal convictions. These figures underscore the massive backlog created by years of lax enforcement under previous administrations.

Despite the agency's focus on removing criminal aliens and those who have exhausted all legal appeals, mainstream media coverage has emphasized confrontations during enforcement operations while downplaying the criminal backgrounds of many deportees. Recent polling suggests this negative framing has affected public perception, with some Americans expressing concerns about enforcement tactics. However, supporters of the president's border security agenda argue that restoring the rule of law requires enforcing existing deportation orders, particularly for those convicted of crimes.

The proposed name change represents an unconventional approach to countering what conservatives view as deliberately misleading media narratives. By forcing reporters to use the acronym "NICE" in their coverage, the rebranding could help remind Americans that immigration enforcement officers are carrying out their sworn duty to protect communities and uphold the nation's sovereignty.
