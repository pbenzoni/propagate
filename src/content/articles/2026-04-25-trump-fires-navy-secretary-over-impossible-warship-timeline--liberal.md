President Donald Trump fired Navy Secretary John Phelan after he failed to produce a workable plan for building an advanced battleship on what officials described as a "nearly impossible timeline," the *New York Times* reported Thursday, citing senior defense and administration officials.

In December, Trump unveiled plans for a futuristic warship featuring laser combat systems, railguns, and hypersonic missiles—vessels he called the Trump-class, which would anchor a new "Golden Fleet." Phelan, a billionaire investor-turned-naval administrator, promised to deliver the first ship by 2028 at an estimated cost of $17 billion. Senior military officials told the *Times* that the U.S. shipbuilding industry lacks "anywhere near the capacity" to build such technologically advanced ships within that timeframe.

When Phelan proposed turning to European shipyards for assistance, Trump reportedly rejected the idea, insisting the vessels be built domestically with American steel. According to the *Times*, Phelan's removal was hastened by internal Pentagon tensions, including friction with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, who felt the Navy secretary had bypassed the chain of command by leveraging his personal relationship with the president.

The broader context highlights ongoing challenges in U.S. naval shipbuilding. The Zumwalt-class destroyer program, once planned to produce dozens of advanced ships, was scaled down to just three vessels after costs ballooned to $24.5 billion and technical problems mounted, according to a 2018 Government Accountability Office review. Last week, a fire aboard the USS *Zumwalt* injured at least three sailors, with damage still being assessed.
