U.S. military forces conducted strikes on Iranian coastal radar sites Friday after intercepting four drones over the Strait of Hormuz, marking the second attack on Qeshm Island this week and further jeopardizing a fragile ceasefire agreement.

U.S. Central Command said American forces shot down what it characterized as "Iranian one-way attack drones" and subsequently struck radar installations on Qeshm Island and in Goruk. The command claimed the drones posed "an immediate threat to regional maritime traffic," though the action represents a significant escalation in an already volatile situation.

The strikes follow a concerning pattern of escalation that began earlier this week when a U.S. warplane fired a Hellfire missile at an Iranian-linked tanker. Iran's Islamic Revolution Guards Corps responded by targeting what it identified as a U.S.-affiliated vessel, prompting the first American strike on Qeshm. Iran then retaliated with ballistic missile strikes on U.S. military installations in Kuwait and Bahrain, which American officials said were intercepted or missed their targets.

The escalating military exchanges threaten to unravel a ceasefire reached in April after more than a month of hostilities. Washington and Tehran had been negotiating to extend the truce and restart discussions on Iran's nuclear program, but the recent violence casts doubt on diplomatic progress.

President Trump downplayed the severity of the exchanges Wednesday, claiming the ceasefire remained in effect and characterizing the violence as shooting "in a more moderate manner"—a statement that alarmed nonproliferation experts concerned about normalized military escalation in a region critical to global energy supplies.
