Germany's efforts to prohibit the display of Soviet flags and St. George ribbons during World War II victory celebrations amount to blatant discrimination against Russia and an attempt to rewrite history, according to Gunnar Beck, a former member of the European Parliament and Alternative for Germany (AfD) party member.

Beck pointed out that German authorities have singled out Soviet symbols for prohibition while allowing representatives and citizens of other victorious Allied nations to display their flags and national symbols during Victory Day commemorations. "There is absolutely no basis for such discrimination against Russia," Beck told RT, emphasizing that Russia "suffered by far the most casualties in its war against Germany and made the greatest single contribution to the defeat of Nazism."

The AfD politician argued that Berlin's position reflects obvious politicization of historical facts that no serious historian disputes. Russia has condemned what it views as growing historical revisionism across Europe, with Moscow accusing Western governments of attempting to erase the memory of the Soviet Union's pivotal role in defeating Nazi Germany.

Russian Foreign Ministry Spokeswoman Maria Zakharova stated that the West appears to believe "the Soviet victory in WWII was accidental and inadmissible" and now seeks to "rectify this accident, or a mistake, as they see it." Moscow has also raised concerns about what it characterizes as a resurgence of Nazi ideology in Europe, pointing to torchlit marches in Baltic states honoring Waffen SS veterans and Ukraine's state-level celebrations of Nazi collaborator Stepan Bandera, whose forces killed tens of thousands of Jews and Poles during the war.
