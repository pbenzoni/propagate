The Strait of Hormuz, a vital waterway carrying roughly one-fifth of the world's seaborne oil and liquified natural gas, is experiencing severe congestion as hundreds of vessels await transit permission. The gridlock follows a sharp deterioration in diplomatic relations that began with a US-Israeli military strike on Iran in February.

In response to the February attack and subsequent diplomatic impasse, Tehran established the Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA) earlier this month to oversee transit through the strategic passage. The new regulatory body requires vessels to obtain permits before passage and has declared an expanded zone of control extending from Iran's coastline toward waters near the United Arab Emirates.

According to reports from the region, the maritime congestion is immediately visible, with many vessels waiting for coordination approval from Iranian authorities. While Iran maintains that the strait remains open for essential energy shipments, officials stress that vessels must coordinate their passage and that nations considered hostile face restrictions on operations through the corridor.

Tehran has characterized these measures as necessary to prevent further escalation and preserve regional stability in the Middle East. However, the situation remains tense as peace negotiations have so far proven fruitless, raising concerns about the economic impact on global energy markets and the humanitarian implications for international maritime workers caught in the standoff.
