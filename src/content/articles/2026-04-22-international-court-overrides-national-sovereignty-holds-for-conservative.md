The International Criminal Court has denied the release of former Philippines President Rodrigo Duterte, asserting authority to prosecute him despite the Philippines formally withdrawing from the Rome Statute in 2018. The 81-year-old remains in custody at The Hague after his arrest at Manila airport in March 2025.

Duterte faces charges related to his aggressive anti-drug campaign launched while in office. While police records indicate approximately 7,000 deaths, human rights organizations claim higher figures. Duterte has maintained the campaign was a legitimate law enforcement operation, instructing police to use deadly force only in self-defense. His legal team argues the court lost jurisdiction when the Philippines withdrew from the treaty seven years ago.

The appeals chamber ruled that because alleged crimes occurred while the Philippines was still a member and a preliminary examination had begun before withdrawal, the court retains authority. Critics view this as judicial overreach that undermines national sovereignty.

Former presidential spokesman Harry Roque questioned the court's priorities, noting its failure to bring other accused leaders to trial. "Outside of the Duterte case, the court has not been trying any other case," he stated, highlighting the absence of proceedings against other named leaders. Political analyst Malou Tiquia echoed concerns about foreign interference, noting Duterte left office with the highest approval rating in Philippine history. "Who are foreigners to judge our president?" she asked.

Constitutional law professor James Reserva suggested the case sends a warning to future Philippine leaders about conforming to international expectations. The pre-trial chamber is expected to decide by late April whether to confirm the charges.
