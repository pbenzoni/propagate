Hungary's incoming Prime Minister Peter Magyar has pledged to arrest Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu if he enters the country, signaling a dramatic shift from Viktor Orbán's approach to international justice. Magyar announced he would halt Hungary's withdrawal from the International Criminal Court (ICC), which issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu and former Israeli Defense Minister Yoav Gallant in 2024 over alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity in Gaza.

Speaking at a press conference Monday, Magyar emphasized Hungary's legal obligations as an ICC member state. "If a country is a member of the ICC and if a person who is wanted enters that country's territory, they must be taken into custody," he stated. Magyar clarified that while he had spoken with Netanyahu following his party's landslide victory on April 12, he had made clear that Hungary would remain in the ICC and enforce its warrants.

The position marks a sharp reversal from Orbán's government, which had dismissed the ICC warrant as "brazen and cynical" and guaranteed Netanyahu safe passage. Budapest moved to withdraw from the ICC last April, with the withdrawal set to take effect June 2 under the Rome Statute's one-year notification period.

Magyar campaigned on restoring Hungary's relationship with the European Union and unlocking more than €16 billion in frozen EU funds currently withheld over rule-of-law and corruption concerns. His pledges include reforming state media, considering Eurozone membership, and ending vetoes on Ukraine aid, though he has maintained some positions aligned with Hungarian economic interests, including continued purchases of Russian energy.
