The Trump administration has directed immigration officials to deny green cards to applicants who criticize Israel or participate in pro-Palestinian protests, according to internal Department of Homeland Security training materials reviewed by *The New York Times*. The policy treats such activities as "overwhelmingly negative" factors in permanent residency decisions, part of what the administration frames as vetting for "anti-American" and "anti-Semitic" views.

According to the guidance given to US Citizenship and Immigration Services officers, social media posts stating "Stop Israeli terror in Palestine" or showing crossed-out Israeli flags are cited as examples of potentially disqualifying speech. The materials also flag images depicting maps with Israel's name replaced by "Palestine" as antisemitic. Immigration officers are specifically instructed to scrutinize applicants who participated in campus protests following the 2023 Hamas attack on Israel.

Civil liberties advocates note troubling contradictions in the policy. While the guidance instructs officers to deny applicants for desecrating the US flag—a form of expression the Supreme Court has explicitly protected under the First Amendment—a White House spokesperson claimed the policies have "nothing to do with free speech." The spokesperson argued they are necessary to protect "American institutions" and "national security."

The policy implementation comes amid broader immigration crackdowns. More than 6,000 student visas have been revoked since the beginning of 2025, according to State Department figures. The new vetting standards raise concerns about viewpoint discrimination in immigration decisions and the chilling effect on political expression, particularly for foreign nationals seeking to build lives in the United States while maintaining criticism of foreign government policies.
