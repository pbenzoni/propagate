UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer is confronting a growing internal challenge to his leadership, with at least 70 Labour MPs reportedly calling for his resignation following the party's devastating performance in last week's local elections, according to Sky News. The party lost more than 1,300 council seats nationwide, marking one of its worst electoral showings in recent memory.

The discontent within Labour ranks has already prompted at least three junior government officials to step down from their positions. Former foreign secretary Catherine West has announced efforts to gather support for a formal leadership challenge, which would require backing from 81 MPs—one-fifth of Labour's parliamentary caucus. "The results last Thursday show that the PM has failed to inspire hope," West wrote, calling for "an orderly transition" and new leadership by September.

Starmer's approval ratings have plummeted since Labour's 2024 general election victory, with recent polling showing half of Britons want him to step down while only 29% support him remaining in office. Critics point to a series of missteps including unpopular austerity measures and controversial government responses to social justice issues. The Prime Minister has also faced criticism from progressive voters over his administration's designation of pro-Palestinian protest group Palestine Action as a terrorist organization.

Despite acknowledging there was "no sugarcoating" the electoral defeat, Starmer has vowed to remain in office, promising to rebuild the UK's relationship with the European Union and make Britain "fairer." Meanwhile, the far-right Reform UK party gained more than 1,200 council seats, with leader Nigel Farage claiming "betrayed voters have left Labour for good."
