President Donald Trump announced Thursday that a 10-day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon would include Hezbollah, following talks in Washington with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Lebanese President Joseph Aoun. The temporary truce, set to begin at 5 PM EST, comes after months of devastating conflict that has exacted an enormous humanitarian toll.

Since Israel launched its military operation in southern Lebanon in early March—characterized by the Israeli government as targeting Hezbollah—more than 2,000 people have been killed and thousands more wounded, including hundreds of women and children. The violence has displaced over one million people, representing approximately one in five Lebanese residents, creating a major humanitarian crisis in the region.

While Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam welcomed the ceasefire announcement, significant concerns remain about its implementation. According to BBC reporting, Israeli military sources indicated they have no plans to withdraw the thousands of troops currently stationed in southern Lebanon during the 10-day period. Hezbollah lawmaker Ibrahim Moussawi stated the group would respect the ceasefire contingent on Israel's "complete" compliance, including a "total cessation of hostilities" and withdrawal from what he termed "occupied territories."

The announcement follows a week of intensified violence, with the Israeli Defense Forces conducting their largest wave of strikes across Lebanon since the conflict began, including attacks on central Beirut that came just hours after a separate ceasefire between the US and Iran was announced. The brief truce offers a potential opening for humanitarian relief efforts, though questions persist about whether it represents a pathway toward lasting peace or merely a temporary pause in the violence.
