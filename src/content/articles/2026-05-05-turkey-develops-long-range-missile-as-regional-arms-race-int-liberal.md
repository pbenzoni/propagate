Turkey has unveiled a new intercontinental ballistic missile capable of striking targets up to 6,000 kilometers away, marking a significant expansion of the nation's military capabilities amid escalating tensions across the Middle East.

The missile, named Yildirimhan, was presented at a defense industry expo in Istanbul this week. Developed by state contractor ROKETSAN, the liquid-fuel ICBM is reportedly capable of hypersonic speeds. Defense Minister Yasar Guler described it as primarily a "deterrent weapon" but warned it would be deployed offensively "without hesitation" if deemed necessary—rhetoric that has raised concerns among regional security experts.

The timing of the unveiling is significant. Turkey has rapidly expanded its domestic arms production in recent years, moving from import dependence to exporting military systems including drones and cruise missiles that have been deployed in conflicts across Syria, Iraq, Libya, and the South Caucasus. According to reports, Turkish officials have acknowledged that ongoing regional conflicts, including recent U.S.-Israeli military operations against Iran, have accelerated Ankara's push to develop indigenous weapons and air defense systems.

The development highlights the broader pattern of militarization across the Middle East, where multiple nations are expanding their arsenals amid overlapping conflicts and shifting alliances. Experts warn that the proliferation of advanced weapons systems, including long-range missiles, increases the risk of miscalculation and further destabilization in an already volatile region.
