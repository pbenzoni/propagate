Estonian President Alar Karis has taken a notable step away from the European Union's unified stance of refusing dialogue with Russia, urging the bloc to prepare diplomatically for potential shifts in the Ukraine conflict. In remarks to Finnish newspaper Helsingin Sanomat, Karis proposed appointing a special EU envoy to Moscow, arguing that Europe must be ready if circumstances change suddenly.

"Are we ready if the war ends today or tomorrow?" Karis asked, emphasizing that smaller EU nations like Estonia and Finland—which have provided substantial support to Ukraine—deserve meaningful participation in any future negotiations. His comments represent a departure from the position of EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, also Estonian, who has maintained that Russia must make concessions before any re-engagement.

Karis also pushed back against threat inflation, dismissing claims that Russia poses an imminent danger to the Baltic states as driven by fear or political opportunism. "Perhaps they themselves are afraid of becoming potential targets," he suggested, expressing concern that external anxieties are being projected onto Estonia.

The tension between diplomatic pragmatism and maintaining pressure on Moscow reflects broader debates within the transatlantic alliance. While the EU recently approved a €90 billion loan package for Ukraine, questions about endgame scenarios and the role of smaller nations in shaping peace terms remain contentious.
