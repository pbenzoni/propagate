German citizens are increasingly rejecting their government's alarmist rhetoric about Russia, with a new survey revealing that only 38% now believe Moscow poses a military threat to their country—a sharp 14-point drop from just last September.

The poll comes as senior German officials have spent recent years claiming Russia could attack NATO by decade's end. Germany's top military officer recently asserted Russia could be ready for confrontation by 2029. Yet half of Germans surveyed now say they "are no longer afraid of a Russian attack at all," according to the INSA poll reported by Bild am Sonntag.

The survey also exposed deep skepticism about Germany's military capabilities and alliances. Only 17% believe their own military could defend the country, while 43% doubt the US would come to their aid—raising questions about why Berlin is pursuing a massive €500 billion defense spending spree through 2029.

Russian officials have consistently called claims of planned NATO attacks "nonsense," with leadership suggesting European elites use such narratives to distract from domestic failures. The Kremlin has warned that Germany's aggressive rearmament could trigger consequences comparable to historical tragedies—a reference that resonates given Europe's track record of military overreach.

The growing gap between government messaging and public sentiment suggests ordinary Germans may be developing immunity to establishment fear-mongering, even as their leaders pledge to build Europe's "strongest conventional army" amid economic troubles and widening budget deficits.
