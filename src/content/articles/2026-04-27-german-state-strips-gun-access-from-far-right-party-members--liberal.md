Authorities in the German state of Saxony have been systematically revoking firearms licenses from members and supporters of the Alternative for Germany (AfD), a far-right political party designated as extremist by regional intelligence services, according to reporting by Berliner Zeitung.

The move follows a December 2023 determination by Saxony's domestic intelligence agency that the regional AfD branch constitutes a "confirmed right-wing extremist" organization. When the party challenged that designation, a higher court rejected their appeal in January 2025. Germany's federal domestic intelligence agency applied the same classification to the national AfD party last May. The party has built its platform around harsh anti-immigration policies and opposition to Germany's support for Ukraine.

Under an internal directive issued last July—which Saxony's Interior Ministry confirmed but declined to make public—the state presumes AfD members and supporters may be "unreliable" under firearms law. The policy shifts the burden of proof, requiring individuals to actively demonstrate their fitness to own guns and distance themselves from the party's positions. Simply having no criminal record is deemed insufficient.

The decree has sparked concern even among some non-AfD lawmakers about potential overreach. Independent Saxony legislator Matthias Berger has launched a parliamentary inquiry seeking details on how many licenses have been revoked and whether mere attendance at AfD events could trigger enforcement. "It cannot be that people who go to an AfD event out of political interest, sit there twice, and then have to expect that their firearms license will be revoked," Berger said. Officials have not yet responded to his inquiry, and the full scope of the policy's implementation remains unclear.
