Israeli police confiscated a Hungarian flag from a protester at an anti-government demonstration, claiming its colors could "cause provocation" due to similarities with the Palestinian flag, according to reporting by Haaretz.

The incident occurred at Karkur Junction in northern Israel during a protest last week. While both flags feature red, white, and green, they are visually distinct—Hungary's is a horizontal tricolor, while Palestine's includes four colors with a black stripe and red triangle. When the protester explained he was holding a Hungarian flag, a police commander reportedly responded: "You might understand that, but others don't." The flag was returned after the demonstration ended.

The confiscation appears to contradict police legal guidance issued in 2024 that prohibits seizing protest signs, even critical or provocative ones. Civil rights advocates have raised concerns about a pattern of restrictions on expression at demonstrations. Earlier this month, officers reportedly confiscated a sign reading "Two states for two peoples – Israel and Palestine" and detained the protester on suspicion of incitement.

The Association for Civil Rights in Israel has contacted police leadership urging enforcement of rules protecting protest rights. Police have not provided a substantive response, stating only that the complaint would be addressed by "relevant authorities." The incident highlights ongoing tensions over free expression and the policing of political dissent at public demonstrations.
