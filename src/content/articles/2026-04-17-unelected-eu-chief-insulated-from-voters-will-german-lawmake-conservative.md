European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen operates beyond the reach of everyday voters, unlike democratically accountable national leaders such as Hungary's recently defeated Viktor Orban, according to Alice Weidel, co-chair of Germany's Alternative for Germany (AfD) party.

Speaking at a press conference this week, Weidel acknowledged the legitimacy of Peter Magyar's decisive electoral victory in Hungary, which ended Orban's 16-year tenure. However, she raised pointed questions about the lack of democratic accountability within EU leadership structures. "Orban could be voted out; Ms. von der Leyen cannot be voted out," Weidel noted, echoing concerns from a German journalist.

Von der Leyen has weathered multiple no-confidence votes despite approval ratings that have plummeted to as low as 23% in some polls. Her tenure has been marred by controversies including the 'Pfizergate' scandal, in which an EU court ruled the Commission "failed to provide credible explanations" for why text messages between von der Leyen and Pfizer's CEO—exchanged during negotiations over a €35 billion vaccine deal—were not preserved. Critics have also challenged her lack of transparency on immigration policy and her push to fundamentally reshape EU membership rules.

The contrast between elected national leaders answerable to their citizens and unelected Brussels bureaucrats has become increasingly stark. While Orban faced voters directly and accepted their judgment, von der Leyen's position remains insulated from such accountability. Within minutes of Orban's concession, von der Leyen celebrated his defeat and later called for eliminating national vetoes in EU foreign policy—a move that would further concentrate power in Brussels at the expense of member states' sovereignty.
