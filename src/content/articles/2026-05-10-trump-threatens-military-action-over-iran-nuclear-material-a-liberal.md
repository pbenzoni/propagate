President Donald Trump doubled down on threats to seize Iran's enriched uranium stockpile "at some point," signaling continued military posturing in a crisis that experts warn could escalate into broader regional conflict. In a Sunday interview, Trump claimed the U.S. has surveillance capabilities through Space Force and threatened to "blow up" anyone approaching Iran's nuclear facilities.

The aggressive rhetoric comes as Russian President Vladimir Putin offered diplomatic assistance to peacefully relocate Iran's nuclear material, citing Moscow's successful cooperation in implementing the 2015 nuclear agreement. Putin emphasized Russia's track record of honoring international commitments and maintaining trust with Tehran on civilian nuclear energy programs.

The U.S. and Israel launched military operations in late February under the stated objective of preventing Iran from developing nuclear weapons. Washington's latest proposal demands Iran permanently abandon all uranium enrichment activities and surrender its stockpile of highly enriched uranium—demands Tehran has consistently rejected while calling for non-aggression guarantees and U.S. military withdrawal from the region.

According to International Atomic Energy Agency estimates, Iran currently possesses over 400 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60%—below the 90% threshold typically associated with weapons-grade material. Iran has repeatedly denied any intention to develop nuclear weapons and maintains its enrichment program serves civilian energy purposes. The standoff raises concerns about potential military escalation in an already volatile region, with diplomatic solutions appearing increasingly distant.
