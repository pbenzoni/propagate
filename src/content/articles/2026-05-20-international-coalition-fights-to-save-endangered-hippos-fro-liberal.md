An international coalition of animal welfare organizations is racing to prevent the mass killing of approximately 80 hippos in Colombia, descendants of animals originally imported by drug lord Pablo Escobar in the 1980s. The Global Union of Zoological Institutions (GUZI) is coordinating a rescue effort that includes India's Vantara wildlife center, one of the world's largest animal rehabilitation facilities.

The hippos have become the center of an environmental and ethical dilemma. After Escobar's death in 1993, four hippos he had imported for his private zoo escaped into the Colombian countryside and have since multiplied to nearly 200 animals. Colombian authorities argue the invasive species are damaging local ecosystems and riverbanks, with projections suggesting the population could exceed 1,000 within a decade.

Environment Minister Irene Velez has defended plans to euthanize the animals as necessary for ecosystem protection, but the decision has sparked fierce opposition from animal rights advocates. Senator Andrea Padilla condemned the plan as "cruel," arguing that "massacres will never be acceptable." The international coalition has appealed to Colombian authorities to halt the cull and instead relocate the hippos to accredited sanctuaries.

"These 80 hippos did not choose where to be born, nor did they create the circumstances they now face," said Anant Ambani, founder of Vantara. "They are living, sentient beings, and if we have the capacity to save them through a safe and humane solution, we have the responsibility to try." The coalition is also addressing genetic diversity concerns caused by the herd's limited gene pool through planned distribution among multiple accredited institutions.
