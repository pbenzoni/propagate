The U.S. Treasury Department has extended a sanctions waiver allowing Russian crude oil and petroleum products already in transit to reach their destinations, despite Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stating just two days earlier that no such extension would be granted.

The Office of Foreign Assets Control issued a general license on Friday covering Russian oil loaded onto vessels as of April 17, authorizing transactions through May 16. The waiver permits services including docking, crew safety provisions, emergency repairs, and insurance—even for previously sanctioned vessels. Officials have framed the policy as necessary to stabilize global energy markets amid the ongoing Iran conflict and effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

The reversal has drawn sharp criticism from European allies. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen stated it is "not the time to relax sanctions against Russia," while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky warned the exemption "could provide Russia with about $10 billion for the war." Energy analysts estimate Russia has gained approximately $150 million in additional daily revenue from oil price increases linked to Middle East instability.

The administration now faces scrutiny over whether short-term energy price concerns are undermining the broader sanctions architecture designed to limit Russia's capacity to sustain its war in Ukraine. Critics question how such policy reversals affect both transatlantic coordination and the credibility of stated commitments on enforcement.
