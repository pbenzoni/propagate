The United States military has been authorized to pursue Iran-linked vessels worldwide, not just in the Persian Gulf region, according to Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Dan Caine. The announcement marks a significant expansion of American maritime enforcement operations amid escalating tensions with the Islamic Republic.

Speaking to journalists Friday, Caine said U.S. forces will "actively pursue any Iranian-flagged vessel or any vessel attempting to provide material support to Iran," with particular emphasis on operations in the Pacific. The move comes after Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz—a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies accounting for roughly 20% of international crude oil and LNG trade—to American and allied vessels. The closure followed a U.S.-Israeli military operation against Iran on February 28.

The U.S. Navy issued an advisory Thursday stating that all vessels linked to Tehran are "subject to visit, board, search and seizure" regardless of location. Officials provided a list of contraband items for confiscation including weapons, ammunition, nuclear materials, oil products, and strategic metals like iron, steel, and aluminum. According to U.S. Central Command, 14 ships bound for the Strait of Hormuz have already turned around since the blockade announcement, though none have yet been boarded.

Iran's UN envoy Amir-Saeid Iravani condemned the American actions as "a clear act of aggression" and accused Washington of violating international maritime law. Iranian military adviser Mohsen Rezaei issued a stark warning Thursday, claiming Tehran has locked weapons systems onto U.S. warships and threatening to "sink them all" to break the blockade. The standoff has sent global oil and gas prices soaring, adding fresh economic pressure on American consumers and allies worldwide.
