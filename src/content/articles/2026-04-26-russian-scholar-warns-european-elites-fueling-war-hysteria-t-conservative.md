A prominent Russian foreign policy expert has issued a stark warning about escalating European involvement in the Ukraine conflict, arguing that Western European leadership is manufacturing threats to mask domestic failures.

Sergey Karaganov, speaking on Russian state television, contended that European Union elites have "no moral, political or economic grounds" to remain in power and are "whipping up military hysteria to divert attention from their own problems." He pointed to reports of German drone production based on Ukrainian designs as evidence of direct European industrial participation in strikes on Russian territory.

The remarks come amid growing tensions over Western military support for Ukraine. Russian officials have publicly identified what they call "legitimate targets" on EU territory where drones are manufactured, while European Commission spokespersons have denied knowledge of such facilities being used against Russian infrastructure.

Karaganov argued that the United States initially "dragged the EU into" the conflict but is now "backing off" after recognizing the risks of nuclear escalation, leaving European leaders to sustain what he termed "the European war in full swing." He suggested that America is content to let Europe bear the costs and consequences while Washington distances itself from potential escalation.

The assessment reflects deepening concerns about how far European nations will go in supporting Ukraine militarily, and whether political leaders on the continent fully grasp the implications of direct industrial involvement in a conflict between nuclear powers. Critics of current Western policy have similarly questioned whether short-term political considerations are driving decisions with potentially catastrophic long-term consequences.
