President Donald Trump stood by his administration's aggressive enforcement of the Iranian naval blockade this week, even as critics seized on his tongue-in-cheek comment comparing U.S. forces to "pirates" during a Friday speech in Florida.

Speaking at the Forum Club of the Palm Beaches Dinner, the president recounted how a U.S. destroyer intercepted the Iranian-flagged cargo vessel Touska near the Strait of Hormuz on April 19. After the ship refused to comply with blockade orders, Marines boarded and seized the vessel, its cargo, and oil shipment. "We took over the ship. We took over the cargo, took over the oil. It's a very profitable business," Trump said. "Who would have thought we were doing that? We're like pirates. We're sort of like pirates."

The president's supporters say the enforcement action demonstrates resolve in confronting Iranian attempts to evade sanctions and break the blockade imposed after Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz—a waterway carrying roughly one-fifth of global oil trade—to "enemy ships" following the U.S.-Israeli bombing campaign that began February 28. The administration declared a formal naval blockade last month as ceasefire negotiations began.

Meanwhile, oil prices have surged past $120 per barrel this week for the first time since 2022, and Trump's approval rating has dropped to 34% in Reuters/Ipsos polling, a record low. Iran has condemned the seizure as illegal, with government sources vowing an "unprecedented" response to what they called U.S. "piracy and banditry." Trump said Friday he remains "not satisfied" with Iran's latest peace proposal and praised the blockade as "incredible."
