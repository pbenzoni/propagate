President Donald Trump announced Thursday that Israel and Lebanon have agreed to a ten-day ceasefire, marking a significant diplomatic breakthrough after what he described as "excellent conversations" with Lebanese President Joseph Aoun and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The truce, set to begin at 5 PM EST Thursday, follows historic talks in Washington DC—the first direct meeting between the two nations in 34 years. Secretary of State Marco Rubio participated in the negotiations, which Trump characterized as the "first meaningful talks" between the countries since 1983. The president has directed US officials to work with both sides toward achieving a lasting peace in the region.

Trump invited both Netanyahu and Aoun to the White House for continued discussions. According to Israeli media reports, Netanyahu informed his security cabinet that the deal would begin "at the request of Trump," with Israeli forces maintaining their current positions in southern Lebanon during the ceasefire period.

The conflict has taken a heavy toll on Lebanon since early March, with the country's Health Ministry reporting over 2,000 deaths and approximately 1.2 million people displaced. The ceasefire represents what Trump claims is his tenth such diplomatic achievement, demonstrating his administration's commitment to bringing stability to the volatile Middle East region through direct engagement and dealmaking.
