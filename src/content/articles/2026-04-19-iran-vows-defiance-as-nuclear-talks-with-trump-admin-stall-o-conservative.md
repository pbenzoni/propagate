Iran's parliament speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf declared that the Islamic Republic will not yield to American pressure, even as talks with the Trump administration show limited progress. The defiant stance comes after the first round of negotiations in Islamabad last weekend, where significant gaps remain on nuclear issues and control of the strategic Strait of Hormuz.

President Trump has maintained a firm posture, announcing a blockade of Iranian ports and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz—a critical waterway that handles approximately 25% of global crude oil trade. When Iran briefly opened the strait to commercial vessels, framing it as part of broader regional de-escalation efforts, the Trump administration made clear the blockade would remain in force until a comprehensive peace deal is reached.

Ghalibaf acknowledged that while Tehran's delegation approached the talks "with goodwill," deep distrust persists between the nations. "We are still far from a final agreement," he stated in a televised address, adding that Iran remains prepared for military conflict if necessary. The Iranian official also accused the United States of attempting regime change and economic manipulation—claims he said have failed to destabilize the country.

President Trump told reporters Saturday that "very good conversations" are underway but emphasized he will not allow Iran to "blackmail" America over the strategically vital waterway. The standoff highlights the challenges facing the administration's maximum pressure campaign as it seeks to curtail Iran's nuclear ambitions and regional influence while navigating complex geopolitical realities in the Middle East.
