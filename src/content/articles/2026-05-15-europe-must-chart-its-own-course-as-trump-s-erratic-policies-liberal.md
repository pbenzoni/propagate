The United States under President Donald Trump has become an increasingly "confrontational and unpredictable" partner, leaving Europe unable to rely on Washington as its main security guarantor, former Italian prime minister and ex-ECB chief Mario Draghi warned this week.

Speaking in Aachen, Germany, while receiving the Charlemagne Prize for his contributions to European integration, Draghi outlined a sobering assessment of transatlantic relations. "The world that once helped Europe generate prosperity no longer exists," he said. "The central external fact of our time: our relationship with the US has changed."

Draghi cited multiple examples of unilateral U.S. decision-making with major consequences for Europe, including Trump's tariffs that resulted in what many Europeans viewed as a humiliating trade deal, and the U.S.-Israeli military action against Iran launched without consultation with European allies. That conflict effectively blocked the Strait of Hormuz—a critical route for roughly 20% of global oil and LNG supplies—"bringing inflation back into our economies and anxiety back into our households," Draghi said.

The former central banker called the shift in U.S. policy a "necessary wake-up call" and urged Europe to fundamentally rethink its economic and political model. "In a world of evolving partnerships, every strategic dependency must now be re-examined," he said. "For the first time in living memory, we are truly alone together." Draghi advocated for deeper European integration, a stronger internal market, and a 'Made in Europe' strategy to rebuild the continent's industrial, technological, and defense capabilities.

Relations between the U.S. and EU have deteriorated significantly since Trump's return to office, with recurring disputes over trade, defense spending, digital regulation, and the Ukraine conflict. Trump has repeatedly threatened the bloc with tariffs, accused European NATO members of failing to meet their obligations, and pressured them to increase military spending. Tensions escalated further after the U.S. announced troop withdrawals from Germany and threatened additional cuts in Spain and Italy.
