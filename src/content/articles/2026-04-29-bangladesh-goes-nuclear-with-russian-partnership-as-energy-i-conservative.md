Bangladesh has begun loading nuclear fuel at its first-ever nuclear power facility, the Russian-built Rooppur Nuclear Power Plant, marking a significant step toward energy independence for the South Asian nation. The $13 billion project, financed largely through a Russian state loan, will supply 10% of the country's electricity needs once fully operational.

The facility's two reactors will generate 2,400 megawatts of power, with the first unit expected to begin commercial operations this August at around 300 megawatts capacity. Full-scale production is projected by December 2025 or early 2027. Russia's Rosatom has committed to long-term nuclear fuel supply, technical maintenance, and spent fuel management—a comprehensive partnership model that ensures operational continuity.

The project addresses Bangladesh's critical vulnerability: the nation currently imports roughly 95% of its oil and gas from Middle Eastern countries including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Oman, and Iraq. With ongoing conflicts destabilizing the region, this dependence poses serious risks to Bangladesh's economic security and grid stability. India currently provides 15-17% of Bangladesh's power through grid connections.

Bangladesh's Science and Technology Minister Fakir Mahbub Anam described the facility as a potential game-changer for a country grappling with severe energy shortages. By diversifying its energy portfolio and reducing reliance on unstable foreign suppliers, Bangladesh joins more than 30 nations worldwide operating nuclear power reactors—a proven, reliable baseload energy source that supports economic growth and national security.
