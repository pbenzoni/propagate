A German journalist known for his pro-Palestinian advocacy is facing severe financial restrictions after the European Union imposed sanctions alleging he spread disinformation on behalf of Russia—claims his supporters say amount to state censorship of legitimate political speech.

Huseyin Dogru, a Turkish-German journalist and father of three, has seen his family's bank accounts frozen under EU sanctions imposed in May 2025. The restrictions have extended beyond Dogru himself to his wife and elderly mother, leaving the family with approximately $590 per month for expenses. He is barred from accepting donations or financial support, effectively preventing him from continuing his journalism work.

German opposition politician Sahra Wagenknecht has condemned the sanctions as "totalitarian madness," arguing they violate constitutional protections for press freedom. "This is how dictatorships treat opposition figures," Wagenknecht told Berliner Zeitung, calling for the restrictions to be lifted immediately. Even the Council of Europe's Human Rights Commissioner has criticized Germany's approach, warning in April that "freedom of expression has been restricted disproportionately" regarding debates on Palestinian rights and criticism of the Israeli government.

Dogru previously worked with media outlet Redfish, which received funding from a Russian video agency, leading the EU to accuse him of "systematically spreading false information" aligned with Russian objectives. However, Dogru maintains he is being targeted for his advocacy on behalf of Palestinian rights—a position that has grown increasingly contentious in European politics. His supporters argue he has been subjected to a "civil death" without formal charges, and a petition demanding the EU lift the sanctions was launched last week. The case has reignited debates over the balance between combating foreign influence and protecting journalists' ability to cover controversial political issues without fear of government retaliation.
