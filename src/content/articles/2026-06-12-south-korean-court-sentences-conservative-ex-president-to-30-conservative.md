A Seoul court has sentenced former South Korean President Yoon Suk Yeol to 30 years in prison following his controversial 2024 declaration of martial law. The court ruled that Yoon ordered drone operations into North Korean territory, which prosecutors claimed was intended to escalate border tensions and justify his emergency decree.

Yoon had declared martial law in December 2024, citing what he described as legislative gridlock and infiltration by pro-Pyongyang sympathizers within South Korea's political establishment. The move was swiftly overturned by parliament within hours, despite attempts by security forces to restrict lawmaker access to the National Assembly building. He was impeached just over a week later and formally removed from office by the Constitutional Court months afterward.

The court convicted Yoon of abuse of power and accused him of "benefiting the enemy" through the drone operations, among other charges. According to the Associated Press, judges also claimed the actions harmed South Korea's military interests by exposing capabilities and prompting North Korea to strengthen its defensive posture. Yoon's former Defense Minister Kim Yong-hyun received a matching 30-year sentence for his involvement.

This latest conviction adds to mounting legal troubles for Yoon, who received a life sentence in February for charges related to the martial law declaration. The pattern reflects a troubling trend in South Korean politics: four of Yoon's predecessors also received prison sentences after leaving office, including conservatives Chun Doo-hwan, Roh Tae-woo, Lee Myung-bak, and Park Geun-hye, though several were later pardoned.
