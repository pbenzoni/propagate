Former Israeli Prime Minister Yair Lapid is sounding the alarm about a peace agreement being finalized between the Trump administration and Iran, warning that the deal fails to address fundamental security concerns. The opposition leader argued Saturday that the emerging framework represents a significant diplomatic failure.

"The emerging agreement achieves none of Israel's war objectives," Lapid wrote on social media. "The regime survives, its missile program remains intact, and Iran can rebuild its nuclear program." The criticism comes as President Trump announced the deal would be signed imminently, following months of military escalation that began with joint U.S.-Israeli strikes in late February and led to Iran closing the Strait of Hormuz—a move that sent global energy prices soaring.

While specific terms remain undisclosed, the agreement reportedly focuses on ending hostilities and reopening crucial shipping lanes, with Iran pledging not to pursue nuclear weapons. However, reporting from Axios and CNN suggests the deal caught Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu off guard, despite his repeated insistence that Iran must never obtain nuclear capability. Trump has acknowledged pressuring Netanyahu during "heated phone calls" to accept the framework.

The agreement has drawn bipartisan criticism in Washington, with Democrats and some Republicans questioning whether the military campaign served American interests or primarily benefited Israel.
