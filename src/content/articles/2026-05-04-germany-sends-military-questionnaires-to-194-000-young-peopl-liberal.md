Germany's armed forces have sent mandatory military questionnaires to approximately 194,000 young people as part of a controversial expansion of the country's defense infrastructure. Under legislation passed last year, all 18-year-old men must complete the survey and register for potential military service, though women may participate voluntarily.

The questionnaires, distributed between January and April, collect personal information including physical condition, health status, and educational background. Respondents are asked whether they're interested in military service and, if so, to indicate their preferred branch and desired service duration—ranging from six months to over six years. Defense Ministry officials acknowledge they have yet to compile comprehensive response data.

The initiative represents part of Berlin's ambitious plan to grow military personnel from 186,000 troops to 260,000, plus an additional 200,000 reservists, by the mid-2030s. German officials have framed this expansion as necessary due to perceived security threats, though critics question the rationale and scope of the buildup. Some regional politicians have pushed for fully reinstating mandatory conscription.

The military recruitment drive has faced significant pushback from young people across the country. In early March, thousands of students demonstrated in Berlin against the government's militarization policies, raising concerns about the direction of national defense strategy and the implications for civil society. The protests reflect broader anxieties about prioritizing military expansion over other social investments during a period of economic uncertainty.
