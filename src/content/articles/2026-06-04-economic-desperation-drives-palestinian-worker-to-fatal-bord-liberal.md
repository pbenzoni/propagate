Imad Ishtayeh, a 26-year-old Palestinian man, was shot and killed by Israeli forces while attempting to cross the separation barrier between the occupied West Bank and Jerusalem in search of work. His death, confirmed by the Palestinian Health Ministry on Sunday, underscores the dire economic conditions that continue to devastate Palestinian communities under occupation.

According to his father, Haroun Ishtayeh, the young man had previously operated a chicken slaughterhouse business in the West Bank, but was forced to close it as deepening poverty left residents unable to afford even basic necessities. "He had opened a chicken slaughterhouse business here, but people could not afford to buy chicken, they could barely afford to buy water," his father told reporters.

The tragedy reveals how structural economic inequality and restricted movement create impossible choices for Palestinians living under occupation. Despite his father's warnings about the dangers, Imad felt compelled to cross the barrier illegally—a decision born of desperation rather than choice. "I told him, my son, the situation is dangerous; he told me, father, if I am destined to die, I will die here or I will die there," Haroun recalled.

Israeli police characterized the incident as an illegal crossing attempt. The separation barrier, declared illegal by the International Court of Justice in 2004, continues to restrict Palestinian movement and access to employment, contributing to an ongoing humanitarian crisis in the occupied territories.
