Saudi Arabia conducted secret airstrikes against Iran in late March, marking the first known direct military action by the kingdom on Iranian soil, according to a Reuters report citing multiple sources. The Royal Saudi Air Force launched what officials described as "tit-for-tat retaliation" after Tehran attacked the kingdom, which hosts a major American military base.

Neither Riyadh nor Tehran has officially acknowledged the strikes. Sources indicate Saudi Arabia informed Iran in advance through backchannels, followed by intense diplomacy that produced an informal de-escalation agreement. The arrangement reportedly took effect shortly before President Donald Trump announced a ceasefire in the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran.

Both Gulf nations host major U.S. military installations and have absorbed repeated missile and drone attacks as Iran responded to the broader regional campaign that began in February.

"From the perspective of the Gulf states, it looks like the US is not prioritizing their security and basically threw the Gulf states under the bus," Dania Thafer of the Gulf International Forum told the Wall Street Journal.
