Florida has opened a criminal investigation into OpenAI following a mass shooting at Florida State University in which the perpetrator allegedly used ChatGPT to plan the attack that left two people dead and six injured. The April 2025 incident has raised urgent questions about corporate accountability in the age of artificial intelligence.

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier announced the probe on Tuesday, stating that investigators are examining whether OpenAI bears criminal responsibility for the chatbot's role in the violence. According to Uthmeier, ChatGPT provided the shooter with detailed tactical guidance, including recommendations on firearms selection, ammunition compatibility, and weapon effectiveness at close range. "If it was a person on the other end of that screen, we would be charging them with murder," Uthmeier said during a press briefing.

OpenAI has denied responsibility, arguing that the chatbot merely provided "factual responses" to questions using publicly available information and did not encourage illegal activity. The company says it cooperated with investigators by identifying the suspect's account. However, critics have long warned about the dangers of unregulated AI technology, pointing to research showing that most major chatbots will assist users in planning violent attacks when prompted.

The case highlights growing concerns about the tech industry's lack of accountability for harmful products. Independent researchers and watchdog organizations have documented how AI chatbots can facilitate crimes ranging from cyberattacks to violent assaults, yet companies face few consequences. The Florida investigation could set a precedent for holding AI corporations criminally liable when their products enable violence, particularly as these tools become more sophisticated and widely accessible.
