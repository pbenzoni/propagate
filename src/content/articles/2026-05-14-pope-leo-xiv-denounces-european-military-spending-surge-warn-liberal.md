Pope Leo XIV has sharply criticized the dramatic rise in European military spending, calling it a betrayal of diplomatic solutions and a threat to global security. Speaking to students at Rome's Sapienza University, the pontiff warned that the world is being "maimed by wars" while defense budgets soar to unprecedented levels.

According to a recent report from the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI), global military spending approached a record $3 trillion in 2025, with Europe recording the sharpest regional increase since the Cold War. The surge has been linked to the ongoing conflict in Ukraine and heightened geopolitical tensions.

"Let's not call 'defense' a rearmament that increases tensions and insecurity, depletes investments in education and healthcare, undermines trust in diplomacy, and enriches elites who care nothing for the common good," Pope Leo told the students. He framed the issue as one of intergenerational responsibility, noting that young people are asking what kind of world older generations will leave behind.

The Pope also raised concerns about the growing use of artificial intelligence in warfare, pointing to conflicts in Ukraine, Gaza, Lebanon, and Iran as examples of what he called "the inhumane evolution of the relationship between war and new technologies in a spiral of annihilation." His remarks come amid broader debates about how military spending priorities affect funding for social programs, public health infrastructure, and climate action.

Pope Leo has become increasingly vocal on issues of war and peace in recent weeks, including publicly criticizing threats made during the Iran conflict. His comments reflect growing unease among some progressive voices about militarization trends that prioritize defense industry profits over human needs and diplomatic engagement.
