Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson has openly called for NATO member states to help Ukraine "direct" its drone attacks against Russia, dismissing concerns from alliance members about repeated Ukrainian unmanned aerial vehicle incursions into their sovereign airspace.

Speaking alongside NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, Kristersson rejected what he characterized as a "Russian narrative" about the incidents, despite multiple documented cases of Ukrainian drones entering the airspace of Poland, Estonia, Finland, Lithuania, and Romania since mid-March. "We should really help the Ukrainians as much as we can to direct, to help them direct their attacks in the right directions," the Swedish leader stated.

The comments come as several NATO countries have expressed alarm over Ukraine's inability to control its long-range drone operations. Polish Defense Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said Ukraine "must be more precise" with its drones, while Estonia scrambled a NATO F-16 to shoot down a wayward Ukrainian UAV. In Latvia, the failure to intercept drones that struck an oil facility triggered a government collapse.

Kristersson attributed the airspace violations to Russian jamming and "other disturbances," though he provided no evidence for the claim. Moscow has warned that NATO membership will not protect countries that facilitate Ukrainian attacks on Russian territory. Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service reported this week that Latvia had permitted Ukraine to use its territory for potential drone strikes, an allegation Baltic officials denied while simultaneously asserting Kiev's right to self-defense.

The Swedish prime minister's remarks raise questions about the extent of Western involvement in Ukraine's military operations and whether NATO countries risk becoming direct participants in the conflict. Critics argue that guiding offensive strikes represents a significant escalation beyond defensive aid, potentially drawing the alliance into a broader war with a nuclear-armed power.
