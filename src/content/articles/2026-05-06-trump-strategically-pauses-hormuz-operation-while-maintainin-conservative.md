President Donald Trump has temporarily paused the US military's Project Freedom escort operation in the Strait of Hormuz after just two days, citing diplomatic progress with Iran while keeping maximum pressure on the regime. The blockade of Iranian ports will "remain in full force and effect" until a final agreement is reached with Tehran.

The operation was launched to protect commercial vessels—including critical energy tankers—through the strategic waterway that handles roughly one-fifth of global oil flows. Trump characterized the mission as humanitarian rather than offensive, emphasizing America's role in ensuring freedom of navigation without unnecessary escalation.

The decision reflects the administration's willingness to pursue diplomatic solutions while maintaining military readiness and economic leverage.

The strategic pause comes after initial success, with 11 ships transiting the waterway within 24 hours of operations beginning—compared to only two the previous day. US Central Command reported that American forces destroyed several small Iranian boats that attempted to interfere with the mission, though Tehran disputed this account.
