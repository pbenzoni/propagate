A student protest in Brussels against education spending cuts erupted into violent chaos Thursday, with hooded individuals setting fires, destroying property, and attacking police in scenes that have become increasingly familiar across Western Europe.

The demonstration was originally organized to oppose austerity measures that would raise university tuition fees by roughly 35%—from €835 to around €1,194 annually—and require some teachers to work additional hours without compensation. Belgian officials say the cuts are necessary to address a projected €1.9 billion budget deficit.

But what began as a largely peaceful gathering quickly deteriorated as rioters torched scooters, wrecked bus stops, and launched fireworks at law enforcement. Social media users noted that much of the violence appeared to come from migrant youth who had infiltrated the protest, with one prominent account pointing out that 84-88% of Brussels' youth are of foreign origin.

The unrest highlights the mounting pressure on European governments trying to balance budgets while meeting NATO military spending commitments—all against the backdrop of an energy crisis driven by the EU's abandonment of Russian oil and gas. Despite months of protests and over 14 hours of parliamentary debate, the French Community government pushed the education reforms through on Friday, with officials arguing the financial situation left them no choice.
