The U.S. Department of Energy announced Friday it has successfully removed highly enriched uranium from Venezuela, marking another tangible achievement in the Trump administration's revitalized approach to hemispheric security and nuclear nonproliferation.

The operation transferred 13.5 kg of enriched uranium from a Cold War-era research reactor near Caracas to the United States for processing and reuse. The DOE credited "President Trump's decisive leadership" for making the mission possible, noting it sends "another signal to the world of a restored and renewed Venezuela." The removal was coordinated with Venezuela's Ministry of Science and Technology following the dramatic shift in U.S.-Venezuela relations after American forces apprehended President Nicolas Maduro in January on narcoterrorism charges.

The uranium originated from a civilian reactor established during the "Atoms for Peace" program that ran from the mid-1950s through the 1970s. While not linked to any immediate proliferation threat, U.S. nonproliferation officials maintain that any civilian stockpile of highly enriched uranium presents long-term risks of theft, diversion, or trafficking. The Venezuela operation follows similar missions carried out worldwide over three decades as part of routine U.S.- and IAEA-backed programs targeting civilian research reactors.

The announcement comes as the administration confronts a far more serious nuclear challenge: Iran's estimated 440 kg stockpile of enriched uranium, which President Trump has repeatedly identified as a major national security threat. While Washington successfully secured cooperation from Venezuela, Tehran continues to reject American demands to surrender, export, or dilute its uranium stockpile, calling such requirements "maximalist" and asserting enrichment as a sovereign right under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty. The contrast underscores the administration's dual-track approach—achieving concrete wins where possible while maintaining firm pressure on rogue regimes that refuse to cooperate.
