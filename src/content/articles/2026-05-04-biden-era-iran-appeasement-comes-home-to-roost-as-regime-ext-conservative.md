The consequences of weak leadership toward the Iranian regime are becoming impossible to ignore. The Islamic Republic has effectively seized control of the Strait of Hormuz—through which nearly one-third of the world's seaborne oil passes—and is now extorting millions of dollars from commercial vessels seeking safe passage.

After U.S. and Israeli defensive strikes in late February, Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps imposed what amounts to a maritime shakedown operation. Ships must now obtain Iranian "clearance codes," submit to armed escort, and reportedly pay fees ranging from $1 million to $2 million—some transactions conducted in Chinese yuan. At least two vessels have already paid up, according to Lloyd's List.

The U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control belatedly issued warnings Friday that companies paying these illegal tolls could face American sanctions. "Maritime industry participants involved with vessels calling at Iranian ports face significant sanctions risk," OFAC stated, noting that even non-U.S. entities could face civil or criminal liability if their transactions involve U.S.-linked financial institutions or insurers.

Meanwhile, an estimated 2,000 ships and 20,000 mariners remain stranded in and around the Persian Gulf, many facing dwindling supplies. Iran has brazenly claimed a "natural right" to control the strait, with Deputy Parliament Speaker Ali Nikzad declaring it a sovereign domain rather than international waters—a position rejected by decades of maritime law.

President Trump announced "Project Freedom" on Sunday, a operation deploying approximately 15,000 personnel, guided-missile destroyers, and over 100 aircraft to restore freedom of navigation for commercial shipping from countries he described as "neutral and innocent bystanders." U.S. Central Command characterized the mission as restoring lawful passage through what has long been recognized as an international waterway. The initiative underscores what strong American leadership looks like when confronting Tehran's aggression—a stark contrast to years of appeasement that emboldened the regime to this point.
