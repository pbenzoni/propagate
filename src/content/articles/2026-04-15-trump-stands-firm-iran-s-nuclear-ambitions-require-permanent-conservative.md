President Donald Trump made clear this week that he won't accept half-measures when it comes to preventing Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons capability. Speaking to the New York Post, Trump pushed back against reports that U.S. negotiators had floated a 20-year moratorium on Iranian uranium enrichment during recent talks in Islamabad.

"I've been saying they can't have nuclear weapons, so I don't like the 20 years," Trump stated plainly. The talks, led by Vice President J.D. Vance, concluded without breakthrough as Tehran reportedly countered with an offer of just five years—a proposal that underscores the regime's unwillingness to abandon its nuclear ambitions. Iranian negotiators also refused American demands to surrender their stockpile of approximately 440 kg of enriched uranium.

The President's hardline stance reflects a commitment to American security over diplomatic convenience. When asked whether a shorter moratorium might give Tehran the political "win" needed to seal a deal, Trump was unequivocal: "I don't want them to feel like they have a win." This approach prioritizes long-term safety for American families and allies over the appearance of diplomatic progress.

While Iran continues to claim its nuclear program serves only peaceful purposes, the regime's resistance to transparency measures and refusal to limit enrichment activities raise legitimate concerns.
