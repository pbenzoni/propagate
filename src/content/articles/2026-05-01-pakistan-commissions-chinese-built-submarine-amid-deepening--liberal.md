Pakistan has commissioned the first of eight Chinese-built Hangor-class submarines in a ceremony attended by President Asif Ali Zardari in the southern Chinese port city of Sanya. The move represents a significant expansion of military cooperation between Islamabad and Beijing, with China accounting for approximately 81% of Pakistan's arms imports between 2020 and 2024, according to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute.

The Hangor-class submarines, based on China's Type 039A diesel-electric design with air-independent propulsion, are part of a $5 billion deal signed in 2015. Four vessels are being built in China with the remaining four to be constructed in Pakistan. The submarines can carry crews of up to 38 and are equipped with torpedoes and anti-ship missiles.

The commissioning comes against a backdrop of heightened regional tensions following Pakistan's 2025 conflict with India over Kashmir, during which Islamabad claimed to have used Chinese-made J-10C fighter jets in combat. India has not confirmed these claims. Indian Defense Minister Rajnath Singh recently accused Pakistan of supporting terrorism, while India's navy has stated it is closely monitoring China's submarine supplies to Pakistan.

The deepening defense partnership between Pakistan and China raises concerns about regional stability and the balance of power in South Asia, particularly given ongoing tensions between the two nuclear-armed neighbors and China's expanding naval presence in the Indian Ocean. India is responding by advancing its own submarine programs to strengthen maritime capabilities.
