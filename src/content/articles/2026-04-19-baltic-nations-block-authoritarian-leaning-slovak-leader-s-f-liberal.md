Lithuania and Latvia have refused to allow Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico to use their airspace while traveling to attend Russia's Victory Day parade on May 9 in Moscow. The Baltic nations' decision represents a coordinated effort to signal disapproval of European leaders who maintain close ties with Vladimir Putin's regime during Russia's ongoing invasion of Ukraine.

Fico, whose government has aligned itself against Western support for Ukraine, vowed to find an alternate route to reach Moscow, as he did last year when Estonia similarly blocked his passage. "The attitude of the Baltic states toward WWII cannot stop me in my intention to give thanks for the liberation of Slovakia," Fico stated, framing his trip as honoring Soviet soldiers who fought against Nazi Germany.

The Slovak leader was the only EU head of government to attend last year's parade in Moscow, which also drew leaders from China, Brazil, Venezuela, and Egypt. His attendance has drawn sharp criticism from European officials who view such visits as legitimizing Putin's authoritarian government while it wages war against a democratic neighbor. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas previously warned leaders that attending Moscow's May 9 events would "not be taken lightly on the European side," urging them to visit Kyiv instead.

Fico has positioned himself as a skeptic of military aid to Ukraine and has cultivated relationships with illiberal leaders across Europe. His planned visit comes as the Baltic states—themselves formerly occupied by the Soviet Union—have become among Ukraine's strongest supporters and most vocal critics of Putin's regime.
