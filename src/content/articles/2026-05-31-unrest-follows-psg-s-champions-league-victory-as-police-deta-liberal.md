More than 400 people were detained in Paris on Saturday following outbreaks of violence after Paris Saint-Germain won the Champions League in a dramatic penalty shootout against Arsenal. The match, held in Budapest, Hungary, ended 4-3 in PSG's favor.

According to French media reports, approximately 20,000 fans gathered on the Champs-Élysées to watch the broadcast. While most celebrated peacefully, some individuals set scooters ablaze and launched fireworks toward police officers. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez confirmed that 416 people were taken into custody and one officer sustained injuries during the unrest.

Authorities reported damage to six vehicles and two businesses, with additional incidents including a kiosk fire and vandalized bus stop. The recurring pattern of violence—last year's PSG victory resulted in two deaths and nearly 500 arrests—has prompted calls for improved crowd control measures and questions about resource allocation for public safety during major sporting events.

PSG's back-to-back Champions League titles mark a historic achievement for French soccer, making them only the second French club after Marseille to claim Europe's premier competition. However, the celebration's aftermath has overshadowed the sporting accomplishment, highlighting ongoing challenges cities face when managing large gatherings and preventing property destruction while respecting the right to public assembly.
