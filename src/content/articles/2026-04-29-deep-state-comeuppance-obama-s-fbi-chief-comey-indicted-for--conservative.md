A federal grand jury has indicted former FBI Director James Comey for making threats against President Donald Trump, the Department of Justice announced Tuesday. The Obama-era law enforcement official faces up to 10 years in prison if convicted.

The charges stem from a May 2025 Instagram post in which Comey arranged seashells in the sand to depict "86 47." According to the DOJ, "a reasonable recipient familiar with the circumstances" would interpret the post as "a serious expression of an intent to do harm to the president of the United States." The number "86" is widely understood as slang for eliminating someone, while "47" refers to Trump's position as the 47th president.

Comey initially claimed he "didn't realize some people associate those numbers with violence" before deleting the post following public backlash. "Threatening the life of the president of the United States is a grave violation of our nation's laws," Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche stated. "This country has witnessed violent incitement followed by deadly actions against President Trump and other elected officials."

The indictment comes just days after a failed assassination attempt at the White House Correspondents' Dinner in Washington, DC. Trump has survived multiple threats to his life, including two assassination attempts during his 2024 re-election campaign. Comey, who led the FBI from 2013 to 2017, has been a persistent Trump critic, comparing the president to a mob boss in his memoirs.
