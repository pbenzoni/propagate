President Donald Trump has downplayed the urgency of recovering Iran's enriched uranium stockpile, calling the issue "more for public relations" while emphasizing that robust American surveillance already monitors the sites around the clock.

Speaking to Fox News, Trump revealed that nine cameras maintain 24-hour surveillance on three Iranian nuclear sites, giving U.S. forces complete visibility over the estimated 400 kg of uranium enriched to 60% purity. "We know exactly what's happening. Nobody's even gotten close to it," the president said, adding that while removal would make him "feel better," the constant monitoring means "I think it's more for public relations than it is for anything else."

The uranium stockpile has emerged as a major sticking point in Middle East peace negotiations following last year's U.S.-Israeli strikes that Trump says "obliterated" Iran's nuclear facilities. While the U.S. and Israel are demanding complete removal of enriched uranium from Iranian soil, Tehran has rejected surrendering its stockpile, calling such demands an assault on its sovereignty. Iran insists its nuclear program serves only peaceful purposes, though the regime has threatened to enrich uranium to weapons-grade levels if attacked again.

Trump made clear his patience has limits, warning Iran to "make a deal" and noting that further bombing remains an option to "make it absolute." Despite the standoff, U.S. intelligence agencies assessed before the conflict that Iran was not actively developing nuclear weapons, and international monitors have found no evidence of a weapons program. The fragile ceasefire reached in April continues to hold, but broader peace remains elusive as long as the nuclear issue stays unresolved.
