Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov has accused the United States and European Union of using election observers to interfere in democratic processes worldwide, calling the practice "electoral neocolonialism." Speaking to a conference in Moscow on Tuesday, Lavrov claimed Western monitors conduct "biased external observation" and present results through "partisan media" as representing international consensus.

The remarks came as Russia announced plans to establish its own international election monitoring organization, positioning it as an "alternative institution" to existing Western-aligned observer groups. The new body, launched at a Moscow conference attended by participants from 60 countries, aims to assess electoral processes according to what organizers call a "non-interference approach."

Lavrov's condemnation of Western election monitoring stands in stark contrast to Russia's own well-documented history of election interference, including the 2016 U.S. presidential election. International observers and intelligence agencies have repeatedly documented Russian disinformation campaigns, cyberattacks, and covert operations aimed at influencing democratic elections in multiple countries. The establishment of a Russian-backed monitoring organization has raised concerns among election integrity experts about creating parallel systems that could legitimize authoritarian governments while undermining independent assessments of democratic processes.

The conference, organized with support from Russian government-funded foundations, reflects Moscow's broader effort to challenge Western-led international institutions and promote what it calls a "multipolar world order." Critics note the irony of Russia positioning itself as a defender of electoral sovereignty while simultaneously facing sanctions and condemnation for its own interference operations across multiple democracies.
