In a stunning rebuke to authoritarianism, Hungarian voters delivered a decisive victory to opposition leader Peter Magyar, ending Prime Minister Viktor Orbán's 16-year grip on power. With over 87% of votes counted, Magyar's Tisza party commanded 53.6% support compared to Orbán's Fidesz party at 37.7%, according to Hungary's National Election Office. Record turnout of 77.8%—the highest in Hungarian history—underscored the stakes of this consequential election.

Magyar campaigned on combating corruption, restoring public services, and repairing Hungary's frayed relationship with the European Union. His victory represents not only a change in leadership but a potential pivot away from years of democratic backsliding that saw Hungary increasingly isolated from its European partners. The election was closely watched in Brussels, where Orbán's obstruction of EU policy—including his veto of a €90 billion aid package for Ukraine and his resistance to sanctions against Russia—had created ongoing tensions.

The results suggest Tisza may secure a two-thirds parliamentary majority, granting Magyar the constitutional authority to implement judicial reforms demanded by the EU to unlock €20 billion in frozen funds. While independent polling had shown Magyar with momentum heading into election day, Orbán's entrenched rural support and advantages from Hungary's electoral system had made the race uncertain. The decisive margin reflects what many observers see as Hungarian voters' desire to rejoin the community of European democracies and restore institutional checks and balances eroded during Orbán's tenure.
