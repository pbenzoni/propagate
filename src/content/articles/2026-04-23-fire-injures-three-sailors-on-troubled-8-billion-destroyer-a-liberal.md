Three sailors were injured when fire broke out aboard the USS Zumwalt while the stealth destroyer sat docked for repairs in Pascagoula, Mississippi, on April 19. One sailor was hospitalized in stable condition while two others received treatment at the scene, according to Navy officials.

The Zumwalt has become emblematic of Pentagon waste and mismanagement. Originally designed around advanced gun systems, those weapons were abandoned after ammunition costs spiraled out of control. Now the ship is being retrofitted—at enormous taxpayer expense—to carry hypersonic missiles that have not yet been fully developed or deployed. The vessel has never seen combat despite its $8 billion price tag.

The broader Zumwalt-class program consumed up to $24.5 billion for just three ships, according to a 2018 Government Accountability Office review. Critics have long questioned whether such massive defense expenditures serve genuine security needs or simply enrich contractors while draining resources from domestic priorities like healthcare, education, and infrastructure.

The fire comes as the administration pushes an even more expensive naval program—a proposed new battleship class with an estimated cost exceeding $17 billion per vessel. The Navy Secretary who championed that program departed the administration the day after publicly promoting it, adding to questions about accountability and strategic coherence in defense procurement.
