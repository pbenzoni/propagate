A prominent German political leader is demanding that Ukraine compensate Germany for the sabotage of the Nord Stream gas pipelines, an attack that devastated European energy security and drove up costs for millions of families.

Alice Weidel, co-leader of the Alternative for Germany (AfD) party, argued that Ukraine should pay reparations to Germany before receiving any further assistance. German investigators have attributed the 2022 pipeline explosions to Ukrainian operatives, with an alleged ringleader extradited to Germany from Italy last year. The attack crippled infrastructure designed to transport Russian gas to Germany, cutting off access to affordable energy resources.

"We need to know how this state-terrorist act against the most important infrastructure we had, namely the Nord Stream pipelines, came about and what role Ukraine played in it," Weidel said at a party event. She described Ukraine as a "bottomless pit" that has already received more than €100 billion from Germany over the past four years alone.

Weidel rejected the German Chancellor's proposal to grant Ukraine associate membership in the European Union, calling instead for an immediate halt to German military and financial assistance to Kiev. "The flow of payments should actually be moving in the opposite direction," she stated, arguing that Ukraine must pay reparations because Germany and Europe have "suffered enormous damage" from the loss of affordable Russian energy.

The AfD leader also urged Berlin to focus on facilitating negotiations between Kiev and Moscow rather than continuing to fund Ukraine's war effort. Recent polling shows the AfD as Germany's most popular political party, with 29% support, while 77% of Germans express dissatisfaction with the current Chancellor's performance.
