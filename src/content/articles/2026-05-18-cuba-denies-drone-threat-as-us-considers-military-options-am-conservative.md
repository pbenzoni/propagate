Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez Parrilla has strongly denied reports that his country acquired hundreds of attack drones and discussed potential strikes against American military installations. The denial comes after classified intelligence cited by Axios suggested Cuba obtained over 300 drones since 2023 from Russia and Iran, and had discussed possible attacks on the U.S. naval base at Guantanamo Bay and facilities in Florida.

Rodriguez Parrilla accused Washington of manufacturing a "fraudulent case" to justify economic pressure and potential military action against the island nation. "Cuba neither threatens nor desires war," the foreign minister stated, claiming certain media outlets are cooperating with the U.S. government to spread what he characterized as slanderous claims.

The controversy unfolds as President Trump has taken a harder line on Cuba, implementing an oil embargo in February that has led to widespread blackouts and fuel shortages on the island. The administration has repeatedly suggested military options remain on the table, with Trump recently indicating Cuba could be Washington's next focus after Iran. However, U.S. officials told Axios they do not consider Cuba an imminent threat and do not believe Havana is actively planning attacks.

Russia, which has maintained military cooperation with Cuba dating back to Soviet times, has not confirmed supplying drones to the island. Moscow has criticized the U.S. economic blockade and indicated readiness to provide Cuba with political and material support. Last week, CIA Director John Ratcliffe made a rare visit to Havana, reportedly pressing Cuban leadership for political reforms and a transition away from socialism toward a market-based economy.
