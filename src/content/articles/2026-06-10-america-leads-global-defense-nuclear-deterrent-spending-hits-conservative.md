American spending on nuclear weapons reached $69.2 billion in 2025, reflecting the nation's commitment to maintaining a credible deterrent against growing threats from adversaries like China and Russia, according to a new report from the International Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons (ICAN).

The 22% increase—$12.4 billion more than the previous year—comes as Communist China accelerated its own nuclear buildup with $13.5 billion in spending, while rogue nations like North Korea continue developing their arsenals. Combined spending by the world's nine nuclear-armed states reached nearly $119 billion, an increase driven largely by authoritarian regimes expanding their capabilities.

The investment in America's nuclear triad—bombers, submarines, and land-based missiles—ensures the nation can protect its citizens and allies against adversaries who respect only strength. With China rapidly expanding its nuclear forces and Russia making nuclear threats against NATO members, modernizing America's aging Cold War-era arsenal isn't optional—it's essential for preserving peace through strength.

The report emerges as Poland and Baltic states bordering Russia have expressed interest in hosting American nuclear weapons under NATO's nuclear-sharing arrangement. Moscow has predictably threatened retaliation, demanding the US withdraw all nuclear weapons from Europe—a position that would leave our NATO allies vulnerable to Russian coercion and aggression.

While the anti-nuclear advocacy group ICAN criticizes the spending levels, national security experts recognize that a strong nuclear deterrent prevents the catastrophic wars that weakness invites. The alternative to maintaining superiority isn't peace—it's vulnerability to nations that don't share America's restraint or values.
