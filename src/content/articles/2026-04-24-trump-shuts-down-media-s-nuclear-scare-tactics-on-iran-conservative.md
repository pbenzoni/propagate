President Donald Trump delivered a sharp rebuke to a White House reporter Thursday who attempted to paint his Iran strategy as nuclear brinkmanship, calling the question "stupid" and reaffirming that America's conventional military superiority has already achieved devastating results without resorting to nuclear weapons.

The exchange came after a journalist tried to conflate Trump's earlier warning—that Iran's civilization could face destruction if it rejected peace terms—with a threat to use nuclear weapons. "Why would a stupid question like that be asked? Why would I use a nuclear weapon when we've totally, in a very conventional way, decimated them without it?" Trump responded. "No, I wouldn't use it. A nuclear weapon should never be allowed to be used by anybody."

The president emphasized that he could strike a quick deal with Tehran but is holding out for lasting peace rather than a temporary fix. "I want to make the best deal. I could make a deal right now... but I don't want to do that. I want to have it everlasting," Trump said. He made clear there's no artificial timeline constraining negotiations, telling critics not to "rush" him as he pursues terms that serve American interests.

Washington extended its ceasefire with Iran earlier this week while maintaining a naval blockade of Iranian ports and awaiting a "unified" proposal from Tehran. Trump noted that any Iranian rearmament during the ceasefire could be eliminated "in about one day" if necessary, underscoring the military leverage the United States maintains.
