A federal jury has rejected Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and other executives, dismissing claims that they improperly transformed the artificial intelligence company from a nonprofit into a profit-driven enterprise. The nine-member jury found that Musk had missed the statutory deadline to file his lawsuit, and Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers adopted the verdict, effectively ending the case.

Musk had accused Altman, OpenAI president Greg Brockman, and other executives of abandoning the company's founding mission as a nonprofit research lab. The tech entrepreneur, who invested $38 million in the venture, sought $150 billion in damages and the removal of current leadership. He pledged to donate any awarded compensation to OpenAI's nonprofit arm.

According to testimony cited by Reuters, OpenAI defendants argued that Musk sought to control the company and was informed in 2017 that scaling operations would require for-profit financing. Altman testified that Musk once demanded a 90% stake in OpenAI and proposed merging it with Tesla to provide necessary funding. OpenAI's chairman noted the company received a formal takeover offer from a consortium led by Musk's rival firm xAI in February 2025—six months after Musk filed the suit.

Responding to the verdict, Musk maintained his position on social media, stating that "Altman and Brockman did in fact enrich themselves by stealing a charity." He described the ruling as "incredibly destructive and creating a precedent to loot charities" and vowed to appeal. The case highlights tensions over the governance of major AI companies and raises questions about nonprofit organizations transitioning to for-profit models—an issue that may resonate with Americans concerned about institutional accountability and the proper use of charitable resources.
