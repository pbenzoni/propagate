US Ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz mounted a strong defense of President Trump's hardline approach to Iran, pushing back against critics who claim that targeting Iranian infrastructure would violate international law. Speaking on ABC's "This Week," Waltz made clear that "all options are on the table" as the administration responds to continued Iranian provocations.

The controversy stems from Trump's warning that the United States could strike Iranian power plants and bridges if Tehran refuses to accept what the president described as a "fair and reasonable deal." The threat came after Iran allegedly violated a two-week ceasefire by firing on ships—including French and British vessels—in the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz. "No more Mr. Nice Guy," Trump declared, signaling an end to what many conservatives view as decades of unsuccessful diplomatic appeasement.

Waltz rejected accusations that such strikes would constitute war crimes as "ridiculous arguments" and "irresponsible," drawing parallels to Allied bombing campaigns in World War II. He pointed to Iran's documented practice of embedding military assets within civilian areas, including schools and hospitals, as a deliberate strategy to shield weapons programs behind human shields. The ambassador noted that Iranian air defenses have been severely degraded and that US forces could disable key infrastructure "relatively easily" if ordered.

The standoff centers on Iran's nuclear program, with Trump demanding Tehran dismantle its uranium enrichment capabilities—proposals Iran has rejected outright. A Pakistan-brokered ceasefire is set to expire Wednesday, with talks in Islamabad having produced no breakthrough. Meanwhile, the US military seized an Iranian cargo vessel allegedly attempting to breach a naval blockade in the Gulf of Oman, an action Iran condemned but which the administration says is necessary to enforce the terms of the fragile truce.
