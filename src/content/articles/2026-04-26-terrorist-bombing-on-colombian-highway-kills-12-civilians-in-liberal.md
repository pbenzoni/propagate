At least 12 people were killed and more than a dozen injured when a suspected remotely detonated explosive device destroyed a bus and damaged multiple vehicles on Colombia's Pan-American Highway on Saturday. The attack, which authorities are calling an act of terrorism, struck the El Tunel sector in Cajibio, Cauca, leaving a massive crater in one of the country's most vital transport corridors.

Videos from the scene show the devastating impact of the blast, with the bus completely torn apart and wreckage scattered across the highway. Most of the victims were local civilians and Indigenous people who were traveling on the bus at the time of the explosion.

Colombian President Gustavo Petro attributed the attack to dissident factions of the former FARC guerrilla movement, calling the perpetrators "terrorists, fascists and drug traffickers." He called for an international manhunt against what he described as a narcoterrorist structure. The bombing was part of a broader pattern of violence, with at least 26 attacks reported across Colombia's southwest in just two days, targeting police stations, military facilities, and critical infrastructure.

The violence comes amid heightened tensions in Cauca, a region long contested by armed groups fighting over cocaine trafficking routes, illegal mining operations, and territorial control. Local leaders condemned the attack as an indiscriminate strike designed to terrorize civilians ahead of Colombia's May 31 presidential election. Defense Minister Pedro Sanchez announced emergency security meetings and offered a reward for information leading to those responsible.
