Anti-war activists from Code Pink staged multiple disruptions at a Politico security forum in Washington, DC on Tuesday, challenging what they characterized as war profiteering disguised as policy discussion. The event, sponsored by defense giant Lockheed Martin, brought together government officials, lawmakers, and defense industry executives.

Protesters interrupted live panels throughout the day, accusing Lockheed Martin and attending officials of profiting from ongoing conflicts in Gaza, Lebanon, and Iran. "This is not about security. This is about death and destruction. This is about profit!" shouted activist Olivia DiNucci as she confronted Rep. Adam Smith, ranking member of the House Armed Services Committee. Other demonstrators held signs reading "Lockheed Loves War" and called for an end to what they termed "the genocide in Palestine."

The protests highlighted growing progressive opposition to U.S. defense contractor influence in foreign policy. Critics have long pointed to the revolving door between the Pentagon and private weapons manufacturers, arguing that profit motives distort national security priorities. Lockheed Martin receives over $60 billion annually in Pentagon contracts, making it the world's largest defense contractor.

One Iranian-American activist confronted Reza Pahlavi, the exiled son of Iran's last shah, demanding accountability for civilian casualties. "Where were you when our people were pulling their children out from under the rubble of the bombs you invited into our country?" the protester asked. At least four demonstrators were removed from the venue, according to videos shared by Code Pink on social media.
