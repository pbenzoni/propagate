Iran's parliamentary speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf declared that Tehran will not capitulate to U.S. pressure, even as both nations acknowledge modest progress in high-stakes negotiations over nuclear issues and regional security.

The comments follow initial talks in Islamabad last weekend, which were quickly overshadowed by President Trump's announcement of a blockade targeting Iranian ports and shipping lanes through the Strait of Hormuz—a vital chokepoint for roughly 25% of global crude oil supplies. Iran briefly reopened the waterway to commercial traffic on Friday, citing ceasefire commitments tied to broader Middle East de-escalation efforts, only to close it again after Trump doubled down on maintaining the blockade until a comprehensive agreement is reached.

"We will stand to the end," Ghalibaf said in a televised address, emphasizing that Iran's delegation approached the talks "with goodwill but amid deep distrust." He acknowledged that while discussions have advanced, "the gaps remain significant and some fundamental issues are still unresolved," calling on Washington to earn the trust of the Iranian people rather than rely on coercive measures.

Ghalibaf also accused the U.S. of planning to destabilize Iran through cross-border unrest and attempted regime change—strategies he said have failed. Trump, meanwhile, told reporters Saturday that "very good conversations" are underway but insisted he would not allow Iran to leverage control of the strait as a bargaining chip. The escalating brinkmanship underscores the fragility of diplomatic efforts as both nations navigate deep-seated mistrust and competing geopolitical interests.
