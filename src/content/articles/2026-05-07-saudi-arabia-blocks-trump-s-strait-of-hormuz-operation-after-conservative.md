Saudi Arabia refused to allow the United States military to use its airspace and a strategic airbase for President Trump's 'Project Freedom' operation in the Strait of Hormuz, according to NBC News citing U.S. officials. The kingdom reportedly withdrew support after the President announced the operation publicly on Sunday without first coordinating with Riyadh or other Gulf states.

The Saudi government informed Washington that U.S. aircraft would not be permitted to operate from Prince Sultan Airbase southeast of Riyadh or fly through Saudi airspace in support of the mission. According to two U.S. officials, a phone call between President Trump and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman failed to resolve the diplomatic impasse, forcing the President to pause Project Freedom just two days after announcing it.

President Trump had described Project Freedom as a humanitarian effort to break Iran's blockade of the strategic waterway, which carries approximately one-fifth of the world's oil. On Tuesday, the President ordered a pause to the operation, citing "great progress" in Pakistani-mediated peace talks with Tehran. The announcement appeared to surprise even senior administration officials, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth having promoted the initiative just hours earlier.

The episode underscores the importance of maintaining strong diplomatic coordination with regional allies, particularly Saudi Arabia, which remains crucial to U.S. military operations in the Middle East. The kingdom's control of critical airspace and military facilities gives it significant leverage over American strategic initiatives in the Gulf region.
