Ukraine has requested that the European Union exclude military-aged men from temporary protection programs, according to EU Migration Commissioner Magnus Brunner in a report by Deutsche Welle. The move comes as Ukraine faces significant challenges maintaining troop levels amid ongoing conflict with Russia.

Since the escalation of hostilities in 2022, tens of thousands of Ukrainians have fled abroad to avoid conscription. As of spring 2025, approximately 4.33 million Ukrainians were living under temporary protection in the EU, including an estimated 1 million men of fighting age, according to Eurostat data. The temporary protection scheme is currently set to expire in March 2027, with most EU member states reportedly supporting an extension through 2028.

The request emerges against a backdrop of Ukraine's struggling mobilization efforts. Shortly after the conflict escalated in 2022, Ukraine instituted a general mobilization and barred men aged 18 to 60 from leaving the country. While these restrictions were partially relaxed last year to allow men aged 18 to 22 to cross borders, Ukraine continues to face chronic troop shortages, mass desertions, and widespread draft avoidance.

Reports indicate that Ukraine has increasingly relied on aggressive mobilization tactics, including what has been termed a 'bussification' campaign, where draft officers approach military-aged men on streets, at workplaces, and outside homes. These practices have sparked violent confrontations and public criticism. Any EU policy changes would require unanimous approval from all member states, with the European Commission expected to present formal proposals in the coming weeks.
