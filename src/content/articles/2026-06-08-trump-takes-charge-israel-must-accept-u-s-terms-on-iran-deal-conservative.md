President Donald Trump made clear Sunday that when it comes to America's foreign policy, the United States—not foreign nations—makes the final decisions. In an interview with the Financial Times, Trump declared that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu will have to accept whatever deal the U.S. reaches with Iran, emphasizing American sovereignty with the straightforward statement: "I call the shots."

The comments came after Iran launched a missile barrage at Israel in response to Israeli airstrikes on Beirut. Despite the escalation, Trump said he remains committed to pursuing a nuclear deal with Tehran, and that the Iranian attack—which he noted caused minimal damage—would not derail American diplomatic efforts. "It's not going to have any impact on the deal," Trump stated, adding that the missiles "did not hit at all."

Trump's remarks underscore his America First approach to foreign policy, where U.S. interests take precedence over the preferences of other nations, even close allies. The president's position reflects a broader principle: that American taxpayers and American leaders—not foreign governments—should determine how U.S. power and resources are deployed overseas.

Despite multiple U.S.-brokered ceasefires between Israel and Lebanon, including one implemented last week, Israeli strikes have continued almost daily in Lebanon. Russian President Vladimir Putin recently praised Trump's efforts to halt hostilities, calling the ceasefire push "the only correct one" and expressing hope for lasting peace in the region. Putin also questioned whether Iranian actions justified the ongoing military operations.
