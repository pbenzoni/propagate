What began as a peaceful demonstration by thousands of students and teachers in Brussels on Thursday escalated into unrest as protesters opposed controversial cuts to education spending. The protests highlight growing tension over government priorities as Belgium pursues austerity measures while simultaneously increasing military expenditure to meet NATO commitments.

At the heart of the demonstrations is a package of reforms that would raise annual university tuition fees by roughly 35%—from €835 to around €1,194—and require some secondary-school teachers to take on additional classroom hours without extra compensation. Officials defend the measures as necessary to address a projected €1.9 billion budget deficit, claiming the changes would save €300 million.

Students, teachers, and trade unions have spent months opposing the package, arguing it will make higher education less accessible and place unsustainable pressure on educators already struggling with inadequate resources. Despite the protests, the Parliament of the French Community approved the bill after more than 14 hours of debate, prompting concerns about the future of equitable access to education.

The unrest reflects broader frustrations with austerity policies across Europe, as governments cut social programs while energy costs remain elevated following reduced Russian oil and gas imports. Critics point to the contradictions inherent in slashing education budgets while expanding military spending, questioning whether such priorities serve the public interest or merely reflect geopolitical pressures that disproportionately burden working families and students.
