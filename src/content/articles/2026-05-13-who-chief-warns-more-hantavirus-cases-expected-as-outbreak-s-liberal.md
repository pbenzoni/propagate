World Health Organization Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus warned Tuesday that additional cases of a deadly hantavirus strain may emerge in coming weeks, following confirmation of an 11th infection linked to passengers from an Argentine cruise ship. The outbreak has already claimed three lives and infected eight others with the rare Andes strain, which carries a 38% fatality rate.

Speaking at a press conference in Madrid, Tedros emphasized the importance of international solidarity in responding to the public health threat. He commended Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez for demonstrating "compassion and solidarity" by allowing the Dutch-flagged MV Hondius to dock at Spain's Canary Islands, enabling safe evacuation of passengers to their home countries. The WHO chief urged all nations to maintain 42-day quarantine protocols for potentially exposed individuals.

"At the moment, there is no sign that we are seeing the start of a larger outbreak, but of course the situation could change and, given the long incubation period of the virus, it's possible we might see more cases in the coming weeks," Tedros stated. The Andes strain is uniquely concerning as the only hantavirus variant known to transmit between humans, though Tedros stressed Saturday that "the current public health risk from hantavirus remains low" and the outbreak would not become "another Covid."

The virus causes severe cardiopulmonary syndrome after an incubation period of six to eight weeks, with symptoms progressing from flu-like illness to potentially fatal heart problems and fluid buildup in the lungs. Among evacuated passengers, most countries are implementing strict isolation measures—though Tedros noted that the U.S. approach of releasing 16 of 17 evacuated Americans after brief checkups "may have risks." A 65-year-old French patient was placed on a ventilator Tuesday as her condition deteriorated, underscoring the virus's severity.
