The Palestinian Football Association has condemned Israel's detention of two women's national soccer team players, calling the arrests part of a disturbing pattern of systematic targeting that violates international law and Olympic principles.

Israeli authorities extended the detention of Rand Halawani, 20, arrested on suspicion of "throwing objects," while Natalie Abu Diyeh, 21, a university student, was detained along with three other young Palestinian women on allegations of "promoting terrorist activities." Human rights advocates note these vague charges are frequently used to justify detentions without due process.

The Palestinian Football Association emphasized that these arrests represent "not an isolated incident" but rather a documented pattern affecting Palestinian athletes who are "routinely denied freedom of movement, safety, and the basic right to participate" in sports. The organization called on FIFA and the international sporting community to "move beyond statements and take concrete action" to hold Israel accountable.

Critics point to an apparent double standard: FIFA swiftly banned Russian teams following the 2022 Ukraine invasion, yet has declined to impose similar sanctions on Israel despite its military campaign in Gaza that Palestinian officials say has killed hundreds of athletes and sports officials since October 2023. Legal experts and human rights organizations have repeatedly urged FIFA to apply its own statutes consistently and protect athletes' fundamental rights regardless of geopolitical considerations.
