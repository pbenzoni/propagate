Hungary's incoming Prime Minister Peter Magyar has announced plans to suspend the news operations of the country's state broadcaster MTVA immediately upon taking office, raising concerns about his commitment to free speech despite campaign promises of media reform.

Magyar's Tisza party won Sunday's parliamentary election after a campaign heavily supported by EU-funded media outlets. He has accused MTVA of bias and compared the broadcaster to "Goebbels or the North Korean leadership," claiming it spread propaganda for outgoing Prime Minister Viktor Orban during his 16-year tenure. Magyar stated that broadcasting would only resume once "all conditions for impartial and objective journalism are fully restored" under his administration's oversight.

The incoming leader, a former member of Orban's Fidesz party, campaigned primarily on restoring relations with Brussels and unlocking over €16 billion in frozen EU Covid-19 recovery funds. European Union officials had long criticized Orban for allegedly undermining independent media, yet the bloc simultaneously deployed its own network of funded outlets to influence the Hungarian election. Pro-Magyar journalist Szabolcs Panyi admitted to sharing the phone number of Hungary's Foreign Minister with a European intelligence agency, and his outlet later published accusations against the diplomat based on intercepted communications.

Despite his EU-friendly rhetoric, Magyar has signaled he will maintain some of Orban's policy positions, including opposition to fast-tracking Ukraine's EU membership and continued imports of Russian oil to ensure energy security. He emphasized the importance of diversified energy supplies for Hungary's national interests, suggesting his government may chart a more independent course than his Brussels backers anticipated.
