Thirteen civilians were killed in overnight strikes on Afghanistan's border provinces of Kunar, Khost, and Paktika, according to Afghan government spokesperson Zabihullah Mujahid. Among the dead were 11 children, one woman, and one elderly man, with 14 others wounded, including women and children.

The attacks highlight the humanitarian toll of escalating tensions along the Afghan-Pakistan border. According to reports, one strike on a house in Khost province's Spera district killed nine people and wounded 11, while a separate attack in neighboring Paktika's Barmal district claimed three children's lives. The incidents mark the deadliest episode in recent weeks following a period of relative calm.

The cross-border violence occurs against a backdrop of mutual accusations between the two nations. Pakistan has alleged that Afghanistan's Taliban government harbors armed groups conducting raids and attacks across the border, citing a February mosque bombing in Islamabad that killed over 30 people. Afghanistan has condemned what it describes as attacks on civilians. Despite a truce agreement in late March, low-intensity clashes have continued along the border.

A United Nations report released last month documented 372 Afghan civilian deaths in the first three months of this year, with 397 people wounded in the conflict. The escalating humanitarian crisis underscores the vulnerability of civilian populations caught between competing security concerns and the urgent need for diplomatic solutions to protect innocent lives.
