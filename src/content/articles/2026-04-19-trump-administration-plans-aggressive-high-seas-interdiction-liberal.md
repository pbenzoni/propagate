The Trump administration is preparing to significantly expand its confrontation with Iran through a sweeping maritime interdiction campaign targeting Iranian-linked commercial vessels in international waters worldwide, according to a Wall Street Journal report citing administration sources. The operation, dubbed "Operation Economic Fury," represents a dramatic escalation beyond the existing naval blockade imposed on Iranian ports in mid-April.

Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Gen. Dan Caine indicated this week that U.S. forces would "actively pursue any Iranian-flagged vessel or any vessel attempting to provide material support to Iran," including so-called "dark fleet" tankers carrying Iranian oil. The campaign is expected to begin implementation within days and would not be geographically limited to the Persian Gulf region.

The escalation comes as diplomatic efforts between Washington and Tehran have reached an impasse, with a temporary ceasefire set to expire next week. Iranian officials have rejected what they characterize as "maximalist" U.S. demands regarding the country's nuclear program, including demands to dismantle nuclear infrastructure and surrender enriched uranium stockpiles. Tehran has condemned the existing blockade as "an act of piracy" and responded by restricting transit through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global oil shipping chokepoint.

The administration's strategy has already imposed significant economic pressure, with estimates suggesting Iran is losing over $400 million daily in export revenue due to the blockade. However, the expanded interdiction campaign raises concerns about potential miscalculation and further regional instability, particularly given reports of Iranian forces firing on commercial vessels in response to U.S. actions. White House officials maintain that the pressure campaign is intended to bring Iran to the negotiating table, though no new diplomatic talks are currently scheduled.
