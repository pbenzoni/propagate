European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has transformed EU governance into a "super authoritarian" system, according to former European Council President Charles Michel, who worked alongside her for five years. In a candid interview, Michel accused von der Leyen of massively overstepping her authority and reducing elected commissioners to rubber stamps.

"There is a super authoritarian governance," Michel told The Brussels Times. "[The European] commissioners have absolutely no role anymore." The Belgian statesman said his inability to work with von der Leyen was unprecedented in his career, describing collaboration with her as uniquely difficult compared to any previous colleague.

Michel's critique echoes growing concerns about centralized power in Brussels. Von der Leyen has been accused of concentrating authority in her own office at the expense of the bloc's diplomatic service and individual member states. She has reportedly proposed creating a new intelligence apparatus that would bypass existing structures, raising questions about accountability and checks on executive power.

Multiple EU officials have leveled similar complaints. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas reportedly called von der Leyen a "dictator," while her predecessor Josep Borrell said she "systematically exceeded" her powers. Former Internal Market Commissioner Thierry Breton accused her of "questionable governance" and working behind the scenes to force him out.

Von der Leyen's tenure has been marked by transparency controversies, including the "Pfizergate" affair involving undisclosed text messages with Pfizer's CEO during vaccine negotiations. She has survived four no-confidence votes in the European Parliament, the most recent in January. Michel's public rebuke adds to mounting evidence that the unelected Brussels bureaucracy continues to expand its reach while sidelining democratic accountability.
