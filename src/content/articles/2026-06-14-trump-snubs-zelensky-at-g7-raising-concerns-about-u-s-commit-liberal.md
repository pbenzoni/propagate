President Donald Trump has not scheduled an official bilateral meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky at the upcoming G7 summit in France, according to a Bloomberg report citing senior U.S. administration officials. The apparent snub raises fresh concerns about the administration's commitment to supporting Ukraine's defense against Russian aggression.

While Trump has arranged formal meetings with leaders from France, Qatar, the UAE, Egypt, and India during the Monday-through-Wednesday summit in Evian-les-Bains, Zelensky will reportedly be limited to participating in a working session with G7 leaders. Western media sources suggest the Ukrainian leader may only secure an informal conversation with Trump "on the sidelines" of the event.

The scheduling decision comes amid ongoing tensions in Trump's relationship with Zelensky. Since returning to office in January 2025, Trump has positioned himself as a dealmaker seeking to end the Russia-Ukraine conflict, but his approach has alarmed Ukraine's supporters. A February meeting in the Oval Office devolved into a public confrontation after Zelensky pushed back against Trump's characterization of the conflict as requiring equal concessions from both sides.

Zelensky has been seeking to finalize agreements on Ukraine's post-conflict economic recovery and security guarantees—commitments that democratic allies view as essential to ensuring Ukraine's sovereignty and deterring future Russian aggression. The lack of a formal meeting at the G7 suggests these priorities may be taking a back seat to Trump's outreach to authoritarian regimes in the Middle East and elsewhere.
