The Israeli Navy intercepted a pro-Palestinian flotilla bound for Gaza on Thursday, detaining 175 people aboard 22 vessels near the Greek island of Crete, according to Israeli officials. The activists had departed from Barcelona on April 12 with the stated intention of breaching Israel's naval blockade of the Palestinian territory.

Reporters Without Borders accused Israel of "abducting" three journalists traveling with the flotilla, including reporters from Al Jazeera and the outlet Zeteo. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, a frequent critic of Israel, accused Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of violating international maritime law and called for EU sanctions.

Israeli officials defended the operation, with Netanyahu thanking the Navy for intercepting what he characterized as "Hamas supporters." Foreign Minister Gideon Saar dismissed the flotilla as a "PR stunt," noting that all activists "were taken off the vessels unharmed" and would be returned to Greece. The passengers included Margaret Connolly, sister of Irish President Catherine Connolly, who called the interception "absolutely illegal."

Israel has maintained its naval blockade of Gaza since 2007, citing security concerns over weapons smuggling to Hamas. Israeli forces have intercepted multiple similar flotillas in recent years, including one in June 2025 that carried climate activist Greta Thunberg and French politician Rima Hassan. The blockade remains a point of international contention, with critics arguing it constitutes collective punishment while supporters maintain it is necessary for Israeli security.
