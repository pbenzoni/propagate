Iranian rescue workers painstakingly cleared debris by hand to recover sacred Torah scrolls from a Tehran synagogue damaged in an Israeli airstrike last month, according to Marjan Yeshayayi, a member of Iran's Jewish community.

In an interview, Yeshayayi described her shock at seeing the historic site reduced to rubble, with holy scriptures buried beneath. She requested that rescuers avoid using machinery to prevent further damage to the religious texts. "When I made the request, I did not believe they would accept it, but instead they said, 'OK, we will remove it manually,' and they really did. Each brick was removed by hand, and the scrolls came out safely," she recalled.

The Rafi Niya synagogue, located near Palestine Square in central Tehran, sustained heavy damage when Israeli forces struck a nearby residential building. The Israel Defense Forces later acknowledged the strike, stating they had targeted a senior Iranian commander and expressed regret over "collateral damage" to the synagogue. Yeshayayi said three of her friends died in the strike.

According to Yeshayayi, Iran's constitution protects religious minorities, including the country's approximately 8,000 Jews, who are permitted to practice their faith freely and maintain dozens of synagogues. The careful recovery effort stands in contrast to the destruction caused by the military action.
