Senior military commanders from the United States and Cuba held a rare face-to-face meeting at Guantanamo Bay naval base on Friday, as the Trump administration continues applying maximum pressure on the island's communist government.

According to US Southern Command, General Francis Donovan met with Cuba's chief of the general staff, Lieutenant General Roberto Legra Sotolongo, for what officials described as "a brief exchange on operational security matters." The meeting focused on force protection, safety of American service members and their families, and operational readiness at the strategically important naval installation that the US has maintained since 1903.

The talks come as President Trump has ramped up economic pressure on the Castro regime, including sweeping restrictions on fuel supplies that have contributed to widespread blackouts across the island. The administration has made clear that Cuba's alignment with hostile powers and its continued embrace of socialism pose ongoing security threats to the Western Hemisphere.

Cuban officials called the meeting "positive" and agreed to maintain communication between military commands. However, the island's leadership continues to reject American calls for political liberalization and economic reform, with Cuban President Miguel Diaz-Canel vowing to defend the regime against what he characterizes as illegal pressure under international law.

The meeting occurred against a backdrop of heightened US military presence in the Caribbean, including the deployment of the USS Nimitz carrier strike group to the region. The Justice Department recently indicted former Cuban leader Raul Castro over the 1996 shootdown of two American aircraft operated by anti-communist Cuban exiles.
