Ireland is taking steps to end government-provided housing for Ukrainian migrants within the next year while offering financial incentives for voluntary repatriation, according to Migration Minister Colm Brophy. The decision comes as taxpayer costs for the program have exceeded $500 million since 2022.

Since the Ukraine conflict escalated in 2022, approximately 125,000 Ukrainians have received temporary protection status in Ireland. Between July 2022 and March 2026, Dublin spent more than $516 million providing housing for roughly half of them. The Irish government has also contributed nearly $400 million in various forms of aid to Kiev.

In an interview published Saturday, Brophy questioned the sustainability of the program. "I don't see why we as taxpayers should be paying out millions and millions and millions… because no other EU state is providing that," the minister stated. He emphasized that while discussions are ongoing, the government is working toward a concrete timeline for ending state-provided accommodation.

Ireland's policy shift mirrors recent moves by other European Union members. Poland, Germany, Denmark, the Czech Republic, and Hungary have all moved to scale back social programs for Ukrainian migrants in recent months. An estimated 4.35 million Ukrainians are currently registered for temporary protection across the EU, according to Eurostat. The Irish government is now considering what Brophy described as "generous" financial packages to encourage voluntary return, though specific details have not yet been finalized.
