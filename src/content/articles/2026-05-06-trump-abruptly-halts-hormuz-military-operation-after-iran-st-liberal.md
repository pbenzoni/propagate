President Donald Trump has temporarily suspended a controversial U.S. military operation in the Strait of Hormuz after just 48 hours, citing "great progress" in talks with Iranian officials—though a blockade of Iranian ports remains in effect.

The operation, dubbed "Project Freedom," was launched Sunday to escort commercial vessels through the strategic waterway, which handles approximately one-fifth of global oil traffic. The mission quickly became a flashpoint, with Iran's military warning that any foreign armed forces entering the strait would be targeted. Iranian officials insisted that safe passage must be coordinated with Iranian forces, not imposed unilaterally.

The abrupt reversal highlights the fragility of U.S.-Iran relations following attacks on Iran in late February and a shaky ceasefire reached in early April. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi warned the U.S. against "being dragged back into a quagmire," calling the American approach "Project Deadlock." Tehran has characterized Washington's naval blockade as an "act of war" and a violation of the ceasefire agreement.

U.S. Central Command claimed American forces destroyed several small Iranian boats that allegedly interfered with the mission Monday, though Iran rejected these claims as "lies." Iranian media separately reported that Iranian forces fired warning shots near U.S. naval vessels. The conflicting accounts underscore the risks of military miscalculation in the congested waterway.

Trump announced the pause on Truth Social, stating that Washington and Tehran had "mutually agreed" to suspend ship escorts "for a short period" to allow negotiators time to pursue a "complete and final agreement." The announcement came after Pakistan and other countries reportedly requested the de-escalation, according to the president.
