French intelligence agencies are investigating an alleged disinformation campaign orchestrated by BlackCore, an Israeli private intelligence firm that targeted progressive mayoral candidates who had criticized Israeli government policies. The operation, which involved fabricated social media profiles, AI-generated deepfake images, and false accusations, represents a troubling escalation in foreign interference in democratic elections.

According to reporting by Reuters, Liberation, and Haaretz, the campaign focused on three left-wing candidates from the France Unbowed party in the cities of Marseille, Toulouse, and Roubaix during March elections. In Marseille, candidate Sebastien Delogu faced false rape allegations spread through fake blogger personas and QR code stickers, while AI-generated fake nude images were circulated online alongside content mocking his support for Palestinian rights. Similar tactics were deployed against the other candidates, including baseless accusations and manipulative content designed to exploit religious divisions among voters.

BlackCore's website—taken offline after the investigation became public—described the company as specializing in "information warfare and digital competition," offering services to "shape narratives" for governments and political campaigns. The firm's alleged activities mirror those of Black Cube, another Israeli private intelligence operation that has been linked to election interference in Cyprus and Slovenia earlier this year, raising concerns about a pattern of foreign manipulation of European democratic processes.

French authorities are now working to identify who commissioned BlackCore's services, as investigators examine whether the operation violated France's election laws and the EU's data protection regulations. The case highlights growing concerns about the vulnerability of democratic institutions to sophisticated disinformation campaigns and the need for stronger safeguards against foreign interference in elections.
