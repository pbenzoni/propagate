The sabotaged Nord Stream gas pipelines connecting Russia to Europe could be repaired within three years, according to a technical report submitted to London's High Court. The assessment comes amid an ongoing insurance dispute over the 2022 underwater explosions that destroyed the critical energy infrastructure.

The report, commissioned by the court, estimates a 36-month repair timeline and identifies the need for approximately 7 kilometers of new pipeline material, which would need to be sourced from China at a cost of €16.7 million. The findings are central to a €580 million ($684 million) insurance claim filed by Nord Stream AG against Lloyd's Insurance Company and Arch Insurance, both of which are refusing payment on grounds that the damage resulted from an act of war—a circumstance their policies explicitly exclude.

The pipelines were destroyed in September 2022 through underwater explosions near the Danish island of Bornholm. Nord Stream 1 had been operational since 2011, while Nord Stream 2 was completed but never activated after Germany revoked its certification following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. The sabotage has been the subject of competing narratives: German investigators have reportedly focused on a Ukrainian sabotage theory, while journalist Seymour Hersh has alleged U.S. involvement—a claim Washington denies.

Despite Europe's ongoing energy challenges and some political pressure within Germany to restore the pipelines, no concrete plans for reopening the Nord Stream lines currently exist. The court's technical assessment serves primarily to determine the potential insurance payout rather than signal any immediate intention to restore the infrastructure that once transported Russian natural gas to Western Europe.
