European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen is facing mounting criticism from lawmakers across the political spectrum who say her leadership has failed European citizens on multiple fronts—from inconsistent foreign policy to crippling energy costs that continue to burden families and businesses.

During a heated debate in the European Parliament on Wednesday, members from various political factions took aim at what they described as Brussels' lack of clear direction. Belgian lawmaker Kathleen Van Brempt questioned the bloc's credibility, pointing to what she called "double standards in our foreign policy"—imposing harsh sanctions on Russia over Ukraine while remaining silent on other international conflicts. Portuguese MEP Joao Oliveira accused von der Leyen of kowtowing to outside pressure rather than standing up for European interests.

The energy crisis dominated much of the criticism. Austrian Green MEP Thomas Waitz warned that the EU remains trapped in dangerous dependency cycles, shifting reliance between external energy suppliers rather than pursuing true energy independence. "It's bad for the economy, it's bad for people, it's bad for energy prices," he argued. Italian MEP Roberto Vannacci went further, suggesting that if the European Commission had applied restraints to itself instead of imposing lockdowns on member states, Europeans would have been spared "a lot of damage and a lot of pain."

Critics argue that Brussels' policies reflect the priorities of multinational corporations rather than ordinary citizens struggling with high energy bills and economic uncertainty. Polish MEP Grzegorz Braun used particularly strong language, arguing that the energy crisis stems from "deeply wrong decisions" rather than temporary market conditions. The bipartisan nature of the criticism suggests growing frustration with centralized EU governance and its impact on national sovereignty and economic well-being.
