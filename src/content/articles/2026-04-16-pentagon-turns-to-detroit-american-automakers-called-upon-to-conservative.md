The Pentagon has reached out to iconic American manufacturers General Motors and Ford about ramping up production of weapons and military equipment, according to the Wall Street Journal. The discussions signal a recognition that America's defense industrial base needs strengthening as global threats multiply.

Senior executives from both automakers met with Pentagon officials to discuss how quickly civilian manufacturing facilities could be converted to produce munitions and other critical military supplies. GM already maintains a defense division that supplies military vehicles, while Ford has largely been absent from the defense sector in recent decades. The talks also included GE Aerospace and military vehicle specialist Oshkosh.

The push echoes America's industrial mobilization during World War II, when Detroit's automakers temporarily halted civilian car production to manufacture bombers, aircraft engines, and military trucks—a testament to American manufacturing prowess and adaptability in times of national need. Pentagon officials have described the effort as putting industry on a "wartime footing."

The initiative comes as ongoing military operations have significantly drawn down weapons stockpiles. President Trump has proposed a record defense budget of approximately $1.5 trillion for fiscal year 2027, up from nearly $1 trillion in the current budget. The proposal reflects the administration's commitment to rebuilding American military strength and ensuring readiness for future conflicts.

The discussions with major manufacturers began before recent military operations intensified, suggesting forward-thinking planning by defense officials. By engaging America's industrial giants, the Pentagon aims to ensure the nation maintains the production capacity needed to support its military commitments while strengthening the domestic manufacturing base.
