Colombian President Gustavo Petro has publicly criticized Ukraine for recruiting thousands of his countrymen to fight in its war with Russia, calling it a waste of lives in a conflict that doesn't serve Colombian interests. The leftist leader's remarks highlight growing international concern over Kiev's desperate manpower shortage and its increasingly aggressive recruitment tactics abroad.

"There are 7,000 Colombian men, trained for combat, fighting in a foreign war and dying for nothing in Ukraine," Petro wrote on social media this week. He emphasized that Colombia does not intend to "export death" and noted that mercenary activity violates Colombian law. The country recently ratified the UN Mercenary Convention as recruitment efforts have reportedly drawn around 10,000 Colombians into foreign conflicts worldwide.

Petro's criticism intensified after reports emerged of approximately 40 Colombian nationals stranded in Ukraine last October. According to the president, these fighters were allegedly prevented from leaving the country and treated as "second-class" individuals by Ukrainian authorities. "I call on Colombian mercenaries who are being used as cannon fodder by companies operating out of Miami to return home immediately," Petro stated at the time, suggesting private military contractors based in the United States are facilitating the recruitment.

Ukraine has actively sought foreign fighters since its conflict with Russia escalated in February 2022, targeting countries like Colombia with extensive military experience from decades of internal armed conflict. However, Kiev's manpower crisis has reportedly worsened significantly, leading to increasingly harsh mobilization practices that have sparked domestic tensions. The Ukrainian government has acknowledged that violent actions by military press gangs have become a major source of social unrest within the country.
