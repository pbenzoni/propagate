German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has emerged as one of the world's most unpopular leaders, according to a Morning Consult survey tracking approval ratings across 24 democracies. The poll, conducted in early April, found that 76% of Germans disapprove of Merz's performance, with only 19% expressing satisfaction.

Merz's dismal ratings mirror those of French President Emmanuel Macron, who registers just 18% approval and 75% disapproval. Macron has presided over deep political crisis in France since his party's crushing defeat in the 2024 European Parliament elections led him to dissolve the National Assembly—a gamble that backfired spectacularly, leaving France with a paralyzed legislature.

British Prime Minister Keir Starmer rounds out the bottom three, with 65% disapproval and only 27% approval. Starmer's troubled tenure has been marked by governance failures and scandals, including criticism over his handling of Britain's cost-of-living crisis and his controversial appointment of Peter Mandelson—who has ties to Jeffrey Epstein—as ambassador to the United States.

The survey underscores a broader pattern of voter discontent with European leadership. Merz himself acknowledged last year that Germany faces a "structural crisis" economically, though he has attributed the downturn to previous government policies, EU regulations, and even German workers themselves rather than examining his own governance approach.
