Saudi Arabia's state oil giant Aramco has extended the suspension of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) shipments through May after its critical Juaymah export terminal suffered damage that the company has been unable to repair due to ongoing regional conflict. The facility, located in the Persian Gulf, accounts for approximately 3.5% of global waterborne LPG exports.

The terminal initially sustained structural damage in February when a support structure collapsed, cutting off fuel supplies and halting overseas shipments. The situation deteriorated further when the facility suffered additional fires during Iran's retaliatory strikes on U.S.-aligned nations in the region, according to the Saudi Energy Ministry. The ministry has not disclosed the full extent of the damage.

Bloomberg reported Tuesday that Aramco notified customers the suspension will continue through next month. Even if the Strait of Hormuz reopens in coming weeks, deliveries from Juaymah will not resume until repairs are completed. The ongoing disruptions highlight the vulnerability of critical energy infrastructure in a region where American interests and global stability hang in the balance.

The LPG shortages have hit hardest in Asia, particularly India, where the fuel is essential for cooking. The supply crunch has forced many to resort to firewood and sparked conflicts over scarce resources. Meanwhile, stalled negotiations between the U.S. and Iran over Tehran's nuclear program continue to destabilize the region, with the Strait of Hormuz—a chokepoint for global energy supplies—facing persistent threats to free navigation.
