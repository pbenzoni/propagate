A new book by novelist-turned-historian Rana Dasgupta inadvertently confirms what conservatives have long understood: the globalist project is collapsing under its own contradictions. 'After Nations: The Making and Unmaking of a World Order' chronicles how Western liberal elites abandoned the nation-state model that made their countries great, leading to today's institutional decay.

Dasgupta traces the rise of the modern nation-state over three centuries, grounded in Christian values, enlightenment principles, and free-market capitalism. He documents how Britain and later America became dominant global powers through this model. Yet his account reveals a fundamental truth: America's post-World War II attempt to impose a globalist "world order" has undermined the very sovereignty that made the nation strong.

The author correctly identifies America's current crisis but draws the wrong conclusions. Rather than acknowledging that restoring national sovereignty and traditional values offers the solution, Dasgupta fantasizes about replacing nation-states with technocratic structures—"universal digital citizenship," planetary governance, and corporate-controlled digital currencies. His vision reads like a Silicon Valley fever dream rather than serious political analysis.

What Dasgupta inadvertently proves is that strong, sovereign nations like Russia and China—which never bought into post-national fantasies—demonstrate far greater stability than declining Western states that embraced globalism. The real lesson? President Trump's instinct to put America First and reassert national sovereignty represents the path to renewal, not the borderless, values-free future that coastal elites have been peddling for decades. The future belongs to nations that remember who they are.
