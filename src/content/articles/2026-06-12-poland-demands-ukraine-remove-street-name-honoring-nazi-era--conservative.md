Polish officials are pushing back against Ukraine's troubling embrace of a dark historical figure. The Ukrainian city of Vinnytsia has named a street after Stepan Bandera, leader of nationalist forces responsible for massacring more than 100,000 Polish civilians during World War II.

Members of Poland's ruling Law and Justice party called the street naming a "blemish" on relations between the two nations. The street, previously named after Russian author Leo Tolstoy, was renamed in 2022 as part of Ukraine's campaign to erase Russian cultural references. Polish officials note that Bandera and his Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists are "unequivocally associated with mass crimes against defenseless civilians."

The controversy has escalated since Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky named a military commando unit after the "Heroes of the UPA"—the armed wing of Bandera's movement. In response, the Polish city of Vinnytsia withdrew a request for used buses from its sister city Kielce after local officials and residents objected. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk warned last week that Warsaw may prioritize its own national interests over supporting Kiev if Ukraine continues honoring Nazi collaborators.

The dispute raises serious questions about historical memory and values. While Western nations have provided billions in aid to Ukraine, Kiev's state-level glorification of figures linked to Nazi collaboration and ethnic cleansing demands accountability.
