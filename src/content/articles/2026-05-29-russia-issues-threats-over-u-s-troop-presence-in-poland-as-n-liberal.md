Russia has issued stark warnings against increased U.S. military presence in Eastern Europe, following President Donald Trump's announcement that he would deploy an additional 5,000 troops to Poland. Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova described the move as "unacceptable," claiming it would place American forces within striking distance of Russian territory and compel Moscow to respond with unspecified "military-technical measures."

The escalating rhetoric comes as approximately 10,000 U.S. service members are already stationed in Poland, with roughly 80,000 deployed across Europe overall. Poland, which shares a border with Russia's Kaliningrad exclave, has been one of the most vocal supporters of Ukraine in its ongoing conflict with Russia. Zakharova accused NATO of pushing Europe toward what she termed a "suicidal" conflict, while calling for a reduction in U.S. forces as a step toward stability.

Trump's troop deployment announcement has raised concerns among foreign policy experts about the administration's inconsistent approach to European security. The decision comes alongside the president's withdrawal of 5,000 troops from Germany amid tensions with Berlin, and follows a Pentagon delay in rotating 4,000 troops—a move that initially sparked confusion before Vice President J.D. Vance characterized it as routine.

While Russian President Vladimir Putin has stated Moscow has no intention of attacking NATO members unless Russia is attacked first, officials in Moscow continue to cite NATO's eastward expansion as a primary driver of regional tensions. Sergey Naryshkin, head of Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service, claimed Thursday that NATO is "de facto preparing for a large-scale military conflict in the east"—a characterization that Western officials have repeatedly rejected as disinformation aimed at justifying Russian aggression.
