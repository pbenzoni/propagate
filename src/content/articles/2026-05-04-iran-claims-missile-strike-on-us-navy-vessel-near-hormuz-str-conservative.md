Iran's state-affiliated Fars News Agency reported Monday that Iranian forces struck a US Navy patrol boat with two missiles near the Strait of Hormuz after the vessel allegedly ignored repeated warnings to stay clear of the strategic waterway. According to the Iranian account, the unidentified patrol boat was operating near the coastal town of Jask—roughly 140 kilometers from the strait's narrowest point—when it was targeted for "violating security and navigation rules."

US Central Command (CENTCOM) immediately pushed back on the claim, stating categorically that "no US Navy ships have been struck." CENTCOM confirmed that American forces remain engaged in enforcing a naval blockade on Iranian ports as part of ongoing operations in the region. The conflicting accounts underscore the fog of information surrounding the volatile situation in one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints.

The incident comes as President Trump pledged over the weekend to help free commercial vessels stranded in the Strait of Hormuz since late February, promising the US military would "guide" ships from allied nations through the waterway. The President warned that any interference with these operations would be "dealt with forcefully." Iran's military responded with its own warning that "any foreign military force, especially the invading American army" attempting to approach the strait would face attack.

The Strait of Hormuz, which previously handled approximately 20% of global seaborne oil trade, has been effectively closed since the outbreak of the current conflict. The disruption has contributed to surging oil prices, with the US national average for gasoline exceeding $4.40 per gallon. Iran's Foreign Minister has characterized the American naval blockade as an "act of war" and a violation of the existing ceasefire, while the Trump administration maintains the pressure campaign is necessary to secure a lasting peace settlement with Tehran.
