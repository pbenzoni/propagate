Italian Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani is urging his fellow citizens to focus on having more children as a solution to the nation's worsening demographic crisis—and as an alternative to increased migration.

Speaking at Rome's Festival del Lavoro, an annual gathering focused on labor market issues, Tajani laid out the stark reality facing Italy: the country's fertility rate has plummeted to a record low of 1.14 children per woman, well below the 2.1 needed for a stable population. Over the past decade, Italy has consistently had one of the lowest birthrates in Europe.

"We have a problem of demographic decline, and we must understand if we want to have more children," Tajani stated. "If we do have more children, then we can also say: right, let's reduce the number of legal migrants coming to work in our businesses. But if not, we won't have any workers." The minister acknowledged the direct connection between falling birth rates and the need for foreign workers, noting that "the lower the birth rate, the more we need foreign workers in our country."

While Tajani did not present a specific policy proposal to encourage higher fertility rates, his comments reflect growing concerns among center-right governments across Europe about preserving national identity while addressing labor shortages. Left-wing opposition politicians criticized the remarks, with Democratic Party senator Valeria Valente calling them "absurd" and arguing Italy should instead embrace further European integration. The demographic challenge remains urgent: even if birth rates were to increase immediately, it would take decades before those children entered the workforce.
