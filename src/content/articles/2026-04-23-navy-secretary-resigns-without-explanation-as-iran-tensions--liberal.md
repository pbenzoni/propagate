Navy Secretary John C. Phelan has abruptly resigned from the administration, effective immediately, the Pentagon announced Tuesday with no public explanation for his departure. The timing has raised concerns among defense policy experts, coming as the U.S. maintains a naval blockade of Iranian ports.

Phelan, a businessman and investor with no prior military experience, had served just over a year after his nomination by President Trump. His exit marks the latest in a series of sudden leadership changes at the Pentagon, following the recent forced removal of Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George and two other senior Army generals by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

Undersecretary Hung Cao will assume the role of acting Navy secretary. While the position does not directly authorize combat operations—which flow through the president and defense secretary—the Navy secretary oversees critical departmental functions including readiness, deployments, and policy implementation during the ongoing standoff with Iran in and around the Strait of Hormuz.

The abrupt personnel shifts have sparked questions about continuity and accountability within military leadership at a moment when diplomatic efforts with Iran remain stalled. Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell offered only a brief statement thanking Phelan for his service, providing no insight into the circumstances surrounding his departure or whether it was connected to current naval operations in the Middle East.
