The British Foreign Office has dismantled a specialized unit responsible for tracking potential violations of international humanitarian law by Israeli forces in Gaza, according to a report from The Guardian. The move comes even as UK Foreign Secretary Yvette Cooper recently emphasized that supporting international law represents a "core British value" central to her department's mission.

The shutdown of the International Humanitarian Law (IHL) cell also means the end of funding for the Conflict and Security Monitoring Project, operated by the Center for Information Resilience. British officials have been cautioned that this decision will eliminate their access to a database containing 26,000 verified incidents compiled since Israeli military operations began in Gaza following the October 2023 attack by Hamas. This database is believed to be the world's most comprehensive repository of such documentation and had previously been utilized by UK authorities when making decisions about arms export licenses to Israel.

The closure appears connected to the UK cabinet's decision to reduce the overseas aid budget to 0.3% of GDP. Katie Fallon, advocacy manager at Campaign Against Arms Trade, expressed concern that the shutdown could allow the government to "obscure unimaginable violations and crimes committed against the most vulnerable people in conflict and sustain arms sales at any cost."

While the UK suspended approximately 30 of its 350 weapons export licenses to Israel, citing concerns about international law violations, Israeli customs data indicated that UK-made munitions imports nearly doubled in early 2025 compared to the previous three years. Palestinian health authorities report that over 72,000 people have been killed and more than 172,000 wounded in Gaza during the ongoing conflict.
