German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, a longtime champion of transatlantic ties and former BlackRock executive, delivered a sobering assessment of America's current state at a Catholic youth conference in Wuerzburg. The conservative leader said he would no longer recommend the United States as a destination for his own children to study or work, citing what he called a deteriorating "social climate" and vanishing economic opportunities.

"I am a great admirer of America," Merz said during the panel discussion. "At the moment my admiration is not growing." He pointed to deep polarization and argued that even highly educated Americans are increasingly struggling to find meaningful employment—a stark departure from the nation's historic promise as the "land of opportunity."

Recent Federal Reserve data supports his concerns. The New York Fed reported unemployment among young degree holders aged 22–27 at 5.7%, with underemployment exceeding 41%. Nearly half of recent graduates now work in jobs that don't require their degrees, while hiring for entry-level professional roles has ground to a halt. White-collar layoffs in tech, finance, and corporate sectors have accelerated as companies embrace AI and slash costs.

Merz's remarks come amid tensions with the Trump administration over Iran policy, and follow the Pentagon's recent announcement of troop withdrawals from Germany. Yet around 31,000 American personnel will remain stationed there—more than in Italy, the UK, and Spain combined—underscoring Germany's continued dependence on U.S. security guarantees even as Berlin publicly questions American leadership.
