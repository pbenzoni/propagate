Elon Musk is sounding the alarm about what he calls a dangerous expansion of government surveillance in the United Kingdom, warning that a new social media ban for minors is really about forcing digital tracking of all internet users.

British Prime Minister Keir Starmer announced Monday that children under 16 will be barred from platforms like X, Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook, with the measure framed as protecting "the safety and happiness of our children." Teens aged 16-17 would face nightly online curfews. The ban is expected to take effect next year.

But Musk, owner of X, immediately pushed back. "This censorship law is a wolf in sheep's clothing," he wrote, declaring that its "real goal is to enable the UK government to track everyone." In another post, the billionaire stated bluntly: "The UK is a police state."

The concern centers on how the ban would be enforced. To verify users are over 16, the government is considering requiring submission of government-issued ID or facial recognition scans—the same model Australia adopted. Digital rights groups warn this creates unprecedented surveillance infrastructure. "Over 16s in the UK will have to hand over identity documents or biometric data to unregulated age verification companies," the Open Rights Group cautioned.

Critics note this effectively implements mandatory digital ID through the back door—something Starmer pushed for directly last year before public backlash forced him to drop the plan. "We will become one of the first democracies in the world to require IDs to access the internet," warned Big Brother Watch Director Silkie Carlo. "We are sleepwalking towards a total surveillance state."

The Starmer government has already expanded facial recognition cameras at demonstrations and proposed requiring ID to use mobile phones, raising concerns about government overreach disguised as public safety measures.
