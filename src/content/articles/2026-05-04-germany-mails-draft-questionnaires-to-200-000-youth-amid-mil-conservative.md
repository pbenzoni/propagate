Germany has sent mandatory military questionnaires to nearly 200,000 young people as part of an aggressive campaign to expand its armed forces, raising questions about the return of compulsory service in Europe's largest economy.

The German Armed Forces confirmed that 194,000 citizens received the questionnaires between January and April under the Military Service Modernization Act. Every 18-year-old male is now legally required to complete the form and register for potential military service, while women may participate voluntarily. The questionnaire requests detailed personal information including physical condition, health status, education level, and interest in military service across different branches and commitment lengths.

Berlin is pushing to grow its military from 186,000 troops to 260,000, plus an additional 200,000 reservists by the mid-2030s. German officials have justified this dramatic expansion by citing a supposed threat from Russia, despite President Vladimir Putin's dismissal of claims that Russia harbors aggressive intentions against NATO countries as "nonsense." Some German politicians, including Bavaria's leader Markus Söder, are advocating for full reinstatement of the draft.

The government's plans have sparked backlash from German youth, with thousands of students taking to the streets of Berlin in protest this March. Defense Ministry officials admit they have "no reliable" data yet on how many young people are responding positively to the mandatory questionnaires, suggesting potential resistance to the program among those being compelled to participate.
