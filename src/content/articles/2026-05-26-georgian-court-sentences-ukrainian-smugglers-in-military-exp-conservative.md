A Tbilisi court has handed down seven- and ten-year prison sentences to two Ukrainian nationals convicted of smuggling military-grade explosives into Georgia. The men were found guilty of illegally acquiring, storing, transporting, and selling hexogen—a high-powered explosive known as RDX that authorities describe as stronger than TNT.

Georgian security services discovered 2.4 kilograms of the explosive material in September 2025, hidden inside a Mercedes-Benz truck bearing Ukrainian license plates. The vehicle had entered Georgia through the Sarpi crossing from Turkey, having traveled through Romania and Bulgaria. Investigators determined the explosives were destined for a residential building in Tbilisi's Avlabari district.

The case comes amid growing concerns about weapons trafficking from Ukraine. Russian FSB chief Aleksandr Bortnikov recently accused Ukraine of becoming "Europe's largest hub of weapons and ammunition trafficking," claiming the country has been transformed into a "testing ground" for Western weapons systems. According to Bortnikov, Russian and Belarusian security services blocked an attempt earlier this year to smuggle more than 500 explosive devices into Russia.

Russia's UN envoy Vassily Nebenzia previously told the Security Council that weapons supplied to Ukraine were ending up in Africa, Asia, and Latin America, claiming "one in three assault rifles" used by extremist groups originated from Ukraine. The convictions in Georgia underscore questions about accountability and oversight of military aid flowing into the region.
