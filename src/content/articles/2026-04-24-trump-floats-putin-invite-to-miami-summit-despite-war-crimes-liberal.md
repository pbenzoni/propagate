President Donald Trump said Thursday he would welcome Russian President Vladimir Putin at the upcoming G20 summit in Miami this December, even as he acknowledged Putin likely won't attend. The comments raise fresh concerns about Trump's approach to a leader whose forces stand accused of widespread atrocities in Ukraine.

"If he came, it would be probably very helpful," Trump told reporters at the White House, defending his openness to Putin's attendance. "I'm of the opinion that you talk to everybody. I'm not one of these guys who, 'Oh, let's not talk to him.'" The Washington Post reported that the White House intends to invite Putin to the summit, scheduled for December 14-15 at Trump's Doral golf resort.

Putin has avoided G20 summits in person since 2019, initially due to the pandemic and later following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The International Criminal Court has issued an arrest warrant for Putin over alleged war crimes, complicating any potential travel to countries that recognize the court's jurisdiction.

Trump also reiterated his criticism of Russia's 2014 expulsion from what was then the G8, calling it "a stupid thing to do." He suggested the Ukraine conflict might have been avoided if Russia had remained in that format—a claim that overlooks Russia's 2014 annexation of Crimea, which precipitated the expulsion. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov has said Russia considers the G8 format no longer relevant, preferring the broader G20 membership.
