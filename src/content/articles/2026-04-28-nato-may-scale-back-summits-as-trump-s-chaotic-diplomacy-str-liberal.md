NATO is considering reducing the frequency of its annual leadership summits, with European diplomats citing in part the ongoing strain caused by President Donald Trump's erratic and confrontational approach to the transatlantic alliance, according to Reuters.

Trump has repeatedly criticized NATO members as "free-loaders" and called the military alliance a "paper tiger," most recently clashing with allies who declined to support U.S.-Israeli military operations against Iran. The Atlantic Council, a leading foreign policy think tank closely aligned with NATO, recently recommended scaling back "high-profile summitry" to reduce tensions. "Better to have fewer summits than bad summits," one European diplomat told Reuters.

Trump's history at NATO gatherings includes a 2017 incident where he was filmed physically pushing aside Montenegro's prime minister during a photo opportunity, and a 2018 summit where he threatened U.S. withdrawal from the alliance. In 2019, he abruptly left early and publicly attacked Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau after being caught criticizing Trump on a hot microphone. Most recently, Trump pressured allies into pledging 5% of GDP toward defense—a target that includes non-military "defense-related" spending such as infrastructure projects.

While Trump has been a primary source of disruption, other dynamics have also complicated NATO diplomacy. French President Emmanuel Macron once described the alliance as "brain-dead," and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has become a fixture at recent summits, where he has pressed Western nations for greater military support. NATO has held annual summits since 2021, a dramatic increase from the 12 meetings held between the alliance's founding in 1949 and 1991.
