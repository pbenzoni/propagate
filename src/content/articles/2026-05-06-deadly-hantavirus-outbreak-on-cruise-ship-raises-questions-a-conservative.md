A hantavirus outbreak aboard a Dutch-flagged cruise ship has claimed three lives and left multiple passengers critically ill, raising serious concerns about disease transmission across international borders. The MV Hondius, which departed from Ushuaia, Argentina on April 1, was finally cleared to dock in the Canary Islands after evacuating three passengers at Cape Verde.

Seven people aboard the vessel have contracted hantavirus, with one passenger in critical condition and at least three others showing symptoms, according to the World Health Organization. Suspected cases have now emerged in France, Germany, and Switzerland among passengers who disembarked at earlier ports. The ship carries 149 passengers and crew, including 23 British citizens and 17 Americans.

Argentinian officials believe a Dutch couple contracted the deadly Andes strain while visiting a landfill site in Ushuaia before boarding. This particular strain, carried by rodents in Argentina and Chile, has a fatality rate of 40 percent and is the only hantavirus known to spread between humans through close contact. The pathogen causes hantavirus cardiopulmonary syndrome (HPS), with symptoms progressing from flu-like illness to severe respiratory distress and heart complications.

Hantaviruses infect between 10,000 and 100,000 people globally each year, with South American strains proving far deadlier than their European and Asian counterparts. While 'old world' strains kill less than 1 percent of those infected, 'new world' strains can kill up to 50 percent. The Andes strain was first identified in 1993 after a mysterious outbreak in the Four Corners region killed 17 people, and localized outbreaks continue to occur across the Americas.
