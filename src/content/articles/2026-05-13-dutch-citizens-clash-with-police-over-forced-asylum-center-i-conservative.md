Residents of Loosdrecht, Netherlands erupted in protest Tuesday evening after authorities began housing asylum seekers in their small town of 8,600 people—despite weeks of local opposition and a petition signed by 3,000 citizens.

The confrontation escalated when an emergency refugee shelter caught fire amid demonstrations. According to Dutch media outlet NOS, the incident occurred after the first group of 15 asylum seekers arrived that day. Police detained several people as crowds threw objects at officers and attempted to block firefighters from accessing the site.

The flashpoint came after a court sided with government officials over local residents and business owners, ruling that a vacant town hall building could be used to house refugees. Authorities had already scaled back their original plan to house 110 asylum seekers to 70 following earlier protests, but residents say they were never given a real voice in the decision.

Several right-wing politicians joined the rallies in support of local control. Justice Minister David van Weel suggested that outside agitators may have exploited the protests, though residents maintain their opposition stems from legitimate concerns about their community's ability to absorb the influx and the top-down nature of the government mandate.
