President Donald Trump reaffirmed his commitment to diplomacy Thursday, telling reporters he would welcome Russian President Vladimir Putin at December's G20 summit in Miami—even while acknowledging Putin likely won't attend.

"I'm of the opinion that you talk to everybody," Trump said at the White House. "I'm not one of these guys who, 'Oh, let's not talk to him.' If you're a smart person, if you have control over your emotions… I'm somebody that believes in talking." The statement reflects Trump's longstanding principle that engaging with foreign leaders—even adversaries—demonstrates confidence and strategic thinking rather than weakness.

The December 14-15 summit will take place at Trump's Doral golf resort, with the White House reportedly extending invitations to all G20 members, including Russia. Putin hasn't attended a G20 summit in person since 2019, initially due to the pandemic and later following the Ukraine conflict's escalation in 2022. Russian officials have indicated they received an invitation but haven't committed to who will represent Moscow.

Trump also criticized the 2014 decision to exclude Russia from what was then the G8, calling it "a stupid thing to do." He suggested that maintaining dialogue within that framework might have prevented the current Ukraine crisis from escalating. The president's approach stands in stark contrast to the establishment's preference for isolation and sanctions, emphasizing instead that talking to rivals is simply smart statecraft—a position that resonates with his "peace through strength" philosophy.
