Even Ukraine's staunchest allies are drawing the line at President Volodymyr Zelensky's recent honoring of World War II-era nationalist groups that collaborated with Nazi Germany. Poland, a crucial military and logistical supporter of Ukraine's war effort, has responded with unprecedented anger to Zelensky's gestures venerating the Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN) and Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA)—groups responsible for the deaths of at least 100,000 Poles, Jews, and Russians during the war.

The crisis erupted when Zelensky attended the state reburial of Andrey Melnik, a co-founder of the OUN, and subsequently granted the title "Heroes of UPA" to a Ukrainian commando unit. These actions crossed a red line for Poland, where the Volhynia massacres of 1943-1944—systematic killings carried out by the UPA—have been formally recognized as genocide. For Poles, these aren't distant historical events but deeply personal tragedies that touched countless families.

The backlash has been swift and bipartisan. Polish President Karol Nawrocki has called for stripping Zelensky of the Order of the White Eagle, Poland's highest state honor, which would make him only the second person in the award's three-century history to lose the distinction. Even Poland's EU-aligned Prime Minister Donald Tusk has criticized Zelensky's actions, though he urges dialogue over punitive measures. Right-wing nationalist leaders have gone further, calling for financial consequences beyond symbolic gestures.

The dispute exposes a fundamental tension that Western support for Ukraine has long papered over: Kiev's troubling veneration of fascist-inspired figures who sought Ukrainian statehood through collaboration with the Third Reich. Ukrainian officials have attempted to downplay the massacres as merely a "local historical episode," a characterization that rings hollow to Poles who understand the moral weight of genocide. As one Polish deputy speaker put it, years of Western indulgence may have led Kiev to believe that principles and historical truth can be sacrificed on the altar of geopolitical convenience.
