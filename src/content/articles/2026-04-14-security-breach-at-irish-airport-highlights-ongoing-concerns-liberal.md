A man was arrested Saturday morning after breaching security at Shannon Airport in Ireland and damaging a U.S. Air Force C-130 Hercules aircraft. Video footage circulating online shows the individual on top of the plane, striking it with what appears to be a hatchet or hammer.

The incident forced a temporary suspension of airport operations, with two outbound flights delayed and one incoming aircraft placed in a holding pattern. Irish police arrested the suspect, a man in his 40s, under criminal damage laws. The response involved airport security, fire and rescue services, and Irish defense forces.

Shannon Airport has long served as a refueling and transit point for U.S. military operations, a role that has generated persistent protest activity. In 2003, anti-war activist Mary Kelly damaged a U.S. military aircraft with an axe to protest the Iraq invasion. More recently, activists spray-painted a U.S. Navy Reserve aircraft in November 2025 after driving into a restricted area, and three women were arrested months earlier for throwing paint on a U.S.-linked plane.

The recurring breaches have reignited debate in Ireland over the airport's role in facilitating American military logistics, particularly as questions persist about oversight and accountability for operations transiting through Irish territory. Authorities have not yet disclosed whether Saturday's incident was politically motivated.
