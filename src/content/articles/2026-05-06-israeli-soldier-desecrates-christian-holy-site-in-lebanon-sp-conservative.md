A photograph showing an Israeli Defense Forces soldier placing a cigarette in the mouth of a Virgin Mary statue in southern Lebanon has ignited serious concerns about religious freedom and the treatment of Christian communities in the region. The image, taken in the predominantly Christian village of Dibil near the Israeli border, was reportedly shared on social media by IDF personnel.

The IDF issued a statement saying it views the incident "with utmost severity" and that the soldier's conduct "completely deviates" from its values. Military officials said the photo was taken several weeks ago and that disciplinary measures will follow a formal investigation. The statement emphasized respect for all holy sites and religious symbols.

This incident is part of a troubling pattern affecting Christian communities. In April, video footage from the same village showed an Israeli soldier destroying a statue of Jesus Christ with a mallet. Two soldiers were subsequently jailed for 30 days after that footage went viral. Christian communities have reported multiple incidents at religious sites in recent years, including damage to Gaza's Holy Family Church and the Greek Orthodox Church of Saint Porphyrius, one of the region's oldest Christian shrines.

The incidents raise important questions about the protection of religious minorities and sacred sites in conflict zones. Lebanese authorities report that more than 2,700 people have been killed and approximately 1.2 million displaced since Israel launched military operations in Lebanon in early March, targeting Hezbollah positions. Despite a ceasefire agreement that began in mid-April, Israeli forces have continued operations, establishing an expanded buffer zone extending roughly 10 kilometers into Lebanese territory.
