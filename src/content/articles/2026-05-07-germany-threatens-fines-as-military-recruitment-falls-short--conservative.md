Germany is threatening to impose fines on young men who fail to complete mandatory military questionnaires, according to a report by Der Spiegel. The move comes as Berlin pushes an ambitious plan to expand the Bundeswehr from 186,000 active troops to over 260,000 by the mid-2030s—part of a broader European Union military buildup.

Approximately 10,000 men who ignored the government's online survey despite reminder notices now face potential penalties of €250 ($294), with officials previously warning that fines could reach up to €1,000. The questionnaire, which asks about physical fitness, health conditions, and willingness to serve, was introduced this year under Defense Minister Boris Pistorius's voluntary military service program. Under the Military Service Modernization Act, all 18-year-old German men must register and complete the form, with provisions allowing for a lottery system to draft recruits if manpower shortages occur.

The initiative has sparked public backlash. In March, thousands of high school students protested in Berlin against measures that could restore compulsory military service, which Germany abolished in 2011. Senior officials, including Pistorius, have suggested conscription could return, citing security concerns. Chancellor Friedrich Merz has stated his goal of making Germany's military the strongest conventional armed force in the EU.

German officials have justified the expansion by pointing to perceived threats, though critics question whether government mandates and financial penalties are appropriate tools for recruitment in a democratic society. Moscow has dismissed Germany's threat assessments and warned that continued militarization could have serious consequences.
