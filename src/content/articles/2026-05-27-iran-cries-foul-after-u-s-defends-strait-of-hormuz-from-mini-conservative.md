Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has accused the United States of breaking a fragile ceasefire after American forces struck Iranian military assets in the country's southern Hormozgan province. The confrontation represents the largest military clash between the two nations since early May.

According to U.S. Central Command, American fighter jets conducted what they characterized as "self-defense strikes" targeting missile sites and naval vessels that were actively attempting to mine the Strait of Hormuz—a critical waterway through which nearly one-third of the world's seaborne oil passes. Iranian media reported that several IRGC naval speedboats were hit, resulting in at least three Iranian sailor casualties.

Iranian officials quickly condemned the action. Seyyed Majid Mousavi, commander of the IRGC Aerospace Force, declared the strikes a ceasefire breach and warned that Iranian forces stand ready to deliver "a decisive and rapid response." The Iranian Foreign Ministry released a statement calling it a "gross violation" and vowing to hold Washington accountable for consequences.

The military action comes amid ongoing negotiations between the two nations, with talks mediated by Qatar addressing Iran's nuclear program, sanctions relief, and control of the strategic Strait of Hormuz. Secretary of State Marco Rubio indicated Tuesday that reaching an agreement could still take "a few more days," though President Trump has maintained his firm stance that Iran must relinquish its enriched uranium stockpile—a red line Tehran has consistently rejected.

The timing of Iran's mining operation—occurring while negotiations were reportedly nearing a potential breakthrough—has raised questions about Tehran's commitment to diplomatic solutions. U.S. officials have emphasized America's longstanding commitment to keeping international shipping lanes open and secure from hostile actions.
