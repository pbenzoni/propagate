Ukraine has resumed allowing Russian oil to flow through the Druzhba pipeline to Hungary and Slovakia after a nearly three-month suspension, according to Hungarian energy company MOL. The Ukrainian operator JSC Ukrtransnafta notified European partners that flows restarted Wednesday, with deliveries expected to reach landlocked EU member states by Thursday.

The suspension began in late January when Ukraine halted the pipeline, citing damage from Russian military strikes—a claim Moscow denied. The stoppage created significant diplomatic friction, as Hungary had blocked a planned €90 billion ($105 billion) EU emergency loan package for Ukraine, arguing that Kyiv was withholding energy supplies as political leverage.

The timing of the restart is notable: it comes less than two weeks after Hungary's parliamentary elections, where the pro-EU opposition Tisza party defeated longtime Prime Minister Viktor Orbán's ruling Fidesz party. Orbán, a frequent critic of Western aid to Ukraine, had accused Kyiv of deliberately timing the pipeline closure to create domestic political turmoil ahead of the vote. Hours after the pipeline resumption was announced, EU ambassadors moved to approve the transfer of the multi-billion-dollar aid package to Ukraine, with formal approval expected Thursday.

Both Hungary and Slovakia remain heavily dependent on Russian energy due to their geography and existing infrastructure. The dispute highlighted ongoing tensions within the EU over energy policy, sanctions enforcement, and support for Ukraine during the ongoing war—particularly among member states with different levels of exposure to energy disruptions and varying political perspectives on the conflict.
