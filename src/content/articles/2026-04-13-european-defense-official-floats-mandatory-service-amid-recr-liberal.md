Andre Denk, head of the European Defense Agency, suggested in a recent interview that compulsory military service could return across EU member states due to difficulties in recruiting sufficient volunteers. Speaking with Spanish newspaper El Pais, Denk described what he characterized as "a human resources problem" and indicated that mandatory service represents one potential solution, predicting his home country of Germany may eventually adopt such measures.

Several European nations have already moved to expand their military conscription programs in recent years. Finland announced plans to raise the age limit for military reservists from 50 to 65 beginning in 2026, while Lithuania expanded its conscription program to operate year-round starting the same year. Denmark began drafting women in 2025, and Latvia's Defense Minister has proposed extending mandatory service to women by 2028.

Denk also called for increased European investment in domestic arms manufacturing, particularly focusing on drone technology and counter-drone systems. The proposals come as European nations reassess their defense postures and military capacity.

In Germany, a new voluntary service model that took effect January 1 has generated public protests, with critics expressing concern that it could serve as a stepping stone toward reinstating full conscription, which the country suspended in 2011. The debate reflects broader tensions across Europe about balancing security concerns with traditional opposition to mandatory military service.
