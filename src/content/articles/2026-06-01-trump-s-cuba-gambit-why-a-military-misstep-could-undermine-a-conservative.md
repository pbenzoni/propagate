While the world remains focused on the ongoing U.S.-Iran standoff, another potential flashpoint has emerged closer to home. Following the successful January 3 operation that removed Venezuelan dictator Nicolas Maduro from power and helped restore order to the troubled nation, the Trump administration has turned its attention to Cuba—a move that could prove far more complicated than Venezuela's swift regime change.

President Trump's bold warning to Cuba was unmistakable: "THERE WILL BE NO MORE OIL OR MONEY GOING TO CUBA—ZERO!" he declared on Truth Social, noting Cuba's longtime dependence on Venezuelan support. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has since labeled Cuba "one of the leading sponsors of terrorism in the entire region," and the Justice Department unsealed indictments against former Cuban President Raul Castro. A U.S. carrier battlegroup now patrols Cuban waters.

Yet serious questions remain about whether military action against Cuba serves America's national interests. The February-March conflict with Iran demonstrated the limits of even overwhelming military superiority—despite 37 days of operations, the campaign failed to achieve its objectives and left Iran controlling critical oil shipping lanes through the Strait of Hormuz. That costly lesson suggests caution before committing American blood and treasure to another foreign intervention.

Conservative principles of limited government, fiscal responsibility, and avoiding nation-building quagmires should guide policy here. While no friend of communist tyranny, prudent patriots recognize that not every bad regime requires American military intervention. The real question facing the Trump administration: Does pursuing regime change in Cuba advance core America First priorities, or does it risk becoming another expensive distraction from securing our borders, rebuilding our economy, and putting American families first?
