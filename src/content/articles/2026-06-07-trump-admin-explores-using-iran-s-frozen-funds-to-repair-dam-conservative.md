The Trump administration is examining whether frozen Iranian assets can be redirected to help Gulf Arab allies rebuild critical infrastructure damaged by Tehran's retaliatory strikes, according to reports from Bloomberg and CBS News.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has directed officials to assess damage conditions among U.S. partners in the Persian Gulf region and obtain comprehensive repair estimates. The Treasury Department plans to use "all available authorities" to make Iranian assets accessible for reconstruction efforts tied to damage caused by Iran, a source familiar with Bessent's thinking told CBS. Officials are also considering whether these funds could cover "past damage" attributed to Iran-backed groups.

The initiative comes as indirect negotiations between Washington and Tehran have hit a roadblock over Iran's insistence on accessing $24 billion in frozen funds. Iranian officials are demanding at least $12 billion be released immediately upon signing any agreement, with the remainder following within one or two months. "This is our own money, not America's money," said Mohsen Rezaei, a military adviser to Iran's Supreme Leader, framing the demand as a test of trust for President Trump.

Energy facilities in Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, and Bahrain—all hosting American military forces—have sustained damage in the escalating conflict before a Pakistan-brokered ceasefire took effect in April. Tehran has declared that energy infrastructure in Gulf states with U.S. bases constitutes "direct and legitimate targets," raising the stakes for regional security and American interests abroad.
