Latvia's Prime Minister Evika Siliņa has dismissed Defense Minister Andris Sprūds following a troubling security incident in which Ukrainian drones crossed into the Baltic nation's airspace and struck oil storage facilities. The incursion has sparked intense debate about air defense coordination among NATO allies supporting Ukraine's resistance against Russian aggression.

"The drone incident this week clearly showed that the leadership of the defense sector failed to deliver on its promise of a safe sky over our country," Siliņa wrote, noting that Sprūds had lost both her trust and public confidence. The minister countered that he had already planned to resign, accusing the prime minister of politicizing his departure.

The incident underscores the complex challenges facing NATO's eastern members as they balance robust support for Ukraine with their own territorial security concerns. Two Ukrainian Lyuty-type drones reportedly entered Latvian airspace near the Russian border, with one sparking a fire near Rėzekne, approximately 40 kilometers from Russia. One drone remains unlocated.

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrey Sybiga addressed the incident Friday, apologizing to Latvia and neighboring Baltic states while emphasizing Russia's ultimate responsibility for the war that has created these cascading security complications. The episode reflects the difficult coordination required as democratic nations work together to support Ukraine's defense while managing the spillover effects of an active conflict zone along NATO's border.
