A congressional investigation into Jeffrey Epstein's network of powerful elites heard testimony this week from Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates and Epstein's longtime executive assistant, as lawmakers probe how the convicted sex offender maintained access to America's most influential figures even after his 2008 conviction.

In closed-door testimony Wednesday, Gates told the House Oversight and Government Reform Committee he failed to grasp the full scope of Epstein's crimes when he began meeting with the financier in 2011. Gates said he believed Epstein could connect him with wealthy donors to advance his philanthropic work—connections that never materialized. He described the association as a "grave error in judgment" and claimed that after ending contact in 2014, Epstein attempted to leverage knowledge of his extramarital affairs to force re-engagement.

While no public evidence has emerged linking Gates to Epstein's criminal activities or placing him on the financier's private island, the relationship has drawn intense scrutiny. Released correspondence shows the two men met repeatedly over several years. Gates' ex-wife Melinda reportedly considered Epstein a serious concern and cited her former husband's dealings with him among factors in their 2021 divorce.

The probe also heard from Lesley Groff, who served as Epstein's executive assistant from 2001 until his death in jail in 2019. Groff characterized Epstein as a "master manipulator" who compartmentalized different aspects of his life, claiming she considered resigning after his 2008 conviction but stayed after he convinced her the accusations were false.

Lawmakers expressed skepticism that a senior aide managing Epstein's schedule, travel, and daily operations for nearly two decades could remain unaware of his misconduct. Groff admitted arranging massages for Epstein but claimed she never met the women involved and didn't know some were minors—despite prosecutors identifying these massages as a key recruitment mechanism for exploiting young women. The House inquiry has also taken testimony from other prominent figures including former President Bill Clinton, former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton, and former Attorney General Pam Bondi.
