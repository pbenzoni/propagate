China has openly instructed its domestic companies to ignore American sanctions targeting oil refiners with alleged ties to Iran, marking a significant challenge to Washington's extraterritorial enforcement of unilateral restrictions. The Chinese Ministry of Commerce cited "national sovereignty, security and development interests" in its directive, arguing that sanctions imposed without United Nations authorization lack legitimacy under international law.

The US Treasury had previously warned banks against dealing with Chinese "teapot" refineries—privately owned companies that allegedly handle the majority of oil China purchases from Iran. Treasury officials claimed this revenue "ultimately benefits the Iranian regime, its weapons programs, and its military." However, China's government and major state-owned companies have consistently denied direct purchases of Iranian crude, and customs data have not recorded imports from Iran since 2023.

The dispute comes as oil prices have surged past $120 per barrel following recent military operations in the region and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint that handles approximately one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas flows. Iran has kept the waterway closed to "hostile ships," while the US Navy maintains a blockade of Iranian ports in the Persian Gulf.

Beijing's position reflects a broader principle: that individual nations should not unilaterally impose their legal frameworks on global commerce without international consensus. A Chinese government spokesperson emphasized that the country "will continue to closely monitor the improper extraterritorial application of foreign laws and measures." The ministry stated the directive would not affect China's international obligations or its protection of foreign-invested firms, suggesting a careful balance between asserting sovereignty and maintaining global economic relationships.
