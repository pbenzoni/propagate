Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu disclosed Friday that he received treatment for early-stage prostate cancer but kept the diagnosis secret for two months during military operations involving Iran. The 76-year-old leader's decision to withhold medical information has raised questions about transparency and accountability in government.

According to Netanyahu's annual medical report, doctors discovered the tumor at a "very early stage" with no metastases, and subsequent treatment has been successful. Hadassah Hospital oncology chief Aharon Popovtser confirmed that follow-up tests showed "the disease has disappeared." Netanyahu had previously undergone surgery for an enlarged benign prostate in 2024 and was under routine monitoring when the cancer was detected.

Netanyahu justified the delay by claiming he wanted to prevent the information from being released "at the height of the war" and generating what he characterized as "false propaganda against Israel." The Prime Minister stated that physicians informed him the condition was minor and common for men his age, giving him the option to either monitor or treat it immediately.

The revelation follows weeks of public speculation about Netanyahu's health after he missed multiple consecutive security cabinet meetings during the early phase of the conflict. In March, he released a video of himself at a Jerusalem cafe in an apparent attempt to counter rumors that his public appearances were AI-generated. The rest of his medical report indicates he is in good health, though his office has not clarified the exact timing of the cancer treatment.
