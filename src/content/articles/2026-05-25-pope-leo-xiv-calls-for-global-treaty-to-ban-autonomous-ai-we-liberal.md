Pope Leo XIV issued a powerful condemnation of autonomous weapons systems on Monday, warning that artificial intelligence is enabling the "normalization of war" and transferring life-and-death decisions to unaccountable tech corporations.

In a 42,000-word encyclical titled 'Magnifica Humanitas' (Magnificent Humanity), the American-born pontiff called for a global treaty to regulate military AI development, arguing that "the development and use of AI in warfare must be subject to the most rigorous ethical constraints, to guarantee respect for human dignity and the sanctity of life." The document highlights how power is shifting from democratic governments to "major economic and technological actors" in Silicon Valley.

While not naming companies directly, Leo's encyclical clearly targets defense contractors whose AI systems are being used to select military targets. The Pope emphasized that it is "not permissible to entrust lethal or otherwise irreversible decisions to artificial systems," and demanded that "those who design, train, authorize and employ technology must be held accountable for their decisions."

The encyclical stands in sharp contrast to recent statements from defense tech executives who have argued that American companies have an "affirmative obligation" to aid military operations without ethical constraints. Leo found support from some within the AI industry, however, including an executive from AI company Anthropic who told Vatican leaders that regulating the AI industry is "a moral imperative of historic proportions."

The Pope's stance has reportedly drawn criticism from President Donald Trump, who has clashed with Leo over opposition to military escalation. The encyclical follows the pontiff's repeated warnings about technology's threat to "human dignity, justice, and labor" since his election last May.
