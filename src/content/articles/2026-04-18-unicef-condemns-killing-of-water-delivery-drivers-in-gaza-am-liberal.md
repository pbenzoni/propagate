UNICEF has condemned the killing of two truck drivers contracted to deliver clean water to families in Gaza, stating the workers were killed by Israeli fire during routine water transport operations on Friday morning. The incident occurred at the Mansoura water filling point in northern Gaza, the only operational site serving hundreds of thousands of people in Gaza City.

"UNICEF is outraged by the killing of two drivers of trucks contracted to provide clean water to families in the Gaza Strip," the agency said in a statement, adding that two other individuals were injured in the attack. The organization has suspended activities at the site and called for an investigation into the incident. Israeli officials have not yet commented publicly.

The killings come amid an October ceasefire that halted two years of conflict, though the truce has proven fragile. According to Gaza's Health Ministry, more than 750 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire took effect, with multiple reports of civilian casualties including children. UN agencies have documented continued fatalities among aid workers and journalists despite the truce, with UN Human Rights Chief Volker Turk recently calling the toll on humanitarian personnel "unprecedented."

The ongoing humanitarian crisis and mounting civilian casualties have intensified international criticism of Israel's actions in Gaza, with declining support even among Western allies. The suspension of water delivery operations threatens access to clean water for vulnerable populations, including children, in an already devastated region where basic services remain critically compromised.
