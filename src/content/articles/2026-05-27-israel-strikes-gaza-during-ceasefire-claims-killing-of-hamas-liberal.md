Israeli forces have claimed responsibility for killing Mohammed Odeh, the recently appointed commander of Hamas' military wing, in an airstrike in Gaza. The strike, which Palestinian media reports also killed Odeh's wife and sons, occurred despite a US-brokered ceasefire that has been in place since October 2025.

The announcement comes less than two weeks after Israel killed Odeh's predecessor, Izz al-Din al-Haddad, highlighting the continuation of targeted strikes even during the nominal truce. Gaza's civil defense agency reported at least seven people were killed in Israeli strikes on Tuesday alone, including five in a single attack near the Maghazi refugee camp.

The ongoing military campaign in Gaza, launched in response to the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack that killed approximately 1,200 people, has resulted in a devastating toll on Palestinian civilians. According to Palestinian health authorities, more than 72,000 people have been killed and over 172,000 wounded since the conflict began. Approximately 900 of those deaths occurred during the ceasefire period, raising questions about the effectiveness and terms of the truce.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office stated that Odeh was "responsible for the murder, abduction, and wounding of many Israeli citizens and IDF soldiers," while Defense Minister Israel Katz vowed that all those involved in planning the October 7 attack are "marked for death, everywhere." Of the 250 people taken hostage during the 2023 attack, 85 have died, with the remainder having been returned to Israel.
