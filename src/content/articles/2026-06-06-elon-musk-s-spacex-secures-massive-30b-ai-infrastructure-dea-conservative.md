SpaceX has locked in a lucrative $30 billion contract with Google to provide artificial intelligence computing infrastructure through June 2029, according to SEC filings. The deal calls for Google to pay Elon Musk's space-tech company $920 million per month for access to approximately 110,000 NVIDIA GPUs and related computing components.

The arrangement demonstrates SpaceX's strategic pivot to monetize its data center capacity while maintaining flexibility for internal operations. Google stated the contract will support its Gemini Enterprise AI platform, describing SpaceX as a long-standing cloud partner. This follows an even larger agreement with AI startup Anthropic, which committed to paying SpaceX $1.25 billion monthly—totaling roughly $45 billion over three years—for similar computing access.

The massive AI infrastructure deals represent a major revenue stream for SpaceX ahead of its anticipated $1.77 trillion public listing next week. Following its January merger with X and xAI, the combined entity generated $4.7 billion in revenue during the first quarter of 2026, though it also reported $4.3 billion in losses during that period.

The agreements come as the Pentagon has expanded partnerships with major tech providers to integrate AI systems into military operations. The Department of Defense announced deals with SpaceX, OpenAI, Google, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services, and Oracle to deploy AI capabilities for defense applications, including data analysis and decision-making support in operational environments.
