Canada has imposed a temporary ban on livestock imports from Texas following the detection of two cases of screwworm fly, a flesh-eating parasite not seen in the United States since 1966. The move has drawn sharp criticism from Texas Governor Greg Abbott, who characterized Ottawa's action as "more political than science-based."

The first case was identified Wednesday roughly 50 miles from the Mexican border, followed by a second case Friday in the same area. Governor Abbott declared a state of disaster over the outbreak, calling it an "imminent threat" while simultaneously pushing back against Canada's sweeping trade restrictions. "This pest affects live animals and does not impact inspected Texas beef," Abbott stated, emphasizing that Canada's "broad restriction on Texas livestock is an overreaction."

The outbreak originated in Central America in 2023 and has been spreading northward, with Mexico reporting thousands of animal cases and dozens of human infections by late 2025. The screwworm parasite lays hundreds of eggs in wounds or natural openings of warm-blooded animals, hatching into maggots that feed on living flesh for approximately a week before burrowing into soil to pupate.

The Canadian Food Inspection Agency's ban applies to all livestock that originated from or were present in Texas during the three weeks prior to entering Canada. Canadian officials say they will "continue to work closely with US counterparts to assess developments and adjust measures as needed." The screwworm was successfully eradicated from the U.S. in 1966 and Mexico in 1991 through programs involving mass-breeding and release of sterile male flies. Experts attribute the parasite's resurgence to changing weather patterns and warmer temperatures expanding the insects' range.
