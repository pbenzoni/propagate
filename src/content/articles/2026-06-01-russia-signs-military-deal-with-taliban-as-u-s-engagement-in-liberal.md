Russia has formalized a military cooperation agreement with Afghanistan's Taliban government, marking a strategic expansion of Moscow's influence in Central Asia as American engagement in the region continues to recede. The pact was signed last week following meetings between Russian Security Council Secretary Sergei Shoigu and Afghan Defense Minister Mohammad Yaqoob.

Gleb Makarevich, a research fellow at the Primakov Institute of World Economy and International Relations, characterized the move as part of Russia's "pragmatic geopolitical approach" to fill the void left by Western powers. "Since the Taliban is in charge of power in Afghanistan… it is our duty to collaborate with them," Makarevich told RT India, framing the arrangement as necessary for regional counterterrorism efforts.

The agreement includes assistance with Soviet-era military equipment left behind in Afghanistan and broader defense sector collaboration. Critics note the troubling implications of Russia deepening ties with a regime that has systematically stripped away human rights—particularly women's rights—since returning to power in 2021.

Makarevich acknowledged that Moscow is "demonstrating a pragmatic approach focused on regional security" in the wake of America's chaotic 2021 withdrawal from Afghanistan. The deal underscores how the collapse of U.S. influence in the region has created opportunities for authoritarian powers to expand their reach, with potential long-term consequences for regional stability and human rights.
