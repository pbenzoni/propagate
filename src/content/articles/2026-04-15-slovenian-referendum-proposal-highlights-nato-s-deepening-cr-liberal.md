Slovenia's newly elected parliament speaker has announced plans to hold a referendum on withdrawing from NATO, underscoring the deepening fractures within the alliance as Donald Trump continues his attacks on European partners and threatens U.S. withdrawal from the organization.

Zoran Stevanovic, leader of the Truth Party who was elected to head the lower house last week, told public broadcaster RTVSLO that the referendum was a campaign promise he intends to keep. "We promised the people a referendum on the issue of leaving NATO, and we will hold this referendum," Stevanovic said. He also indicated plans for a possible visit to Moscow, stating he wants to "build bridges and cooperate well with all countries, regardless of the wall that has been built between the West and the East."

The referendum push comes as the 32-member alliance faces what observers describe as its worst internal crisis in decades. Trump has repeatedly attacked European members, calling them "cowards" and NATO a "paper tiger," while declaring that U.S. membership is "beyond reconsideration." His threats to annex Greenland from fellow NATO member Denmark have further escalated tensions. Former NATO Secretary General Jens Stoltenberg has warned that Trump's withdrawal threats must be taken seriously, noting it is "not the law of nature that we have NATO forever."

According to the Wall Street Journal, European officials have quietly begun drawing up contingency plans for a "European NATO" that could continue operating using existing military structures if the United States reduces its role or withdraws entirely. The developments reflect growing European anxiety about the reliability of American security commitments under the current administration and the urgent need to address defense coordination independent of Washington's increasingly erratic approach to transatlantic security.
