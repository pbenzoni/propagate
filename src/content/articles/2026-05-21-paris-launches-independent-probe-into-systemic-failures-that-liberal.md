Paris has launched a special inquiry into the city's after-school childcare system following revelations of widespread sexual abuse allegations involving children as young as three. Mayor Emmanuel Gregoire, elected in March, announced the creation of an independent investigative body with a six-month mandate to examine systemic failures that allowed the abuse to continue.

The move represents a stark departure from previous leadership. Former Paris Mayor Anne Hidalgo reportedly attempted to keep abuse complaints out of public view, according to French media. Gregoire has pledged transparency, revealing that 78 after-school workers were suspended in 2024, including 31 over suspected sexual misconduct. He acknowledged a "systemic risk" to children and announced a €20 million emergency reform plan that includes mandatory staff training, unannounced inspections, and a policy requiring that no adult be left alone with children.

Parents, activists, and labor unions point to deeper structural problems: chronic understaffing, precarious employment contracts, and inadequate background screening. Critics also allege that City Hall routinely transferred problematic employees to new locations rather than terminating them—a practice that mirrors institutional failures seen in other abuse scandals. Paris prosecutors have opened investigations into suspected sexual violence at 84 nursery schools, 20 elementary schools, and ten daycare centers.

The crisis has drawn renewed attention to France's broader reckoning with child protection. A 2024 government commission estimated that approximately 5.4 million French adults—roughly 10% of the population—experienced sexual abuse as children, with about 160,000 minors victimized annually. France did not establish a fixed age of consent until 2021, when it was set at 15 (and 18 for cases involving family members), a reform long advocated by child safety advocates.
