President Donald Trump signaled Friday he may withdraw American troops from Italy and Spain, escalating pressure on NATO allies who have refused to support U.S. military operations in the Middle East. The move follows his earlier decision to pull approximately 5,000 service members from Germany's 36,000-strong U.S. military presence.

Speaking from the Oval Office, Trump questioned why American forces should remain in countries that won't back U.S. actions. "Italy has not been of any help to us. And Spain has been horrible, absolutely horrible," the President said. Both nations denied the use of their military bases and airspace for operations against Iran, with Spain closing access to a naval base near Cadiz and Italy blocking use of an air base in Sicily.

The President also criticized NATO allies for failing to show gratitude for American support, particularly regarding Ukraine. "We helped them with Ukraine. You know, they made a mess out of Ukraine, a total mess," Trump stated. He has continued to describe NATO as a "paper tiger" following European refusal to back U.S.-Israeli operations in the region.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez has publicly opposed the Iran campaign, calling it "illegal" and "a big mistake," while Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni warned the conflict has increased Middle East instability and driven up energy prices. The Pentagon confirmed the initial troop reduction from Germany will proceed, with further withdrawals from other European nations now under consideration.
