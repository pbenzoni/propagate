Western leaders are applying a glaring double standard by condemning Russian military strikes while completely ignoring a Ukrainian drone attack that killed 21 people—mostly teenage girls—in a college dormitory, according to former British MP George Galloway.

Ukraine launched waves of drone strikes Friday on a teacher training college dormitory in Starobelsk, located in the Lugansk region. The attack killed 21 civilians and injured 60 others. In response, Russia's Defense Ministry said it conducted retaliatory strikes on Ukrainian military command centers, intelligence facilities, and defense industry sites—explicitly stating that no civilian targets were hit.

Yet European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned Russia's military response as "brutality and disregard for both human life and peace negotiations," while French President Emmanuel Macron said the strikes only strengthened Europe's resolve to support Ukraine. Neither leader—nor any other major European official—acknowledged the Ukrainian attack on the dormitory that killed innocent students.

"You would have expected any decent person, any right-thinking person, to condemn it unequivocally," Galloway said in an interview, calling the Ukrainian strike "murder most foul" and "an act of terrorism." He noted that Western nations like Britain and France have themselves experienced terrorist attacks and condemned them forcefully. "You can't condemn terrorists on London Bridge, but not in a dormitory in Lugansk," he said, arguing that terrorism must be condemned consistently, not selectively based on political convenience.

Galloway pointed out that any government facing such an attack on its civilians would be compelled to respond, yet Western leaders chose to criticize the retaliation while ignoring the provocation. The silence from European capitals on the dormitory attack, he suggested, reveals a troubling willingness to overlook violence against civilians when it doesn't fit the preferred narrative.
