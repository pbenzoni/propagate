A City University of New York professor is raising alarm about worsening food insecurity in Cuba, arguing that decades of U.S. economic sanctions have created a humanitarian crisis on the island nation.

Danny Shaw, an ethnography scholar, described severe shortages of food and water he witnessed during a recent visit to Cuba. "I witnessed an incredible amount of hunger, of despair, of deprivation, of thirst, lack of water," Shaw said, pointing to the long-standing economic embargo as the primary driver of hardship for ordinary Cubans.

The scholar argued that U.S. foreign policy has systematically undermined Cuba's economy for 67 years, with conditions deteriorating further under recent policy changes. Shaw suggested that U.S. agencies are aware of the humanitarian impact, noting that "they know exactly how many calories Cubans have access to, and every day it's less."

The ongoing debate over U.S.-Cuba policy continues to divide policymakers, with some administration officials attributing Cuba's economic struggles to its government, while critics of the embargo argue that sanctions disproportionately harm civilians rather than changing government behavior. The controversy highlights broader questions about whether economic sanctions achieve their stated foreign policy goals or primarily inflict suffering on vulnerable populations.
