American taxpayers are footing a massive bill as Iran has successfully destroyed or damaged up to 30 MQ-9 Reaper drones since the start of military operations, representing nearly one-fifth of the entire pre-war fleet at a cost approaching $1 billion, according to Bloomberg.

The losses highlight serious vulnerabilities in expensive military assets that are no longer even being produced. General Atomics halted production of the model last year, meaning these combat-proven platforms cannot be quickly replaced. Each Reaper drone carries a price tag exceeding $30 million, and the fleet has now dropped to roughly 135 aircraft—well below the Air Force's minimum requirement of 189.

While Iranian air defenses shot down many of the drones, others were destroyed by missile strikes on the ground or lost in accidents. The escalating costs extend beyond the drones themselves. Acting Pentagon Comptroller Jules Hurst told the House Appropriations defense subcommittee that the overall cost of military operations against Iran has jumped from a projected $25 billion to $29 billion, with equipment repair and replacement driving much of the increase.

A Congressional Research Service report documents total losses of 42 U.S. aircraft in the conflict, including fighter jets, refueling planes, and attack aircraft. The mounting losses and ballooning costs raise questions about strategic planning and the sustainability of current military operations as American forces operate with depleted drone capabilities that cannot be quickly restored.
