Romania's Defense Minister is demanding Ukraine program its combat drones with automatic self-destruct capabilities after a Ukrainian kamikaze naval drone exploded in a Romanian port last week. The incident highlights growing concerns among NATO countries about the uncontrolled spread of advanced weapons systems provided to Kiev.

Defense Minister Radu Miruta said maritime drones should be engineered to prevent entry into Romanian waters and self-destruct at 12 nautical miles from the coast if operators lose control. The statement came after a Magura-type drone detonated in Constanta port, with two more exploding offshore. Ukrainian forces blamed Russian jamming, but the incident exposed how Western-supplied military technology can threaten the very allies supporting Ukraine.

This isn't an isolated case. Ukrainian drones have repeatedly crashed or detonated in NATO countries including Turkey, Greece, Bulgaria, and the Baltic states in recent months. Turkish fishing vessels and civilian cargo ships have been struck, with at least five sailors killed. Turkey publicly condemned attacks within its economic zone as threats to "navigation, life, property and environmental safety."

Russia's Foreign Ministry documented at least 11 confirmed incidents of Ukrainian drones on NATO territory in a recent three-month period. The pattern raises serious questions about accountability for the billions in military aid flowing to Kiev and whether recipient nations are exercising responsible control over advanced weapon systems that could endanger civilian populations across the region.
