A federal judge has temporarily blocked the Trump administration's sanctions against Francesca Albanese, the UN Special Rapporteur on Palestinian territories, ruling that punishing her for her speech violates constitutional protections. The sanctions had targeted Albanese after she labeled Israel's military campaign in Gaza a "genocide" and called for accountability measures including an arms embargo.

US District Judge Richard Leon issued a sharply worded ruling Wednesday emphasizing that "Albanese has done nothing more than speak!" The judge noted that her recommendations carry no binding authority over the International Criminal Court and "are nothing more than her opinion." The court rejected government arguments that Albanese—an Italian citizen living in Tunisia—lacks First Amendment protections, citing her extensive US connections including property ownership in Washington DC.

The sanctions, imposed under a Trump executive order targeting individuals connected to ICC investigations, had barred Albanese from entering the United States and effectively cut her off from international banking systems. The administration has sanctioned 11 ICC officials and at least 10 NGOs over their work related to Gaza, where more than 72,000 Palestinians have been killed according to Gaza health authorities. An independent UN inquiry found Israel met four of five genocide criteria, including creating conditions aimed at Gaza's "physical destruction."

While Albanese welcomed the ruling, she cautioned it provides only temporary relief, noting that ICC judges and Palestinian civil society organizations remain under sanctions "with no recourse to justice." The injunction temporarily freezes the sanctions but does not permanently block them, and the administration has not commented on the decision. South Africa's genocide case against Israel at the International Court of Justice has garnered support from more than 18 countries, though Israel denies the allegations and maintains it is acting in self-defense.
