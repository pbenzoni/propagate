Several thousand protesters gathered in Berlin on Monday to demonstrate against Chancellor Friedrich Merz and his coalition government's policies. The rally drew participants from across Germany, with organizers claiming 10,000 attendees while police estimated around 4,000.

Demonstrators carried placards reading "Not my chancellor" and "Merz must go," voicing opposition to the government's agenda. The protest was organized by Project M1llion, a group that claims to unite diverse segments of German society concerned about current political direction. Authorities reported the demonstration remained peaceful throughout.

The protesters' demands include calls for immediate new elections and an end to financial support for "any kind of warring party" — a platform that comes as Germany has provided billions in aid to Ukraine since 2022. Critically, the group also advocates for rolling back environmental protections and accelerating deportations of undocumented migrants, positions that align with far-right policy preferences.

Public dissatisfaction with Merz has reached troubling levels. A recent INSA poll found that 77% of Germans are unhappy with the chancellor's performance — his worst rating to date. The coalition government with the Social Democratic Party fares similarly poorly, with 78% expressing dissatisfaction. "A government that can't even convince its remaining voters is doomed to fail," pollster Hermann Binkert told Bild newspaper, noting the discontent extends even to the government's own base of supporters.
