The European Union is providing €50 million ($58 million) in emergency support to Armenia as the country faces economic coercion from Russia just days before a crucial parliamentary election. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced the aid package following discussions with Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan, condemning Moscow's use of trade restrictions as political leverage.

The timing is significant: Armenian voters head to the polls on June 7, with Pashinyan's pro-democracy Civil Contract party leading at 32% despite pressure from Moscow. Von der Leyen specifically called out Russia for "weaponizing economic relations for political pressure," pointing to recent import restrictions Moscow imposed on Armenian agricultural products—restrictions the Kremlin claims are safety-related but which many observers view as retaliation for Armenia's democratic reforms and westward tilt.

The EU aid aims to offset some of the economic damage from Russia's trade barriers, including assistance for Armenian flower growers whose products have been blocked from Russian markets. A symbolic shipment of 10,000 Armenian flowers is arriving in Latvia as part of the effort to diversify the country's export markets away from dependence on Moscow.

However, the challenge facing Armenia remains stark. The country's annual trade with Russia totals $6-8 billion, dwarfing both the EU's aid package and Armenia's €2.5 billion in total EU trade. Moscow also provides natural gas to Yerevan at heavily subsidized rates—$177.50 per thousand cubic meters compared to European spot prices around $600. The Kremlin has warned that Armenia cannot simultaneously benefit from EU integration and membership in Russia's Eurasian Economic Union, illustrating the difficult geopolitical tightrope the small democracy must walk.
