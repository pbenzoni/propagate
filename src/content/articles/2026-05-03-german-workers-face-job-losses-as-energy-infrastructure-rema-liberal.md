Workers and residents in the eastern German city of Schwedt are expressing deep concern about the future of their local refinery and the jobs it provides, as shifting geopolitical realities threaten to disrupt oil supplies that keep the facility running. The PCK refinery, which provides approximately 90% of fuel to Berlin and the surrounding Brandenburg region, faces an uncertain future as supply chains remain vulnerable to the ongoing conflict in Eastern Europe.

The facility had previously transitioned from Russian oil to Kazakh supplies after Germany implemented sanctions banning Russian pipeline imports in 2023. However, Russian officials announced last week that Kazakhstan's oil can no longer be delivered directly via the Soviet-era Druzhba pipeline starting May 1, citing technical limitations. Kazakh authorities are now working to reroute supplies through Baltic and Black Sea ports, though this alternative would force the refinery to operate at significantly reduced capacity.

Local refinery council member Danny Ruthenburg warned that operating at just 65-70% capacity due to limited port infrastructure at Rostock could necessitate shutting down production lines and cutting jobs. "That would inevitably lead to staff reductions," he told German broadcaster ARD. The situation highlights the broader vulnerability of European energy infrastructure and the ripple effects felt by working communities when supply chains are disrupted.

Kazakh Energy Minister Yerlan Akkenzhenov suggested the supply changes may be connected to recent attacks on Russian infrastructure. The ongoing conflict has repeatedly disrupted critical energy facilities, including a strike last month on a hub in Novorossiysk used to transport Kazakh oil to European markets. For Schwedt residents, the instability represents not just abstract geopolitical maneuvering, but a direct threat to their livelihoods and community's economic survival.
