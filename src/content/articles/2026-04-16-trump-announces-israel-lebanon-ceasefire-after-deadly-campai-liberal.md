President Donald Trump announced Thursday that Israel and Lebanon have agreed to a ten-day ceasefire beginning at 5 PM EST, following what he described as "excellent conversations" with Lebanese President Joseph Aoun and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in Washington.

The ceasefire comes after Israel's invasion of southern Lebanon in early March, which has killed over 2,000 people and wounded thousands more, including hundreds of women and children, according to Lebanon's Health Ministry. Approximately 1.2 million people have been displaced by the conflict. Israeli forces have reportedly sought to annex some 15% of Lebanese territory during the operation.

The announcement drew immediate controversy, with Israeli officials reportedly expressing "outrage" that Trump revealed the ceasefire publicly before their own security cabinet could formally discuss the agreement. Netanyahu told ministers the deal would begin "at the request of Trump" and confirmed that Israeli forces would remain in their positions in southern Lebanon.

The ceasefire was announced even as deadly Israeli strikes continued against Lebanese civilians. On Wednesday, Netanyahu had ordered an expansion of military operations, and strikes on the village of Mayfadoun reportedly killed four paramedics and wounded six others. Additional strikes were reported near one of the last functioning hospitals in southern Lebanon, in the town of Tebnine.

Trump claimed the agreement represented his "10th" such diplomatic achievement and invited both leaders to the White House for what he called the "first meaningful talks" between the two countries since 1983. The talks in Washington were facilitated by Secretary of State Marco Rubio.
