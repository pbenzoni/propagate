A major Russian financial institution has significantly expanded its ability to move money outside Western-controlled payment rails, launching direct transfers to Chinese UnionPay cards across 61 countries. The move underscores how sanctions-targeted nations are building alternative financial infrastructure independent of American and European oversight.

T-Bank announced that customers can now send funds to UnionPay Global cardholders using only a card number and recipient name, with instant processing similar to domestic transfers. The service covers nations across Asia, Europe, the Middle East, and former Soviet republics—allowing Russians to transfer up to $5,000 per transaction without needing foreign currency accounts.

"A transfer to Dubai, Guangzhou, or Tashkent now works as simply and quickly as a domestic transfer within Russia," stated Artyom Avladeev, the bank's head of transfers. The expansion opens access to what the bank describes as "billions of recipients" outside traditional Western financial channels.

The development reflects a broader pattern of Russia and China constructing parallel economic systems that reduce dependence on dollar-based transactions and SWIFT messaging networks. Previously limited to China-issued cards only, the service now reaches countries where UnionPay has established presence, regardless of where cardholders physically reside. T-Bank promotes the feature for supporting family members abroad, freelance work with international clients, and cross-border business arrangements—all conducted beyond the reach of sanctions enforcement mechanisms centered in Washington and Brussels.
