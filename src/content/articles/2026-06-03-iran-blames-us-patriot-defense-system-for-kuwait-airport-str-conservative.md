Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) is deflecting responsibility for a deadly strike on Kuwait International Airport, claiming the damage was actually caused by a misfiring American Patriot missile defense system rather than Iranian ordinance.

According to the IRGC, the Wednesday incident that killed at least one person and damaged Terminal 1 occurred when U.S. Patriot systems failed to properly intercept incoming Iranian missiles targeting American military installations in Kuwait. "Our investigation shows that the IRGC's air force did not fire at this target, and the destruction was caused by an error in the American Patriot systems," an IRGC spokesman stated. The Pentagon, however, maintains that all Iranian missiles failed to reach their intended targets.

The missile exchange represents the latest escalation in ongoing tensions between Washington and Tehran, coming nearly 100 days into the current conflict. Iran launched the strikes on U.S. bases in Kuwait and Bahrain—including Fifth Fleet headquarters—claiming they were retaliatory for American attacks on Iranian infrastructure, including a telecom tower on Qeshm Island. The strikes also followed a U.S. attack on an Iranian tanker that Washington said was violating its unilateral blockade against the Islamic Republic.

Kuwait's Foreign Ministry condemned what it called Iran's "aggressive attacks" and warned that the Gulf nation "reserves its full and inherent right" to respond. The incident highlights the risks posed to civilian infrastructure and neutral parties when regional powers engage in military exchanges, particularly in the densely populated and strategically vital Gulf region. Video footage obtained by media outlets appears to show extensive smoke and fire damage inside the airport terminal.
