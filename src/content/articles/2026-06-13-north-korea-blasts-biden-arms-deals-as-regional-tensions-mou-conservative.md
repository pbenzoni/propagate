North Korea has sharply criticized the Biden administration's approval of advanced air-to-air missile sales to South Korea, denouncing the transaction as part of what Pyongyang calls America's destabilizing "war exports" to the region.

According to a statement from a senior North Korean Foreign Ministry official, Washington and Seoul are "systematically tightening their military collusion" despite growing concerns about stability on the Korean peninsula. The official warned that "US arms exports are war exports, and importing American weapons means accumulating tension and confrontation."

The rebuke follows State Department approval of a nearly $300 million package that includes 70 AIM-120C-8 Advanced Medium-Range Air-to-Air Missiles and related equipment. The administration has greenlit several other major arms packages for South Korea in recent weeks—including naval helicopters, attack helicopters, and guided bombs worth billions of dollars. Under a 2025 defense agreement, Seoul has pledged to purchase $25 billion in U.S. military equipment by 2030.

North Korea argues these sales are transforming South Korea into "an outpost of intense confrontation" and claims similar American arms deals with Japan and Taiwan are fueling broader Asia-Pacific tensions. Pyongyang maintains that its own military programs, including nuclear and missile development, constitute purely defensive measures necessary to counter what it views as hostile American military presence and joint exercises with regional allies.

The Korean peninsula has remained in a technical state of war since the 1950-1953 conflict ended in armistice rather than a peace treaty. The United States maintains nearly 30,000 troops in South Korea and regularly conducts joint military exercises that Washington and Seoul describe as essential for deterrence and security.
