Ben Roberts-Smith, Australia's most highly decorated war hero and recipient of the Victoria Cross, was arrested last week by federal police and charged with war crimes allegedly committed in Afghanistan between 2009 and 2012. The charges accuse him of complicity in the murder of five unarmed Afghan civilians during his multiple tours of duty.

The case has its roots in 2018 media reports alleging Australian soldiers committed atrocities in Afghanistan—claims that Roberts-Smith vigorously denied. With financial backing from media mogul Kerry Stokes, the decorated veteran filed defamation proceedings against the outlets and journalists behind the stories, triggering one of Australia's most controversial legal battles. A civil court judge eventually ruled in 2023 that key allegations against Roberts-Smith were proven on the balance of probabilities, a finding upheld on appeal.

The prosecution has fractured Australian politics and public opinion. For many on the right, Roberts-Smith represents a warrior who served his country with distinction under impossible battlefield conditions, now being sacrificed to political correctness and media hostility toward the military. His supporters point to his undisputed battlefield courage and question whether soldiers can receive fair treatment when judged years later by those who've never faced combat.

For the left, the case represents necessary accountability for alleged atrocities and a reckoning with military culture. The charges were brought under the Commonwealth Criminal Code's war crimes provisions, carrying potential life imprisonment for each count if convicted. The saga has permanently damaged the Australian military's reputation and forced the Australian War Memorial to add explanatory notes to its prominent Roberts-Smith display—a move conservatives view as historical revisionism, while progressives see it as truth-telling.
