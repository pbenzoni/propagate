A new United Nations Development Program report warns that ongoing conflict in the Middle East could push up to 32 million people worldwide into poverty, even as a tentative ceasefire holds. The economic shockwaves from disrupted energy and food markets are projected to impact 162 countries, with the heaviest toll falling on low-income nations with limited fiscal capacity to weather the storm.

UN Under-Secretary-General Alexander De Croo emphasized the far-reaching consequences of regional instability. "Conflict can undo in weeks what countries have built over years," he stated, noting that countries far removed from the fighting face disproportionate harm through higher energy and food prices. The report identifies vulnerable regions including the Gulf, Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Small Island Developing States as particularly at risk.

At the heart of the global economic concern is the Strait of Hormuz, a critical maritime chokepoint handling roughly 20% of the world's oil supply. Disruptions have pushed oil prices above $100 per barrel, while also threatening shipments of fertilizer and natural gas essential to global agriculture. The UN warns that governments now face difficult trade-offs between economic stabilization and maintaining spending on essential services like health and education.

The report estimates that up to $6 billion in targeted cash transfers may be needed to protect the most vulnerable populations from the worst economic impacts. The conflict has already displaced an estimated 3.2 million people in Iran and more than a million in Lebanon, underscoring the human cost alongside the broader economic disruption that threatens development gains built over years.
