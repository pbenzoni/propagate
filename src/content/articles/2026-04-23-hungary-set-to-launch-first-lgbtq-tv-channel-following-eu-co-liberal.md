Hungary is poised to welcome its first LGBTQ-themed television channel following a landmark ruling by the European Union's top court that struck down the country's discriminatory media law. An application to register Rainbow TV was reportedly filed just days after the EU Court of Justice found Hungary's 2021 legislation—which banned content about LGBTQ identities from media accessible to children—violated fundamental EU values.

The court ordered Budapest to repeal the law or face additional sanctions, including financial penalties. The legislation, championed by former Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, had been widely condemned by human rights advocates as targeting LGBTQ communities under the guise of child protection.

Rainbow TV aims to offer diverse programming including cultural content, arts, music, culinary shows, talk shows, and interviews. According to Hungarian outlet Media1, the channel will follow age-appropriate classifications and use technical restrictions for adult content, similar to other broadcast networks.

The development comes amid a political transition in Hungary. Orbán recently lost decisively to former ally Peter Magyar, who campaigned on repairing Hungary's relationship with Brussels and securing over €16 billion in EU funding that had been withheld due to concerns about democratic backsliding. During his campaign, Magyar pledged to build a Hungary "where no one is stigmatized for thinking differently than the majority or loving differently than the majority."

The new government will need to balance EU reconciliation with potential resistance from conservative constituencies as it navigates repealing the discriminatory law and moving toward greater inclusion.
