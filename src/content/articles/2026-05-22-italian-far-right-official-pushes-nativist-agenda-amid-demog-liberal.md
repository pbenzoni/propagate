Italian Deputy Prime Minister Antonio Tajani has sparked controversy by framing the country's demographic crisis through an explicitly anti-immigration lens, suggesting that Italians should have more children to avoid reliance on migrant workers.

Speaking at Rome's Festival del Lavoro on Thursday, Tajani—who serves in Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's center-right government—acknowledged Italy's record-low fertility rate of 1.14 children per woman, far below the 2.1 needed for population stability. Rather than proposing concrete family support policies, however, he framed the issue as a choice between native births and legal migration. "If we do have more children, then we can also say: right, let's reduce the number of legal migrants coming to work in our businesses," he stated.

Critics quickly condemned the remarks as both impractical and rooted in nationalist ideology. Senator Valeria Valente of the center-left Democratic Party called Tajani's comments "absurd," noting that the Meloni government "has done little and has done it wrong because it continues to look at the world from the keyhole of nationalisms." Valente argued Italy should instead embrace its role within a united Europe rather than pursue exclusionary policies.

Tajani offered no policy proposals to address barriers to family formation—such as childcare costs, housing affordability, or workplace protections—that demographers cite as driving Italy's decade-long decline in birthrates. The minister also did not explain how any hypothetical increase in births would address the country's immediate labor shortages, given that children would not enter the workforce for at least two decades.
