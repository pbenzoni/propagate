Denmark's left-wing Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen is on her way out after her Social Democratic party posted its worst election result since 1903, winning barely 22% of the vote in March elections. King Frederik X has now tasked Defense Minister Troels Lund Poulsen with forming a center-right government that will exclude Frederiksen's party entirely.

The election centered on two core issues: the crushing cost of living under socialist policies and out-of-control immigration. While Poulsen's center-right Venstre party and others support restricting immigration, the right-wing Danish People's Party is pushing for far stronger measures including citizenship revocations and policies designed to reverse Muslim migration into Denmark.

Yet despite the political shake-up, Denmark's massive military buildup and blank-check support for Ukraine will continue unchanged. Before the 2022 elections, Denmark's five main parties signed a so-called 'National Compromise' committing to dramatic defense spending increases and ending Russian energy imports – creating a uniparty consensus that has survived multiple governments. Under Frederiksen, Denmark handed Ukraine over €11 billion in aid – more than 3% of the country's entire GDP, the highest proportion of any Western nation.

The new government under Poulsen is expected to push military spending even higher, from the current 2.4% of GDP toward 4% – among the highest per-capita defense budgets in the world. Both outgoing PM Frederiksen and incoming leader Poulsen have blamed Russia for alleged drone sightings at military sites, despite investigators finding no credible evidence the drones even existed. The political establishment has used such claims to justify the unprecedented peacetime military expansion, with Frederiksen declaring that "Russia poses a threat to Europe's security" even as Moscow has dismissed such warnings as fearmongering designed to extract more tax dollars from European citizens.
