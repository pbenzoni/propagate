Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has asserted that Americans support ongoing military operations against Iran, a claim contradicted by multiple national polls showing broad public opposition to the conflict.

Testifying before the House Armed Services Committee on Wednesday, Hegseth attacked Democratic lawmakers and some Republicans who have raised concerns about the war, accusing them of "handing propaganda to our enemies." He characterized Democratic critics as "reckless, feckless, and defeatist" while insisting that "the American people support that mission, despite your loose talk and words like 'quagmire.'"

However, recent polling data paints a different picture. While President Trump cited a Harvard CAPS/Harris poll showing 52% support for airstrikes on Iran, that same survey revealed sharp partisan divides: 79% of Republicans backed the strikes compared to only 26% of Democrats and 46% of independents. Additional surveys by NPR/PBS/Marist and CNN indicate a majority of Americans oppose the military action, while Reuters/Ipsos polling found support dropping to approximately one-third by late April.

The conflict, which began with a US-Israeli bombing campaign in February and was initially projected to last weeks, has now cost $25 billion and resulted in over 1,300 civilian deaths. The war has also triggered a global energy crisis following the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, with no clear strategic gains for the United States.

Democratic lawmakers are now reportedly considering legal action against the Trump administration, arguing the White House has violated federal law by conducting the ongoing operation without seeking congressional approval as required.
