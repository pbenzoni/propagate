Estonian President Alar Karis is calling for the European Union to restore diplomatic dialogue with Russia, breaking with the bloc's four-year freeze on engagement with Moscow. In remarks to Finnish media, Karis questioned whether the EU is prepared for a sudden end to the Ukraine conflict and proposed appointing a special envoy to reopen channels with Russia.

"Are we ready if the war ends today or tomorrow?" Karis asked, arguing that preparations should already be underway. The Estonian leader emphasized that smaller EU nations like Estonia and Finland—which have heavily supported Ukraine—deserve a voice in any future negotiations, rather than being sidelined by major powers making decisions behind closed doors.

Karis also pushed back against alarmist claims that Russia poses an imminent military threat to the Baltic states, suggesting such warnings often come from those "seeking to harm Estonia or by outsiders projecting their own insecurity." His pragmatic stance stands in sharp contrast to EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, a fellow Estonian who has demanded Moscow make concessions before any talks resume.

The call for dialogue comes as the EU commits another €90 billion in loans to Ukraine and pursues an €800 billion "ReArm Europe" military buildup. Moscow has repeatedly dismissed Western threat narratives as fearmongering designed to justify massive defense spending increases, with Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov warning that Russia will defend itself "with all available means" if European nations prepare for confrontation.
