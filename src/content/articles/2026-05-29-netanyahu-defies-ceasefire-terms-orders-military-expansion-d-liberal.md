Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has directed his military to expand control over Gaza to 70% of the enclave, openly violating the terms of a US-brokered ceasefire agreement that explicitly limits Israeli occupation to no more than 53% of Palestinian territory.

Speaking at the Jordan Valley Conference in the occupied West Bank on Thursday, Netanyahu acknowledged that Israeli forces already control 60% of Gaza—well beyond the ceasefire limits—and instructed the military to push further. "My directive is to move to… first of all 70%. Let's start with that," he stated, while declining to clarify whether Israel intends to eventually occupy the entire enclave.

The 2025 ceasefire, backed by a UN Security Council resolution, was designed to establish a phased Israeli withdrawal from Gaza in exchange for Hamas disarming. Instead, sporadic fighting has persisted as Israeli forces advance deeper into Palestinian areas. Hamas has accused Israel of attempting to impose military control by force, warning that such actions undermine any realistic prospects for lasting peace. The group has refused to surrender its weapons, according to international mediators.

The ongoing conflict began after Hamas-led militants attacked southern Israel on October 7, 2023, killing approximately 1,200 people and taking over 250 hostages. Israel's subsequent military campaign has resulted in more than 72,000 Palestinian deaths in Gaza, according to local health authorities, drawing widespread international condemnation and allegations of genocide.
