North Korea has sharply criticized the United States for approving hundreds of millions of dollars in advanced weapons sales to South Korea, calling the transfers "war exports" that threaten to escalate military tensions on the Korean peninsula. According to North Korean state media KCNA, a senior Foreign Ministry official accused Washington and Seoul of "systematically tightening their military collusion" despite international concerns about regional stability.

The statement follows U.S. State Department approval of a nearly $300 million package that includes 70 advanced air-to-air missiles and related equipment. North Korea pointed to several other recent arms deals worth billions of dollars, including attack helicopters, naval helicopters, and guided bombs. Pyongyang also highlighted a 2025 defense agreement under which South Korea pledged to purchase $25 billion in U.S. military equipment by 2030.

"U.S. arms exports are war exports, and importing American weapons means accumulating tension and confrontation," the North Korean official said, arguing that Washington is transforming South Korea into "an outpost of intense confrontation." The statement also referenced U.S. arms sales to Japan and Taiwan, claiming they are fueling broader instability across the Asia-Pacific region.

The Korean peninsula has remained divided since the end of World War II, with the 1950-1953 Korean War ending in an armistice rather than a formal peace treaty—leaving the two sides technically still at war. The United States maintains nearly 30,000 troops in South Korea and regularly conducts joint military exercises that Pyongyang views as hostile provocations. In response, North Korea has continued developing its nuclear and missile programs, which it describes as defensive deterrents against what it perceives as threats from Washington and its regional allies.
