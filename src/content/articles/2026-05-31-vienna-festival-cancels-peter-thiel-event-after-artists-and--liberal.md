A major European cultural event has canceled a scheduled appearance by billionaire tech investor Peter Thiel after facing mounting pressure from artists, sponsors, and city officials. The Vienna Festival had planned to host Thiel for a discussion titled "Armageddon and the Antichrist? From Theology to Realpolitik," but organizers ultimately pulled the event following what they described as a mass exodus of participants.

The co-founder of PayPal and Palantir Technologies—a surveillance technology company with controversial government contracts—has become known for espousing far-right political views and promoting "transhumanist" ideologies. Vienna's City Councillor for Culture, Veronica Kaup-Hasler, publicly criticized the decision to platform Thiel, telling *Der Standard* newspaper that "the invitation to Peter Thiel is quite rightly causing great discontent among the public."

Artistic Director Milo Rau acknowledged in a statement that while he initially found the event "extremely compelling," the controversy had weakened the festival "to an unacceptable extent." The decision to cancel came after multiple participants threatened to withdraw entirely from the festival, forcing organizers to choose between platforming the billionaire or preserving the integrity of their broader artistic program.

Thiel has previously sparked controversy with invitation-only lectures on apocalyptic religious themes held in Rome near the Vatican. His public activities have drawn criticism from progressive communities concerned about providing platforms for individuals whose companies are deeply embedded in military and surveillance infrastructure while promoting divisive ideological frameworks.
