A new border security report landed this week and, surprising no one who has been paying attention, its recommendations look a great deal like the procurement wishlist of the three vendors who funded the underlying "working group." The report calls for drones, for sensor arrays, for an expansion of the detention footprint. It calls, in other words, for more of what we already bought and which has not delivered what we were told it would.

The humanitarian arguments are familiar and worth repeating: people fleeing violence and climate shocks are not a logistics problem and cannot be engineered into one. But even on its own terms — as a procurement document — this report is a bad deal. It does not audit the last round of spending. It does not benchmark outcomes. It is a press release with footnotes.

A real policy conversation would start by asking what we are actually trying to accomplish and whether the tools in the current toolbox accomplish it. This report does not ask that question, because the people who commissioned it already know the answer and would prefer you didn't.
