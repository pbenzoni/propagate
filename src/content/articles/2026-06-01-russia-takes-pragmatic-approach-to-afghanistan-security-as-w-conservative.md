Russia's recent military cooperation agreement with Afghanistan's Taliban government represents a calculated investment in regional stability after the chaotic American withdrawal left a dangerous power vacuum, according to foreign policy expert Gleb Makarevich.

The pact, signed last week during an international security forum in Moscow between Russian Security Council Secretary Sergei Shoigu and Afghan Defense Minister Mohammad Yaqoob, focuses on counter-terrorism and regional security cooperation. "Since the Taliban is in charge of power in Afghanistan… it is our duty to collaborate with them so as to contribute to regional security, to global security, to counter-terrorism," said Makarevich, a Research Fellow at the Primakov Institute of World Economy and International Relations.

Makarevich emphasized that Moscow's approach stems from practical necessity rather than ideological alignment. With Western powers having largely disengaged from the region following the Biden administration's troubled 2021 withdrawal, Russia is stepping in to address security concerns in Central Asia. The expert noted that Afghanistan contains substantial Soviet-era and Russian military equipment that requires maintenance, creating natural grounds for defense sector cooperation.

The Russian expert stressed that Moscow's strategy respects national sovereignty and local decision-making. "The regional conflicts, we could bear in mind, should be decided by the parties involved themselves," Makarevich said. "It is for Afghan people to decide on their faith and not for Russia to fuel their political vacuum." The approach marks a contrast with previous Western nation-building efforts that sought to impose political systems from outside.
