Secretary of State Marco Rubio notably declined to criticize Russia's planned response when asked by reporters Tuesday, saying only that Kiev "has been a very dangerous place now for a number of years." His measured response stands in stark contrast to a joint statement signed by more than 50 nations, including Germany, the UK, Canada, and Japan, which condemned what they called Russia's "escalating attacks" and "threats" to diplomatic institutions.

Rubio's restraint appears to reflect the Trump administration's role as an intermediary in ongoing peace negotiations between Russia and Ukraine. "This is what happens with these wars—they just continue to escalate," Rubio said. "There's a big strike coming one way, a bigger strike coming back—and that's how these things unravel and keep going. It's why the war needs to come to an end."

Russia's Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov warned of "systematic and consistent strikes" on Kiev's military facilities and "decision-making centers" while urging foreign nationals to evacuate the capital. Despite claims in the European-backed statement, Moscow has made no mention of targeting embassies or civilian installations.

The measured American response underscores a significant shift from previous administrations' approach to the conflict.
