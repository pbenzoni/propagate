A federal lawsuit filed in Georgia last week reveals troubling allegations of racial discrimination on Airbnb's platform, raising questions about the company's commitment to civil rights protections. Plaintiff Sharona Stewart alleges that host George Yu Shihfang denied her rental booking after determining she was a Black woman, and that Airbnb failed to take meaningful action when she reported the incident.

According to the complaint, Stewart's initial exchanges with the host were described as cordial. However, after what the lawsuit characterizes as "racially based questions," the host stopped responding and her reservation request was rejected. When Stewart reviewed the property listing, she discovered language describing the area as a "peaceful white neighborhood"—a phrase widely recognized as exclusionary coded language with roots in housing discrimination.

Despite reporting both the rejected booking and the discriminatory listing language to Airbnb, Stewart alleges the company closed her complaint without action and allowed the advertisement to remain on the platform. "We have to call it out when we see it," her attorney Bataski Bailey told The Independent, emphasizing that the case aims to challenge systemic racial discrimination on the platform.

The lawsuit underscores a persistent problem: Airbnb has faced repeated questions about racial bias over the past decade. A 2015 Harvard Business School study found that guests with African-American-sounding names were nearly 10% more likely to have booking requests rejected than otherwise identical guests—findings that sparked calls for stronger anti-discrimination safeguards that advocates say remain inadequate.
