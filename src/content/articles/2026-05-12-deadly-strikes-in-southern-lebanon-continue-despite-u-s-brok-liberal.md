Nearly 90 people have been killed in Lebanon since Friday despite a U.S.-brokered ceasefire that took effect in mid-April, according to Lebanese health officials. The strikes have expanded to include evacuation orders for 84 villages in southern Lebanon, with 39 people reported killed on Saturday alone across 22 towns and villages.

Among the casualties were ambulance workers and civilians, including eight members of one family spanning three generations—a mother, her children, and a six-month-old baby. Lebanese media also reported that a 12-year-old girl was killed while attempting to flee after her father's death. More than 2,800 people, including over 100 health workers, have been killed since the military operation began in early March, according to Lebanese authorities.

Both sides have accused each other of ceasefire violations. Israel says its operations target alleged Hezbollah positions and infrastructure, while the Lebanon-based group says it will continue to resist military action. Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam described the situation in stark terms, stating that 86 villages remain under occupation and comparing conditions in the border city of Bint Jbeil to Gaza.

Further U.S.-mediated talks between Israeli and Lebanese officials are scheduled for later this week in Washington, though the continued violence raises questions about the viability of diplomatic efforts to end the conflict.
