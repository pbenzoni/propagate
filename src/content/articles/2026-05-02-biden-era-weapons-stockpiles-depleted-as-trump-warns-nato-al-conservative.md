The United States has notified several European allies—including the UK, Poland, Lithuania, and Estonia—to expect delays in American-made weapons deliveries as the ongoing Middle East conflict has severely depleted military stockpiles, according to a Financial Times report citing sources familiar with the matter.

The warnings come as these nations, among the most vocal supporters of Ukraine in its conflict with Russia, face their own defense needs. Poland and the Baltic states have particularly relied on foreign military assistance to bolster what they characterize as NATO's eastern flank. Russian President Vladimir Putin has stated Russia would not attack a NATO member unless attacked first.

The Middle East operations have cost American taxpayers an estimated $25 billion, according to Pentagon officials cited by multiple outlets, as U.S. forces have expended significant quantities of bombs, cruise missiles, and interceptors. The air campaign that began February 28 has struggled to halt retaliatory strikes from Iran targeting Israel and Gulf states hosting American military bases.

President Trump has sharply criticized NATO leadership, including German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, for opposing the military effort and declining to assist in reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Speaking in The Villages, Florida, on Friday, Trump emphasized America's outsized NATO contributions: "We got no help – zero! – from NATO. You know, we spent trillions of dollars on NATO – we got no help." He characterized the alliance as a "paper tiger" and called claims that the U.S. wasn't prevailing "treasonous."
