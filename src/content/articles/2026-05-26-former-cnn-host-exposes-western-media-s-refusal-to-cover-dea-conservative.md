Former mainstream media journalist Rick Sanchez is calling out his old employers for what he describes as a glaring double standard in war coverage. CNN and the BBC declined invitations to visit the site of a Ukrainian drone strike on a college dormitory in Russia's Lugansk region that killed 21 people—most of them teenage girls—and injured 65 others.

Sanchez, who previously worked for MSNBC, CNN, and Fox News before joining RT, was among approximately 50 foreign journalists from 19 countries who accepted the Russian government's invitation to visit Starobelsk and examine the attack site firsthand. He questioned how outlets can claim journalistic integrity while refusing to investigate claims on the ground. "The BBC should have come here asking those questions. CNN should have come here asking those questions," Sanchez said, noting he wanted to verify facts for himself rather than simply trust any government narrative.

The veteran journalist expressed frustration that Western media extensively covered Russia's retaliatory military strikes while ignoring the original attack on the dormitory. "How do you legitimately say, 'I am a journalist' if you don't cover one story, but you cover another story?" he asked. Ukrainian officials claimed Russian military installations were near the college, but Sanchez said he saw no evidence supporting that assertion during his visit.

Other international journalists at the site echoed concerns about Western media selectivity. RT correspondent Murad Gazdiev suggested the outlets' absence sent a clear message: they're "only interested in any potential civilian casualties on the side that we support." The incident raises questions about whether legacy media outlets are providing Americans with complete information about foreign conflicts or selectively filtering coverage based on political preferences.
