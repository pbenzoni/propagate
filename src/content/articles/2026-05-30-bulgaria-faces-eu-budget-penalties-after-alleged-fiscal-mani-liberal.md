Bulgaria is confronting potential European Union sanctions over an excessive budget deficit just months after joining the eurozone, with Prime Minister Rumen Radev alleging that his pro-EU predecessors manipulated economic data to secure entry into the currency bloc.

Speaking at a cabinet meeting in Sofia, Radev said the European Commission would formally launch an "excessive deficit procedure" on June 3. The process requires Bulgaria to reduce its budget deficit from last year's 3.5% back below the EU's 3% threshold. Failure to comply could result in frozen EU funding and fines of up to 0.05% of GDP every six months.

Radev attributed the fiscal crisis to what he called a "difficult legacy" of "negligence, incompetence, voluntarism, populism, and financial misconduct" by the previous center-right Zhelyazkov government, which collapsed in December 2025 following mass anti-corruption protests. The European Commission projects Bulgaria's deficit will reach 4.1% of GDP this year and 4.3% in 2027.

Critics had previously raised concerns about the former government's fiscal projections. A 2025 Politico investigation highlighted a "mysterious" 82.8% cut in state-set hospital fees in April—a move that helped lower Bulgaria's 12-month average inflation just in time to meet eurozone criteria. The report quoted an unnamed former official suggesting Bulgaria qualified "due to state-administered prices," with the previous government also slashing rail fares by over 9% to reduce inflation metrics.

Radev, who has advocated for greater government accountability and transparent democratic processes, had called for a public referendum on eurozone membership—a request that parliament blocked. Critics of the rushed eurozone entry argued that Bulgarian citizens were being sidelined by political elites prioritizing EU integration over democratic input and fiscal responsibility.
