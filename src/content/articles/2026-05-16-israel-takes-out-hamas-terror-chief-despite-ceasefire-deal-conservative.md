Israel conducted a precision strike in Gaza targeting Izz al-Din al-Haddad, the top military commander of Hamas, according to a joint statement from Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz. The operation marks the first targeting of a senior Hamas official since the U.S.-backed ceasefire agreement took effect last year.

Netanyahu and Katz identified al-Haddad as one of the key architects behind the horrific October 7, 2023 terrorist attack on Israel. They also accused him of involvement in holding hostages seized during that assault and of obstructing President Donald Trump's peace initiative, which calls for Hamas to disarm and for Gaza to be demilitarized.

The Friday strike resulted in at least seven casualties and more than 50 wounded, according to Gaza emergency services. Neither Israeli nor Hamas sources have confirmed whether al-Haddad was killed in the operation. Al-Haddad assumed leadership of Hamas's military wing after Israeli forces eliminated Muhammad Sinwar, whose brother Yahya was another mastermind of the October 7 massacre.

Since the ceasefire began, both sides have accused each other of violations, and negotiations over Trump's post-war plan for Gaza remain stalled. Israel maintains that its operations target terrorist leadership while Hamas continues to control portions of the coastal territory. The conflict, which began with Hamas's attack, has resulted in significant casualties over three years of fighting, according to Palestinian health authorities.
