First Lady Melania Trump is calling for ABC to terminate late-night host Jimmy Kimmel after he made what she characterized as a threatening joke about her becoming a widow—mere days before an armed man attempted to breach the White House Correspondents' Dinner where President Trump was present.

In a sketch on 'Jimmy Kimmel Live,' the comedian appeared in a tuxedo delivering what he claimed was practice material for the White House dinner. Spotting Melania in his imaginary audience, Kimmel quipped, "Mrs. Trump, you have a glow like an expectant widow." Just two nights later, 31-year-old California teacher Cole Allen, armed with guns and knives, attempted to enter the actual dinner at the Washington Hilton. Secret Service intercepted him and security personnel opened fire. Allen was subsequently arrested and charged with attempted assassination of the president.

"Kimmel's hateful and violent rhetoric is intended to divide our country," the First Lady wrote on X Monday, calling the comedian "a coward" and demanding ABC take action. "His words are corrosive and deepen the political sickness within America. People like Kimmel shouldn't have the opportunity to enter our homes each evening to spread hate… Enough is enough. It is time for ABC to take a stand." President Trump echoed her sentiments, describing Kimmel's remarks as a "despicable call to violence" and "something far beyond the pale."

Kimmel remained defiant in his Monday night show, claiming his joke was merely a "light roast" about the couple's age difference and insisting he has been "very vocal for many years speaking out against gun violence." He dismissed the First Lady's criticism as a "Twitter vomit storm" while maintaining that all parties have free speech rights. ABC has declined to comment on potential disciplinary action, though the network briefly suspended Kimmel last fall over controversial remarks following the assassination of conservative speaker Charlie Kirk before reinstating him with a contract extension.

The incident has reignited debate over whether mainstream entertainment figures have crossed the line from political satire into dangerous rhetoric that could inspire violence against conservative leaders and their families.
