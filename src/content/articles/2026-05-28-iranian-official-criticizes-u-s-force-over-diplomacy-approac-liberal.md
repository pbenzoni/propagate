A senior Iranian security official has sharply criticized what he characterized as an American preference for military pressure over genuine diplomatic engagement, highlighting the deep mistrust that continues to plague U.S.-Iran relations.

Ali Bagheri, deputy secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council and former foreign minister, told Russian media that U.S. negotiators are making demands he described as disconnected from geopolitical realities. "Iran, like other independent countries, believes in peace through dialogue and negotiations," Bagheri said during meetings in Moscow. "The US does not believe in peace through diplomacy. It believes in peace through force, aggression, and barbarism."

The comments come amid renewed tensions following Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz to vessels from countries it deems hostile, a move prompted by military strikes. Bagheri defended Iran's assertion of control over the strategic waterway as essential to national security, while attributing broader regional instability to the military presence of the United States and Israel.

Despite reports this week that negotiators may be nearing a preliminary framework, significant gaps remain. Both Washington and Tehran have publicly accused the other side of imposing unacceptable conditions, underscoring the fragility of diplomatic progress. Bagheri specifically blamed U.S. regional military positioning as an obstacle to sustainable peace, while expressing confidence in Iran's relationships with Gulf states when outside powers are not involved.
