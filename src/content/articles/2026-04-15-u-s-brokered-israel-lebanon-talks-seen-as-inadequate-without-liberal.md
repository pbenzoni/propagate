US-mediated negotiations between Israel and Lebanon are unlikely to achieve lasting peace without meaningful American pressure on Israel to halt its airstrikes and ground operations, according to Naim Joseph Salem, a professor of international affairs and diplomacy at the Lebanese Army Military Academy.

Israel and Lebanon held rare direct talks in Washington this week, though the Lebanon-based armed group Hezbollah boycotted the negotiations as "futile." While both parties characterized the discussions as "constructive," Salem expressed skepticism that the format would yield results, warning of "drawn-out negotiations with no end in sight and no conclusive outcome" given Washington's apparent unwillingness to leverage its influence over Israel.

The Israeli military has conducted extensive airstrikes on Beirut and other Lebanese cities, displacing more than 1.2 million people through sweeping evacuation orders in southern Lebanon. Israel has framed these operations as efforts to expand a "security zone" following Hezbollah's rocket and mortar attacks in support of Iran.

Salem described Israel's approach as aimed at forcing mass displacement, stating that "several towns have been leveled" by Israeli forces. He warned that the campaign threatens to turn approximately 450,000 residents of southern Lebanese cities and towns into refugees, raising concerns about a humanitarian crisis and regional instability.
