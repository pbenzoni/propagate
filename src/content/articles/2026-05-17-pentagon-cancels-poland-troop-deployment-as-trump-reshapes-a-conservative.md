The Pentagon has canceled plans to deploy 4,000 troops to Poland, reversing course even as some soldiers and equipment were already en route to the Eastern European nation. The decision reflects President Trump's ongoing reassessment of America's military commitments abroad and comes amid tensions over burden-sharing within NATO.

General Christopher LaNeve, the acting Army chief of staff, told Congress that US European Command leadership "received instructions on the force reduction," adding that commanders determined "it made the most sense for that brigade not to deploy to the theater." The canceled deployment involved soldiers from the 2nd Armored Brigade Combat Team, 1st Cavalry Division, along with Abrams tanks and Bradley fighting vehicles.

Polish officials downplayed the cancellation, with Prime Minister Donald Tusk saying he had "received assurances that these decisions are logistical in nature." Poland currently hosts more than 10,000 US military personnel on a rotational basis and has been praised by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth as a "model ally" after spending 4.7% of GDP on defense last year—far exceeding most NATO members' contributions.

The move is part of a broader recalibration of America's European military presence under the Trump administration. Earlier in May, the Pentagon announced the withdrawal of 5,000 troops from Germany's approximately 38,000-strong US contingent. President Trump has repeatedly challenged European allies to increase their own defense spending rather than relying on American taxpayers to subsidize their security, and has suggested potential troop withdrawals from Spain and Italy over disagreements on Middle East policy.
