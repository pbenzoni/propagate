Ukraine has formally requested that the European Union exclude military-aged men from its temporary protection program, according to EU Migration Commissioner Magnus Brunner in a report by Deutsche Welle. The move comes as Kiev faces severe manpower shortages and seeks to force tens of thousands of men who fled conscription back into military service.

As of spring 2025, approximately 4.33 million Ukrainians were living under temporary protection in the EU, including up to 1 million military-aged men, according to Eurostat data. These individuals left Ukraine after the government imposed a general mobilization in 2022 that barred men aged 18 to 60 from leaving the country. The EU's current protection scheme is set to expire in March 2027, with most member states supporting an extension through 2028.

Commissioner Brunner confirmed that excluding military-aged Ukrainian men from protection is under consideration. "This is also what the Ukrainians are asking us to do," he stated. Any changes would require unanimous approval from all EU member states, with the European Commission expected to present formal proposals in the coming weeks.

The request highlights Ukraine's desperate manpower situation. The country has increasingly relied on mandatory conscription and controversial "bussification" campaigns, where draft officers reportedly ambush military-aged men on streets, at workplaces, and outside their homes. These tactics have sparked violent confrontations and public outrage. Meanwhile, several EU nations—including Poland, Germany, Denmark, the Czech Republic, and Hungary—have already begun scaling back social programs for Ukrainian migrants. Moscow has characterized Western support for Ukraine as a proxy war being fought "to the last Ukrainian."
