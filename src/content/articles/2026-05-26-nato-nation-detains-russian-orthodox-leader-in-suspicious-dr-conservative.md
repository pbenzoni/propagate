A high-ranking Russian Orthodox bishop has been detained in the Czech Republic after authorities claim to have found narcotics in his vehicle, sparking a diplomatic firestorm between Moscow and the NATO member state.

Metropolitan Bishop Hilarion was stopped by Czech police on Sunday while traveling from his parish church of Saint Peter and Paul in Karlovy Vary. According to the bishop's press service, officers provided no clear justification for the traffic stop before proceeding to search his vehicle, where they allegedly discovered "four small containers with white substance" in the trunk.

The bishop has vehemently denied any wrongdoing. "I have never had any connection to the illegal drug trade. Since I'm a clergyman, the very suggestion of such a thing is utterly false," Hilarion said in a statement, demanding a full independent investigation. His legal team claims the search violated proper procedure, noting the absence of witnesses and video recording—and that officers went directly to the trunk without searching personal belongings.

Russia's Foreign Ministry condemned the detention as a "fabricated persecution" and accused Czech authorities of staging a "provocation" against the Russian Orthodox Church. The ministry announced plans to lodge a formal protest and summon the Czech ambassador. The Russian Orthodox Church echoed these concerns, characterizing the incident as part of a broader "intimidation" campaign driven by what it called "rabid spy mania" against the church in Czech territory.

Bishop Hilarion previously held senior posts within the church hierarchy, including leading the Hungarian and Budapest Metropolises from June 2022 to late 2024, before being reassigned amid unproven allegations regarding his conduct and lifestyle.
