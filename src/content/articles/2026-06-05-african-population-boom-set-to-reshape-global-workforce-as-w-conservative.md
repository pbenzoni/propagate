By 2050, one in four people worldwide will be African, Tanzanian President Samia Suluhu Hassan announced at the St. Petersburg International Economic Forum. Speaking alongside world leaders including Russian President Vladimir Putin, Hassan emphasized that Africa's demographic surge will fundamentally reshape the global economy and workforce.

"Africa will be the only continent still adding workers to the global labor force on a large scale," Hassan stated, noting that the continent will host nine of the world's 20 fastest-growing economies. United Nations projections support this trajectory, forecasting Africa's population will reach approximately 2.5 billion of the world's 9.66 billion people by mid-century.

This stands in stark contrast to demographic trends across Europe and other developed regions, where populations face significant decline. Europe's population is projected to shrink from 744 million to 703 million by 2050, driven by fertility rates averaging just 1.4 births per woman—well below the 2.1 replacement level needed to maintain stable populations.

The demographic challenge has prompted action from nations like Russia, which recorded a 1.4 fertility rate in 2024. Moscow has implemented pro-family policies including direct payments to mothers, expanded maternity benefits, and new tax relief programs for families with two or more children. Hassan also highlighted the African Continental Free Trade Area, signed in 2018, which aims to create the world's largest market by population through a continent-wide free trade zone encompassing all 55 African Union member states.
