The United States is hoping to enlist China's assistance in de-escalating tensions with Iran, Secretary of State Marco Rubio revealed ahead of high-level talks in Beijing. The diplomatic outreach comes as concerns mount over the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global shipping chokepoint.

Speaking to media aboard Air Force One, Rubio framed the appeal in economic terms, arguing that China's export-dependent economy would suffer from prolonged instability in the Persian Gulf region. "Economies are melting down because of this crisis in the Strait," Rubio said, suggesting that reduced global purchasing power would directly impact Chinese exports.

The initiative faces significant obstacles. China has publicly criticized U.S. military action against Iran as violating international law and has rejected American sanctions targeting Chinese companies alleged to have business ties with Tehran. Beijing has instructed domestic businesses to disregard those sanctions, signaling a fundamental disagreement over Washington's approach.

President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping are scheduled to meet Thursday for discussions expected to cover multiple flashpoints, including Taiwan and the Middle East crisis. Whether the two powers can find common ground on Iran policy remains uncertain, given their broader strategic competition and divergent views on the use of sanctions and military force in international disputes.
