A senior Iranian government official issued a stark warning Sunday that Tehran may respond militarily to ongoing U.S. naval operations restricting access to Iranian ports, raising fears of further escalation in a region already destabilized by weeks of conflict.

Mohsen Rezaei, who serves on an advisory body to Iran's Supreme Leader, told state television that the naval restrictions—which he characterized as a blockade and therefore an act of war under international law—are causing significant disruption to global shipping and commerce. "The longer they continue the naval blockade of Iran, the greater the damage to the world economy will be," Rezaei stated, adding that Iran's patience with the maritime restrictions was "running out."

The warning comes as the Trump administration has intensified pressure on Iran through both military and rhetorical means. Over the weekend, President Trump posted threatening messages on social media, including imagery that appeared to suggest the partition or invasion of Iran by neighboring states. Critics described the posts as reckless provocations that undermine diplomatic efforts and risk wider regional conflict.

The current crisis stems from a U.S.-Israeli military operation that targeted Iranian leadership and infrastructure, prompting retaliatory strikes from Tehran. Despite a fragile ceasefire announced in early April, both sides have continued to reject conditions for a broader settlement.
