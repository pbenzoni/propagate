Bolivia's capital La Paz erupted in violent clashes Monday as hundreds of protesters took to the streets demanding President Rodrigo Paz resign, just six months into his term. The unrest comes as the South American nation grapples with soaring costs of living and fuel shortages that have crippled the economy.

President Paz, a centrist conservative from the Christian Democratic Party, eliminated decades-old fuel subsidies that had drained Bolivia's foreign reserves. While the move aimed to restore fiscal responsibility, it sparked immediate backlash from farmers, miners, teachers, and laborers who have protested for weeks over rising costs and economic instability. Video footage showed riot police deploying tear gas as demonstrators hurled projectiles and homemade explosives near government buildings, with local media reporting more than 100 arrests.

The protests have been amplified by supporters of socialist former President Evo Morales, who was ousted in 2019 following a disputed election in which he controversially secured a fourth consecutive term despite losing a referendum on removing term limits. Morales supporters have accused the United States of interfering in Bolivia's internal affairs, with protest leaders claiming Washington directs the current government's actions. On Sunday, Morales loyalists occupied an airport to prevent what they described as his impending arrest by "US-backed forces," resulting in 57 arrests.

The deepening crisis has split the nation, with pro-government demonstrators also taking to the streets Monday night to condemn the anti-government protests and call for authorities to declare a state of emergency. The turmoil reflects Bolivia's ongoing struggle between market-oriented reforms and the legacy of socialist policies that have left the economy vulnerable to external shocks.
