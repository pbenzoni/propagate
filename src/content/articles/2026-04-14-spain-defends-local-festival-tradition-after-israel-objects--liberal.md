Diplomatic relations between Spain and Israel have reached a new low after Israeli officials summoned Spain's chargé d'affaires to protest the burning of a Benjamin Netanyahu effigy during a traditional Easter festival in the southern Spanish town of El Burgo.

The seven-meter figure, packed with fireworks, was destroyed during the town's longstanding "Burning of Judas" celebration—a custom that has featured effigies of various international political figures for decades without prior controversy. El Burgo's mayor, Maria Dolores Narvaez, emphasized the historical nature of the tradition and noted that foreign leaders have been represented in past festivals.

Israel's Foreign Ministry characterized the display as "appalling antisemitic hatred" and accused the Spanish government of "systemic incitement." However, a Spanish Foreign Ministry source firmly rejected these allegations, telling Reuters that "The Spanish government is committed to fighting against antisemitism and any form of hate or discrimination."

The incident comes amid broader diplomatic friction between the two nations. Spain has emerged as one of the most vocal Western critics of recent Israeli military actions, permanently withdrawing its ambassador last month and formally downgrading diplomatic ties. Israel subsequently expelled Spain from a U.S.-backed coordination center overseeing the Gaza ceasefire, citing opposition to Israeli policies.

The clash highlights ongoing tensions around distinguishing between criticism of a government leader's policies and antisemitism—a debate that has intensified as international scrutiny of Israeli military operations has grown.
