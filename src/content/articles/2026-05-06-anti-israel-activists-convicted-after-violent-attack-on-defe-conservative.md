A British court delivered guilty verdicts Tuesday against four pro-Palestinian activists who orchestrated a violent raid on an Israeli-linked defense facility in Bristol, causing an estimated £1 million ($1.36 million) in property damage and seriously injuring a police officer.

The defendants—Charlotte Head, 30, Samuel Corner, 23, Leona Kamio, 30, and Fatema Zainab Rajwani, 21—drove a decommissioned prison van through the gates of Elbit Systems' Filton facility on August 6, 2024. Armed with sledgehammers and crowbars, they destroyed computers, drones, and other equipment while clashing with security personnel and responding officers. Woolwich Crown Court found all four guilty of criminal damage after more than 14 hours of jury deliberation.

Corner was separately convicted of inflicting grievous bodily harm on Sergeant Kate Evans, whom he struck in the back with a sledgehammer during the raid, fracturing her spine. The officer remained off duty for three months and continues on restricted duties more than 20 months later. Corner claimed he acted in panic after being pepper-sprayed, though prosecutors emphasized the premeditated nature of the break-in itself.

The activists claimed they were acting to prevent what they characterized as weapons manufacturing for the Israeli military. Elbit Systems, which operates more than a dozen sites across the UK, has consistently denied manufacturing or exporting weapons to Israeli forces. The convicted defendants face sentencing on June 12, with maximum penalties of up to ten years for the property damage charges and five years for Corner's assault conviction.

The incident was among those that led the British government to designate Palestine Action as a terrorist organization in 2025, though a High Court later ruled that ban unlawful. The case highlights ongoing tensions over lawful protest versus violent property destruction and attacks on law enforcement.
